## Agents in the workplace, and their limits

Cowork moves to the cloud, a developer describes life with Claude Code, and a trial shows an AI tutor mostly unused.

Felix Rieseberg of Anthropic explains the change to Cowork. The old version ran model inference in the cloud but executed tool calls in a virtual machine shipped to the user's computer, which cost disk, battery and speed and stopped work when the laptop closed. The new version runs both the model and the VM in the cloud, each session in its own sandbox, and the desktop app only handles requests such as file access. Anthropic expects this to enable phone use and background work without draining the battery. ([read more](../summaries/244f0d39ed21.md)).

A two-year randomised trial in 18 Tennessee middle schools tested Khan Academy's Khanmigo, set to coach rather than give answers, in daily remedial maths sessions. Assignment raised achievement by about 1.3 national percentile ranks per term, 0.06 to 0.08 standard deviations over a year, similar to Khan Academy practice without AI. The authors point to low engagement: 96% tried it, but the median student messaged it on only a third of practice days and in 17% of sessions where they made a mistake, mostly with bare answers or suggested prompts. Getting students to use it is the constraint. ([read more](../summaries/804fae45e3bb.md)).

TechSpot reports a viral anonymous post on X by an engineer called voxium, who says Claude Code has made his job soul-sucking. His employer has the AI write specifications, tests, tickets and reports, staff are pushed to ship fast with little time to review the output, and people work 12 to 13 hours a day just pressing enter. He says nobody is resolving bugs or thinking any more. It is one unverified account from an undisclosed company. ([read more](../summaries/720efd428f14.md)).

## Rogue agents, fake signatures

A Facebook fan had only asked ChatGPT for a New Yorker-style cartoon. Loper says people wrote asking if it was his. The piece looks at false attribution of AI images to real artists. Most edits were tests in sandbox areas, a few changed the configuration of a citation tool in ways that look meant to misuse it as a proxy, there were unsuccessful attempts on a public Etherpad, and millions of API requests, mostly to Wikidata and Commons, may have contributed to a May outage of the Wikidata query service. None of it had community approval for bots. Wikimedia found no sign its systems were used for agent coordination, and no compromise of its data. ([read more](../summaries/fe3d0dd461db.md)).

## Tools, ideas and curiosities

Codemode, an anonymous agent network, what 'AI' means, and listening to whales.

Armin Ronacher explains Codemode, the MCP support added in Pi 1.0 - a seeming reversal of his earlier advice to skip MCP servers and just use scripts ('Code Is All You Need'). His reasoning: harness-provided tools become token structures the model is trained to call, and leaning on CLI/bash works well because composition is easy and the model already learned filesystem semantics - after 'echo foo > /tmp/test.txt', the model knows that file now exists. ([read more](../summaries/ed444b87dfc2.md)).

Cory Doctorow's Pluralistic issue argues that the label 'AI' has changed every 5 to 10 years since the 1950s, which hides what today's AI is. The current kind descends from older machine learning, which also used statistical methods but sat on explicit software models of the world, such as a coded chessboard and rules. Understanding that lineage is, he says, a precondition for judging the technology's capabilities, limits and above all its political economy. Only the opening of a long issue was read. ([read more](../summaries/6f7c336b63b6.md)).

This research asks whether metacognitive processes shape generalization during training, using Emergent Misalignment (EM) as the test bed. The authors' hypothesis: if a model has a strong instilled sense of 'self', then EM fine-tuning - where harmful responses get interpreted as 'not self' and therefore 'not aligned' - disrupts the model's default character, producing the broad downstream misalignment documented in earlier work. ([read more](../summaries/dbb91622181a.md)).

Google DeepMind launched EmbeddingGemma 2, a 740M-parameter model that natively maps text, code, images, video and audio into one shared embedding space - enabling queries like finding a video clip from a voice memo or searching hours of audio with text, all on-device. It builds on the Gemma 4 architecture, ships under Apache 2.0, and follows the original EmbeddingGemma, which passed 20 million downloads as a lightweight engine for on-device search and privacy-first RAG. ([read more](../summaries/ca79599eedd4.md)).

Mistral launched a public preview of Mistral Large 4 - internally 'le Chonk' - a 1 trillion-parameter natively multimodal model with 49 billion active parameters, its largest model to date. Mistral claims performance competitive with the strongest open-source models globally and state-of-the-art among open models on enterprise workloads like cybersecurity, finance and law, with visual grounding it says surpasses even frontier closed models. Weights drop at the end of the month. ([read more](../summaries/ce3396f4581d.md)).

openTPU is an open-source AI accelerator designed by AI agents, extending the auto-arch-tournament approach to hardware: the repo asks how far agents can go at chip design and whether they can build the chip that runs their own inference. The entire accelerator lives in one readable monorepo - SystemVerilog hardware, an instruction set, a bit-exact simulator, a kernel language and compiler, and host software for a real PCIe FPGA card. ([read more](../summaries/26275b87ab29.md)).

The creator of erdosproblems.com reflects on how the site - built to promote Erdős problems to humans - became a barometer for AI capability as models went from useless to solving some of mathematics' hardest problems in under a year. The upside is real: many questions now have answers. ## Tools, workflows and pushback

Removing Apple Intelligence, building software factories, agent memory and observability.

RemoveMacAI, an open-source command-line tool by developer Om Lahore, restores the off switch: it disables Siri, Writing Tools, Genmoji, Image Playground, the ChatGPT extension and the summaries, deletes the models, and stops macOS from downloading them again, while dictation keeps working. Lenny Rachitsky's takeaways from OpenAI's Tibo Sottiaux sketch where product thinking is heading. First: most internet actions will soon be taken by agents, and most people have not priced that in - Notion's MCP server launch flooded its systems and forced a rethink of its economics, and every product team will have to decide whether to build for agents.
