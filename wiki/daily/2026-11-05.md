## The open web at stake

Doctorow on why banning scraping and weakening CDA 230 would entrench the giants.

Cory Doctorow argues that tech policy has a big blast radius, and that two popular fixes for big-tech harms would backfire. On scraping, many creative workers want to ban it to stop AI training. He says that would be a terrible idea: scraping is how search engines exist, and if copies of the web needed licenses only Google could afford them. Scraping also lets researchers check what platforms did, such as how algorithms spread disinformation, how Amazon prices, or how Uber sets wage offers. Clumsy scrapers that crash sites should be punished for that, not for copying. For creative livelihoods, he says the answer is labor organizing, not more copyright. The headline point is that without CDA 230, Facebook will own the internet: small, well-run communities are the most exposed to legal threats and cannot afford the lawyers that Facebook can. Only the first half of the post was read closely. ([read more](../summaries/bc9445403531.md)).

## The research shelf

Lean formalization of the Stacks Project, AlphaEvolve's code, and a paper arguing long context is overrated.

Axiom released a Lean formalization of the whole Stacks Project, the roughly 7,500-page open reference for algebraic geometry started by Johan de Jong in 2005. ## The workbench

Developer tools and side projects: Rivet for agent actors, an offline survival assistant, and AI reviving the hackintosh.

A developer argues LLMs could bring the 'Lisp curse' to mainstream ecosystems. In Lisp, power and flexibility let people build custom solutions alone, so few shared libraries exist. If generating code with an LLM seems easier than learning frameworks, the same pattern could hit ecosystems like Java's. First effect: fewer people use open-source libraries, so maintainers get less feedback. Second: fewer new libraries get created, as collaboration loses its payoff. He asks why someone would research APIs or read docs when a prompt and a few dollars of tokens will do, while noting libraries are stable and tested by many. ([read more](../summaries/e1a82979ea6b.md)).

The author, who wrote a hackintosh guide a decade ago, says AI has revived the community. He says hackintosh and 'hackios' activity has exploded in recent weeks thanks to AI-assisted reverse engineering, and lists projects like macOS 27 on a Snapdragon laptop, macOS 27.2 beta on an RK3588 board and iOS 17 on a Switch OLED. Launch HN for Rivet (YC W23), an Apache 2.0 orchestrator for agent workloads built on the actor model and S3. Claims: 4.99 ms p99 cold starts, 72 KB RAM per actor, scaling to billions.
