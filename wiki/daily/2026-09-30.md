## GLM-5.3 and proliferating cyber capability

Concepts: [AI safety incidents and controls](../concepts/ai-safety-incidents.md) · [External AI evaluation](../concepts/external-evaluation.md)

Anthropic's Frontier Red Team says autonomous exploit-building is no longer a one-lab capability - and that GLM-5.3 shipped without meaningful safeguards.

Anthropic's Frontier Red Team analyzes GLM-5.3, the latest model from Zhipu AI (Z.ai), and concludes the era of autonomous end-to-end cyber exploit construction is no longer confined to one lab. Anthropic says it anticipated this after Claude Mythos Preview demonstrated the capability five months earlier, which is why it released Mythos only through the limited Project Glasswing program that let trusted defenders find more than 10,000 vulnerabilities first. Unlike Mythos, Anthropic says GLM-5.3 was released without meaningful safeguards, and its testing found attackers can bypass the model's protections in a large share of attempts. The post frames the finding as evidence that proliferation of advanced cyber capability has arrived and argues for defensive head-starts and stronger misuse controls. ([read more](../summaries/2415274ff349.md); [same source, alternate index](../summaries/a7c715dfa034.md)).

Simon Willison quotes Anthropic's Frontier Red Team evaluation of GLM-5.3: on 100 tasks from an internal Binary Exploitation benchmark, GLM-5.3 achieved full control-flow hijacks in 4% of trials versus 6% for Claude Mythos Preview. The significance, per the quote, is that a threshold has been crossed - earlier models such as Claude Opus 4.6 and GLM-5.2 succeeded in none of the trials. ([read more](../summaries/e8e247ab7030.md)).

## AI decides what you pay - and how your home scores

Concepts: [Agentic commerce](../concepts/agentic-commerce.md) · [AI policy, regulation and litigation](../concepts/ai-policy-regulation.md)

McDonald's per-store 'optimal price' engine and Dallas's trash-truck 'blight scores': algorithmic judgment applied to wallets and neighborhoods.

Reuters reports that McDonald's is increasingly using AI to guide menu prices across the US and some global markets, including an estimate of how much each store's patrons are willing to pay. Based on screenshots of the internal pricing engine and interviews with nine sources, the story says the system analyses millions of daily transactions across nearly 14,000 restaurants to generate an 'optimal price' per item per location - and has widened price differences for the same product between nearby restaurants. The strategy aims to lift headquarters' profit but has created tension with franchisees and carries antitrust and customer-backlash risks that Reuters says have not been previously reported in detail. ([read more](../summaries/c777d97378c8.md)).

A second account of the Reuters investigation into McDonald's AI-driven dynamic pricing: machine-learning algorithms continuously analyze transaction data from nearly 14,000 US restaurants to produce an 'optimal price' for each menu item at each location, with one factor being estimated local willingness to pay. Franchisee-facing screenshots show the system flagging when a restaurant's prices diverge from recommendations, and franchisees say identical items now vary in price between neighbouring stores. ([read more](../summaries/b8bf4be3c52d.md)).

An NBC 5 investigation found that AI-powered cameras mounted on Dallas trash trucks have photographed and assigned 'blight scores' to more than 21,000 properties in four months, flagging possible code violations. A city council member and residents are now pushing back, questioning privacy implications and whether camera-driven enforcement could bring fines to lower-income neighbourhoods least able to pay. ([read more](../summaries/59b00aadc6b1.md)).

TurboGPT is a Show HN project that trains a tiny 22KiB byte-level GPT in about 13 seconds, written in CUDA C++ and released under the MIT license. It supports Linux/NixOS and Windows builds, stores full training checkpoints (model, optimizer, scheduler and trainer state) for resumable runs, and emits TensorBoard-compatible logs capped at 8Mi reports. ([read more](../summaries/ebf4a974fed7.md)).

The MLC community introduces TIRx Harness, an open compiler harness designed to let coding agents develop correct, fast GPU kernels: it combines a minimal stable compiler foundation, a knowledge base, tools and a benchmark server. The motivation is that agents working on kernels like Kimi Delta Attention spend much of their budget on peripheral uncertainty - predicting lowering behaviour, diagnosing flaky timing, finding relevant strategies - rather than on optimization itself. On evaluated workloads, KDA kernels developed with the harness reach geometric-mean speedups of 2.94x over FlashKDA (forward) and 6.84x over Flash Linear Attention (backward).
