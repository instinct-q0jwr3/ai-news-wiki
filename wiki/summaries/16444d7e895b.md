# A researcher says a flaw in Meta's Muse app for Mac lets any app or terminal command gain access to the token that authenticates users to their Muse account

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-22 · updated: 2026-09-25 · confidence: high_

`techmeme` `meta` `muse` `ai-safety-incidents` `ai-policy-regulation`

## Summary

Ars Technica's Dan Goodin reports a zero-day in Meta's new Muse AI assistant for macOS, discovered by Mac security expert Patrick Wardle, that lets any locally installed app or terminal command seize the token authenticating a user to their Muse account - effectively handing an attacker the agent's full privileges. Meta markets Muse as 'built from the ground up for privacy and security'; it books appointments, fills forms, makes purchases, and connects to WhatsApp, email, calendar and social accounts, requiring sweeping macOS permissions (disk, mic, camera, location, calendars) that Apple's defaults exist to restrict.

The flaw chains two design decisions: Muse processes dictation in the cloud rather than on-device, and any local process - regardless of permissions - can change a list of undocumented settings, one of which controls the transcription endpoint. Pointing that endpoint at an attacker server yields the account token. 'Instead of us having to write a very comprehensive Mac malware stealer, we can just leverage the AI assistant itself,' Wardle said, demonstrating proof-of-concept attacks that write malicious files and snap photos. Roughly 12 hours before disclosure, Amazon began blocking Muse as an 'unauthorized AI agent' that violates its Conditions of Use. Meta did not answer emailed questions.

## Highlights

- Zero-day in Meta's Muse macOS assistant discovered by Patrick Wardle; disclosed to Ars Technica (Dan Goodin).
- Any local app or terminal command can take the token authenticating the user's Muse account, inheriting the agent's privileges.
- Root cause: undocumented settings any process can change, including the endpoint where speech transcription occurs.
- Dictation is processed in Meta's cloud rather than on-device, making endpoint redirection exploitable.
- Wardle's PoCs write malicious files to disk and snap pictures, often with no visible indication.
- Muse requires broad macOS permissions: disk, mic, camera, location, calendars, plus linked WhatsApp, email and social accounts.
- About 12 hours before disclosure, Amazon blocked Muse as an 'unauthorized AI agent' violating its Conditions of Use.
- Meta published two security-design posts in as many weeks but did not respond to Ars' questions.
- Wardle: 'when you take a look at Muse, it's like they didn't, in my opinion, think about security.'
