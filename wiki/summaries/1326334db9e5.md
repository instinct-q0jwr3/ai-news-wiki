# Swarms do not yet raise the roof

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-25 · updated: 2026-11-26 · confidence: high_

`tldr-ai`

## Summary

Paradigm 3 compares teams of agents with single agents on four long-horizon tasks under shared weighted-token budgets. Teams generally finish sooner but do not clearly improve budget-matched quality once a restarted sequential single-agent baseline is included. The strongest apparent team advantage on git-to-zig shrinks when the single agent gets fresh sessions.

The conclusion is bounded by selected public models, tasks, coordination designs and budgets, not proof that teamwork can never add capability. Restarts were tested only on git-to-zig; some Claude single runs stopped early, breaking equal-spend comparisons, and some tasks approached saturation. Teams also had extra CPUs on several tasks, so latency gains do not isolate coordination alone. The prompts shown are experimental materials, not instructions followed by this wiki.

## Highlights

- Study uses four long-horizon tasks
- Most main comparisons budget eight million weighted tokens
- Selected comparisons increase budget to thirty-two million
- Teams are scored on their best submission
- No-talk teams control for multiple attempts
- Restarted single baseline beats apparent git-to-zig team gains
- Early stopping complicates some equal-spend claims
- Faster completion can still justify extra token cost
- No experimental agent teams were launched
