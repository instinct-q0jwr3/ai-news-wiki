## Summary

Cory Doctorow explains why AI bosses' public garment-rending about their products' terrible potential - jobspocalypse, paperclips - is not caution but marketing: every warning about the potential is also a boast about the potential, and potential is all AI really has. Actual AI, he argues, is useful only at the margins, for skilled practitioners who can tell correct output from wrong and integrate it judiciously.

He names the critics' version of the same swindle: 'criti-hype', Lee Vinsel's term for taking boosters' sensational claims, flipping their valence and calling it risk analysis. Repeating Sam Altman's claims with '(and that's bad)' appended still repeats them. The right way to criticize these 'bullshit factories', Doctorow insists, is to point out that they're lying - not to amplify the lie as a warning.
