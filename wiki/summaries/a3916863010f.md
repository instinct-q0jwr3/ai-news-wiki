# Prize-winning image which sparked backlash was AI-generated, Nikon rules

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-11 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

Nikon disqualified the winner of its Small World In Motion microscopy competition after other scientists flagged the winning video as fake. The entry, from Dr Ning Xu of Tsinghua University, showed cilia moving in the airway of a child with the respiratory condition PCD; Nikon ruled that generative AI used in the composition broke contest rules, while stressing the decision was not a judgement of Xu's professional reputation.

Xu acknowledged using AI but said he believed he had acted within the rules, and cooperated with the investigation. Dr Robert Hirst, a Leicester scientist who diagnoses PCD by examining cilia, said he knew immediately the video was fabricated - the cells looked nothing like real PCD patients' - and noted the controversy hurt patients, doctors and PCD support networks. Nikon says it will review the 15-year-old contest's rules and procedures.

## Highlights

- Nikon disqualified the Small World In Motion winner over generative AI use.
- Entry: video of cilia in a PCD child's airway by Dr Ning Xu (Tsinghua University).
- Xu admitted AI use but believed he followed the rules; Nikon disagreed after re-evaluation.
- PCD specialist Robert Hirst spotted the fabrication immediately from the cilia morphology.
- Nikon will review contest rules; no judgement passed on Xu's professional reputation.
