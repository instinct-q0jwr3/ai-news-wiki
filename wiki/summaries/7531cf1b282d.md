# Supporting a shared AI training control for websites

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-13 · confidence: high_

`tldr-ai`

## Summary

Google will support a shared AI training control in robots.txt: a single 'policy token' that lets a website express its training preferences once, across multiple AI providers, instead of maintaining per-company instructions. The mechanism builds on the IETF AIPREF working group's vocabulary; Google's own behavior when the token opts a site out of training (and Google-Extended is not separately specified) will be to treat it as a Google-Extended disallow. Company-specific controls like Google-Extended keep working alongside the shared signal, and sites that explicitly allow Google-Extended remain eligible for grounding appearances and clicks in Gemini Apps.

The politics are as notable as the plumbing: publishers, civil society and regulators have been demanding industry-wide controls, and the proposal aligns with the EU's GPAI Code of Practice under the AI Act, which already recognizes robots.txt as the primary channel for machine-readable training preferences. If the other majors adopt the token, robots.txt becomes the first cross-industry, durable switch for training consent - a small file carrying a very large argument about who gets to learn from the web.

## Highlights

- Google backs a shared AI training control in robots.txt - one policy token across providers
