# Bitter Lessoned in Automated Theorem Proving

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-26 · updated: 2026-10-29 · confidence: high_

`tldr-ai`

## Summary

John Loeber, who has believed since 2013 that machines would write the great proofs, walks through why that conviction finally looks right - and what went wrong on the way. LLMs originally could barely do arithmetic; reasoning models from late 2024 fixed the tripping-up problem with test-time compute, and by this year frontier models had both the intuition to generate useful ideas and the reasoning to write bite-size novel proofs. Add Lean for formal verification and you can grind through proofspace in a loop; in May, OpenAI published a proof of the Planar Unit Distance problem.

The title's twist - 'Bitter Lessoned' - is the essay's caution: the path that worked was not the clever, structured approach researchers favored, and the post examines what automated theorem proving teaches about which intuitions survive contact with scale.
