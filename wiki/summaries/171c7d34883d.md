# Dozens of staff at HarperCollins, Simon & Schuster, Hachette: without author consent, publishers are quietly using AI to make back-cover copy, cover art, more

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-10 · updated: 2026-10-10 · confidence: high_

`techmeme` `labor-displacement`

## Summary

Wired reports that while American book publishers publicly fight generative AI, suing over copyright and canceling authors suspected of using LLMs, at least three of the Big Five, HarperCollins, Simon & Schuster and Hachette, have quietly folded AI tools into their processes without public disclosure or author consent.

More than two dozen anonymous workers describe LLMs like Claude and ChatGPT being used, often at executives' direction, to email literary agents, write publicity and back-cover copy, design cover art and generate marketing materials. Staffers add that the publicized cancellations, like Hachette's Shy Girl or Macmillan's Call Me, I'll Hide the Body, are the tip of the iceberg: every imprint has a story of an esteemed author whose book was canceled over blatant AI use.

## Highlights

- Three Big Five publishers quietly use AI internally
- Uses: agent emails, jacket copy, cover art, marketing
- No public disclosure and no author consent
- Public anti-AI stance contrasts with private practice
- Canceled-author cases called the tip of the iceberg
