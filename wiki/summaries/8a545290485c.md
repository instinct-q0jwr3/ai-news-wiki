# OpenAI Feared "Optics" of what might appear on Hacker News

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-27 · updated: 2026-09-30 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `openai` `ai-safety-incidents`

## Summary

The Authors Guild says new court filings in Authors Guild v. OpenAI show that executives and employees at OpenAI and Microsoft knowingly trained their models on pirated books, putting the companies' guilty knowledge at the center of the lawsuit. The guild's press release frames the documents as a turning point: internal material allegedly shows OpenAI weighed the 'optics' of its choices - including concern over what might surface on Hacker News - while using books from a site it knew was sketchy and releasing products it acknowledged could make people unemployed.

The plaintiffs are the Authors Guild and a roster of prominent writers including David Baldacci, John Grisham, George R.R. Martin, Jodi Picoult and Jonathan Franzen. The piece is an advocacy statement from one side of the litigation, but it anchors its claims in the newly released briefs, which it says quote the companies' own internal discussions about the damage their actions could do to authors and publishers.

## Highlights

- New filings in Authors Guild v. OpenAI released last Thursday.
- Alleged to show OpenAI and Microsoft staff knowingly used pirated books.
- Internal worries about 'optics' and Hacker News reaction cited.
- Brief claims OpenAI saw GPT models as a threat to book authors.
- Plaintiffs include Grisham, George R.R. Martin, Baldacci, Franzen, Picoult.
- Guild CEO Mary Rasenberger calls it 'shocking disdain for writers'.
- Source is the plaintiffs' guild, so framing is adversarial.
