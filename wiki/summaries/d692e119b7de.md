# Microsoft and Epic Games Suddenly Agree That AI Is Theft

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-28 · updated: 2026-10-29 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `microsoft`

## Summary

An essay argues the games industry is having its 'reap what you sow' moment: AI decompilation advances led by Claude Opus 5.5 are pumping out browser ports and native PC recompilations of games daily, and publishers can't keep up. Epic Games is DMCA-ing posts featuring a browser-playable Fortnite recreation while Microsoft stood up 'Xbox Labs' with a note invoking 75 years of game history.

Tim Sweeney responded to the karma charge by separating the pieces: Epic does not let people use Fortnite IP without permission or compensation, while it does restrict AI training on Unreal Engine and MetaHuman outputs. The author's point: publishers that admitted to using AI tools are now discovering those same tools turned against their own IP.

- AI decompilation floods the web with browser ports and recompilations of games
