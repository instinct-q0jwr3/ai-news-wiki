# Binance says hackers used fake job interviews, AI tool to steal $6.3B

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-03 · updated: 2026-11-10 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `ai-safety-incidents`

## Summary

Binance's co-CEO Richard Teng has described the October 29 attack, the largest crypto theft on record, as a months-long intrusion rather than a single-laptop failure. In June attackers set up a fake developer tool and lured an engineer into installing it; per Binance's security report, a Wallet DevOps engineer had downloaded a tampered Homebrew installation while searching for a popular AI coding tool. In September they posed as recruiters and ran job interviews with another engineer that ended in a coding exercise hiding evasive malware. From there they moved through Binance's internal AI tools to reach the system that authorized wallet transactions.

Over $6 billion in assets were affected; Binance says it has replenished 98% of the $6.34 billion and is buying back the more illiquid remainder. Teng did not attribute the attack himself but acknowledged the FBI links it to North Korean hackers tied to TraderTraitor.

The notable detail is the AI angle: search-engine hunts for AI tools and compromised internal AI systems served as both the lure and the lateral path.

## Highlights

- FBI links it to North Korea's TraderTraitor; Teng stopped short of attribution
