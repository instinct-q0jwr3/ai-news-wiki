# The Economics of Open-Weight Inference

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-22 · updated: 2026-09-22 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

Ornn Data challenges the assumption that each new NVIDIA generation makes the last one obsolete, arguing open-weight demand extends the economic life of older GPUs. Across eleven open-weight and eight closed models on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, the cheapest qualifying open-weight model completes a task at roughly one fifth the cost of a comparable closed model, and self-hosting on rented hardware can reach $0.12 to $0.35 per million output tokens at full utilization.

The economics invert hardware rankings: on gpt-oss-120b (5.1B active parameters), the A100 produces output more cheaply than the H100 at spot and term prices. Ornn's rental data shows the market agreeing - five-year A100 contracts hold 80% of the one-month price versus 44-60% for Hopper and Blackwell - and the paper notes that long-running agents, batch evaluation and RL tolerate latency, routing price-elastic demand to older hardware.

## Highlights

- Cheapest qualifying open-weight model completes tasks at ~1/5 the cost of comparable closed models
- Self-hosting on rented hardware: $0.12-$0.35 per million output tokens at full utilization
- On gpt-oss-120b, A100 output is cheaper than H100 at spot and 3- and 5-year terms
- Five-year A100 rental price holds 80% of one-month price vs 44-60% for Hopper/Blackwell
- Compute-intensive workloads (agents, batch eval, RL) tolerate latency and are hardware-agnostic
- Closed-model token rate cards reset by providers; open weights keep marginal cost predictable
