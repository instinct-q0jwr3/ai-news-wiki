# Advertisers Pull Meta Ads That Had Unwanted AI Glow-Ups

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-24 · updated: 2026-10-28 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `meta`

## Summary

Advertisers are pulling back from Meta ads after the platform's generative AI features modified their creative without consent, just as Meta's new Commercial Terms prepare to make those AI changes the advertiser's legal problem from October 30. Bookshop.org said an automated account update opted it into a generative AI feature that added an AI-generated illustration to a human-made ad without its knowledge; it has since switched off all of Meta's AI ad features. Agency Exverus reports Meta's AI tacking on sitelinks, translating copy and generating images and carousels from brand photos, sometimes on ads where the feature was never enabled.

The liability shift sits in a new 'AI Features' section of the Commercial Terms: previously advertisers bore responsibility only when they chose to use the tools, now they are solely responsible for whatever AI features create or change, including features Meta turns on. The story lands in a heavy week for Meta: a New Mexico judge ordered it to pay $25 billion over data-control misrepresentation, and it reported $64.6 billion in Q3 revenue, beating estimates. The piece also covers OpenAI's ad push (over 100,000 advertisers on ChatGPT, expansion to 26 more countries), its talks to raise roughly $30 billion at a valuation above $1.4 trillion ahead of Anthropic's expected mid-November IPO, and Semrush data showing news sites supply 29.5% of ChatGPT's cited sources in the UK versus 19.6% in the US.

## Highlights

- Bookshop.org pulled Meta's AI ad features after an automatic opt-in added an AI-generated illustration to its ad without consent.
- Agency Exverus reports Meta's AI adding sitelinks, translations and AI-generated images to ads that never enabled the feature.
- From October 30 a new 'AI Features' section makes advertisers solely liable for AI changes, including features Meta switches on.
- Previously liability attached only when advertisers deliberately chose to use Meta's AI creative tools.
- The terms land the same week Meta was ordered to pay $25 billion in New Mexico and reported $64.6B in Q3 revenue, above estimates.
- OpenAI says over 100,000 advertisers have run ChatGPT campaigns and is expanding ChatGPT Ads to 26 more countries.
- OpenAI is reportedly in talks to raise about $30B at a $1.4T+ valuation before Anthropic's expected mid-November IPO.
- Semrush: news sites made up 29.5% of ChatGPT's cited sources in the UK and 19.6% in the US from January to July.
