# Readers can't identify watermarked AI text

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-10-04 · confidence: high_

`seangoedecke`

## Summary

Sean Goedecke has been arguing that AI text watermarking is neither anti-consumer nor quality-degrading, and decided to test the practical question: can readers actually tell watermarked output apart? He built a quiz site serving thirty responses from Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 on a rented H200 (about two dollars of GPU time), with one response per question secretly watermarked using SynthID-Text.

The first round, with 278 participants, produced a mean score of 3.92 out of 10 versus 3.33 for pure guessing - apparently above chance, but confounded: the watermarked answer happened to be option A in six of ten questions, producing a suspicious 6/10 spike from always-pick-first respondents. After re-shuffling, scores clustered at chance. His conclusion: readers can't identify watermarked AI text, which undercuts the common objections to watermarking - if the watermark were ruining outputs, people would be able to find it.
