# Experts: since June, $100M+ in US data center freight has been stolen; in two successful heists, robbers struck escorts protecting trucks hauling $10M+ in goods

## Summary

NBC News reports that organized criminal groups have stolen more than $100 million worth of US data-center freight since June, targeting the physical supply chain of the AI build-out. In one early-June case, a trailer carrying some $13 million of server equipment was headed toward Fayetteville, Georgia, home to one of the country's largest data-center developments.

The thefts use deceptive techniques against a cargo that is both enormously valuable and easy to fence into a market starving for hardware. The story is a physical-world corollary to the week's financial headlines: when single servers cost as much as luxury cars and the industry is spending half a trillion dollars a year, the trucks moving GPUs have become targets worth hijacking.

## Highlights

- Over $100M in US data-center freight stolen since June, per experts cited by NBC
- One heist: a trailer with ~$13M in server equipment bound for Fayetteville, Georgia
- Organized groups use deceptive techniques against the AI supply chain
- Two successful heists are detailed in the report
- GPU scarcity makes stolen data-center hardware easy to monetize
