# [AINews] OpenAI reports Navier-Stokes singularity find in 88 hours using Astra-next, roughly 10,000 agents and 130B tokens (>$40M), a contender for second ever Millennium Prize awarded

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-19 · updated: 2026-09-20 · confidence: high_

`latent.space` `openai` `meta` `agentic-systems`

## Summary

OpenAI claimed a solution to the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Problem, saying the proof was produced by a group of agents using a next-generation model 'significantly more capable than GPT-6 Astra'. OpenAI-affiliated researcher Ethan Knight described the effort as a collaboration of roughly 10,000 agents, trained over the past year via multi-agent RL, with hard problems yielding to 'huge amounts of unstructured parallel test-time compute' and models deciding how to organize themselves.

Latent Space's account is careful about what is established: no theorem statement, preprint, proof sketch or independent referee commentary had appeared, and the widely repeated '88 hours' detail came from a satirical post, not from OpenAI-adjacent statements, so it should not be treated as confirmed. Reactions split between technical interest and skepticism; Theo Jensen called it the science world's 'AI can't ACTUALLY code' crash-out moment, while others read it as evidence of a new high-compute regime. AINews' own verdict: the achievement looks real, though the process remains in dispute.

## Highlights

- OpenAI announced a Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem solution produced by agents on a model beyond GPT-6 Astra.
- Ethan Knight: ~10,000 collaborating agents, trained ~1 year with multi-agent RL.
- Method framing: unstructured parallel test-time compute with model self-organization, not one long proof chain.
- Unverified at announcement: no theorem statement, preprint, proof artifact or referee commentary public.
- The viral '88 hours' figure traces to a satirical post, not an OpenAI statement.
- Reactions split: stress test for 'AI can't do research' beliefs vs demands for mathematical acceptance.
- AINews' read: OpenAI's achievement is likely real; the process is in dispute.
