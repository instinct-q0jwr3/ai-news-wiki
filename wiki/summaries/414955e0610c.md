# Navier-Stokes lost in translation: Why Lean verification of AI autoformalisation does not guarantee correct natural language proofs

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-08 · updated: 2026-10-10 · confidence: high_

`lobsters` `openai` `ai-coding-agents`

## Summary

An arXiv paper argues that Lean verification of AI autoformalisation may give no confidence in the original natural-language argument, using OpenAI's announced Navier-Stokes blow-up proof as the motivating example. Translating text into Lean requires resolving ambiguity, so a checked formal proof may not mean the same thing as the original claim.

The authors, from Cambridge, show that resolving such ambiguity sits arbitrarily high in the Solvability Complexity Index hierarchy, so a semantically faithful automatic translation is harder than any computable problem in that hierarchy. This is a theoretical argument: it says verification cannot be assumed faithful, not that a particular proof is wrong.

## Highlights

- Lean-checked autoformalisation may not guarantee the original argument
- Motivated by OpenAI's Navier-Stokes blow-up claim
- Ambiguity resolution is arbitrarily hard in the SCI hierarchy
- Theoretical result, no particular proof shown wrong
- Cambridge authors
