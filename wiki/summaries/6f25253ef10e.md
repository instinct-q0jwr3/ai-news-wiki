# On the Brutality of Amdahl's Law

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-16 · updated: 2026-11-25 · confidence: high_

`tldr-ai`

## Summary

Connor Blake applies bottleneck reasoning to argue that faster AI reasoning need not translate into equally fast economic transformation. Physical experiments, shipping, equipment installation, regulation and noisy information gathering can become the limiting steps after intellectual work speeds up.

The essay distinguishes faster execution from expanding capacity and notes that repeated removal of successive bottlenecks could still compound. Its factory examples illustrate a position about growth, not empirical proof of a universal speed limit or a forecast that AI has little economic value.

## Highlights

- Argument extends Amdahl-style bottleneck reasoning
- Physical steps can constrain intelligent planning
- Scaling capacity has its own dependencies
- Noisy experiments take time to yield information
- Examples are illustrative rather than universal bounds
