## Summary

Cory Doctorow's Pluralistic column argues creative workers have been conned into treating copyright as their primary economic protection when it is really a labor question: creative workers are workers, not asset speculators, and when they bargain with a media company over copyright they are selling labor, not property. Property rights are alienable - sell your house and you can't move back in - which is exactly why 'property' framing serves the buyer.

The hook is how labor rights, not copyright, rescued Ultima from a monopolist: a case study in collective worker power beating ownership framing in the game industry, extended to the fights creators face now.

## Highlights
