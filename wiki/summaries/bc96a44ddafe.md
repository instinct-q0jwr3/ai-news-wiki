# As AI automates parts of pure mathematics research, a look at formalization challenges and why human imagination remains key to deciding which questions to ask

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-04 · updated: 2026-10-10 · confidence: high_

`techmeme`

## Summary

Stephen Wolfram responds to claims that AI will make human math research unnecessary. He says this misunderstands what pure mathematics is, and recalls that when Mathematica launched in 1988 people said the same, yet it only raised the level of math people could do.

His point is that solving problems, such as symbolic integrals, is not the core of pure math, which is an old human enterprise about abstract, rational thought. Only the first part of the essay was read.

## Highlights

- Wolfram says AI replacing math researchers misreads what math is.
- Mathematica in 1988 prompted the same fear and raised the level instead.
- Problem solving is not the core of pure mathematics.
- Basis: opening of the essay.
