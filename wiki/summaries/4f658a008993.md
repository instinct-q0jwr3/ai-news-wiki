# Character.AI's Chapter 11 court filings indicate a two-part deal with Disney to transfer talent and tech while insulating Disney from mounting legal liabilities

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-19 · updated: 2026-10-30 · confidence: high_

`techmeme` `ai-safety-incidents` `ai-policy-regulation`

## Summary

Wired reads Character.AI's Chapter 11 filings and concludes the bankruptcy was not a collapse but a deal mechanism. The two-part structure lets Disney hire most of Character.AI's staff - including now-former CEO Karandeep Anand - and potentially acquire most of its technology, while insulating Disney from the startup's mounting legal liabilities: multiple private lawsuits over chatbot safety and investigations by regulators around the world. In a court declaration, Character.AI's newly appointed CEO said the bankruptcy's goal was a 'consensual resolution' with personal-injury claimants and the investigating states while maximizing asset value for shareholders. Whatever remains of Character.AI keeps the liabilities; Disney gets the talent and tech. The filings make the arrangement - bankruptcy as a get-out-of-lawsuits card - unusually explicit.
