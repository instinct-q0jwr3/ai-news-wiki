# AI children's books, body horror edition

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high_

`lcamtuf`

## Summary

lcamtuf bought one of the AI-generated children's encyclopedias flooding Amazon - category bestsellers from the '100,000 whys' collage he published the week before. His earlier point was sameness; this time it's quality, and the review is a photo essay of body horror: garbled anatomy, a reflection that appears to reach out of a mirror, nightmare fauna - the sort of imagery no one vetted because no one was meant to.

His analysis of why children's encyclopedias are the target: they sell reliably, the buyer is not the reader (gifts judged by the cover), and 'authors' can undercut publishers without touching guarded IP. The kicker: these books shape young minds, published mid-2026 with flagship-model art - and this is what 'PhD-level intelligence' shipped.
