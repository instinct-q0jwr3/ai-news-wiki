# UC Berkeley math community left at crossroads

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-19 · updated: 2026-10-28 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

The Daily Californian reports on UC Berkeley's math community reckoning with OpenAI's quasi-Riemann proof. It opens with Noam Brown's cost curve: o3's 87.5% on ARC-AGI 1 cost roughly $500,000, Astra beats it for about $20, and a problem that took OpenAI and DeepMind enormous compute for IMO gold in 2025 is now solvable by anyone with a $20/month subscription - Brown's point being that massively scaled test-time compute is a glimpse of the near future. At Berkeley, mathematicians including Fields medalist Richard Borcherds are weighing what this means for the field; Borcherds admits he did not expect capabilities of this caliber so soon. The department stands at a crossroads between embracing AI tools and defending what human mathematics is for.
