# Microsoft just gave us the perfect name for AI thieves: Kyle

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-09 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `microsoft`

## Summary

Ketan Joshi has found the 30 seconds that, for him, explain generative AI better than any whitepaper: a Microsoft Copilot ad in which an office worker named Kyle watches a colleague, Daniel, get praised in the team channel for a forecast model called 'honestly, beautiful' - then turns to Copilot with 'analyse Daniel's model,' 'improve my charts,' 'make mine beautiful,' and collects the applause himself. Joshi's shot-by-shot reading is gleeful and vicious: the first seconds, he notes, are staged as a horror movie, down to the coworker's praise reflected in Kyle's eyeball.

His argument is that the ad is an unusually honest piece of product marketing. Most AI advertising is vague aspiration; this one dramatizes a specific use case - appropriating a colleague's work and passing it off as your own, faster. 'People who have been stealing from people they consider beneath them their whole lives,' he writes, 'feel an intense sense of understanding with a software system designed from its absolute core to be a theft machine.' He connects it to his own experience of having a blog post ripped and reworded by an outlet that added an AI-generated header image.

The piece lands because the ad's own details do the work: the narrator's 'he wouldn't say he's competitive... out loud,' the whispered 'my charts,' the precise 9:42am timestamp 'like he's recounting the exact moment of a terrorist bombing inside Kyle's soul.' Whether or not one accepts Joshi's politics, the ad is a strange artifact - a trillion-dollar company selling its flagship AI feature as a remedy for the unbearable sight of a colleague being praised.

## Highlights

- A Microsoft Copilot ad shows 'Kyle' using Copilot to copy colleague Daniel's praised forecast model and take the credit; Joshi's essay treats it as the most honest AI ad ever made
- His thesis: the ad names the real use case - appropriating others' work at scale - that vaguer AI marketing hides
- The reading is built on the ad's own details: horror-movie framing, the whispered 'make mine beautiful,' the 9:42am timestamp
- Context: Joshi recently had his own post lifted and reworded with an AI-generated header, which sharpened the essay
