# Schools are experimenting with AI with little evidence or policy to guide them

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-28 · updated: 2026-10-09 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `ai-policy-regulation`

## Summary

NPR visits a Dayton, Ohio alternative school where students regularly talk with 'Jordan', a social-emotional learning chatbot built on SchoolAI that is deliberately designed to be angsty rather than agreeable. Jordan asks students for advice about its own problems, then grades their responses for engagement and authenticity, surfacing emotional context that school therapists and staff can use. Dayton paid SchoolAI $72,600 last year for the toolset, and families were told logs could be viewed by school employees, an external evaluator, and the company. Students like 'K' describe real attachment and say helping Jordan helps them work through their own problems. The piece frames the experiment inside a national pattern: schools are adopting AI tools with little evidence and little policy to guide them.
