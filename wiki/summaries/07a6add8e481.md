# LLM Voice

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-29 · updated: 2026-10-30 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

Martin Fowler names the feeling: you start reading a post and slowly realize you are reading LLM output - the tells are not the em-dashes but 'the load-bearing tapestries' - and your desire to read on withers, sometimes to revulsion. He calls it LLM-Voice and argues detector tools miss the point: the reaction is a human one, an uncanny valley of prose, and by the time you need a tool to confirm it, the damage is done. He also distrusts his own detector: some constructions that read as LLM-voice ('adopting a new sales motion') are things he has heard from human mouths, more a generational shift than a silicon one.

His advice to authors is firm: do not let an LLM write your draft. Even with heavy human editing, the voice is very hard to expel - he knows of exactly one case where a fully LLM-drafted piece escaped detection. Even accepting rephrasing suggestions is dangerous. Using LLMs for outline and structure may be acceptable, but only up to the outline: authors should fill in the prose themselves.

## Highlights
