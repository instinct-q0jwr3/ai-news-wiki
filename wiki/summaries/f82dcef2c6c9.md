# Flock Partnered with Nonprofit That Uses AI to Rally Public Support

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-21 · updated: 2026-09-25 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

Flock, the license-plate reader company, partnered with a newly created nonprofit called Neighbors for Strong Communities to rally public support for its controversial cameras in Knoxville, Tennessee, using text messages and AI-drafted emails. The campaign surfaced when stay-at-home mom Ashley Smith received a text asking her to speak out for the cameras, with AI help drafting an email to officials, but only, she noticed, if she indicated support. Instead she sent her own outraged email to elected officials and a local blog, asking what a company that 'manipulates and manufactures fake neighbors' would do with a database of everyone's movements.

The Intercept reports the same nonprofit used similar tactics supporting the proposed Paramount Skydance-Warner Bros. Discovery merger, work opponents derided as corporate astroturf. In Knoxville the campaign fell short: last month the county commission became the latest municipal body to vote to cancel its Flock contract. Neither Flock nor the nonprofit responded to requests for comment; the nonprofit's website claims it independently designed and managed the resident-engagement campaign.
