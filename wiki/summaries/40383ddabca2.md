# By attacking Chinese Al industry, the US has burst its own Al bubble

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-22 · updated: 2026-11-26 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

Robert Wu argues that US restrictions on China's AI supply chain can damage American firms because customers, manufacturing and data-center components remain interdependent. His commentary connects chip licensing, reported foundry order suspensions, possible model-use disclosures and equipment controls to the risk of losing Chinese demand. A cited short-seller estimate puts Chinese demand above one-fifth of Nvidia's fiscal-2026 compute revenue, including alleged indirect channels.

The article advances a causal interpretation of market movements, not an empirical identification of what caused them. Its market figures, personnel claims and descriptions of regulatory actions were not independently checked against filings or official rules in this pass. The demand estimate is attributed to Culper Research and includes allegations about diversion; it is not an audited sales breakdown. The useful question raised is how restrictions propagate through a connected supply chain, rather than whether this essay proves a particular policy burst a financial bubble.

## Highlights

- Wu argues that export restrictions can hurt US suppliers as well as Chinese customers
- The essay emphasizes interdependence in demand, fabrication and components
- The cited China-demand estimate comes from short-seller Culper Research
- Indirect-sales and diversion claims remain attributed allegations
- Market movements do not by themselves establish policy causation
- Regulatory descriptions and market figures were not independently verified
- Commentary, not a neutral forecast or audited revenue analysis
