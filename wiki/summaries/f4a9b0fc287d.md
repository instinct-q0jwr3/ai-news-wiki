_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-20 · updated: 2026-10-26 · confidence: high_

## Summary

Simon Willison links Jeremy Scheff's 'obituary for programming', which poignantly names what coding agents take from career programmers who loved the puzzle: programming became one of the greatest jobs imaginable - paid to solve tricky little logic puzzles, the trickier the better paid, and even simple puzzles paid well because regular people couldn't do them. Programmers set the strategy, made the timeline, defined what was possible. Scheff's point is not that the job disappears but that the part people loved - the puzzle-solving - is being automated out of it, leaving the judgment and responsibility without the fun.
