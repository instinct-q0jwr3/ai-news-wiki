# The Great Unbundling of Intelligence

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-22 · updated: 2026-09-24 · confidence: high_

`tldr-ai`

## Summary

This essay argues the LLM era's great trick was bundling: GPT, Claude, Gemini and Kimi collapsed extraction, search, ranking, suspicion checks, tool choice, browsing, writing, verification and hard reasoning into one general-purpose product, replacing stacks of narrow systems. That convenience hid radically different computational requirements across those capabilities - and models are already better at different parts of the bundle.

The trigger for unbundling is agents: one human objective becomes hundreds or thousands of machine decisions, so small per-step differences in cost and latency compound into product economics, and inference has become a real cost of goods sold. As variance in performance, latency and cost across the bundled capabilities grows, the economically rational architecture stops being one model in the middle and becomes a routed mix - the Great Unbundling of Intelligence.

## Highlights

- The LLM miracle was bundling many capabilities into one general-purpose model.
- Bundled capabilities have radically different compute, latency and cost profiles.
- Agents multiply one objective into hundreds of machine decisions, compounding per-step economics.
- Inference is now a real COGS line, forcing cost/ROI discipline.
- Predicts a routed, multi-model architecture replacing the single model in the middle.
