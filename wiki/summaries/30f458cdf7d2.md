# Apple v. OpenAI: a US judge partly grants Apple's motion for expedited discovery, ordering OpenAI to hand over forensic images of devices and other documents

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-14 · updated: 2026-10-23 · confidence: high_

`techmeme` `openai` `apple`

## Summary

In Apple v. OpenAI, a US judge partly granted Apple's motion for expedited discovery over alleged trade-secret theft tied to OpenAI's consumer hardware. Judge Edward Davila cited a risk that forensic evidence on poached engineers' devices could be destroyed, pointing to a conversation between former Apple employees Chang Liu and Alyssa Peng about wiping their Apple-owned electronics.

OpenAI must hand over forensic images of devices, summaries of certain Apple employee interviews, the Liu-Peng LINE conversations and documents about the former Apple employees' responsibilities. Liu, Peng and ex-Apple hardware chief Tang Yew Tan must sit for early depositions. The order came a day before a hearing on OpenAI's motion to dismiss and Apple's bid for a preliminary injunction (Bloomberg Law).

## Highlights

- Judge partly grants Apple expedited discovery against OpenAI.
- Reason: risk of destroyed forensic evidence on engineers' devices.
- OpenAI must produce device images, interview summaries and chat logs.
- Three former Apple staff face early depositions.
- Hearing on dismissal and injunction followed the next day.
