# Investigation: a network of 400+ domains and apps linked to Chinese-language developers bypasses app store reviews to generate nonconsensual deepfakes and CSAM

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-06 · confidence: high_

`techmeme`

## Summary

WIRED's investigation starts with a hamster in beach shorts. Viefur, a self-described 'pet portrait' app selling coins for anthropomorphic animal pictures, is cover: under certain conditions the app stealthily loads a web interface for generating explicit AI sexual images and videos from uploaded photos - including scenes named 'bedroom rape' and 'threatening knife.' Viefur is one of more than 400 connected domains and apps in a single network, masquerading as language tuition, workout coaches and other everyday services while bypassing app store review, with payments running through Apple and Google Pay.

The network was found hiding in plain sight: researchers traced thousands of explicit deepfake video ads on Facebook and Instagram - including AI-generated child sexual abuse material with images of real children and a member of a European royal family - back to the deceptive apps. The ads reached tens of thousands and have triggered regulator investigations in multiple countries. The operation, publishing dozens of apps per week for about a year, appears run by Chinese-language developers and a Hong Kong entity, KeLing Technology Limited, which did not respond to requests for comment. The enforcement gap is the story's charge: Google, Apple and Meta all say they prohibit this content - and the network ran on all three platforms at once.
