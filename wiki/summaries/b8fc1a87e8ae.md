# Scientists wonder: Is my AI stealing my ideas?

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-13 · updated: 2026-10-18 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

Scientific American reports growing worry among researchers that AI companies may present users' ideas as their models' own. A mathematician at York University implied OpenAI stole progress he shared with a chatbot, after OpenAI said an internal model solved one of the seven million-dollar math problems, and the New York Times reported a similar dispute between Anthropic and a Copenhagen biologist over a group of enzymes.

Both companies say they did not train on the uploaded data, but scientists, who increasingly use the models as sounding boards, for literature review and for proofs, don't fully trust them. Some suspect the companies may not know everything their agents access.

## Highlights

- Researchers fear AI firms may claim users' ideas as model output.
- Cases: a York University mathematician and OpenAI; a Copenhagen biologist and Anthropic.
- Both firms deny training on the uploaded data.
- Scientists use LLMs for brainstorming, literature review and proofs.
- Distrust persists because companies haven't consistently met academic norms.
