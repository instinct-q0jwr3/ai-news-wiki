# AI is creating a 'human premium' for art created by people

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-11 · updated: 2026-10-22 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

A BBC technology column argues that as generative AI floods creative fields, people are starting to pay extra attention to work that is visibly made by humans. The author opens with a night he DJs in Falmouth, where the live, one-off, human nature of the event is the point, and uses it to ask whether audiences care who or what made the art they enjoy.

The evidence is mixed. Deezer says 44% of tracks uploaded to it are AI-generated and 97% of listeners could not tell the difference, yet more than half of those surveyed said they still care whether AI made a piece. Economist Daniel Susskind says the value of art lies partly in knowing a person got us there, and compares it to chess, which grew more popular after Deep Blue. Journalist Kyle Chayka sees readers and designers moving toward messier, more personal work, and academics are working on a 'human made' label similar to Fairtrade. Philosopher C Thi Nguyen frames it as a question of what sits at the end of our 'why chain' of values.

## Highlights

- Deezer: 44% of uploaded tracks are AI-generated; 97% of listeners could not tell them apart.
- More than half of respondents still say they care whether art is human-made.
- Susskind compares art to chess: people value that a human is behind the moves.
- Chayka sees demand rising for personal, imperfect writing and stranger design.
- Researchers are working on a 'human made' label and some products already carry 'AI-free' marks.
