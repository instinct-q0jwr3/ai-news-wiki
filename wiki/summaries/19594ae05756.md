# AI data centers comprehensively defeated at US midterm elections

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-04 · updated: 2026-11-11 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `compute-buildout`

## Summary

Tom's Hardware reports that US voters rejected data centers at the ballot box. Per the Data Centers on the Ballot tracker, 33 measures restricting data-center development had passed and six more were leading, with only one, a review requirement in Harwood, North Dakota, failing and five without results. A second tracker of about 46 measures in 10 states found all 33 limiting measures set to pass, while 11 of 13 measures to allow data centers looked set to be defeated.

Voters also recalled the mayor and two council members of Piqua, Ohio, and the mayor of Yukon, Oklahoma, for earlier support of data-center bans. The measures vary widely: permanent bans, moratoriums, size or consumption limits, and project-specific votes. The article calls it a setback for the Trump administration's AI push and cites polling that 70% of Americans oppose local build-outs.

It adds a caveat: it is unclear the votes will stop projects already underway. Of more than 300 local bans, only three developments totaling 2.3 gigawatts have been delayed, while a far larger amount of capacity (38 GW in the text) is still expected.
