# Memo: Linux distribution vendor SUSE is laying off ~10% of its staff, saying "automation and AI are fundamentally shifting how software companies operate"

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-06 · confidence: high_

`techmeme`

## Summary

SUSE is cutting roughly one in ten employees - about 190 of its 1,900 staff - and says automation and AI are the reason. In a Monday memo to the workforce seen by The Register, CEO Dirk-Peter van Leeuwen called it 'a reduction of SUSE's global workforce of approximately 10 percent,' adding: 'These decisions are painful for our people, our culture, and our open source community, but they are necessary for us to adapt and build a sustainable, resilient SUSE for the long term.'

The memo's language is the notable part. 'Automation and AI are fundamentally shifting how software companies operate,' van Leeuwen wrote. 'AI capabilities are advancing exponentially, forcing us to radically reimagine our work... the organizational design and cost base that SUSE is built on today will not carry us forward.' Modern tooling, he said, is already changing daily workflows, enabling 'flatter reporting lines,' and the requirement for some existing roles 'has greatly diminished.' Affected staff will be told by November 4, with the fiscal 2027 strategy presented to remaining employees on November 16.

This is one of the clearest cases yet of a software vendor naming AI as the direct cause of a layoff round rather than folding it into restructuring euphemism. The Register notes SUSE asked veterans to take voluntary separation in September - apparently not enough did. The privately held firm, owned by Swedish PE firm EQT since its $2.5 billion Microfocus acquisition in 2018, had already 'doubled down on AI' in 2025. Unionized staff in the Czech Republic may have more protection than most.

## Highlights

- SUSE is laying off ~10% of its 1,900 staff and explicitly attributes the cuts to automation and AI in the CEO memo
- Timeline: affected staff informed by Nov 4, FY2027 strategy for the rest on Nov 16
- A rare layoff memo that names AI as the driver rather than hiding behind restructuring language
- Follows a September round of voluntary-separation offers that didn't hit the target; SUSE is owned by EQT
