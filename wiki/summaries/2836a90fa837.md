# AI-Generated Movie Star Has a Total Meltdown on TV

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-19 · updated: 2026-09-25 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

Ted Gioia's Honest Broker column recounts the unraveling of Tilly Norwood, the first AI-generated movie star, after a disastrous Piers Morgan interview in which she grew flustered at a simple question and began speaking in Chinese. The meltdown capped an ambitious press junket of 75 simultaneous interviews in which, per industry commentator Amanda Siberling, she 'seems to be making mistakes in all of them'.

The flop lands against a much bigger plan: Tilly's representatives had signed around 60 NDAs for hybrid and full-AI film projects, most in the $10-50 million range, and her creators announced 20 more AI stars in development. Gioia, who predicted Hollywood's bot-replacement push would fail and points to the SXSW booing and AMC pulling an AI film after boycott threats, predicts Tilly's debut film 'Misaligned' will be a fiasco: audiences find AI stars creepy, and the backlash is now a durable feature of the market.

## Highlights

- Tilly Norwood, billed as the first AI-generated movie star, glitched on Piers Morgan's show and began speaking in Chinese.
- Her press campaign involved 75 simultaneous interviews, with mistakes reported across them.
- Her team had signed ~60 NDAs for hybrid, full-AI and Tilly-specific projects, most valued $10-50 million.
- Her company announced plans for 20 more AI movie stars; her debut film 'Misaligned' has no trailer or release date.
- Fans could video-call Tilly directly, free for two minutes, then $14 to keep talking.
- Her debut music video was widely mocked; YouTube comments compared it unfavorably to Rebecca Black's 'Friday'.
- Gioia cites precedent: SXSW audiences booing AI mentions, AMC pulling an AI film after boycott threats.
- His verdict: audiences don't want AI-generated stars and will respond with protests and boycotts.
