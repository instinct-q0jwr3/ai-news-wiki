# 'Reverse-Engineer Anything' Is the New Wild West of AI

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-20 · updated: 2026-10-20 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

404 Media chronicles a weekend trend: people using AI to reverse-engineer and replace the software on consumer devices, from swapping smart-TV operating systems for debloated AI-written ones, to reviving bricked Lululemon mirrors, turning smart picture frames into AI calendars, and stripping ads out of baby monitors. It is vibe coding pointed at existing proprietary software: decompiling, cloning features, and installing open-source replacements, partly plagiarism machine, partly users taking back control of devices locked down, ad-laden, or abandoned by their makers. Open-source politics researcher Mike Nolan tells 404 Media that engineers keep saying SaaS is dying as a business model because recreating functionality has become so easy, though he questions whether the clones will be maintained reliably enough for companies to adopt.

## Highlights

- Trend: AI reverse-engineering of TVs, picture frames, baby monitors, bricked Lululemon mirrors
- Goal is usually debloating, de-advertising, or resurrecting abandoned devices
- Mix of decompiling, cloning, vibe coding, and open-source replacement
- Mike Nolan: 'software-as-a-service is dying as a business model'
- Caveat: maintenance burden may limit clone reliability and adoption
- Framed as users reclaiming control from arbitrary lockdown and surveillance
