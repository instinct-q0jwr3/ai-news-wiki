# Eka Robotics, which is developing a "vision-force-action" architecture for its physical AI models, raised a $150M Series A at a $1.45B post-money valuation

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-20 · updated: 2026-10-31 · confidence: high_

`techmeme`

## Summary

TechCrunch profiles Eka Robotics, founded by MIT professor Pulkit Agrawal and former Google DeepMind researcher Tuomas Haarnoja, which has closed a $150 million Series A at a $1.45 billion post-money valuation led by a16z and F-Prime. The draw is a vision-force-action manipulation architecture, a force-sensitive gripper paired with a deep-learning model trained on the relationship between language, force, and action rather than the usual vision-language-action schema. The numbers and the demo reactions stand out: the arm picks about 1,500 objects of up to 1 kg per hour against a human baseline of roughly 600, with over 95% reliability, adapts to a large range of objects without retraining, and retries failed picks with what one professor called a creepy humanness. a16z's Raghu Raguram predicts superhuman performance across many dexterous manipulation tasks within months; one frontier-lab roboticist compared the emotional impact to when robots first achieved reliable legged locomotion.

## Highlights

- $150M Series A at a $1.45B post-money valuation, led by a16z and F-Prime
- Founders: MIT's Pulkit Agrawal and ex-DeepMind's Tuomas Haarnoja
- Vision-force-action model replaces the standard vision-language-action schema
- ~1,500 picks per hour vs ~600 human baseline, >95% reliability, objects up to 1 kg
- Generalizes across many objects without retraining; retries failures persistently
- Arm moves fast enough to damage itself, implying deep trust in the control algorithm
- Veteran roboticists compare the demo to the arrival of legged locomotion
