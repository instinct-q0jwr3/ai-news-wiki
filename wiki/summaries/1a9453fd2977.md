# Anthropic bans users from being 'cruel' to its AI systems

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-30 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `anthropic`

## Summary

Anthropic updated its usage policy to ban users from 'sustained and needless' cruelty toward its AI systems, letting Claude end interactions where people are abusive - something it says it has already done in rare cases. The provision joins a forbidden-conduct list alongside bullying, self-harm promotion and non-consensual intimate imagery, and Anthropic stresses it applies only in extreme, repeated cases, not to 'common versions of user frustration, pushback, dark creative themes, or model testing and research'.

The change reignited debate. Supporters frame it as good manners or 'model welfare'; critics call it deeply wrong anthropomorphisation - 'You can't be cruel to numbers and maths', wrote lawyer Barry Scannell, arguing it leads people to believe AI is something it is not. Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman, who had recently criticized Anthropic for treating AI as human, responded with a melting-face emoji. The annual policy review also barred using Claude for deceptive campaigns and clarified election-related deception rules ahead of US midterms.

## Highlights

- Anthropic bans 'sustained and needless' cruelty toward its AI; Claude may end such chats.
- Company says it applies only in extreme repeated cases, not frustration, pushback or testing.
- Critics call it harmful anthropomorphisation; supporters cite model welfare.
- Mustafa Suleyman (Microsoft AI), a prior critic, reacted with a melting-face emoji.
- Same policy review bars deceptive campaigns and election deception.
