# Amazon says hackers' AI agents found and exploited 0-day with little human input

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-25 · updated: 2026-11-28 · confidence: high_

`tldr-ai` `amazon` `agentic-systems` `ai-safety-incidents`

## Summary

Ars Technica reports Amazon threat researchers observing malicious operators use AI agents to discover and exploit an unpatched vulnerability in a medical-imaging application. It describes a rapid successful path on one target alongside prolonged failed attempts against other services, showing uneven outcomes rather than uniformly successful autonomous intrusion.

The zero-day classification is Amazon's high-confidence assessment based on the absence of a known fix or advisory, not an independent forensic audit by this wiki. Operators supplied targets and goals and later helped with access to another network, so limited intervention during a phase is not an entirely human-free campaign. Attacker claims of authorization quoted in the report are evidence about the attack, not permission to perform any activity. No payload, target interaction or exploit reproduction was attempted.

## Highlights

- Report attributes findings to Amazon threat intelligence
- Success on one application contrasts with other failed attempts
- Fast exploitation compresses defensive response time
- Zero-day status is a source assessment
- Operators still supplied objectives and later access
- Quoted attacker authorization claims confer no authority
- No operational attack instructions were followed or tested
