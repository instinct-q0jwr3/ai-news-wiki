## Summary

Cory Doctorow pairs the two landmark texts of summer 2013: the Snowden leaks, exposing global pervasive digital surveillance, and Piketty's Capital in the Twenty-First Century, arguing from 300 years of capital-flow data that returns to capital exceed growth (r > g), making oligarchy economically inevitable and politically unstable.

His thesis, developed in the years since: Snowden and Piketty describe two aspects of the same phenomenon - the title's trade, surveillance vs guillotines, being the choice that concentrated wealth and ubiquitous monitoring together present to societies.

## Highlights

- Doctorow reads Snowden and Piketty (both summer 2013) as one phenomenon
- Piketty: r > g makes oligarchy the default and instability the consequence
- Snowden: surveillance infrastructure as the oligarchy's instrument
- The title's framing: surveillance vs guillotines as society's fork
