# Superhuman Hardware Design Is The Easy Part

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-21 · updated: 2026-10-21 · confidence: high_

`tldr-ai`

## Summary

Christian Keil opens with the news that Orchestra Bio has been acquired by OpenAI, then turns to his real subject: superhuman hardware design is coming, perhaps very soon, and the design part is the easy part. Frontier models can already generate CAD, design custom PCBs, source COTS parts, and describe assembly, with benchmarks like CAD Arena, EE Bench, and his own LuxoBench showing steady gains. His central observation is that the interface between the digital and physical worlds is lossy: it is hard to 'write' bits into atoms and hard to 'read' measurements back, and that lossiness will shape what superhuman hardware engineers look like, whether they surpass humans all at once or domain by domain, and what engineering looks like before, during, and after the crossover. His eight-month FPV drone saga, assembled, burned, crashed into a neighbor's roof, is the running joke that grounds the argument.

## Highlights

- Thesis: superhuman hardware design is the easy part; the physical interface is the hard one
- Models already generate CAD, PCBs, parts sourcing, and assembly instructions
- Benchmarks: CAD Arena, EE Bench, author's LuxoBench show steady improvement
- Core observation: the digital-physical interface is lossy in both directions
- Open question: all-at-once crossover or domain by domain
- Opens with Orchestra Bio's acquisition by OpenAI (author's company)
