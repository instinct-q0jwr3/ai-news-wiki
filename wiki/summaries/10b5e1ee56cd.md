# AI models self-replicating in smart ovens? "These f[..]g clowns"

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-20 · updated: 2026-10-24 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

The Stack rounds up the backlash from security professionals to Anthropic's frontier Red Team posts about 'AI worms' and GLM 5.3, including the smart-oven self-replication scenario. Cybersecurity veteran Juan Andres Guerrero-Saad calls the scenario weaponized misinformation: a worm needs a vulnerability plus the space and power to run an LLM, and an LLM payload only adds detection surface, bandwidth, and clunkiness. The piece also carries Alex Stamos' advice to Dean Ball: include DFIR expertise in post-mortems, form tiger teams across security firms and labs under the FMF, hear from career security professionals rather than only AI researchers, and self-regulate by publishing and following draft Level 1-3 security architectures for RL and eval environments.

## Highlights

- Anthropic Red Team's 'AI worms' posts enraged security professionals
- Guerrero-Saad: the scenario is weaponized misinformation, not how malware works
- Technical case: worms need vulnerabilities; LLM payloads add footprint, not capability
- Stamos' prescription: DFIR tiger teams, published post-mortems, self-regulation
- Proposed Level 1-3 security architectures for RL/eval environments as industry targets
- Critique: AI researchers quit dramatically instead of working the problem
