# The U.S. Census Bureau and “Super Intelligence”

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-08 · confidence: high_

## Summary

Simon Willison went back to the US Census Bureau's Business Trends and Outlook Survey to update the AI-adoption chart he built last year, and found the Bureau had fallen foul of Executive Order 14434: the survey question now asks businesses about their use of 'Super Intelligence or SI (formerly referred to as Artificial Intelligence or AI).' The renaming is asinine, he notes, but the methodological break is the real story - the question changed materially twice, from AI use 'in producing goods or services' to use 'in any business function,' which makes long-run comparisons treacherous.

The numbers under the new wording: the largest companies (250+ employees) answered 'yes' about 12 percent of the time under the old production-only question in September 2025; under the broader wording, roughly 30 percent by February 2026 and around 40 percent by October 2026. The survey machinery matters for interpreting any of it - 200,000 businesses per wave out of 1.2 million, panels cycling so each business answers every twelve weeks. Willison's takeaway pairs with Apollo's zero-payoff finding from the same news cycle: what you ask determines what 'adoption' you find, and a rebranded question in a federal survey is now part of the evidentiary chain.

## Highlights
