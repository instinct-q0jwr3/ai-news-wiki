# Pacing the frontier may be sincere - and strategically useful for labs

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-21 · updated: 2026-09-21 · confidence: high_

`techmeme`

## Summary

Ben Thompson argues that Anthropic's 'Pacing the Frontier' position is sincere but also strategically convenient. Framed as safety concern, pacing model improvement would also relieve several overhangs the frontier labs face precisely because models are improving so fast. The essay extends his 'Anthropic's Safety Superpower' argument from three months earlier, that genuine belief in safety rhetoric gives the company license to pursue extraordinary goals aligned with its business interests, but approaches from the opposite direction: the business problems pacing would solve.

The piece opens with a broadside against the Effective Altruism-adjacent philosophy he sees behind doomsday concern: granting equivalent moral weight to all beings who may ever exist tilts the scales absurdly toward safetyism, making innovation impossible, and taps a religious psychology where questioning the premise is heresy. He declares himself a heretic: doubt in our ability to foresee the future, faith in humanity figuring things out. The essay references his 'Agents Over Bubbles' argument from six months prior that the agentic paradigm, kicked off by Opus 4.5 in late November 2025, is extraordinarily capable and token-hungry.
