# Adventures in Weight Space Programming

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-12 · updated: 2026-11-13 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

Beaker reports experiments in which an agent edits a small language model's weights directly, without gradient-based training. On Qwen3.5-0.8B, a one-neuron change reduced a repetitive thinking habit and raised MATH-500 accuracy from 7.4% to 37.4% in one run. A simple decoding ban on the recurring token achieved a similar result, suggesting much of the gain came from avoiding wasted reasoning rather than adding mathematical ability.

The team also combined edits, reproduced a stronger MIT result and tested brevity and abstention goals. The tradeoffs matter: shorter answers often lost accuracy, and a score could miss unwanted side effects. These are experiments on a small model and selected tasks, not evidence that direct weight editing generally improves every capability.

## Highlights

- Agent searches weight changes without gradient training
- One-neuron edit suppresses a repetitive thinking habit
- Reported math gain largely reflects fewer unfinished answers
- A decoding-only intervention achieved similar performance
- Combined edits and original-paper reproduction were also tested
- Brevity experiments exposed accuracy and scoring tradeoffs
