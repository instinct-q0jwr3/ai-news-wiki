# Inside the Frontier Labs: The First AGI Organizations

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-12 · confidence: high_

`tldr-ai`

## Summary

A researcher with friends at the frontier labs describes what recursive self-improvement looks like from the inside, and it is mundane in the way revolutions often are: researchers start the day asking their agent what they should do; the tests they used to run are the agent's job; the human steps in where the agent gets stuck - approving a PR, unblocking another team. His test for an 'AGI organization': does what it builds go straight back into building the next version of itself? An insurer's claims AI never will; the frontier labs' tooling already does.

The numbers he cites: at OpenAI's research org, every human workday was matched by 3.1 agent workdays as of mid-August, after agent runtime passed human working time in June; at Anthropic, more than 80 percent of merged code by line count was written by Claude as of May. Researcher Zuxin Liu wrote in September that he had stopped writing code by hand two months earlier - his workspace is Codex and Slack. The argument: internet-era companies were never built this way, and the frontier labs have rebuilt their organization, context and infrastructure around how AI works - which is why the next kind of company is taking shape there first.
