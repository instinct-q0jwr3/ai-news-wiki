# The AI graveyard: a running list of projects and startups that didn't make it

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-25 · updated: 2026-10-28 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

TechCrunch opens a running 'AI graveyard' list of projects and startups that shut down, pivoted or badly missed expectations, framing it as a record of what failed bets can teach the industry rather than a scorecard of flops. Anchor stat: S&P Global Market Intelligence says about 42% of corporate AI initiatives are ultimately abandoned. Recent startup deaths include Relay, an AI workflow-automation tool squeezed out when OpenAI and Google built automation into their own platforms; Character.ai, which filed for bankruptcy five years after launch (despite Google's $2.7B licensing deal); Huxe, the NotebookLM-alumni audio app; Yupp, an 800-model comparison playground that never found product-market fit; and Figgs AI, a role-play character platform too expensive to keep free.

Big-tech entries are equally instructive. OpenAI's super-app redesign of ChatGPT (Chat/Codex/Work modes, 'ChatGPT Classic') was rolled back after user backlash; its Atlas browser lasted less than a year; Operator was folded into ChatGPT; Sora shut down in March 2026 over costs and retention. Apple's improved Siri slipped for years, contributed to a $250M marketing settlement, and only reached the iOS 27 beta this July. Microsoft's Recall survived a privacy backlash and a year-long delay but still faces researcher demonstrations of data extraction. Hardware fills the rest: Humane's $230M AI Pin died and sold to HP for $116M, while Rabbit's R1 endures as a cautionary tale now pivoting to 'Project Cyberdeck.' Notion Mail closes September 22 after noticing users preferred their own AI agents.

## Highlights
