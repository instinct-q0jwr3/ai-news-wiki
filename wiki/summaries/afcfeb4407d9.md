## Summary

Cory Doctorow argues the hyperscaler AI business can't work: gigantic capex, negative unit economics that worsen with each model generation, and profitability claims that rely on non-GAAP 'secret mathematics' - the same trick Softbank pulled with WeWork, which he flatly calls accounting fraud. But even granting the labs their claimed turn to profitability, he says, the structure of the market kills it: chatbot switching costs are near zero, so the last time Anthropic shipped a major Claude update, OpenAI's best customers moved almost overnight. Every CES gadget maker he asked said they could swap chatbot providers 'with the click of a mouse.'

That makes AI a Red Queen's Race - a beggar-thy-neighbor doom loop where each company must keep building a faster railroad because customers will jump to whichever is newest. The only escapes are a nonaggression pact (blatantly illegal under antitrust) that would still leave the field to Chinese open-weight models, currently just four months behind and distillable through public APIs. Hence the cynical reading of the 'superintelligence moratorium' campaign: it is the one move that gets the government to permit the labs to stop competing and to ban the Chinese competition, all dressed as safety. The word-guessing program has to be about to wake up and devour us all - otherwise the math never works.

## Highlights

- Doctorow: hyperscaler AI has negative unit economics that get worse with each generation, and the industry denies it
- Profitability claims lean on non-GAAP definitions - 'so cool that profitability requires a novel, secret mathematics'
- He compares it to Softbank-era WeWork: 'it was accounting fraud then, and it's accounting fraud now'
- Even if unit economics turned positive, near-zero switching costs mean each new model release poaches rivals' best customers
- Evidence from CES: every AI gadget maker said they could switch chatbot providers with a mouse click
- The result is a Red Queen's Race - a fatal beggar-thy-neighbor loop of perpetual railroad-building
- A nonaggression pact would be 'incredibly, fantastically illegal' under antitrust, and wouldn't stop Chinese open-weight models
- Chinese models lag the frontier by about four months and can be distilled into trusted models
- His thesis: the superintelligence moratorium is the legal path to stop competing - government-granted cartel plus a ban on Chinese models
