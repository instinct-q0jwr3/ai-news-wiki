# Code like a surgeon

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-10-08 · confidence: high_

`geoffrey-litt`

## Summary

Geoffrey Litt pushes back on the idea that AI turns programmers into managers or editors - a view he calls dangerously incomplete. His model is the surgeon: someone who does the actual work, but whose time and skill are leveraged by a team that handles prep, secondary tasks and admin. His goal is to spend 100% of his own time on what matters - as a UI prototyper, tinkering with design concepts.

The tasks he now hands to agents: writing guides to relevant parts of the codebase before a big task, spiking a rough first attempt at a large change as a sketch to react to, fixing typescript errors with a clear spec, and writing documentation - often run async in the background or overnight. The key discipline is Karpathy's autonomy slider: core design work stays hands-on with tight feedback loops (Cursor tab-complete), while secondary work runs loose and unsupervised for hours (Claude Code, with Codex CLI becoming a contender). Conflating the two patterns, he warns, is where AI workflows go wrong.

## Highlights

- Not manager or editor: code like a surgeon - do the work, leveraged by a support team
- Goal: 100% of his time on design-concept tinkering
- Delegated tasks: codebase guides, spike attempts, spec'd typescript fixes, docs
- Secondary tasks run async - during lunch or literally overnight
- Karpathy's autonomy slider separates primary from secondary work
- Core work: hands-on, fast feedback (Cursor tab-complete)
- Background work: Claude Code, with Codex CLI a rising contender
