# US Hedge Funds Just Sold Every AI Stock They Bought Since 2025

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-03 · updated: 2026-11-12 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `small-specialist-models`

## Summary

Goldman Sachs data shows US hedge funds sold AI stocks for five straight days in October and erased every AI purchase made since the start of 2025. Morgan Stanley says funds have cut more than 8% of their stock bets since June, the biggest retreat since at least 2017. Big tech fell hard enough last week to wipe $2 trillion off the market, but the damage concentrated in memory chips: Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron and SanDisk lost over $700 billion in under two weeks while the S&P 500 barely moved.

The mechanism is leverage. Funds borrow to enlarge bets, so falling prices force selling, which forces more selling; on the heaviest day they sold holdings about 3.5 times faster than they opened shorts. On October 22 Goldman's basket of hedge funds' favorite stocks fell 4.2%, its worst day since 2011.

The counter-argument is demand. Macro investor Jordi Visser blames borrowed money rather than weak fundamentals, estimating the leverage took 18 months to build and 75 days to unwind. Profits are up about 20%, AI compute demand still exceeds supply, and SK Hynix forecasts a memory shortage until 2030.

## Highlights

- Hedge funds sold AI stocks five days straight in October, undoing all purchases since 2025 (Goldman)
- Memory makers lost over $700B in under two weeks; S&P 500 barely moved
- Leverage unwinding: forced selling 3.5x faster than new shorts; Oct 22 worst day for the favorites basket since 2011
- Bull counter: profits up ~20%, compute demand above supply, SK Hynix sees shortage to 2030
