# Fast and Hard Code

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high_

`armin-ronacher` `agentic-systems` `small-specialist-models` `ai-coding-agents`

## Summary

Armin Ronacher argues that LLMs have made language choice far less consequential: agents don't feel the friction that shaped human preferences, and if you dislike a language choice you can seemingly have the codebase rewritten in another - including one you don't know. As a result, people increasingly choose languages 'based on the marketing'.

As a long-time Rust programmer he finds it fascinating that people now ship Rust who never would have picked it before. He credits two vibe shifts: renewed hunger for fast software, and LLMs being exceptionally good at optimizing code without changing behavior - a combination long championed by Mitchell Hashimoto, Charlie Marsh, Jarred Sumner, Daniel Lemire and others.

## Highlights

- LLMs flatten language-choice friction: rewriting in another language is suddenly cheap
- Ronacher: people now pick languages 'based on the marketing' more than before
- Rust is being shipped by programmers who would never have chosen it pre-LLM
- Two vibe shifts: demand for fast software plus LLM strength at behavior-preserving optimization
- Names Hashimoto, Marsh, Sumner and Lemire as carriers of the performance-obsessed, agent-friendly culture
