# How I changed teaching after AI managed to do all my homework assignments

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

Christian Kästner, who teaches the Machine Learning in Production course, describes what changed once AI agents could do all of his assignments. Back in 2021 GPT-3 was already passing his reading quizzes without seeing the assigned paper and he changed nothing; five years later he has redesigned most assessments around one strategy: no longer test understanding with anything done at home - evaluate through interactions with a TA, on exams, and on a video demo. The learning goals barely moved: engineering tradeoffs, anticipating and mitigating risks, teamwork - skills he thinks are still worth acquiring even if they can be simulated.

The uncomfortable part, he says upfront, is that AI is actively undermining evidence-based teaching practices. Frequent low-stakes assessment with feedback loses to exams when AI does the low-stakes work. His resubmission safety net - a core grading-for-equity recommendation - was being gamed: students submitted generated solutions unread, then used the grading feedback to resubmit, so resubmissions now carry a 10% penalty. In-class discussion questions get offloaded to models too, but pen-and-paper fixes would raise stress and delay feedback, so he errs toward keeping low-stakes interactions even when abusable. Students may use AI anywhere, in any form, without attribution, except in written and oral exams - policing is infeasible, and students need to learn responsible use anyway.
