# Two Futures for LLMs in Mathematics

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-19 · confidence: high_

`lobsters`

## Summary

This essay contrasts two recent visions for LLMs in mathematics and theoretical computer science. In one corner, Anthropic shared a result with Columbia's Josh Alman and MIT's Virginia Williams - the researchers known for shaving the matrix-multiplication exponent via careful operation counting in the laser method - pointing to LLMs as collaborators in elite algorithmic research.

The backdrop is how stubborn these problems are: 3SUM has seen only polylogarithmic improvements over the quadratic textbook algorithm in decades, and 55 years after Strassen, researchers are still nudging the matrix-multiplication exponent downward. The 'two futures' framing asks whether LLMs will be tools that accelerate this kind of human ingenuity or something that changes the research process itself.

## Highlights

- Two competing visions for how LLMs will matter in math and TCS research.
- Anthropic engaged Josh Alman and Virginia Williams, known for improving matrix-multiplication bounds.
- Their approach: careful counting of operations inside the existing laser method.
- Context: 3SUM remains essentially quadratic after decades; omega is still being shaved 55 years after Strassen.
- The open question: LLM as research assistant versus LLM changing the research process.
