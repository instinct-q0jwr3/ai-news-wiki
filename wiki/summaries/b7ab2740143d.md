# Joanna Stern Interviews Mark Zuckerberg

## Summary

Joanna Stern's exclusive interview with Mark Zuckerberg around Meta's Connect conference, where the company unveiled audio-only Ray-Ban smart glasses, new VR glasses and its Muse voice features. Pressed on the Washington debate over whether AI development is moving too fast, Zuckerberg rejected industrywide coordination: each lab should police itself and 'take the time' internally when it sees issues.

Zuckerberg laid out his vision of personal computing's future: most people eventually wearing smart glasses with a personal AI agent that sees what you see, hears what you hear and whispers in your ear. Stern spent much of the interview on trust, since that vision hands Meta an extraordinarily intimate view of users' lives - part of Zuckerberg's latest reinvention of the company around AI and a closer relationship with its users. The full interview is on YouTube; the newsletter promises four big takeaways.

## Highlights

- Exclusive Stern-Zuckerberg interview timed to Meta Connect: audio-only Ray-Ban glasses, new VR glasses, Muse voice
- On AI going too fast: 'I don't think that we need some kind of industrywide coordination'
- Zuckerberg's safety model: each lab polices itself and slows down internally when it sees issues
- His endgame: most people wearing smart glasses with a personal AI agent seeing and hearing what they do
- Stern's angle is trust - the vision requires giving Meta access to an unusual depth of personal life
- Framed as Zuckerberg's latest reinvention of Meta: AI, personal computing, and intimacy with users
- And yes, she asked whether AI will kill us all
