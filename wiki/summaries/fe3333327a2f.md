# Why I'm Short the AI Buildout Even Though I'm Long Computers

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-26 · updated: 2026-10-31 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `compute-buildout`

## Summary

Paul Baumgart lays out a short thesis on the AI infrastructure build-out while staying long on computers themselves: LLMs are a genuine breakthrough, but the financing behind the data centers has detached from economic reality. His four premises: interest rates are rising, partly on capital competition and partly on the Iran War; the build-out is funded increasingly by debt, with AI-related issuance up more than fourfold to nearly $500 billion in 2026; US AI labs are the primary buyers whose projections justify the scale; and those labs' revenue is plateauing while cheap open-weights rivals like DeepSeek, Z.ai and Moonshot compete their margins away.

The numbers have moved fast. Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Oracle issued more than $228 billion in bonds by mid-October 2026, over double their entire 2025 total, and JPMorgan expects the big cloud companies to spend $800 billion this year and over $1 trillion a year by 2027, mostly debt-financed. Put together, Baumgart argues, companies borrowing half a trillion dollars at rising rates are relying on customers who will not be able to pay the tab - which is why he is short the build-out even while believing in the technology.
