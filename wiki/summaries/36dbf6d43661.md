# Updates to site guidelines and AI/LLM tags

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-19 · updated: 2026-10-21 · confidence: high_

`lobsters` `ai-policy-regulation` `labor-displacement`

## Summary

Lobsters' moderators announce two policy changes prompted by a rising volume of moderation work around the vibecoding tag and AI/LLM content, aiming to keep submissions respectful of readers' time and intelligence. First, the about page is being rewritten for clarity on topicality and self-promotion: the site restates its narrow computing focus, its tests for what makes a good submission (will it improve the reader's next program or still matter in five years), and its limits on self-promotional posting. Second, and more substantively, the site will no longer allow submissions that are bare code repositories: the mods found repo links made up the bulk of low-effort, self-promotional submissions, and being dropped into an unfamiliar codebase rarely starts a good discussion because readers lack the context to engage. The framing emphasizes good-faith community norms over an anti-AI stance.

## Highlights

- Lobsters will stop accepting submissions that are just links to code repositories
- Rationale: bare repo links are the bulk of low-effort self-promotion and rarely seed discussion
- Changes were prompted by growing moderation load around the vibecoding tag and AI/LLM content
- The about page is being rewritten to clarify topicality and self-promotion rules
- Site restates its tests for good stories: improves your next program, deepens understanding, stays interesting for years
- Self-promotion stays capped at roughly a quarter of a user's activity; new users (first 70 days) cannot self-promote
- Framed as refocusing on reader-respecting content, not as an anti-AI policy
