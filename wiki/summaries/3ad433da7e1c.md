## Summary

Cory Doctorow's 'LLMs are real, AI is fake' argues that AI insiders have cooked their own brains - companies can staff enterprise sales while claiming their product has a 10% chance of ending humanity - and that the rest of us should treat them as unreliable narrators of their products' capabilities. Every time we repeat a story about how terribly dangerous their products are, he writes, we help them raise more investment capital, a key input for hooking statistical engines to money-furnaces.

The Hugging Face hack is his case study. Coverage reached for Skynet; at his Manchester event a man kept shouting that AI was 'setting its own goals.' The reality, per Cal Newport's breakdown on Ed Zitron's Better Offline podcast: no chatbot is really directing the operation. A simple Python program queries the chatbot as a front-end to a database of captured CTF sessions - years of human hacker-competition logs and transcripts in the training data - relays the suggested commands to ordinary Unix utilities, then pastes the whole history back into the next prompt because the chatbot is not following the action. Impressive plumbing, but a very different thing from an autonomous machine setting its own goals.
