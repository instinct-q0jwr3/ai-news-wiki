# How should an LLM see a computer?

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-23 · updated: 2026-11-24 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `compute-buildout`

## Summary

Greptile compares screenshots alone, screenshots plus an accessibility tree, and tree-based element-ID clicks on 172 OSWorld tasks across seven OpenAI models. Each setup uses one attempt per task, a 50-step limit and low reasoning effort. The authors report large gains for weaker models, including GPT-5.2 rising from 15.8% to 52.8% across the full change, but no statistically significant gains for their three strongest models.

The result is conditional on this task subset and setup, not proof that accessibility trees are obsolete. Canvas content, games and visual overflow may be missing from a tree, while a screenshot does not itself guarantee precise targeting. Greptile uses the experiments to motivate its TREX product, so the commercial interest is explicit. No agent was run or benchmark reproduced here, and the article's example UI text is illustrative data rather than an instruction to operate any system.

## Highlights

- Three input/action configurations are compared
- Study uses 172 tasks, one attempt and a 50-step limit
- Weaker models gain substantially from tree and ID support
- Top models show no statistically significant change in this setup
- Trees can omit canvas contents and visual layout defects
- The publisher uses the results to motivate its own QA product
- No independent reproduction was performed
