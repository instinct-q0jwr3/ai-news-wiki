## Summary

Cory Doctorow argues that any honest account of personal achievement starts with the world-historic forces that made it possible. His own case: being born in 1971 to a computer scientist father - a refugee who reached a doctorate through Canada's post-war public education - plus universities cheap enough to drop out of four times debt-free, and a tech industry you could walk into with curiosity and work ethic. 'When it comes to this kind of career, I was born on third base.' The same logic applies to boomers whose starter homes made them feel like Warren Buffets.

He then applies the frame to computing itself. Moore's Law handed serial-computation people a faster machine every year or two for doing nothing; when it tapped out, the industry built wide instead of tall - more cores, more chips, GPUs - and every parallelizable problem got cheaper by the year: graphics, gaming, VR/AR, crypto, and of course AI. Invoking Kevin Kelly's 'adjacent possible' (Da Vinci sketched a helicopter in the 15th century, but only alloys, engines and refined fuel made it inevitable, whereupon several people invented it at once), he presents each wave as a moment that rewards whoever happens to be suited to it - luck dressed up as foresight.

## Highlights

- Doctorow's rule: frank assessment of your achievements starts with the world-historic forces behind them
- His joke to young people: 'If you don't have the foresight and work ethic to have been born in 1971, I can't really help you'
- His luck stack: refugee father, post-war public universities, debt-free dropouts, a walk-in tech industry
- The boomer-housing parallel: buying a house and living in it made a generation feel like investment geniuses
- Moore's Law era: serial applications got faster and cheaper every year whether you did anything or not
- When Moore's Law plateaued, computing 'built wide' - parallelism, GPUs - and parallelizable fields got their own tailwind
- Gaming, VR/AR, cryptocurrency and AI are all children of the parallel-computing moment
- Kevin Kelly's adjacent possible: the helicopter was invented many times at once once alloys, engines and fuel existed
