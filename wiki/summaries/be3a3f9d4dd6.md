# 'AI' might be the end of our careers

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-26 · updated: 2026-11-27 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

Baldur Bjarnason argues that coding automation may weaken developers' bargaining power by letting employers reclassify specialist work as generic labor, even before complete replacement is possible. The essay uses a hypothetical team assembled from observed cases to distinguish deskilling from the question of whether models can fully replace developers.

This is labor-market analysis and advocacy, not a measured wage study or an inevitable forecast. Its historical comparisons and judgments about generated-code quality are not independently tested here. Calls to organize and resist particular industry choices express the author's position; they do not establish the views of all developers or authorize action against any person.

## Highlights

- Main mechanism is perceived deskilling and substitutability
- Full occupational replacement is not required by the argument
- Illustrative team is explicitly hypothetical
- Wage effects are predictions rather than measured estimates
- Advocacy is separate from empirical evidence
- No action targeting people or projects was taken
