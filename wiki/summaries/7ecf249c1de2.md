# Open models can do 90 percent of agentic work at a twentieth of the cost

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-03 · updated: 2026-11-14 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `agentic-systems`

## Summary

Gartner's new strategic planning assumption: by 2030, 90% of enterprise operational agent workflow steps will run on open-weight models. The Register's writeup quotes analysts Mark Petty and Raymond Paquet: nine in ten agent workflow steps are routine - classification, tool calling, passing, validating, reconciling - and open-weight models already handle them 'at parity for a quarter of the price of the cheapest closed model.' Against a frontier model run on every step, the saving reaches a twentieth; even adjusting for open models' verbosity and retries, it's five to six times per task.

But the analysts frame cost as the by-product, not the argument. The argument is control: workflow history is a large part of what an acquirer pays for in a SaaS company, and every routine step sent to a frontier API moves a slice of it outside the perimeter. Domain-specific open models win where 'a small number of highly stable steps run millions of times a month.'

Their corollary reshuffles the value chain: models are commodities, and the value sits in harnesses - the tooling that routes each step to the cheapest adequate model, imposes guardrails and manages agent fleets. It's the enterprise-architecture version of this week's Trajectory funding (post-training as a service) and the seldo hiring essay: the frontier labs' real competition for routine work is free.

## Highlights

- Gartner: by 2030, 90% of enterprise agent workflow steps will execute on open-weight models
