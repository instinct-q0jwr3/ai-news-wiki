# METR updates its time horizon plot to add judgment-heavy tasks, finding some AI capabilities still grow exponentially but models lag at designing AI evaluations

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-20 · updated: 2026-11-27 · confidence: high_

`techmeme` `external-evaluation`

## Summary

MIT Technology Review examines METR’s expanded time-horizon evaluation and explains why progress on verifiable coding tasks does not straightforwardly predict research automation. New judgment-heavy tasks show improvement but still fall short of usable results in the reported evaluation-design examples.

The report highlights sparse task samples, estimated human completion times and dependence on individual raters. Those limits make a clean trend harder to establish for judgment than for precisely scored tasks. This reporting is not an independent reproduction of METR’s primary results, nor proof that future progress must stop.

## Highlights

- Time horizon measures task difficulty in human time
- It is not uninterrupted autonomous running time
- Judgment tasks complicate success measurement
- Some human-time values are estimates
- Small samples constrain extrapolation
- Agreement with one rater is not universal judgment
- Observed improvement differs from usable research output
