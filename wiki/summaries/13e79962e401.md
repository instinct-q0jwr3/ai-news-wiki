# AI Contributions to GHC

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-29 · updated: 2026-10-29 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

GHC now has a concrete policy for LLM-assisted contributions, and by mid-October maintainers were already citing it in reviews - Julian Ospald invoking 'P3 of the AI policy', Andreas Klebinger describing an LLM-generated bug fix as 'fine, it's policy'. Serokell's writeup traces the document's lineage: it follows the text Simon Peyton Jones developed through the ghc-devs discussion rather than the competing 'shared statement' floated in July, whose own authors said it was never meant to be a contribution policy. The structure evolved in debate: 'prioritise people and community' was promoted to P1, the original three points (make merge requests easy to review, take full responsibility, declare direct LLM use) became P2-P4, and 'write the final version yourself' survived as P5, a strong preference rather than a requirement.

The framing is pragmatic, not ideological. With more than 6,500 open tickets, GHC's scarce resource is reviewer attention, and tool-assisted work is only welcome if it does not leave maintainers doing the contributor's thinking for them. The policy explicitly takes no position on whether LLMs are good or bad in general. Serokell discloses its stake: Vladislav Zavialov and Andrei Borzenkov work on GHC there, and Borzenkov participated in the policy discussion.
