# Imagine an AI future where being banned means you are now economically useless

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-25 · updated: 2026-10-25 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

A viral post by boneGPT sketches a dystopian endgame of platform-controlled AI: in a fully integrated AI future, a terms-of-service ban makes you economically useless - cut off from the superhuman tooling everyone around you relies on. The argument lands on a policy point: open source is the only real protection of economic viability, and it notes that OpenAI and Anthropic recently sought legal permission to collaborate, which would make a single ban hit across platforms. A reply from log4j grounds the fear in the present: he says he was just banned (apparently from OpenAI) for local reverse-engineering work that tripped the Daybreak security system, losing $500 and banked resets.

## Highlights

- boneGPT's scenario: in an integrated AI future, a TOS ban equals economic uselessness while everyone else stays superhuman.
- His prescription: open source as the only protection of freedom and economic viability.
- He notes OpenAI and Anthropic recently sought legal cover to collaborate - one ban could then span both platforms.
- A reply claims a real ban for local reverse engineering that tripped OpenAI's Daybreak system, costing $500 and banked resets.
- The exchange frames account bans as an emerging systemic risk of agentic AI dependence.
