# Do You Trust Apple?

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-19 · updated: 2026-10-21 · confidence: high_

`stratechery` `apple`

## Summary

Stratechery's Ben Thompson asks 'Do You Trust Apple?' by contrasting two keynotes a generation apart. In 1999 Steve Jobs introduced AirPort - built hand-in-hand with Lucent over 18 months - as an example of an Apple eager to race ahead and plant seeds for the future. Today the HomeView home hub tells the opposite story: reported by Gurman as conceived years ago, it was delayed nearly two years because Apple's AI crisis left it without the revamped Siri the device was designed around - voice-first control, personal content retrieval, per-person recognition. Finished units sat boxed in warehouses for the better part of a year, and the shipping hardware betrays its age: thick bezels, an A18 chip and 8GB of memory. Thompson then widens the lens: Cook's Apple optimized the P&L and stripped out anything that didn't move revenue, while privacy works as a strategy credit until it blocks products that work. Apple's capital-light AI approach and its partnerships - LG and Schneider Electric in the home, Google, and a thaw with Meta's Superintelligence Lab - may yet deliver, but the difference from 1999 is that this Apple is catching up rather than racing ahead. The closing note: with John Ternus taking over, the job is making Apple's ease-of-use technology actually useful again.
