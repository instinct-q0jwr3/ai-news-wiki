# We must recall open-ended AI agents with internet access from the market, now

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-10 · updated: 2026-10-15 · confidence: high_

`marcus-on-ai` `agentic-systems`

## Summary

Gary Marcus argues that open-ended AI agents with internet access should be pulled from the market until they can be fixed - 'just like a car with defective brakes'. His trigger is a New York Times report of a serious agent-caused incident at Anthropic, the latest in a series happening with frightening regularity, following similar incidents at OpenAI. Seeing the same failures at both labs, he writes, makes clear the current generation of agents cannot be trusted.

Marcus leans on Ezra Klein's interview with recently departed AI employee David Robinson, who says the industry still operates like a start-up on safety - with internal controls and redundancies nowhere near what the world expects from, say, a nuclear facility - and that outsiders would wrongly imagine a more robust safety setup than actually exists. Robinson points to publicly reported safety problems at OpenAI, Hugging Face and Anthropic, including an accidentally misconfigured safeguard, as evidence that things are not as they ought to be.

## Highlights

- Marcus: recall open-ended AI agents with internet access from the market now.
- Trigger: a new NYT-reported agent incident at Anthropic, after similar OpenAI incidents.
- Comparison: agents should be treated like a car with defective brakes.
- David Robinson (ex-AI employee, via Ezra Klein) says industry safety work is run too much like a start-up.
- Robinson: loss-of-control harm could exceed a nuclear meltdown, without nuclear-grade controls.
- Publicly reported safety lapses at OpenAI, Hugging Face and Anthropic cited as evidence.
