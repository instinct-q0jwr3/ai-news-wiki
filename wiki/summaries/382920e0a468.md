# We are going to kill “unalive”

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-06 · updated: 2026-10-13 · confidence: high_

`anil-dash` `google` `meta` `ai-policy-regulation`

## Summary

Anil Dash wants to kill 'unalive' - the euphemism people use for dying, death and killing on platforms where the accurate words supposedly trigger algorithmic punishment. He notes the term is meaningless to most people born in the 20th century, yet has escaped the feed: he has heard 'he unalived himself' said out loud in the physical world, where no algorithm exists to appease.

The immediate trigger is the online conversation around the Cornell rape case, where people routinely write r*pe, 'grape' or use the grape emoji - often without knowing whether the word is actually banned, since nobody dares find out. Dash is careful to support content warnings and sensitivity markers; his objection is to a censorship regime that is assumed, unaccountable and now colonizing offline speech.

## Highlights

- 'Unalive' is algorithm-dodging vocabulary that has jumped from feeds into spoken language.
- The Cornell case discussion shows people writing r*pe, 'grape' and the grape emoji reflexively.
- Much of the censorship is assumed rather than verified - nobody tests the forbidden words.
- Dash supports content warnings; his target is unaccountable, self-censoring speech norms.
- Older generations mostly have no idea what the word means.
