# We are going to kill “unalive”

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-06 · updated: 2026-10-08 · confidence: high_

`anil-dash` `google` `meta` `ai-policy-regulation`

## Summary

Anil Dash argues that platform algorithms are reshaping everyday language. People use 'unalive' for death and 'grape' or a grape emoji for rape because they assume, or have experienced, that plain words get them punished. He supports content warnings and abbreviations chosen for care, but says euphemisms made to appease platforms defeat the filters people rely on and hand a generation's vocabulary to companies. He ties it to habits such as children saying 'please like and subscribe', and says the trend has to stop. It is an opinion piece and AI is only a side thread. Basis: first half of the essay was read.
