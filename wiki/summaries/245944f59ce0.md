# Anthropic releases FlashProve, an open-source prototype that cryptographically verifies an AI response came from a given model, not one that a router swapped in

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-04 · updated: 2026-11-12 · confidence: high_

`techmeme` `anthropic`

## Summary

Anthropic released FlashProve, an open-source prototype for provable inference: a way for a user to verify that a response came from the model a provider claims, without access to the weights and without being able to rerun the model. Authors come from Anthropic, EPFL, StarkWare, MIT and Succinct.

The motivation is trust. Today a user cannot tell whether a router secretly substituted a cheaper model, or whether an attacker tampered with the token stream. Independent evaluators need to know the model they test is the model that gets served, and customers of third-party routers want to know tokens came from the model they paid for. Verification is hard because frontier weights are private and re-running the model costs too much. The approach relies on cryptographic proofs, such as zero-knowledge proofs, where the provider commits to a model and proves each response came from it.

Anthropic frames it as a prototype it wants others to take and improve, and says it could matter for pacing frontier AI and for regulation, since providers could demonstrate they meet commitments. Cost and speed figures were beyond the portion read.
