## Summary

Armin Ronacher wanted to see if a cheap Chinese CarPlay dongle could run something more interesting than its stock firmware. Through a conversation with LLM assistants he learned about CatPlay, a Rust reimplementation of the CarPlay protocol that runs on Carlinkit devices, and bought a Carlinkit Mini Ultra to try it.

The device that arrived was branded correctly but turned out to use entirely different hardware from the Ingenic model the CatPlay author used - normally where the story would stop. But it's 2026: armed with an LLM, he keeps going, and the post chronicles what latent capability these tools add to a hobbyist willing to push past a dead end.

## Highlights

- Goal: run custom code on a cheap CarPlay dongle while keeping CarPlay passthrough
- Found CatPlay, a Rust CarPlay-protocol reimplementation for Carlinkit devices, via an LLM chat
- His Carlinkit Mini Ultra arrived with different hardware than expected
- 2026 twist: with an LLM's help, a wrong-hardware dead end becomes solvable
- A study in the latent powers AI adds to hobbyist hacking
