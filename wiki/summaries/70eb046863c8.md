## Summary

I’m not the kind of person that develops a strong attachment to their own work.

When I decided to leave Redis, about 1620 days ago (~ 4.44 years), I never looked at the source code, commit messages, or anything related to Redis again.

From time to time, when I needed Redis, I just downloaded it and compiled it.

## Highlights

- I just typed “make” and I was very happy to see that, after many years, building Redis was still so simple.
- My detachment was not the result of me hating my past work.
- While in the long run my creative work was less and less important and the “handling the project” activities became more and more substantial — a shift that many programmers are able to do, but that’s not my bread and butter — well, I…
- However, I don’t share the vision that most people at my age (I’m 47 now) have: that they are still young.
- At the same time, I saw the Redis community fragmenting, something that was a bit concerning to me, even as an outsider.
- So I started to think that maybe, after all, I could have a role back in the Redis ecosystem.
- When I started to develop Redis in the context of a company where I was one of the two founders and where we kept the software code closed (Redis was opened as it was considered not part of the core product).
