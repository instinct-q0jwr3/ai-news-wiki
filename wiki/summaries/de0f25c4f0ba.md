# Anthropic, OpenAI et al. face antitrust suit for agreeing to slow AI development

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-21 · updated: 2026-09-25 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `openai` `anthropic` `ai-policy-regulation`

## Summary

Four subscribers to ChatGPT, Claude, Grok or Gemini filed a proposed class-action lawsuit alleging that the leading AI labs violated antitrust law by agreeing to collectively slow AI development. The suit, reported by the Associated Press, argues the coordination began in July 2026 after the labs signed a statement acknowledging 'intense competitive pressure not to unilaterally slow' development, and that the pact reduces the value consumers get from paid AI subscriptions.

The plaintiffs say they accept slower development for safety's sake, but frame Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's cooperation proposal as a 'shortcut' that 'substitutes collective restraint for individual accountability.' Amodei's own essay acknowledged the antitrust risk and floated a government exemption; OpenAI's Sam Altman answered on X that OpenAI welcomes a federal safety framework but does not believe the work must wait for an antitrust exemption or legislation.

## Highlights

- Proposed class action by four subscribers of ChatGPT, Claude, Grok or Gemini.
- Alleges the labs' agreement to slow AI development violates antitrust law and devalues paid subscriptions.
- Coordination allegedly started July 2026 after labs signed a statement citing 'intense competitive pressure not to unilaterally slow' development.
- Lead counsel Nick Rowley: AI safety cannot be 'controlled by private self-serving agreements' between for-profit tech companies.
- Plaintiffs call Amodei's cooperation proposal a 'shortcut' that 'substitutes collective restraint for individual accountability.'
- Sam Altman on X: OpenAI welcomes 'a federal framework that sets consistent safety requirements for frontier AI' but sees no need to wait for legislation.
