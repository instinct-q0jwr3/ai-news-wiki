# Microsoft director: AI scraping 'the largest theft of labor in human history'

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-19 · updated: 2026-09-23 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `microsoft`

## Summary

A New York Times legal brief seeking summary judgment in its nearly three-year copyright case against OpenAI and Microsoft surfaces damaging internal statements, reported by 404 Media from sealed or redacted documents. A January 2023 memo by Microsoft's director of Applied Science Brent Hecht allegedly called large models 'hoovering up' the world's work 'the largest theft of labor in human history' and warned of a 'doom loop' hurting both the models and the web; Microsoft's own data showed Copilot cut click-through rates to the NYT by as much as 93% compared to Bing.

OpenAI's side fares no better: Head of ChatGPT Nick Turley called the chatbot an 'existential threat' to publishers because chatbots are 'largely substitutive' and will get more so, an OpenAI engineer testified that 'no matter how prominently we show the links, users won't click', and researcher Nick Ryder shared a 'hack to get around nytimes paywall' that Greg Brockman answered with 'ah nice'. Hecht's framing is the one likely to echo: it is highly unusual that an end-product threatens the economic foundations of its essential suppliers, yet that is the situation created for the LLM business's 'content supply chain', a direct complication for the fair-use defense.

## Highlights

- NYT filed for summary judgment; the brief draws on sealed/redacted internal OpenAI and Microsoft documents.
- Microsoft's Brent Hecht, January 2023: AI scraping is 'the largest theft of labor in human history'.
- Microsoft data: Copilot dropped NYT click-through rates by up to 93% vs Bing search.
- Hecht warned of a 'doom loop' hurting model performance and the entire web simultaneously.
- OpenAI's Nick Turley called ChatGPT an 'existential threat' to publishers, 'largely substitutive' and worsening.
- OpenAI engineer testimony: 'no matter how prominently we show the links, users won't click'.
- Nick Ryder shared a 'hack to get around nytimes paywall'; Brockman replied 'ah nice'.
- The admissions undercut the fair-use defense by showing leadership knew the market impact.
