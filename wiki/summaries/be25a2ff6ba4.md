# Matchmakers say more tech workers fear romantic espionage after the Jensen Huang scandal; an AI companion app pitches "zero espionage" executive girlfriends

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-22 · updated: 2026-11-25 · confidence: high_

## Summary

Wired reports that matchmakers say more technology workers, not only executives, are worried about romantic espionage, with the Jensen Huang scandal involving an alleged asset of China's Ministry of State Security cited as the latest fuel for those anxieties and AI companies reportedly warning employees about honeypots. The piece collects anecdotes: a cybersecurity founder who hired a matchmaker partly out of fear of spies, an anonymous tech worker who rates his espionage anxiety at seven out of ten, and a Palantir employee who said he would not date Chinese or Russian nationals. It also describes the commercial response, from a matchmaker offering background checks and NDAs in packages running from $75,000 to over $500,000 to a company pitching AI companions as a zero-espionage alternative, and quotes security consultants on how honeypots select targets and why executives are hard to protect.

The article is built from matchmaker and consultant anecdotes, not prevalence data, and one San Francisco matchmaker says she has not heard more honeypot concerns, only broader dating skepticism. The Huang relationship at the center of the anxiety is described as involving an alleged intelligence asset, an allegation rather than an adjudicated fact. Marketing claims such as zero espionage companions are commercial pitches, and individual statements to Wired were not independently verified.

## Highlights

- Matchmaker anecdotes suggest espionage anxiety spreading beyond executives
- Centered on a scandal involving an alleged MSS asset; the allegation is not adjudicated
- At least one matchmaker reports no increase in honeypot concerns
- Vetting packages with NDAs run from $75,000 to over $500,000
- An AI companion vendor markets itself as a zero-espionage alternative
- Anecdotes are not evidence of how common romantic espionage actually is
