# You can just ask AI to do things

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-29 · updated: 2026-10-29 · confidence: high_

`seangoedecke`

## Summary

Sean Goedecke argues that getting good at AI is a real skill, and the core of it is agency: noticing that agents can automate parts of your work and actually asking them to. His daily examples are concrete. He no longer starts his dev server or hand-crafts curl requests against it; an agent does both. When he deploys or flips a feature flag, an agent polls metrics and logs before and after - including hourly polls for the rest of the day, a patience he would never spend himself. After writing a code change he asks Claude Opus 5.5 to open subagents so GPT-6-Astra and Gemini Argon can give second opinions.

The more interesting cases are agents managing themselves: a scheduled 6am job combs his unread messages and logs while his American colleagues work hours ahead of his Australian morning and drafts a triage report; the agent sets up its own MCP servers, skills and log access when he pastes in API keys; and it catalogs and clears the spiderweb of stale sessions a desktop agent tool accumulates. He keeps the judgment calls - his own messages, design decisions and code review - partly because he is better at them, partly because he is accountable for them.
