# AI Benchmarks: Climbing the Wrong Hills

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-20 · updated: 2026-10-26 · confidence: high_

`tldr-ai` `external-evaluation`

## Summary

Contrary Research argues that AI benchmarks have fallen to Goodhart's Law: now that scores drive rankings, sales, and even bets, frontier labs explicitly hill-climb them through reinforcement learning, so recent gains reflect targeting rather than broad capability. The report's clearest case is Arena, the former Chatbot Arena, which drew over a million monthly visitors by April 2025 and became a fixture in model launches: Meta's Llama 4 Maverick posted an experimental-chat Elo of 1417, second on the leaderboard ahead of GPT-4.5, Grok 3, and DeepSeek R1, only for users to find the ranking did not survive contact with reality. With Big Tech planning more than $700 billion of 2026 capex on models sold by these numbers, the report examines why flawed benchmarks actively steer training in wrong directions, why good evaluation is expensive to build, and what a new generation of benchmarks could look like.

## Highlights

- Applies Goodhart's Law to AI benchmarks: targets stop being measures
- Modern RL training hill-climbs whatever benchmark is scored
- Llama 4 Maverick's Arena Elo of 1417 placed second, then failed real-world use
- Arena (launched May 2023 as Chatbot Arena) passed 1M monthly visitors by April 2025
- Flawed benchmarks steer models wrongly, not just mismeasure them
- $700B+ of 2026 Big Tech capex rides on benchmark-ranked models
- Report covers why good benchmarks are costly and what better ones require
