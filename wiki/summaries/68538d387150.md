# OpenAI and Microsoft knew they were starting a 'doom loop' for the web

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-20 · updated: 2026-09-24 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `openai` `microsoft`

## Summary

Recently unsealed court documents in the New York Times' case against OpenAI and Microsoft contain internal warnings that the companies' AI products would damage the web they were trained on. An internal Microsoft document says its AI content strategy started a 'doom loop' hurting both model performance and the web, calling it 'highly unusual that an end-product threatens the economic foundations of its essential suppliers'.

The 92-page filing quotes Microsoft applied-science director Brent Hecht calling the data harvesting 'the largest theft of labor in human history' and a 'complete mockery' of fair use; Microsoft distances itself, saying Hecht speaks only for himself. Other passages: an OpenAI representative unaware of any effort to remove paywalled content from training data, employees admitting GPT-4 'memorized a ton of data' and was 'insanely good at regurgitation', Nick Turley saying there is 'no good reason to click' through to sources, and OpenAI's own experts attributing referral-traffic drops of as much as 60 percent to AI summaries.

## Highlights

- Internal Microsoft document: 'Our AI content strategy has started a doom loop that will hurt the performance of our models and the entire web.'
- Microsoft's Brent Hecht called the scraping 'the largest theft of labor in human history' and its fair-use defense 'a complete mockery'.
- Microsoft spokesperson: Hecht's comments are one employee's perspective, not a legal analysis or the company's views.
- OpenAI's Nick Turley: once the chatbot answers, there is 'no good reason to click' to the source; ChatGPT described internally as 'the modern newsstand'.
- An OpenAI representative was 'unaware' of any effort to detect or remove paywalled content from training data.
- Employees admitted GPT-4 'memorized a ton of data' and would be 'insanely good at regurgitation'; filing cites verbatim outputs from Times, Mercury News and others.
- OpenAI's own experts attributed publisher referral-traffic drops, speculated to be as much as 60 percent, to AI summaries like AI Overviews.
- Microsoft admitted 'almost no one intended' their content to be used this way and called LLMs 'a product that destroys its own supply chain'.
