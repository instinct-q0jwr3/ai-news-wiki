# AI is giving everyone the same advice

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-20 · updated: 2026-11-23 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

An a90 experiment repeatedly asks many models for advice, names and random-looking choices and reports strong convergence on common answers. It compares new chats with follow-up requests, tests richer personal context and examines a small open model across training stages.

The prompts, sampling counts, grouping rules and selected questions shape the results; some life-advice plots explicitly show only questions exceeding a concentration threshold. This does not establish that every personalized assistant gives everyone identical advice or that a model fingerprint proves undocumented ancestry. Repeated recommendations can sometimes be appropriate, and generated numbers should not be confused with reliable randomness.

## Highlights

- Repeated sampling reveals concentrated defaults
- Fresh chats differ from requests for another answer
- Some plots select high-concentration questions
- Short labels personalize less than detailed context in this experiment
- Training-stage investigation covers particular open models
- Family similarity is not proof of hidden lineage
- Variety and recommendation quality are different metrics
