# AI text watermarking and quality loss

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-01 · updated: 2026-11-07 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

With the EU AI Act's transparency guidelines in force and most major labs saying they will watermark text output, Keyvan Kambakhsh's essay asks whether watermarking makes AI writing worse - and lands on a careful yes-ish. The debate started with John Gruber's blast at Anthropic's watermarking as a perversion of writing: a tool must not sacrifice an iota of clarity or meaning to embed provenance clues. Daniel Jalkut countered that watermarking is just a different way of using randomness - like reversing the digits of a random number, the output distribution is unchanged. The author's answer: possible in theory, highly unlikely in practice.

The essay's concrete point is about diversity rather than single outputs. Even if a watermarking scheme guarantees each individual response is undistorted, sampling several responses to the same prompt shows watermarked answers clustering more tightly than unwatermarked ones - which matters for best-of-n workflows and for the overall quality of systems that generate many candidates and pick one. Underneath runs a second theme: as the labs' focus shifted to code, the written output of the models has already gotten worse, and watermarking sits on top of that trend rather than causing it. Gero's line about corporations not wanting a weirdo chatbot does the explaining.

## Highlights

- EU AI Act transparency rules have major labs committing to text watermarking; the quality debate is live
- Gruber: sacrificing any clarity to embed provenance is a perversion of writing
- Jalkut's defense: watermarking just uses randomness differently - distribution unchanged in theory
- Author: single-response quality may survive, but cross-response diversity shrinks
- Watermarked samples to the same prompt cluster more tightly - bad for best-of-n workflows
- Second theme: prose quality already declined as labs optimized for code; watermarking is not the main suspect
- Gero: the chatbot that makes money is not the weirdo
