# First Token Cutoff LLM sampling

## Summary

antirez's 2024 proposal for LLM sampling: First Token Cutoff (FTC), an alternative to top-p nucleus sampling. The flaw in top-p: accumulating tokens up to probability mass p can admit tokens vastly weaker than the top choice (a p of 0.4 can let a 0.01 token ride along at ~3%), derailing the generation - while the avalanche effect means variety comes free anyway from context perturbation.

FTC instead bounds the worst selectable token relative to the top token T0: compute softmax, sort, and keep only tokens with r = 1 - T[i]/T0 below a cutoff 'co' (e.g. 0.5). When the model is confident, FTC picks the top token; when several candidates are genuinely close, it samples among them - a hard, understandable bound instead of TFS-style cluster heuristics. He published it as work in progress, hoping to stimulate hacking.

## Highlights

- FTC sampling: bound the worst token relative to the top token's probability
- Top-p's flaw: mass accumulation can admit tokens 25x weaker than the leader
- Rule: keep tokens with 1 - T[i]/T0 <= co; confident states go greedy, close states diversify
- The avalanche effect supplies variety without picking weak tokens
- Published as a work in progress to invite discussion
