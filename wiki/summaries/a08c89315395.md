## Summary

Cory Doctorow opens with a concession: statistical extrapolation is a fine method, and LLMs are astonishingly good at it. Calling an LLM 'a word-guessing program' is not reductionism, he argues - the remarkable thing is how plausible the output of pure word-guessing turns out to be. The deeper surprise is about us, not the machines: routine human sentences, like much of the natural world, have far more statistical regularity than our intuition admits. Every wave of 'AI' has refined our understanding of which things truly require consciousness, and this one is no different.

But the method has hard limits, and the limits live exactly where the value is: surprises. Doctorow's example is a podcast host who claimed his phone understood his wife because it could predict her typing - a theory-free prediction that collapses the day she says 'I want a divorce.' Coping with surprise requires a theory of the other, not a lookup table. The statistical picture always sands off the final decimal places, and those smoothings are where the gold seam is - Dylan going electric, Miles Davis not playing a note. Because AI bosses won't acknowledge the limits, he argues, we're spending trillions and emitting gigatons for diminishing returns while replacing workers with defective chatbots.
