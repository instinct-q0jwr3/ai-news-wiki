## Summary

A Latent Space interview with Eric Nguyen (CEO, Radical Numerics) frames bio-security as an AI arms race, opening from the OpenAI-to-Hugging-Face attack and Anthropic's filters flagging cyber-security and biology as the sensitive domains. Clem Delangue argues cyber defensive capabilities need to be open so they keep pace with frontier models' attack capabilities.

Nguyen describes a self-optimization experiment with RNA aptamers: the model is shown progressively better-scoring RNAs in sequence and asked to continue the trajectory, and it recapitulates some of the best held-out scores it was never shown - evidence that models can push along an optimization gradient toward designs beyond their training examples, with obvious dual-use implications for biology.

## Highlights

- Casts bio-security as an AI arms race in the wake of the OpenAI-Hugging Face incident.
- Anthropic's filters flag cyber-security and biology as the two sensitive areas.
- Clem Delangue: defensive cyber capabilities should be open to keep pace with frontier attack capabilities.
- Radical Numerics showed a model progressively better RNA aptamers and it continued the optimization trajectory on its own.
- The model recapitulated top held-out aptamer scores it had never been shown.
