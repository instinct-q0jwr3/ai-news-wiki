# Anthropic urges Australia to consider "conditional approval" for Big Tech to train its models on copyrighted works, giving copyright holders a choice to opt out

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-02 · updated: 2026-10-08 · confidence: high_

`techmeme` `anthropic` `ai-policy-regulation`

## Summary

Anthropic has told an Australian parliamentary inquiry that it no longer expects a blanket copyright exemption for AI training. It proposes instead a 'conditional approval' scheme: big tech could train on Australian copyrighted works under an opt-out model, so rights holders keep a choice.

The public broadcasters push the other way. The ABC and SBS argue in their submissions that AI services act as publishers while escaping the copyright, defamation and privacy rules media outlets face, which the ABC says will 'cannibalise' Australian news. They want AI firms pulled into the news bargaining incentive so they must strike paid deals with large outlets.

## Highlights

- Anthropic concedes it won't get a blanket copyright exemption and asks for conditional approval with an opt-out.
- The ABC wants AI firms bound by the same copyright, defamation and privacy rules as media outlets.
- ABC and SBS warn of 'cannibalisation' of the news industry by AI services.
- Both broadcasters suggest including AI companies in the news bargaining incentive.
- Submissions went to a federal parliamentary inquiry.
