# Rails and AI

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-09-25 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

A Rails essay argues that convention over configuration set the path for 20+ years of great training data, and that this is why coding agents do so well with Rails today: most Rails codebases look the same and are, on average, high quality, so an AI already 'knows' a project that stays close to the defaults.

The author credits this for getting very good LLM output quality in the early days of AI-assisted coding, when many developers still found it unusable, and claims keeping close to Rails conventions makes things '100x faster'. Humans benefit too: they can review agent output quickly 'without a jungle of distracting boilerplate'.

## Highlights

- Thesis: convention over configuration made Rails ideal training data.
- Agents do well because Rails codebases look alike and average high quality.
- Staying close to Rails defaults makes AI assistance '100x faster'.
- Conventions also let humans review agent output quickly.
- The author saw strong LLM output quality from the early days.
