# Big AI to humanity: drop dead

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-21 · updated: 2026-09-22 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

Matthew Butterick - author, designer, programmer and lawyer - argues that the public debate about AI catastrophe is stuck in what he calls the 'Skynet fallacy': sci-fi scenarios of rogue AI that generate fear but no realistic policy paths. His own 2024 prediction was that a catastrophe from alignment failure is far more likely than one from malignant machine agency, and he sees that prediction 'ripening.'

Butterick has standing beyond punditry: after learning in 2022 that his works were in generative-AI training datasets, he helped invent the first lawsuits challenging the practice and is co-counsel for plaintiffs in eight of the 142 such US cases. The essay is prompted by a run of unusual events - a US government 'statement of interest' supporting AI training as fair use in a copyright case, AI-company workers posting about human-extinction risk, and newspaper op-eds demanding that 'something' be done.

## Highlights

- Coins the 'Skynet fallacy': media catastrophe scenarios are 'primarily a vessel for fear' and don't illuminate realistic policy change.
- 142 US lawsuits now challenge the legality of training on copyrighted works; Butterick is co-counsel in eight.
- The US government filed a 'statement of interest' backing AI training as fair use in a copyright case.
- Butterick's 2024 view: alignment failure is a more likely catastrophe path than sci-fi malignant agency.
- He declines to say whether AI companies have committed 'crimes against humanity,' keeping the essay on legal and policy ground.
