# Survey: the chatbot gender adoption gap among US adults has closed since 2024, though men are more likely to use them daily; ChatGPT has identical use rates

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-19 · updated: 2026-10-29 · confidence: high_

`techmeme` `openai`

## Summary

Pew Research Center's February 2026 survey of 5,119 US adults finds the chatbot gender adoption gap has closed since 2024 - though usage patterns still differ. Men are more likely to use chatbots daily and more likely to use specific brands (Copilot 22% vs. 13% among women, with similar gaps for Gemini, Grok and Claude), while ChatGPT, the most-used chatbot, has identical usage rates for men and women. Both genders use chatbots mainly for search and work tasks; men edge ahead on information search, work, entertainment and news, while women are slightly more likely to use them for emotional support or advice, with no difference on companionship. On impact, 35% of men say chatbots help their productivity versus 25% of women. Attitudinally the gap persists: women remain more skeptical, about twice as likely to say AI will affect them negatively over the next 20 years (33% vs. 17% positive).
