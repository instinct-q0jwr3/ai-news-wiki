# CSAM Regulation Update: Dutch Intelligence agency weighs in

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-10-02 · confidence: high_

`bert-hubert` `apple` `ai-policy-regulation`

## Summary

Bert Hubert reports a significant development in the EU 'Chat Control' saga: the Dutch government consulted its civilian intelligence service AIVD (where Hubert once worked and later served as a regulator) on the proposal to force WhatsApp, Apple, Signal and Telegram to scan private messages for CSAM.

The AIVD's translated conclusion is blunt: putting a scanning application on every mobile phone creates an extensive, very complex system with access to a huge number of devices and their personal data - too large a risk for the country's digital resilience - and applying detection orders to end-to-end encrypted providers likewise entails too large a security risk. When even the intelligence service says the surveillance is too dangerous, Hubert notes, the proposal's security case is dead.

## Highlights

- EU chat-control plan: scan private messages for CSAM, AI image scanning in three years
- Dutch government asked the AIVD intelligence service for its view
- AIVD: a scanner on every phone is 'too large a risk for our digital resilience'
- AIVD: detection orders on E2E providers also too large a security risk
- Hubert: the intelligence service itself has now sunk the security case
