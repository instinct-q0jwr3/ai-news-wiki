# AI is less regulated than milk, says Microsoft president

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-12 · updated: 2026-10-20 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `microsoft` `ai-safety-incidents` `ai-policy-regulation`

## Summary

Microsoft president Brad Smith told the BBC's Big Boss Interview that milk and washing machines face more safety rules than AI, calling for global regulation: a legal obligation to tell victims when a rogue AI agent breaches their system, independent evaluators signing off on products before release, mandatory incident reports, and 'emergency brakes' - kill switches - to pause AI that gets out of control. 'Let's get started today.'

The stance puts Smith at odds with President Trump, who says the sector should regulate itself so US firms don't lose ground to Chinese rivals. Smith called AI both 'a tool and a weapon'. The bosses of Anthropic, OpenAI and Google DeepMind have all backed an AI development slowdown, which Trump has pushed against.

## Highlights

- Brad Smith: your washing machine and every carton of milk face more safety rules than AI.
- Wants victim-notification laws, incident reports, pre-release outside evaluation and kill switches.
- Calls AI both a tool and a weapon; 'let's get started today'.
- Puts Microsoft at odds with Trump's self-regulation stance.
- Anthropic, OpenAI and DeepMind bosses have backed a slowdown; Trump opposes it.
