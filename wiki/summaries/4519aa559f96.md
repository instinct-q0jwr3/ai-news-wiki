## Summary

LLMs-as-classifiers, prompts applied to a context and returning a label, suck to work with. This is especially painful because they often perform pretty decently.

But let’s consider some of the things we’d want in a classifier and see how an LLM-as-classifier stacks up: The LLM has some prior information baked in which might be a poor fit for our distribution.

## Highlights

- For instance the LLM won’t know whether we’re testing on a population where our positive class is rare or an enriched population where our positive class is relatively prevalent.
- And I guess you can give it that context but now you’ve got to modify that for each new population and also, as in our first point, it’s not clear that this will be appropriately incorporated into the LLM’s judgement.
- These failures are not the fault of the LLM: it’s not designed as a classifier and indeed has no mechanism for plausibly doing some of these things.
- But only because we’re thinking of things incorrectly… With the proper framework that harnesses the LLM’s power we can get the power of the LLM with the convenience of stock ML algorithms.
