# We fine-tuned a 235B model on 4 GPUs. Then we didn't need it

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-19 · updated: 2026-10-21 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `compute-buildout`

## Summary

A team fine-tuned a 235B-parameter open model on four H100s to classify entities in customer documents - and one epoch on about 3,600 examples beat their fine-tuned OpenAI and Gemini API models by 7.6 F1 points on average, at about a third of the per-token cost, in a 26-hour run. They never shipped it. The first half of the post is the engineering: five training setups, four of which failed before a single step, for a reason they say applies to anyone fine-tuning a model this size on one machine. The second half is the more interesting question - why a cheaper, more accurate self-hosted model still wasn't worth deploying, once the operational reality of serving and maintaining it was priced in.

## Highlights

- Fine-tuned a 235B model on 4 H100s; one epoch on ~3,600 examples
- Beat fine-tuned OpenAI/Gemini APIs by 7.6 F1 points on average
- About a third of the per-token API cost; 26-hour training run
- Four of five training setups failed before the first step
- They never shipped it - the post's second half explains why
- Lesson: beating the API on quality and cost doesn't settle the operating-cost question
