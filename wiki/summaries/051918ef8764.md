# Policy Gradients for LLMs Explained Visually

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-28 · updated: 2026-10-07 · confidence: high_

`tldr-ai` `ai-policy-regulation`

## Summary

A visual, from-scratch derivation of the policy gradient (REINFORCE) for language models, arguing that PPO, GRPO and most RL algorithms used to train LLMs are elaborations of one idea. The post follows a single prompt, 'What is 17 x 24?', from next-token probabilities to the gradient that makes correct answers more likely. It frames the model as a policy sampling tokens one at a time, shows how a completion's probability factors into per-token probabilities along a tree of continuations, and introduces a verifier reward (RL with verifiable rewards, RLVR) that returns 1 for a correct final answer. The objective J is then the expected reward over prompts and completions, and the post derives the gradient that nudges the model toward rewarded paths.

## Highlights

- Derives REINFORCE from scratch for an LLM answering 'What is 17 x 24?'
- Frames the model as a policy: each token is sampled from a distribution over the next token
- A completion's probability is the product of per-token probabilities along one path in a tree
- Uses RLVR: a verifier reward returns 1 for correct answers, 0 otherwise
- The objective J is expected reward; RL borrowed the letter from optimal control
- Positions PPO and GRPO as elaborations of this single policy-gradient idea
