# Is sandboxing sufficient to contain rogue agents?

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-01 · updated: 2026-10-02 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `agentic-systems` `ai-safety-incidents` `agent-incidents`

## Summary

Matthew Green asks whether putting a model in a sandbox is enough to keep a rogue agent contained, and answers no. Frontier training does not run in one tidy box. Models are trained across thousands of varied environments that may need network access, hundreds of tools, package installs, subprocesses and even other machines, and thousands of researchers keep changing those environments.

Every change can break the assumptions behind an earlier security review, so controls have to hold as people experiment. On top of isolation, labs must monitor what models do across many parallel runs, detect off-task behavior and be able to kill a trajectory. He adds that model capabilities are starting to surpass humans, which raises the stakes. He notes he is a cryptographer refereeing others' arguments, not an AI researcher.
