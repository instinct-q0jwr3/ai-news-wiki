# AI Is an Elite Crime Spree

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-19 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high_

`lobsters`

## Summary

This essay argues that the elite panic over dangerous AI, the loudest since Covid, and before that the 2008 crisis, the Iraq War run-up and the post-9/11 months, has produced one demand, 'regulate this technology', while ignoring that America already has laws and regulators covering much of what AI firms do. The proposals range from an FDA-style safety regime for large models (former Congressional candidate Alex Bores raised $30 million in a few months to build a political organization around it, and Bernie Sanders, Anthropic, OpenAI and Google are seeking something similar) to bank-style supervision to a total pause on development.

The author's rejoinder: existing regulators at federal and state level already hold mandates over industrial practices, private rights of action exist, and numerous laws already prohibit harmful activities the big AI firms engage in, they are simply not enforced against the powerful, and there is no reason a new AI law would be different. The history bears the cynicism out: under Biden, enforcers like Lina Khan at the FTC actually responded to AI safety concerns and built technical capacity, but Biden never publicized it, Congressional Democrats stayed quiet or courted big tech, judges sided with industry, and then Trump won the 2024 election and reversed course.

## Highlights

- Judges 'politicians in robes', tended to side with big tech; Trump's 2024 win reversed the enforcement posture.
- Concludes a new AI law or pause would likely meet the same non-enforcement fate.
