# The end of Microsoft Research's AI Frontiers group

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-07 · updated: 2026-11-09 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `microsoft`

## Summary

Microsoft Research's AI Frontiers group has been wound down, according to its founding lead on hunch.net. He says the group (about 50 people, formed after ChatGPT to organize research more coherently than one-person projects allow) worked on making agents work, including the Autogen framework and the Fara computer-use agent, and on fundamentals such as the Next Latent objective, Dion3 (an improved Muon optimizer) and the Full Bandwidth Transformer.

Then the group was reorganized into Microsoft AI. The author argues the fit was poor: the ex-Inflection leadership there centers on scaling and, in his words, aggressively not research. He defends research returns, pointing to ByteDance experiments suggesting a Full Bandwidth Transformer variant could match a model twice the size. He and the other founding leaders have left, and he says he could not return to MSR because research there lacks a path to real use and the 'do good work and good things happen' deal has broken down. No internships from the group are planned.

## Highlights

- AI Frontiers was a roughly 50-person group inside Microsoft Research, founded after ChatGPT.
- Work covered Autogen, the Fara computer-use agent, Next Latent, Dion3 and the Full Bandwidth Transformer.
- It was folded into Microsoft AI, whose leadership favors scaling over research.
- The author cites ByteDance results hinting a model could match one twice its size.
- He says the founding leadership has left and MSR return was not an option.
