# Current Efforts Aren't Enough to Slow the AI Cyber Virus

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-22 · updated: 2026-11-27 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

Bridgewater argues that current efforts are not enough to slow what it calls an AI cyber virus: AI is helping attackers exploit vulnerabilities faster than defenders can patch them, open-source models have reached dangerous cyber capability, and the firm says it manages money based on a one-in-three chance of an economically destabilizing attack within the next year. The piece describes a time series of cyber incidents assembled from scraped public disclosures, media coverage and corporate filings, a taxonomy linking incidents to root causes, and a real-time exploit-tracking pipeline. It frames the head start defenders gained before cyber-capable open models appeared as a one-time gift whose benefit will wane, and notes threat actors are not yet running fully autonomous attack workflows.

This is an investor's risk thesis, not a neutral measurement: Bridgewater discloses it may hold significant financial interests in positions discussed, and its one-in-three probability is a judgment used for portfolio decisions rather than a consensus estimate. The incident time series is self-assembled and adjusted for reporting lags by the authors' own rules, and the exponential framing is an extrapolation. Recent outages it cites are acknowledged as not clearly malicious.

## Highlights

- One-in-three chance of a destabilizing attack within a year is the firm's own judgment
- Incident history scraped from disclosures, media and filings with self-set adjustments
- Open-source model cyber capability described as near autonomous-workflow levels
- Fully autonomous attacks not yet broadly deployed; human still in the loop
- Investor thesis with disclosed financial interests, not an independent audit
