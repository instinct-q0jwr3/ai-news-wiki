# The Mythical Man-Month in the Age of AI

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-27 · updated: 2026-10-28 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

Fifty years after The Mythical Man-Month, this essay asks whether Brooks's Law survives the age of AI agents. The author's update, coined after watching four people fail to connect a laptop to a screen: adding agents to a project with unclear requirements gets you the wrong answer faster.

At first glance the law should not hold - the classic bottlenecks Brooks described, onboarding time, coordination overhead and office politics, vanish when a new team member is up to speed in milliseconds and takes no coffee breaks. But looking closer, the essay argues, the law was never really about onboarding speed; it was about communication costs and the clarity of the work itself. Agents scale the doing, not the knowing what to do, so unclear projects still fail - just at machine speed.

## Highlights

- A 2026 update to Brooks's Law: adding agents to an unclear project gets the wrong answer faster
- Brooks's 1975 original: adding manpower to a late software project makes it later
- Agents eliminate onboarding and coordination overhead, which suggests the law should fail
- The essay argues the law was about communication costs and requirement clarity, not ramp-up time
- Agents scale execution, not the knowing-what-to-build part
