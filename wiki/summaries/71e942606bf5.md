# Can Skills Learned in Games Transfer to Real-World Work?

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-19 · updated: 2026-09-22 · confidence: high_

`latent.space`

## Summary

Good Start Labs, spun out of Every last October with $3.6M from General Catalyst, Inovia and angels, is betting that games are the best verifiable training environments for AI models. Co-founder Alex Duffy traces the idea to a 2025 Twitch stream of frontier models playing Diplomacy, where OpenAI's o3 won by planning a future betrayal while Claude Opus 4 refused to lie and 'got destroyed'; fine-tuning on Diplomacy later improved performance on customer support and industrial operations benchmarks.

The company's key result concerns transfer: training a 30B model inside the railroad game 1830, they compared single-turn next-move QA against a multi-turn terminal agent that explores, plans and uses tools. Both designs improved in-game objectives, but only the terminal-agent design improved performance on the Finance-Agent benchmark, whose tasks (query a database, build an Excel model, reason over it) mirror real finance workflows. The lesson: how a game is presented to the model determines which skills it learns, and whether they carry into work outside the game.

## Highlights

- Good Start Labs trains AI models on games with verifiable outcomes; spun out of Every with $3.6M in funding.
- Origin: a 2025 Diplomacy stream where o3 won via planned betrayal and Claude Opus 4 refused to lie.
- Diplomacy fine-tuning improved customer support and industrial operations benchmarks, per Duffy's Every article.
- Key experiment: a 30B model trained inside 1830, a railroad strategy game with a stock-market mechanic.
- Only the multi-turn terminal-agent design transferred to the Finance-Agent benchmark; single-turn QA did not.
- Finance-Agent tasks mirror real workflows: database lookups, Excel modeling, reasoning over results.
- September 2026 rankings: Grok 4 Fast least likely to betray in Diplomacy; Gemini 2.5 Pro the most.
- Their harness can add an expert model for denser stepwise rewards and vary how the game is presented.
