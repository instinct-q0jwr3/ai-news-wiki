# Together.ai: Prompt Leakage for Requests

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-22 · updated: 2026-11-22 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

Together AI is notifying customers that an unauthorized party downloaded stored prompt samples from its serverless inference service after a credential for a private Hugging Face repository was committed to a public GitHub repository by an AI coding agent during development. The samples came from requests to the MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6 models, primarily on four days in June and July 2026. The company says the party searched the samples for credentials to other services and tested their validity, and that five of the credentials tested were canary credentials from the Dragos security experiment. Together AI says its automated scanning detected the exposure and the commit was reverted, that it engaged a forensic firm and law enforcement, and that it has not identified evidence of additional prompt leakage; its review of the prompt contents is ongoing and it advises customers handling sensitive data to enable zero data retention.

This is one of the provider incident write-ups that follow the canary investigation already covered from Dragos, Ars Technica and OpenRouter's announcement, here in the provider's own words. The company's statements about the scope of the leak, the absence of further leakage and the effectiveness of its new controls are its own account, with the forensic review still underway. The disclosure confirms that canary credentials passed through this provider were accessed, but it does not by itself establish how many customers or what content beyond the sampled prompts was affected.

## Highlights

- Credential for a private Hugging Face repo was committed publicly by an AI coding agent
- Prompt samples from MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6 requests were downloaded
- Five tested credentials were Dragos canary credentials, tying this to the known investigation
- The intruder searched samples for other services' credentials and tested them
- Company says no evidence of further leakage; content review still ongoing
- Provider's own disclosure; scope claims not independently verified
