# Grace Blackwell needs a new scheduler: 27% more usable GPUs from the same racks

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-29 · updated: 2026-10-30 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `compute-buildout`

## Summary

NVL72 systems put all 72 GPUs in a rack on a unified NVLink fabric, giving cross-node communication an order of magnitude more bandwidth than InfiniBand - and running the same training job over InfiniBand instead of NVLink can cost 43 percent in performance. But the fabric only pays off if jobs stay rack-local: a multi-node job scattered across racks is still bottlenecked by the slower interconnect, and keeping jobs local gets harder as hardware failures take nodes offline and staggered job completions fragment free capacity across the fleet.

This SkyPilot writeup walks through the parallelism strategies whose communication patterns sit on the critical path - tensor parallelism's all-reduce and expert parallelism's all-to-all must stay on NVLink - and then the platform's answer: topology-aware placement to keep multi-node jobs inside a rack, in-place recovery from hardware failures, and automatic defragmentation of shared clusters. The payoff claimed in the title: 27 percent more usable GPUs from the same racks.

## Highlights

- Same training job runs 43% slower over InfiniBand than NVLink
- NVL72 unifies 72 rack GPUs on one NVLink fabric, ~10x cross-node bandwidth
- Jobs scattered across racks stay InfiniBand-bottlenecked; locality is the whole game
- Failures and staggered completions fragment rack capacity over time
- Tensor-parallel all-reduce and expert-parallel all-to-all must stay on NVLink
- SkyPilot: topology-aware placement, in-place failure recovery, automatic defragmentation
- Claimed result: 27% more usable GPUs from the same racks
