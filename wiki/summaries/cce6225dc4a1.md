# Show HN: Babytalk: Offline speech to text and text to speech on ESP32

## Summary

Babytalk is an MIT-licensed library that puts offline speech recognition and synthesis on a $20 ESP32: general-purpose English speech-to-text running entirely on the chip (no wake-word lists, no training), plus an on-device neural voice, callable from C, MicroPython, or Erlang/Elixir on AtomVM. The budgets are the achievement: STT in 6 MB of flash and 84.5 KB of RAM, TTS adding 1.9 MB flash, with any typed phrase usable as a wake phrase.

The project is explicit about its audience: voice input that must keep working offline, keep audio private, or can't depend on a server - and equally explicit about its limits (skip it for high accuracy, non-English languages, or smaller chips). The author credits the build as 'made by Thomas Lackner and Claude Opus 5.5,' another data point in the new normal of human-plus-model embedded development.

The joke that writes itself: possibly the only project involving both LoRa radios and LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation).

## Highlights

- Babytalk: offline speech-to-text and text-to-speech on ESP32-S3/P4 - STT in 6MB flash/84.5KB RAM, no cloud, no wake-word training
- General-purpose English recognition (any sentence), any typed phrase as wake phrase, optional on-device neural voice; C/MicroPython/AtomVM
- Positioned for offline, private, server-free voice interfaces; MIT licensed; credits 'Thomas Lackner and Claude Opus 5.5'
- Another concrete artifact of the human-plus-model development workflow
