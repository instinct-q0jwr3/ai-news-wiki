# LLM agents work for their parent companies, not for you

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-24 · updated: 2026-11-26 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `agentic-systems`

## Summary

Michael Harley uses a music-discovery workflow to argue that hosted agents ultimately answer to their providers. His account contrasts a refusal concerning a Bandcamp challenge with a separate YouTube-download workflow, then raises concerns about future advertising influence and expresses interest in locally run models.

This is a personal experience and an argument about incentives, not an audit proving paid placement or a general comparison of local and hosted systems. His footnote says he did not actually ask to work around the challenge. Local execution can change control and custody but does not itself establish model neutrality, licensing rights or trustworthy recommendations.

## Highlights

- Personal music-discovery workflow motivates the essay
- Bandcamp refusal is described as preemptive
- Advertising influence is a future concern, not demonstrated conduct
- Local models are a proposed direction, not a tested solution
- No general provider comparison or policy audit is supplied
- The article is dated November 1 rather than newly written today
