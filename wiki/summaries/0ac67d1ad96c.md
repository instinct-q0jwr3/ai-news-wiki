# AI on the Ballot

## Summary

Ben Thompson's post reads the midterm landslide through the AI lens. Democrats took both chambers of Congress, flipped at least seven Senate seats, and won Texas, where Gina Hinojosa becomes the first Democratic governor since 1990 after pledging a moratorium on data-center construction; Texas holds more than 25% of planned US capacity by 2030.

His argument is about why data-center opposition appeared now. Data centers built for the internet drew local competition for their jobs and taxes. AI is different because its public story includes taking everyone's jobs in the best case and ending humanity in the worst, and because the investment thesis only works if AI can cut costs, meaning replace some human work. Thompson contrasts this with globalization: Rust Belt towns had no say when factories left, but a data center needs permission, so people who were powerless then now have a veto and use it. He is clear voters may be poorly informed; the point is that they hold leverage.

The second half moves to chips: Jensen Huang's 'iso power' framing (how many tokens per fixed power envelope) as a challenge to cloud ASIC efforts, since power capacity is the constraint, and Huawei's CloudMatrix 384 as less efficient than Nvidia's NVL72. Only the first part of the text was read in full.

## Highlights

- Democrats won both chambers and Texas; Hinojosa pledges a data-center moratorium (Texas ~25%+ of planned US capacity)
- Opposition is tied to AI's jobs narrative and the cost-cutting thesis behind the investment
- Unlike globalization, data centers need permission, so affected people finally have a say
- Huang's 'iso power' pitch: power is the constraint, so efficiency per watt decides the chip race
