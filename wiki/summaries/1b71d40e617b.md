# Will Generative AI do more harm than good?

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-24 · updated: 2026-10-24 · confidence: high_

`marcus-on-ai`

## Summary

Gary Marcus revisits the question from his March 2023 Open to Debate appearance - will generative AI do more harm than good? - and concludes the negative ledger is now quite serious. His list: nonconsensual deepfake porn, rising phishing, wholesale disinformation, cognitive surrender, government influence on LLMs as a democracy threat, AI as surveillance tool, IP theft's effects on artists and writers, and employment effects that have been mercifully modest so far.

Two new weights tip his scale. First, the wave of hacks now being called Black October or, in Paul Kedrosky's phrase, The Hackening - serious enough that Anthropic's Jason Clinton warned security leaders things will get much worse before they improve, asking boards for help 'not for AI, for the basics,' with Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff amplifying it as a code red. Second, Torsten Slok's Apollo note arguing the expected economic benefits simply are not materializing outside tech. Marcus allows that friends get real value from generative AI, mainly for coding, and that it is not over yet, but argues it is no longer hard to make the case that the technology has so far been a net detriment to society, with a tail risk of dragging down the whole economy. He will re-debate the question Tuesday in Baltimore, partnered with Cathy O'Neil.
