# Broken promises, confiscated land: the hyperscale AI datacentre being built

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-10-03 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

The Guardian reports from Tarluvada, an Andhra Pradesh village where Google is building a $15bn hyperscale AI datacentre - set to be its largest outside the US - with the Adani Group as partner. Officials arrived promising jobs, soaring real estate and 'world-famous tech hub' status; a year later, locals say smallholdings have been taken back by the government, protests are gathering, and the village head admits he is torn between prosperity and ruin: 'America does not want the problems of datacentres and so now Google is bringing them here.'

The scale is contested in a telling way. Publicly announced as a 1GW project, state environmental clearances actually permit 2.51GW - the output of two large nuclear reactors, over 30% of the annual electricity consumption of a state of 55 million people, on a grid that is 75% coal. The clearances were issued in nine days with no public consultation, for a complex of three datacentres, one sitting metres from reserve forest, a drinking-water reservoir and a wildlife sanctuary with endangered leopards. Three petitions before India's National Green Tribunal call the risk assessments 'grossly inadequate, scientifically unrealistic, and misleading,' and the Modi-allied state government stands accused of obfuscating the project's true size to dodge stricter scrutiny.
