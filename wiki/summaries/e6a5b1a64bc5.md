# The Rise and Fall of Jev

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-23 · updated: 2026-10-24 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `typesafe`

## Summary

TypeSafe AI's launch post for Jev, its first System One model, by the former OpenAI researcher who helped build the instruction-following methods behind ChatGPT. He recounts concluding that chat models, despite the hype, were missing something big, spending two years in stealth, and emerging with a model that 'can't hallucinate' because it doesn't generate text at all: it scores a fixed set of options in one forward pass. Jev is named for William Stanley Jevons - the efficiency paradox - with the company expecting machine intelligence to follow coal's path, where efficiency gains increased total demand.

The post frames Jev as the opening of a new category: decision models that classify rather than converse. It is the origin story behind the System One API that has since spawned an entire benchmark ecosystem (the Jev Decision Index) and imitators, including OpenAI's own Decisions API, which went generally available the same week - making this a useful primary source on how the decision-model category began and how its creator thinks about hallucination as a generation problem, not a knowledge problem.
