## Summary

Jeremy Stern's profile of Mark Zuckerberg for Colossus opens not with Mark but with his parents, Ed and Karen Zuckerberg - black slacks, zany patterned shirts, unbroken eye contact, and unreconstructed New York accents. The piece mines their account of his childhood for the origin story: a four-year-old who got his nursery school to extend a space unit from a week to a month, and a six-year-old whose demand for logical justifications of every rule forced his parents to prepare what Ed calls a 'mental PowerPoint' before laying down the law. The parents resist the genius-child narrative ('He didn't stand out as the bright one'), crediting a supportive environment and passion instead. The profile frames The Social Network as fiction colliding with a hinge moment in American cultural history.

## Highlights

- Profile opens on Ed and Karen Zuckerberg, not Mark
- Parents reject the standout-genius story: 'He didn't stand out as the bright one in the family'
- At four, he persuaded his nursery teacher to extend a space unit from one week to a month
- At six, he demanded step-by-step logical justifications for rules - the parental 'mental PowerPoint'
- Karen: 'people who have a passion are the luckiest people in the world'
- Frames The Social Network as the theater kid/tech bro collision at a cultural hinge moment
