# It wasn't AI that flipped Proton's charge

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-01 · updated: 2026-11-12 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

A paying Proton customer and longtime recommender reads the 404 Media investigation into Proton's AI push and lands on an uncomfortable distinction: the scandal is not that Proton's engineers are using AI - that is the new industry norm, and fine when reviews, tests and accountability wrap around it. The scandal is a CEO telling an engineer, in front of colleagues on the security Slack channel, that asking for a few days to pentest new code was 'bullshit and loser thinking,' telling staff their AI usage could decide whether they kept their jobs, and then - when that plan leaked and was dropped - saying it had already served its purpose. Plenty of companies are shipping coding agents without humiliating anyone; if Proton cannot, the author argues, the problem is leadership, not AI.

The stakes are specific to what Proton sells. Trading caution for speed is a defensible call at most companies, but Proton is a privacy company: careful, slow shipping is part of the product, and a bug that exposes users' mail would be fatal in a way a B2B vendor's bad release is not. The employee defenses posted on Reddit - Yen was fine with pentesting as long as it did not slow the pipeline - read to the author like astroturfed crisis management, and no dissenting employee voice has appeared. The piece closes where it started: the author would like to keep recommending Proton, but that depends on the engineer who asked for a few more days of testing winning the argument inside the company.
