# Writing as an act of rebellion

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-04 · updated: 2026-11-05 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

A short newsletter essay argues that handwriting and human-made work gain value as AI spreads. The author takes the TV series Pluribus as his frame: an alien consciousness has absorbed all human knowledge, yet shows a desire to read something new when the main character, a writer, starts a book. From that he concludes that writing is becoming more valuable because AI cannot create what has not been written.

He points to software as evidence: Jepsen published a brutal report on ParticleDB, a vibe-coded database, which he calls the worst-implemented database he has seen, and contrasts it with careful handmade projects such as a Zig implementation of Postgres. He is 'bullish' on handmade articles, books and software standing out. The argument rests on one fiction reference and a couple of examples, and is better read as a manifesto than a measured case.

## Highlights

- Frame: Pluribus, where an all-knowing alien still wants to read something new
- Claim: human writing and handmade software become more valuable and distinctive
- Evidence cited: Jepsen's critical report on the vibe-coded ParticleDB database
- A manifesto-style post resting on thin examples
