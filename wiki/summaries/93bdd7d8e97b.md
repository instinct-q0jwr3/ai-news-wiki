# Scaling Hierarchical Networks

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-29 · updated: 2026-10-30 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

A research post argues for scaling hierarchical, byte-level networks as the next step beyond today's language models. Invoking Sutton's bitter lesson - general methods that leverage computation beat hand-engineered priors - the authors note that LLMs still depend on one conspicuous hand-crafted component: the tokenizer, which blocks true end-to-end learning from raw data.

Byte-level modeling is framed as a litmus test for general learning methods: if models can't learn language well from characters and bytes, claims about tackling data-scarce frontiers like science, robotics and biology ring hollow. The post builds on the authors' 2025 work in this direction.

## Highlights

- Argues tokenizers are the last hand-crafted crutch in LLMs
- Byte-level models as litmus test for general learning methods
- Framed via Sutton's bitter lesson
- Motivation: data-scarce domains need end-to-end learners
