# Warehouse robotics startup Ultra raised a $12M seed and a $50M Series A, and expands its deal with Physical Intelligence to supply AI software for its robots

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-27 · confidence: high_

`techmeme`

## Summary

Ultra, a Brooklyn startup leasing packing robots to warehouses under a monthly 'robots as a service' model, announced $62 million in funding - a $50 million Series A led by Framework Ventures with Y Combinator participating, on top of an earlier $12 million seed led by YC and Next View. It also deepened its partnership with Physical Intelligence, the 'robot brains' AI firm, which will supply AI software for Ultra's machines.

CEO Jon Miller Schwartz makes a contrarian point: humanoids get the attention, but non-humanoid robots do the real work - his machines have packed over half a million orders at third-party logistics sites across the country. With a mechanical-engineering background, Schwartz's team installs and maintains the hardware hands-on, part of a growing robot empire quietly taking over US warehouses while humanoids keep falling over.

## Highlights

- Ultra raised $62M total: $50M Series A (Framework Ventures) plus a $12M seed (YC, Next View).
- Expanded deal: Physical Intelligence supplies AI software for Ultra's warehouse robots.
- Business model: robots-as-a-service monthly leasing for warehouses and 3PLs.
- Its robots have packed 500,000+ orders for shipping.
- Schwartz: humanoids grab headlines, but other robot forms deliver the real-world impact.
