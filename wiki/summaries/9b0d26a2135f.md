# Meta’s Muse has a serious 0-day

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-22 · updated: 2026-09-22 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news` `meta`

## Summary

Meta founder and CEO Mark Zuckerberg has gone to great lengths to hype the security of its new AI assistant, Muse, claiming it is “built from the ground up for privacy and security.” A zero-day vulnerability that gives locally run apps…

Further raising questions, Amazon on Sunday began blocking Muse from its site.

When a task requires a tool that doesn’t exist, Muse creates one on the fly.

## Highlights

- The assistant “books appointments, fills out forms and handles customer service,” “proactively takes tasks off your plate,” and can “make purchases, generate images, create documents, and connect with your favorite apps and services.”…
- Of course, for Muse to do any of these things, users must first give it access to their accounts.
- Apple has spent years developing these defenses to prevent installed apps or commands entered into the terminal from accessing these resources, clearly because the company considers them a security threat.
- The zero-day allows any app or terminal command to gain access to the token that authenticates users to their Muse account.
- Meta developers designed the assistant so that any locally installed app or executed code, regardless of the macOS permissions it has, can change a long list of undocumented settings.
- More than 12 hours after this post went live, Meta said it released a hotfix that patched the 0-day.
- Wardle said that Meta developers made several design decisions that made his exploit possible.
- It’s likely Meta intended for apps working with Muse to control UI settings, and for understandable reasons.
