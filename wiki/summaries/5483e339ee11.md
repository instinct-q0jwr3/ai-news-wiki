# AI as teleportation

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high_

`geoffrey-litt`

## Summary

Geoffrey Litt's thought experiment: imagine teleportation arrives in 2035. It becomes a net win - commerce supercharged, commutes gone - but the landscape reconfigures: Everest crowded with selfie-taking influencers, in-between moments erased, and above all the death of serendipity, because you always decide your destination in advance. Teenagers find wandering unthinkable.

He pairs it with Albert Borgmann's wooden stove: central heating removed the inconvenience, and with it the hearth that organized family life - Chesterton's fence on hard mode. Applied to AI: if reading is 'marinating in ideas', an AI summary is teleportation past the point; if explanations come friction-free from a model, asking your nerdy friends takes new effort; if vibe coding skips sitting with the details, design changes in unpredictable ways. His takeaway isn't anti-AI: sometimes friction is where the good stuff happens, so keep good friction deliberately - his COVID 'virtual commute' walks as the model.

## Highlights

- Thought experiment: teleportation as a model for AI's social effects
- The biggest loss is serendipity - destinations chosen in advance, no wandering
- Borgmann's stove: removing the inconvenience removed the hearth
- AI summaries, frictionless explanations and vibe coding as teleportation past the point
- His practice: deliberately keep good friction, like the COVID 'virtual commute'
