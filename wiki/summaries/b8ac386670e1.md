## Summary

Cory Doctorow discusses a New York vehicle-subscription law as a partial response to manufacturers charging for features built into cars. His argument connects consumer-sales regulation with right-to-repair strategies and the limits state governments face when federal copyright law governs circumvention.

This is advocacy and legal commentary, not an independent legal opinion or a check of the enacted text. The essay itself emphasizes a reported February 27, 2027 effective date, a $250 violation penalty and broad exceptions for connected services, infotainment and driver-assistance features. It therefore should not be read as an immediate ban on every vehicle subscription, and its hypothetical loopholes are predictions about interpretation rather than decided cases.

## Highlights

- Essay frames sales regulation as an indirect repair strategy
- Reported effective date is February 27, 2027
- Author describes a $250 penalty per violation
- Connected services and other categories have exceptions
- Not every software subscription is covered
- Circumvention-law criticism is the author's position
- Hypothetical court outcomes are not established rulings
