# UK energy company Drax partners with Brookfield and neocloud Radiant to develop an AI data center in North Yorkshire, targeting 100MW of capacity by Q1 2028

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-03 · updated: 2026-11-07 · confidence: high_

`techmeme` `compute-buildout`

## Summary

Brookfield is going big on British AI infrastructure: energy company Drax Group signed an agreement with Brookfield and its neocloud platform Radiant to jointly develop an AI data center on the Drax Power Station estate in North Yorkshire. Phase one targets up to 100 MW operational from Q1 2028, another 500 MW across 2028-2031, and ultimately more than 1 GW at the site. Brookfield estimates total investment including chips could exceed £20 billion ($26.8B), with £2.5 billion for phase one. Drax shares jumped 8.8%.

The deal's shape says where the bottlenecks are: phase one runs on grid power, targets commercial customers from early 2028, and is still subject to planning, regulatory and network approvals - plus securing a tenant. Final investment decisions haven't been made. Radiant's CEO sells the sovereignty angle: frontier compute 'at home' for British researchers, startups and enterprises.

The context is Europe's structural problem. The UK has high energy costs and grid-connection queues measured in years - Microsoft reportedly walked from anchoring an Nscale facility near London over connection delays. A power-station estate is precisely the kind of site that short-circuits the queue: land, grid interconnection and generation adjacency already exist. Expect more generation owners to become datacenter developers.
