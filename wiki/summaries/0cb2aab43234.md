# AI is devastating higher education, but the system was broken

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-24 · updated: 2026-10-24 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

A long essay on AI's devastation of higher education - arguing the system was already broken before the models arrived. Any equation, answer, or essay is now instant, and the author dismisses both moral panic and abstinence: everyone is using it and you would be dumb not to. The real warning is different: don't outsource your thinking, because real learning has never been easy and the graduates who do that hard work are exactly the ones still needed.

The essay grounds the argument in a case: Ellis uploaded 40,000 words of her own original materials into Copilot, which summarized her knowledge base and produced prompts - early drafts reflecting her own thinking built up over years. A degree, the author insists, is a public claim as well as a private benefit: employers, patients, clients, and citizens rely on it as evidence its holder can exercise judgment. The crisis is less that students cheat than that the credential's meaning was already hollow where the work behind it was.

## Highlights

- AI made essays and answers instant; the essay argues higher ed was broken before that
- Warning: don't outsource thinking - real learning is hard, and that is why it matters
- Case: a student fed 40,000 words of her own material into Copilot to draft from her own knowledge
- A degree is a public claim: society relies on it as evidence of judgment
- The crisis is the credential's hollowness, not just student cheating
