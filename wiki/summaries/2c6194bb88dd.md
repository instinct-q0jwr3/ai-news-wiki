# Stevens: a hackable AI assistant using a single SQLite table and a handful of cron jobs

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high_

`geoffrey-litt` `agentic-systems` `ai-safety-incidents` `labor-displacement`

## Summary

Geoffrey Litt shows that a genuinely useful personal AI assistant needs no fancy agent frameworks: his family's assistant, Stevens (named for the butler in 'Remains of the Day'), runs on one SQLite table of memories plus a handful of cron jobs for ingestion and updates, hosted on Val.town. The whole architecture is simple enough to copy and extend.

Every morning Stevens sends a formal, butler-style Telegram brief to Litt and his wife: the day's calendars, a weather preview, expected mail and packages, and tracked reminders. Beyond the daily brief they can talk to it on demand - forwarding an email, for instance, to have its contents remembered. The post's quiet point: memory plus scheduling, not agentic complexity, is what makes an assistant feel useful.

## Highlights

- A family AI assistant built on a single SQLite memories table and cron jobs
- Hosted on Val.town; deliberately simple enough to copy and extend
