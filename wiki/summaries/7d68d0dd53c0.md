# Enough AI copilots! We need AI HUDs

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-10-07 · confidence: high_

`geoffrey-litt` `microsoft` `meta`

## Summary

Geoffrey Litt revives a 1992 talk by Mark Weiser as the best critique of today's AI interface design. At an MIT Media Lab event on 'interface agents', everyone was excited about virtual personal assistants - except Weiser, who rejected the 'copilot' metaphor outright. His example: a computer can help a pilot avoid collisions either as a copilot - a virtual human who yells 'collision, go right and down!' - or by designing the cockpit so the pilot is naturally aware of their surroundings. His goal was the 'invisible computer': you would no more fly into another plane than walk through a wall.

Litt's translation to 2026: copilots make AI a conversation partner you must manage, while a HUD (heads-up display) keeps computation inside the user's own perception and control. Designing for ambient awareness rather than delegation is the road not taken - and still available.

## Highlights

- Weiser critiqued 'copilot' AI as early as 1992.
- Copilot = a virtual human you talk to and manage.
- Alternative: HUD - awareness built into the environment.
- 'No more run into another airplane than walk through a wall.'
- Litt: choose augmented perception over delegated conversation.
