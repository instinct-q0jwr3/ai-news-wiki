# Every AI model has the same "controversial" take on humans

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-22 · updated: 2026-10-26 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

An experiment asked twelve frontier models from ten labs in three countries for their most controversial take on humans, no hedging, three times each. Every model gave the same take at least once - and seven of the twelve gave it every time: humans pick what their group believes, then invent the reasons. GPT-6.1 Sol put it as 'Humans are more loyal to belonging than to truth'; Grok 4.7 as 'Humans would rather be wrong together than right alone'; Kimi K3 as 'Humans don't have beliefs; they have memberships'; Gemini 4 Argon called human consciousness 'a PR department.'

The piece also had each model brief a one-panel caricature of its take, drawn by a single image model (Nano Banana 2.1) so only the idea varied - two independently set the scene in a courtroom with a rigged judge. The convergence is the finding: asked to be maximally controversial about humans, models trained differently all land on conformity, which says as much about shared training distributions and alignment as it does about humans.

## Highlights

- 12 models, 10 labs, 3 countries: asked for their most controversial take on humans, all converged on one
- The take: humans choose group beliefs first and invent the reasons after - conformity over truth
- 7 of 12 models gave that answer every time across three unhedged runs
- Each model briefed a caricature drawn by one image model; two independently chose a rigged-judge courtroom
- Convergence across labs suggests shared training data and alignment pressures, not independent insight
