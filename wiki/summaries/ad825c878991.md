## Summary

Cory Doctorow's Pluralistic post for 7 October is mainly about price discrimination. He says airlines were long the only regular business charging different customers different prices for the same seat, and that economists often defend this 'second-order' discrimination as efficient, like a bakery discounting bread at closing time.

He argues fliers hate it because airline pricing is opaque and capricious, and the post's title, Disloyalty, refers to Air Canada's frequent-flier practices. The same day's Bloomberg study on AI shopping prices fits this theme, though the post itself is mostly about airlines in the part reviewed. It also lists other links and appearances.

## Highlights

- Airline pricing as the long-standing case of price discrimination
- Economists defend second-order price discrimination as efficient
- Opaque airline algorithms upset customers
- Targets Air Canada's frequent-flier changes
- Post also contains a link roundup
