# A look at Chinese philosophical views on AI; one says aligning AI to human values may be a "suicidal mistake" and another fears the "machinization of humans"

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-21 · updated: 2026-10-22 · confidence: high_

`techmeme`

## Summary

Arnaud Bertrand surveys how Chinese philosophers, sociologists, and ethicists think about AI, arguing the Western debate is a frog in a well: it assumes intelligence defines humanity, we named ourselves Homo sapiens after all, so artificial intelligence feels existentially threatening, while China's equally old tradition never put intelligence at the core of being human and meets the thinking machine from a different starting point. Zhao Tingyang of the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences argues that aligning AI to human values may very well be a suicidal mistake. Liu Yongmou of Renmin University inverts the usual fear: humanity's biggest problem is the machinization of humans, not the humanization of machines. Bertrand reaches back to Aristotle, who in the Politics imagined shuttles weaving and lyres playing without hands and wondered whether slaves would still be needed, to show how long these questions have been asked.
