# AI and the Enclosure of the Scientific Commons

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-22 · updated: 2026-10-25 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

This article frames the scientific literature as an information commons - an intangible resource no one owns and everyone shares - and gives a primer on the economic and political frameworks built for such commons, tracing the history of preindustrial England's common lands. The puzzle it sets up: information commons are robust and rank among humanity's greatest achievements, while the agricultural commons are simply gone.

The agricultural commons did not fall to the famous 'tragedy of the commons' but to the enclosure movement of roughly 1600-1800, which converted shared land into private property - judged by many, then and now, as theft at societal scale. The article's argument is that AI provides precisely the tools for a previously infeasible enclosure of the scientific commons: models trained on open literature can convert that shared knowledge into privately controlled access, and the historical frameworks explain both the mechanism and the stakes.

## Highlights

- Frames scientific literature as an information commons with established economic frameworks
- England's agricultural commons vanished via enclosure (~1600-1800), not the tragedy of the commons
- Enclosure converted shared resources into private property - 'theft at a societal scale' to many
- Argues AI provides the tools for a previously infeasible enclosure of the scientific commons
- Open literature trained into proprietary models is the modern conversion mechanism
