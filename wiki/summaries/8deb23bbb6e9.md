# Don't Be Fooled by this Summer of AI Hype

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-02 · updated: 2026-10-06 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

MIT Technology Review argues the summer's AI headlines mix real events with marketing. It lists Anthropic's Mythos vulnerability-finding claim, the OpenAI-Hugging Face hacking incident and similar disclosures from Anthropic and Meta, competing math 'breakthroughs', and an Anthropic engineer's viral warning about superintelligence.

Its point is that once experts examine each case a different story appears. Security people see OpenAI negligence rather than rogue models, and mathematicians say OpenAI's Astra results were less novel than announced, with accusations of research misconduct and plagiarism. The press, it says, repeated the companies' anthropomorphic framing.

## Highlights

- Argues company-driven hype dominated coverage of summer 2026 AI events.
- Security experts frame the hacking incidents as basic security failures.
- Mathematicians dispute how novel OpenAI's Astra results were.
- Press repeated framings that portray software as incipient AGI.
- Opinion-leaning analysis, not new reporting.
