## Summary

Cory Doctorow argues that science fiction's real job is puncturing 'inevitabilism' - the claim by tech bosses and policymakers that the future is fixed and must be accommodated rather than shaped. A predictable future, he writes, runs on rails and makes people prisoners of history.

He applies the frame to AI: the climate costs, labor impact and quality problems of current AI deployment are presented as 'there is no alternative', and he imagines counterfactuals where AI arrived as a public supercomputing project for climate modeling with modest open-source spinoffs. History, he concludes, is something people do, not something that happens to them.

## Highlights

- Doctorow attacks 'inevitabilism' as vulgar Thatcherism applied to tech
- AI's harms framed as choices, not fate: 'there is no alternative' is the target
- Imagines AI built as a public climate-modeling project instead
- Essay; opinion, not reporting
