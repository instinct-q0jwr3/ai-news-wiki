# Cognitive Mercantilism: AI panic resembles fears of early capitalism

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-26 · updated: 2026-10-28 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

Reading Joyce Appleby's history of capitalism, this essay lands on a framing it calls cognitive mercantilism: today's AI panic replays the zero-sum worldview that preceded capitalism. Appleby's account describes capitalism as a historically contingent invention, not an instinct, which required escaping the mercantilist assumption that wealth is finite - land, bullion, commandable labor - and discovering that wealth can be created, and that ordinary people can be sources of demand and initiative.

The parallel the author draws is that AI pessimism treats cognition the way mercantilists treated gold: a fixed stock to be hoarded and protected, so that any machine share must come at human expense. If market capitalism was the first planetary intelligence, the essay suggests, then AI is less an alien invasion than the next expansion of the same story - and the mercantilist reflex, the fear that more intelligence anywhere means less for us, will look as dated as bullion-hoarding does now.

## Highlights

- The essay frames AI panic as 'cognitive mercantilism' - a zero-sum worldview applied to intelligence
- Appleby's history: capitalism was a contingent cultural invention, not the expression of a timeless instinct
- Mercantilism rested on four beliefs, starting with wealth as scarce, finite and captured rather than created
- Capitalism escaped that frame by discovering wealth creation and ordinary people as sources of demand
- The author suggests AI is the next expansion of the same story, not an alien invasion
- If market capitalism was the first planetary intelligence, hoarding cognition may look as dated as hoarding bullion
