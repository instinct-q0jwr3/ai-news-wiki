# Artifacts Are Alive

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-10-09 · confidence: high_

`works-on-my-machine`

## Summary

Scott Werner argues that our default formats for communicating ideas - blog posts, slide decks, PDFs, READMEs - are photographs: fixed from one angle, at one level of detail, impossible to explore. Ideas, he says, are more like coral reefs. Bret Victor has spent years making this point with explorable explanations, but building them by hand was too expensive for them to become an everyday medium.

What changed is the artifact: a self-contained HTML+JavaScript mini-app, the shape Claude popularized, which makes interactive explanations something you can build in twenty minutes. Werner runs through what that unlocks - PR reviews where you step through the algorithm before and after, explorable explanations of libraries or business rules, game prototypes you drop into Discord to ask 'is this fun?', interactive essays where the reader pokes the example instead of reading a description of it. The larger claim: software is becoming a content medium, and every medium that gets cheap enough to be abundant eventually gets its village square.

## Highlights

- The core metaphor: a photograph of a reef lets you look; the reef lets you explore - documents are photographs, ideas are reefs
- Static formats capture an idea from one angle at one moment and force confused readers to re-read linearly
- Bret Victor's explorable explanations were the right idea but too costly to hand-build for everyday communication
- Artifacts (self-contained HTML+JS mini-apps) drop that cost to ~twenty minutes, making interactivity a default format
- Use cases: PR review artifacts with before/after sliders, explorable explanations of libraries and business rules
- Also: embryonic game prototypes for feel-testing, and interactive essays where the example is the example
- The framing shift: stop thinking of software as a product, start thinking of it as a medium
- Historical rhyme: photography got cheap and someone built the village square for it - the same is starting for software content
