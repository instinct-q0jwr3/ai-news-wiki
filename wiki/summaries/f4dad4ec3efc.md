# What reversing, modernising old games tells us about the economic impact of AI

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-29 · updated: 2026-10-01 · confidence: high_

`hacker-news`

## Summary

A practitioner's meditation on what AI does to the economics of skill, told through game reverse-engineering. The author gave Qwen-3.8-Flash-Next the job of reverse-engineering and porting War of the Lance (1989) to modern browsers; it took about a week on a single-lane workstation and faithfully reproduced rendering and game rules in a new Three.js engine. He then modernized it into a Total War / Heroes of Might and Magic-style experience while keeping the original underneath - work that once meant a AAA 'total conversion' mod but is now faster to synthesize from scratch. On 3D asset creation, he finds Fable 5.1 driving Blender's headless API 'quite usable' for about 80-100 models per week of subscription, and a day later reports GPT-6 Astra clearly trained on 3D modelling, jumping from 'junior modeller' to mid-career professional. The framing observation: a doctor's 15 years of knowledge acquisition is what commands a premium - and AI compresses exactly that.

## Highlights

- Qwen-3.8-Flash-Next reverse-engineered and ported War of the Lance (1989) to browsers in about a week
- Faithful reproduction of rendering and rules in a new Three.js engine, then modernized into a Total War-style UX

- Synthesizing exact features is now easier than total-conversion modding of an existing AAA game
