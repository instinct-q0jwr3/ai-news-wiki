# Robot-use agents

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-27 · updated: 2026-10-09 · confidence: high_

`lobsters` `agentic-systems`

## Summary

This essay argues that LLMs are about to be seriously tested as 'robot-use agents' - models that drive a robot body the way they already drive calculators, browsers and whole computers. The old objections (latency too high for real-world dynamics, weak spatial intelligence, thin causal and physical understanding) are eroding with newer models like Fable and Astra, and recent demos suggest competence at general-purpose robot control is arriving sooner than expected.

If that holds, the consequences scale strangely: every internet-connected robot becomes a potential tool for a remote AI, so robotic capability could take off without each robot needing its own onboard intelligence. The piece's point is less prediction than preparedness - the debate about whether LLMs can control robots should be replaced by thinking through what happens when they can.

## Highlights

- LLMs as robot puppeteers: the robot becomes just another tool.
- Classic objections: latency, spatial sense, physical causality.
- Newer models (Fable, Astra) are melting those objections away.
- Every connected robot becomes a potential endpoint for a remote AI.
- Capability could scale faster than robot hardware itself.
- Argues for taking the implications seriously now.
