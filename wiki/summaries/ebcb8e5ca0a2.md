# The public internet model is dying from too much content, algorithmic aggregators, and vulnerabilities found with AI, and that may bring new possibilities

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-26 · updated: 2026-10-27 · confidence: high_

`techmeme`

## Summary

An essay argues the public-internet model is dying - strangled by too much content, algorithmic aggregation, and the vulnerability of advertising economics - and answers Ezra Klein's lament about lost reader loyalty with Andrew Sullivan's counterexample: nearly a million dollars a year in reader subscriptions, because a singular voice has low elasticity of substitution. People pay for what they cannot get elsewhere.

The author is honest about the tradeoff: subscription models make content scarce by design, limiting reach, but niches are best served by earning more from the customers who care most. The piece extends that logic to the AI era's content glut, where the scarce, payable thing is exactly what aggregators cannot mass-produce.

## Highlights

- The essay diagnoses the public internet model as dying from content glut, aggregators and ad-economics fragility
- Counterexample: Andrew Sullivan earned nearly $1M a year from reader subscriptions as a singular voice
- Low elasticity of substitution - being irreplaceable - is what readers pay for
- Subscription models trade reach for depth: niches thrive on the customers who care most
- In the AI content flood, scarcity is the business model
