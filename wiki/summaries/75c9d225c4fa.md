# How Claude is uplifting biomolecular modeling

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-20 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high_

`tldr-ai` `anthropic`

## Summary

Anthropic reports that Claude, working inside its Claude Science setup, optimized more than 30 open-source biomolecular models in just under four weeks, making them roughly 4x faster on average (nearly 2x with bit-identical outputs) and far more memory-efficient. A new low-memory mode allows accurate prediction of biomolecular systems larger than 10,000 tokens on a single NVIDIA GPU node, and all optimized code is being open-sourced.

The practical impact is cost: Anthropic's earlier de novo protein binder work spent up to $10,000 per target, about 2,500 H100 GPU hours, which put it out of reach for most labs; the optimizations bring comparable in silico performance with two orders of magnitude fewer GPU hours. Anthropic is also co-sponsoring a protein design competition with Adaptyv Bio backed by up to $1 million in Claude credits, $250,000 in Modal compute and wet-lab validation for over 5,000 designs.

## Highlights

- 30+ open-source biology models optimized in under four weeks, ~4x average speedup, ~2x with identical outputs.
- Low-memory mode enables prediction of systems over 10,000 tokens on a single NVIDIA GPU node.
- Claude developed FlashPairformer, custom kernels for triangle attention and multiplication, beating the field standard by 2.7-2.9x and 1.7-3.2x.
- Targets structure prediction (AlphaFold3, OpenFold3, Boltz-2 class), protein design, genomics and protein language models.
- Triangle operations are cubic in runtime and memory: doubling system size costs 8x, tripling costs 27x.
- Prior binder work cost up to $10,000 per target (~2,500 H100 hours); new results use ~100x fewer GPU hours.
- All optimized code is open-sourced, with a technical report released alongside.
- Competition with Adaptyv Bio: five frontier problems, up to $1M in Claude credits, $250K in Modal credits, wet-lab validation for 5,000+ designs.
