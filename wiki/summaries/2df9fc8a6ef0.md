# Chinese economists warn AI focus crowds out a broader economic crisis

_type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-21 · updated: 2026-09-25 · confidence: high_

`techmeme`

## Summary

As Xi Jinping arrives in Washington for a state visit, Li Yuan argues in the New York Times that China's celebrated AI advances obscure an economy in its worst shape in decades - and that the two facts are related. Youth unemployment excluding students hit 18.9 percent in August, domestic car sales fell 20 percent in the first half of the year, housing sales fell another 14 percent, and the country is in a deflationary spiral. Economists in China, including figures close to the state, warn openly that the government is pouring too many resources into a technology that creates relatively few jobs while doing too little to rescue the broader economy.

The chorus of concern includes Li Daokui, a former central bank adviser at Tsinghua University, who said in July that China's economy is 'running too cold' and that booming high-tech sectors cannot lift it on their own. The piece frames the AI push as both a genuine strategic success and a misallocation debate Beijing will have to confront even as it projects technological parity with the US.
