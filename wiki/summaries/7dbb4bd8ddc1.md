## Summary

Cory Doctorow argues that Uber's labor economics and history of investor promises undermine the idea that replacing drivers with robots is a simple path to better margins. The essay connects worker pay, accounting narratives and transport-market ambition to skepticism about autonomous-fleet economics.

The piece is polemical commentary with linked sources, not a neutral financial audit. Its central question is whether owning and operating expensive vehicles can improve on a model that shifts vehicle costs onto drivers. Predictions about the outcome remain the author's argument rather than an established result.

## Highlights

- Essay challenges autonomous-fleet economics
- Worker compensation and risk allocation are central
- Author criticizes past investor narratives
- Capital costs complicate replacing drivers
- Commentary is not a definitive financial forecast
