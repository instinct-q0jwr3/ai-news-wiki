The End Of Upward Mobility – AI is coming for the meritocracy

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-21 · updated: 2026-09-22 · confidence: high

hacker-news

Summary

This Noema essay reads the AI moment through James Burnham's 1941 'The Managerial Revolution', which declared capitalism finished and wrongly predicted managerial superstates, and asks what class structure AI actually threatens. Its subject is the 'homoploutic' class: households that hold both capital and high-value labor, resolving the capital-labor conflict inside the same person. The class's ideological machinery is meritocracy, per Daniel Markovits's 'The Meritocracy Trap' (2019): an elite convinced its position is earned, even as parental investment per elite child approaches a $10 million inheritance by the end of graduate school, an M-E-M' circuit where E is the education of one's children.

The double grounding in credentials and workplace performance is what gave the class political legitimacy: 'we are rich because we are useful, and you may join us if you are useful too.' AI attacks exactly that claim. The essay is honest about the unknowns, whether AI raises total output or just shifts it, whether capability progress continues given circular data-center financing and political backlash, how fast adoption lands, and locates the fork in one unresolved empirical question: whether AI will be a substitute for or a complement to high-premium cognitive labor. Two very different futures for the meritocratic class, and for everyone else, follow from the answer.
