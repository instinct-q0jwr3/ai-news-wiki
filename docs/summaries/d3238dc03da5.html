The real AI risk is inside the labs

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

antirez openai external-evaluation ai-safety-incidents compute-buildout ai-policy-regulation

Summary

Amodei in his latest blog post wrote a mix of agreeable things and things that I believe misrepresent where the real risk of AI is located.

I want to focus my attention on why, among all the risks, open weight models constitute the mildest one.

The real risk is leaks, not releases, and leaks happen inside frontier companies.

Highlights

  • I write these words as a person who strongly believes AI may be very dangerous in the near future: 1.
  • Closed models that will never even be opened to the public will be just a few TBs of data.
  • All you need to leak one is a single person with access and the wrong goals, and you are back in the situation of open models.
  • Open models are released *after* testing, and after similarly capable models were already available for some time under an API.
  • As Amodei says, open models, once LLMs are dangerous enough in fields like biology, can be trained on a corpus ablated of certain branches of science, while still being useful for a number of other things.
  • We are currently not in a place where open models can constitute that kind of danger.
