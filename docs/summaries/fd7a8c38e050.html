Jevons Paradox in AI: Cheaper Tokens Won't Pay for Capex

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-24 · updated: 2026-11-28 · confidence: high

Summary

Philipp Dubach argues that rising token volume need not finance AI infrastructure if prices fall at a similar rate. His essay distinguishes fixed-capability price declines from the cost of using frontier models, then interprets outcome-based pricing as an attempt to capture budgets formerly spent on labor.

The arithmetic of price times volume is an identity, but the conclusion depends on heterogeneous demand, price, margin and capex series whose scopes differ. The author acknowledges that Google token volume is not sold at the fixed-capability prices used in other studies, and that cheaper inference may enable new products. Reported company finances, labor figures and future revenue needs were not independently reconciled here; the essay is analysis, not proof of an inevitable funding shortfall or investment advice.

Highlights

  • Essay separates token volume from revenue
  • Fixed-capability prices differ from frontier-model costs
  • Outcome pricing is interpreted as reaching labor budgets
  • Mixed datasets do not by themselves establish demand elasticity
  • New applications could change the spending response
  • Financial and employment claims were not independently audited
  • The conclusion is an economic argument rather than a guaranteed forecast
