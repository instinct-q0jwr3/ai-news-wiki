How to spot AI science slop

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-22 · updated: 2026-10-25 · confidence: high

Summary

A practical guide to spotting AI-generated science slop, arguing that the old tells have inverted: typos and bad grammar once signaled careless work, while AI text is formally polished - so the signals are now linguistic quirks, structural habits, and factual wobble rather than surface errors. Because it is easier to write in your own style than to get facts right, content dense with AI tells is likely unverified and should be checked before trust.

The guide is careful about false positives: formal tone, good punctuation, and individual 'tells' all have innocent explanations, AI detectors are unreliable, and every sign appears in human writing too - the method is counting so many signs that coincidence becomes implausible. It also counsels against public callouts as usually unproductive: safer to unfollow and mock in the group chat. The underlying claim: AI science content is super prone to factual error, and verification is the reader's job.

Highlights

  • AI-generated science content is highly prone to factual errors; the guide teaches recognition
  • Old tells inverted: polish is now the warning sign, not typos
  • Method: count many weak signs until coincidence is implausible; single tells prove nothing
  • AI detectors are unreliable; every AI sign also appears in human writing
  • Advises against public callouts - unfollow and verify instead
