A Gallup survey of 15,925 people aged 15 to 22 across 16 countries finds that more than 33% rely on AI for companionship, rising to 50% among daily AI users

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-18 · updated: 2026-11-26 · confidence: high

Summary

Wired reports a Gallup and Boston Children’s Hospital Digital Wellness Lab survey of people aged fifteen to twenty-two across sixteen countries. Around a third reported relying on AI for companionship, with higher shares among daily AI users; the report also associates in-person friendships with stronger support and more positive emotional experiences.

The survey does not determine whether loneliness leads people toward AI companions or whether those interactions reinforce loneliness. Its age range is narrower than all of Generation Z, and associations between relationship types and wellbeing should not be read as individual diagnoses or proof of harm.

Highlights

  • Reported sample includes 15,925 young people
  • Study spans sixteen countries
  • Companionship use differs by AI-use frequency
  • Offline and online connections can coexist
  • Causal direction of loneliness remains unresolved
  • Results do not describe every age in Generation Z
