Pluralistic: How corporate America built a better Roach Motel (07 Sep 2026)

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-10-01 · confidence: high

Summary

Cory Doctorow revisits the Chicago Boys' syllogism - monopolies are evidence of excellence, and any bad monopolist gets competed away - and its obvious failure half a century on: an economy dominated by monopolies and cartels with gigantic margins and palpably worsening products, with no flood of new entrants in sight. The irony he savors is that the University of Chicago's own Stigler Center now houses the heterodox economists who go 'look at horses' instead of theorizing about them.

The horse in question is a new Stigler paper, 'Rising Customer Durability, Falling Business Dynamism.' Between 2002 and 2024, the average customer relationship lengthened from 7.5 to 11.5 years - a 50% surge in 'loyalty' across manufacturing, information, professional services, finance and wholesale, with no matching rise in satisfaction. The authors' hypothesis: dominant firms keep customers not by pleasing them but by making leaving harder. Their clever method: merger filings must truthfully disclose, under audit and legal liability, exactly how the deal will retain customers and extract more from them - a public, verified record of lock-in strategy, assembled into evidence of the modern Roach Motel.

Highlights

  • The Chicago syllogism: a monopoly in the wild is definitionally excellent, or it would already be gone - the $20-bill joke as policy
  • Fifty years later: monopolies everywhere, margins up, quality visibly falling, and no competitors arriving to 'compete it away'
  • The Stigler Center at Chicago now hosts the heterodox opposition (Capitalisn't, Promarket, radical antitrust work)
  • New paper: average customer relationships rose from 7.5 to 11.5 years (2002-2024) - a 50% jump in 'durability'
  • The rise holds across manufacturing, information, professional services, finance and wholesale - and outstrips any satisfaction metric
  • Hypothesis: dominance is maintained by raising the cost of leaving, not by making customers happy
  • Method: merger disclosures must truthfully detail customer-retention and profit-extraction plans - audited, public, legally binding
  • Doctorow's frame: corporate America built a better Roach Motel - customers check in, but they can't check out
