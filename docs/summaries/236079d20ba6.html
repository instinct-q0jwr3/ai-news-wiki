AI is becoming a disaster for the climate

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-22 · updated: 2026-11-23 · confidence: high

Summary

Brian Merchant argues that the environmental costs of the AI buildout are rising faster than public reporting captures. His newsletter brings together a reported Nature Sustainability study of 403 large US data centers, criticism of its coverage, separate projections of AI's future emissions, and opposition to the BAAJA permitting bill. The study is reported to estimate 25.7 million metric tons of annual CO2 from the facilities' electricity use during May 2024 to April 2025. That is a data-center sample, not a direct measurement of all AI emissions.

Merchant's argument turns on what the estimates exclude: on-site fossil generation, facilities outside the sample, upstream methane and other lifecycle effects. He also reports a local health-impact model by his uncle, a retired pulmonologist, concerning a planned Montana development. The family relationship is disclosed in the article. These are several studies and advocacy arguments with different boundaries, not additive measurements. The article's legal interpretation of the permitting bill and market claims were not checked against legislation or filings here; its forecasts remain attributed projections. The separate Anthropic power case study already covered today is related context, not independent confirmation of each number.

Highlights

  • Merchant combines emissions research with an argument against permitting changes
  • The reported study covers 403 facilities in May 2024-April 2025
  • Its reported 25.7 million-ton estimate is not an all-AI emissions inventory
  • On-site generation and lifecycle emissions have different coverage boundaries
  • Long-run AI comparisons are forecasts, not current measurements
  • The Montana health model is attributed to the author's uncle
  • Legislative and market assertions were not independently verified in this pass
