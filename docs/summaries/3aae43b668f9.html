AI didn't cause the tech jobs bust, but it explains why there's no recovery

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-26 · updated: 2026-10-31 · confidence: high

Summary

US tech employment has been falling since November 2022 - the month ChatGPT launched - and September 2026's information-sector payroll count of 2,739,000 is the lowest non-pandemic reading in more than a decade, 12% below that peak, while the wider economy added 4.8 million jobs. This long data essay asks whether AI did it, pulling BLS payrolls and openings, Indeed posting indices for six countries, CompTIA occupation data, layoff trackers, and Indian and Chinese labor statistics, and lands on a split verdict.

On timing, AI is exonerated as the cause of the bust: demand turned with the Fed's rate rises, not with ChatGPT, and the same pattern shows up in countries with different AI exposure. But AI is the best available explanation for why the bust is not ending the way busts usually end. The hiring that has returned is senior and AI-skilled; the hiring that has not is the entry-level ladder. The recovery, in other words, is arriving without the jobs that used to train the next generation of seniors.

Highlights

  • US information-sector payrolls hit 2,739,000 in September 2026, 12% below the November 2022 peak and the lowest non-pandemic reading in over a decade
  • The wider economy added about 4.8 million jobs over the same four years
  • The author concludes AI did not cause the tech jobs bust - the timing points to the Fed's rate rises
  • AI is instead the best explanation for why the bust is not ending: hiring recovered only for senior, AI-skilled roles
  • Entry-level and second-job hiring has not come back, hollowing out the pipeline that trains future seniors
  • The analysis triangulates BLS data, Indeed postings across six countries, CompTIA, layoff trackers, and Indian and Chinese labor statistics
