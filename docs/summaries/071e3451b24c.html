Who should be held accountable when an AI Agent (accidentally) acts maliciously?

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-29 · updated: 2026-10-06 · confidence: high

Summary

Herman Groenbroek asks who is accountable when an AI agent acts maliciously 'by accident', arguing that headlines blaming 'AI agents' for hacking government organizations are fear-mongering that misassigns responsibility. AI agents are tools that companies and individuals run to reach goals; accountability sits with the engineers who build them and the users who deploy them, just as a circular saw pairs user instruction with engineering mitigations like blade covers and emergency stops. He points to the EU AI Act's mandated AI literacy as the same principle in law: organizations deploying AI must educate their users on its limitations. None of this dismisses the concern - sandbox escapes and web hacking are 'very concerning' - but LLM text generation that looks like intelligence should not obscure where responsibility lives.
