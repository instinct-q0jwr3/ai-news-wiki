What Happens If OpenAI Dies?

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

ed-zitron openai

Summary

Ed Zitron's 'What Happens If OpenAI Dies?' reads the financial plumbing of the AI boom and asks the bankruptcy question out loud. Along the way he picks apart credulous coverage: a CNBC-quoted Anthropic 'profitable quarter' was only made possible by Musk's compute discounts in May and June 2026, a fact left out of the article; Bloomberg's 'run rate' is defined without saying how it is derived; and OpenAI calculates annualized revenue by multiplying the last four weeks by 12 - on a one-week spike it would be 'roughly $30 billion.'

The deal machinery gets the same treatment. Nvidia's guarantee on one data-center project backs the asset value, not OpenAI's lease payments: if OpenAI walked away, SB Energy would try to re-lease or sell the site and Nvidia would cover the difference - up to $105 billion if the initial phase is completed - and the guarantee covers completed data centers, not construction. Goldman, Blackstone and Apollo spent months on AI chip debt deals until Jensen Huang went public with a collective $500 billion financing goal, 'a round figure with no obvious provenance.' OpenAI's advisers offered a 2027 IPO at a $1 trillion valuation or a quicker one at a lower number; Altman reportedly called any change from a trillion a nonstarter. Even the smart money is early innings: Thrive's 2022 growth fund has returned 0.3x, still top 5%, and its OpenAI position - spread across at least five funds - is expected to carry the return. Zitron flags the whole exercise as speculation and declines to engage with bailout scenarios (in the excerpt available).

Highlights

  • Zitron asks the question directly: what happens if OpenAI dies?
  • Anthropic's 'profitable quarter' rested on Musk's May-June 2026 compute discounts, undisclosed in coverage
  • OpenAI's annualized revenue math: last four weeks times 12 - a one-week spike would give ~$30B
  • Nvidia's guarantee backs data-center asset value, not OpenAI's lease - capped at $105B for phase one
  • The guarantee covers completed data centers, not ones under construction
  • Jensen Huang's $500B collective financing figure: 'a round figure with no obvious provenance'
  • IPO choice: $1T valuation in 2027 or lower sooner; Altman called a cut a nonstarter
  • Thrive's 2022 fund returned 0.3x (top 5%); its OpenAI bet spans at least five funds
