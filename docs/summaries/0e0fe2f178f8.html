ChatGPT Space: a dystopia of centralised context

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-06 · updated: 2026-10-23 · confidence: high

Summary

Richard Ng's essay argues that ChatGPT Space - OpenAI's new shared workspace where teams tag in ChatGPT, Codex or their 'dot' to build on shared context - is a context-centralization flywheel that could hand one company dystopian levels of power. With Microsoft 365 making around $100 billion a year, the commercial prize is obvious; the societal cost, Ng argues, is that organizations' collective knowledge, IP and expertise become absorbed and commoditized by a single provider.

He pairs the polemic with a quote from Sam Altman's Vanity Fair interview acknowledging how scary people find concentrated AI power, and offers a counterpoint: a 2-minute proof-of-concept for an open-source alternative that keeps the power of owned context without the centralization. The essay is split into two parts - the dystopia case and an upbeat companion piece on the radical potential of owning your own context.

Highlights

  • ChatGPT Space creates shared living documents with ChatGPT, Codex and personal 'dots'.
  • Ng's warning: it centralizes organizational context inside one company's flywheel.
  • At stake: how organizations learn, build IP and differentiate as AI absorbs their expertise.
  • Microsoft 365's ~$100B annual revenue shows the commercial scale OpenAI is chasing.
  • Altman himself acknowledges public unease about concentrated AI power.
  • Ng demos an open-source alternative aimed at keeping context ownership with the user.
