54% faster LLM token generation by removing the Linux OS and llama.cpp

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-28 · updated: 2026-11-28 · confidence: high

Summary

A developer reports baking a Qwen3-0.6B model into a 24,252-byte pure x86-64 assembly operating system that boots in 74 to 94 milliseconds and chats over VGA, keyboard and serial, with no Linux, C, Rust or libc underneath. Self-reported benchmarks on the same AMD EPYC 7642 claim 23.67 versus 16.63 tokens per second at one thread against llama.cpp, rising to 134.94 versus 103.03 at sixteen threads, with bit-exact output across thread counts and 0.99965 cosine similarity against PyTorch FP32. A diskless 2 GiB Proxmox VM reportedly boots to a prompt in under five seconds and decodes at 78 tokens per second. These are single-author measurements posted on X with a repository link; no independent reproduction, and the gains apply to one small quantized model rather than general LLM serving.

Highlights

  • A 24 KB assembly OS runs Qwen3-0.6B inference with no operating system or libc underneath.
  • Claimed speedups over llama.cpp range from 31% to 54% on the same AMD EPYC 7642 CPU.
  • Output is reported bit-exact across thread counts and near-identical to PyTorch FP32 reference.
  • All figures are self-reported in a social post; no independent benchmark exists.
  • The demonstration covers one 0.6B quantized model, not production-scale inference.
