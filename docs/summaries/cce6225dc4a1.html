Show HN: Babytalk: Offline speech to text and text to speech on ESP32

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-10 · updated: 2026-10-10 · confidence: high

Summary

I have been playing with a lot of $20 devices with no keyboards or screens.

I wanted to use speech to interact with them, but without it having to run through a cloud service or use a fixed command vocabulary.

Memory, bottom line: speech to text needs 6 MB of flash and 84.5 KB of internal RAM ; text to speech adds 1.9 MB of flash .

Highlights

  • I worked with Claude to implement high performance 4bit int kernels for the ESP32S3 and P4 and quantize some larger STT and TTS models.
  • Surprisingly, it's quite usable on these tiny boards.
  • Also includes a finetuned model that can better cope with my diesel drones, hacking coughs, and mumbling.
  • Works with Micropython (RAM scarcity issues) and AtomVM (better).
  • MIT licensed; the speech models belong to their authors (see Credits and licenses ).
  • Most "voice on ESP32" projects do one of two things: BabyTalk is different: a general-purpose English speech recognizer that runs entirely on the chip.
  • A wake phrase is any phrase you type ("wake up, tomato face"), also with no training.
  • The original research plan in docs/historical/ explored LoRA-style expert adapters for the speech model.
