Meta removes ToS language that suggested it could scrape websites "associated with" business accounts, saying it applied only to "ads and business products"

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-17 · updated: 2026-10-23 · confidence: high

Summary

After backlash, Meta removed language from its commercial terms that experts said would have allowed it to scrape content from websites 'associated with' business accounts - including news sites - to train its AI. The removed clause said Meta could use any content from such domains to develop, improve, research and train its products.

Meta's response: the consolidated crawling terms only ever applied to 'ads and business products', the change does not alter how Meta uses web crawlers, and content owners can still use robots.txt to signal preferences - 'this is not about scraping news sites, and these updates don't give us new rights that supersede those preferences.'

Highlights

  • Meta drops ToS clause seen as allowing news-site scraping for AI training
  • Clause covered content from domains 'associated with' business accounts
  • Meta says it applied only to ads and business products
  • Claims no change to crawler behavior; robots.txt preferences still honored
  • Change followed public backlash, per The Verge
