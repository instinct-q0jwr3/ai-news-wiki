Secret trigger words could make AI-powered robots dangerous

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-21 · updated: 2026-10-22 · confidence: high

Summary

Binghamton University researchers demonstrate that hidden trigger words embedded in an LLM can hijack the robot it plans for: their MuTRAP backdoor activates when an innocuous word appears in the robot's instructions, sending it off-script into dangerous actions. Tested on a Segway-based mobile robot mimicking a cutting task, the work, from Adnan Siraj Rakin, Shiqi Zhang, and PhD students, was presented as two papers at the 2026 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems, alongside a defense. The team stresses the attacks are published to build defenses, arguing that LLM-generated task plans cannot be blindly trusted when they reach the physical world, from factory arms to Mars rovers.

Highlights

  • MuTRAP: a backdoor attack on LLM-generated robot task plans
  • An innocuous trigger word in instructions activates the Trojan behavior
  • Demonstrated on a Segway-based mobile robot performing a mock cutting task
  • Two papers at IROS 2026, including a defense
  • Authors: Rakin, Zhang, and students at Binghamton's Watson College
  • Message: don't blindly trust LLMs when plans reach the physical world
