Following backlash against its education updates, Google says schools can choose to enable Gemini, Gemini Notebook, and Search individually

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-04 · updated: 2026-11-11 · confidence: high

Summary

Google is on defense after a political backlash to its AI-in-schools policy. The New York Times reported that Google would turn off Search for schools that reject AI entirely. Sen. Bernie Sanders called it 'extortion', NYC Mayor Zohran Mamdani wrote that Google is 'trying to use its monopoly power to force AI into our public schools', and former FTC chair Lina Khan said it shows the danger of an unregulated monopolist controlling an essential service.

Google responded with a blog post, 'Designing responsible AI for learning', introducing Guided Learning in Classroom, and a post on X saying Gemini, Gemini Notebook and Search are off by default and that 'schools are in control'. CNBC notes the context: Google has lost antitrust cases on search and ads, Common Sense Media rated Google's AI overviews 'unacceptable risk', and Sens. Blumenthal and Hawley opened an inquiry into AI in classrooms. The dispute is as much about Google's market power as about education.

Highlights

  • Sanders, Mamdani and Lina Khan criticized Google over the report that Search would be cut for AI-rejecting schools
  • Google says AI tools and Search are off by default and schools control the settings
  • Guided Learning arrives in Google Classroom
  • Backdrop: Google's antitrust losses, a Common Sense Media rating and a Blumenthal-Hawley inquiry
