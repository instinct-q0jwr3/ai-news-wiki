Dylan Patel – Anthropic & OpenAI will have most of the world’s compute by 2028

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-10-01 · confidence: high

Summary

Dwarkesh Patel and Dylan Patel (SemiAnalysis) map out lab economics over the next few years. About a third of the compute coming online this year ultimately serves OpenAI and Anthropic; capex is already past $1 trillion this year and heads above $2 trillion by 2028, with the labs taking a growing share - on track to control most of the world's usable FLOPs because they monetize compute better than anyone else and can outbid every rival. Both labs are turning the corner on profitability (Anthropic in Q2, OpenAI expected around Q3) even as their annual spend climbs from tens of billions toward trillions.

From there the conversation turns macro: whether the $10T+ of cumulative AI capex by decade's end could trigger a sovereign debt crisis - hyperscaler debt raising interest rates, pushing non-AI-exposed countries toward bankruptcy and dragging down non-AI equities. They also cover China's position (under 10% of new compute, but its labs need less), the labs' shift of compute from inference back to R&D as recursive self-improvement approaches, and the unresolved question of whether anything can counter the centralizing forces: economies of scale in training, compute scarcity, and eventually continual learning and RSI.

Highlights

  • Dylan Patel's core claim: Anthropic and OpenAI are on track to control most of the world's usable compute within a few years
  • Mechanism: they monetize compute better than anyone, so they can outbid every other buyer
  • About a third of compute coming online this year ultimately serves the two labs; their share keeps growing
  • AI capex passed $1T this year and projects above $2T by 2028; lab spending heads from billions to trillions per year
  • Anthropic turned profitable in Q2; OpenAI is expected to follow around Q3 on the back of Codex and 5.6
  • Most of US GDP growth last year was AI infrastructure
  • Labs are shifting compute from inference back into R&D as RSI approaches
  • China gets under 10% of new compute, but its labs are more compute-efficient
  • Open macro risk: $10T+ of AI capex could mean hyperscaler debt raising rates and stressing non-AI economies
  • Unresolved: what, if anything, counters the centralizing forces of training scale, compute scarcity and continual learning
