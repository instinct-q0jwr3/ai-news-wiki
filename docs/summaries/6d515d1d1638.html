87% of the Anthropic power will come from gas and 11% from coal in 2028

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-22 · updated: 2026-11-28 · confidence: high

Summary

The Better Data Center Project publishes a case study of Anthropic's power procurement, estimating that about 87% of the company's power will come from gas and 11% from coal in 2028, with CO2 emissions from power use at its contracted sites reaching 17 to 38 million metric tons per year, 32 million in the mid case. The analysis says Anthropic has 15 GW of compute contracted as of October 2026, of which 5.9 to 9.6 GW is tied to specific sites, finds no disclosed US renewable-energy commitments, and notes Anthropic bought commitments for 1.7 GW of gas generators for $3.4 billion. It also argues that new grid load without renewable commitments is met by marginal fossil generators, that 80% of the on-site power uses less efficient simple-cycle turbines, and that the average carbon intensity of the modeled mix runs about 35% above the US grid average.

These are an environmental research group's modeled estimates built from disclosures, filings and its own marginal-generator assumptions, not measurements or Anthropic's own accounting; the report itself gives wide uncertainty ranges for utilization, cooling and deal interpretation. Company statements about broadening its energy mix are quoted but not evaluated against signed contracts beyond what is disclosed. The SpaceX compute figures cited in the methodology come from S-1 filings as interpreted by the authors, with their own uncertainty flags attached. The headline and contextual comparisons use millions of metric tons; the body also prints an inconsistent million-plus-Mt unit label, so that label should not be read as an additional factor of a million.

Highlights

  • Modeled mix: 87% gas and 11% coal for Anthropic's power in 2028
  • Mid-case estimate of 32 million metric tons of CO2 per year, range 17-38
  • 15 GW of compute contracted; 5.9-9.6 GW tied to named sites
  • No disclosed US renewable commitments; 1.7 GW of gas generators purchased
  • Marginal-generator assumption drives the near-term fossil attribution
  • Research-group modeling with stated uncertainty, not measured emissions
