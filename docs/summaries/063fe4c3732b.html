Sam Altman says trust me; Jensen Huang says everything is going to be fine; Bernie Sanders says AI is more dangerous than nukes

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-10-08 · confidence: high

Summary

Gary Marcus recounts a BBC appearance where back-to-back clips from Sam Altman, Jensen Huang and Bernie Sanders were played - all, in his view, nonsensical claims uttered with perfect conviction. On Altman's plea that the world should trust OpenAI to do the right thing (with a long pause after the word 'feel'), Marcus contrasts a clip from three years earlier in which Altman himself answered 'You shouldn't' when asked why we should trust him.

On Huang's claim, transcribed by David Sacks, that giant corporations will police themselves and not release products until they are safe, Marcus counters with the real world: OpenAI is burning cash, needs more of it, and has already shown willingness to compromise safety for press and market share.

Highlights

  • BBC aired clips from Altman, Huang and Sanders; Marcus rebutted all three
  • Altman asks to be trusted to do right - with a conspicuous pause mid-sentence
  • Altman three years ago, asked why we should trust him: 'You shouldn't'
  • Huang claims trillion-dollar corporations will self-police on safety
  • Marcus: OpenAI is burning cash and trades safety for press and market share
