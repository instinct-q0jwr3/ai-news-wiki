Broccoli AI, which sells AI agents to plumbing, HVAC, and electrical companies, raised a $22M Series A and a $30M Series B; a source says it is valued at $300M

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-09 · confidence: high

Summary

Broccoli AI's origin story starts with a plumbing-shop counter in Sacramento. Marcelle Flowers, 62, whose sale of Armstrong Plumbing had just fallen through and who was 'really opposed to AI,' gave two students at her counter five minutes, assuming they were selling chocolate. They were AJ Jain and Aditya Ketkar, mid-twenties engineers whose energy startup had fizzled and who had spent that spring walking into about 140 contractors' shops with a diary instead of laptops - 'curious kids was, like, our persona' - with exactly one working artifact: an automated appointment-reminder call for a San Francisco electrician. Two years on, Broccoli has raised $52 million - a previously unannounced $22 million Series A and a $30 million Series B nine months apart, both led by Khosla Ventures - at a reported $300 million valuation, and Flowers got an apartment in San Francisco and works for them.

The product is vertical and unglamorous: AI agents for plumbing, HVAC and electrical companies that answer inbound calls, book jobs straight into the scheduling software the contractor already runs, chase open estimates and renew service memberships. Broccoli claims more than 1,500 businesses as customers, including Ace Hardware Home Services, Mr. Electric and Any Hour Group, and in the past seven months has added roofing, pool care, garage doors, home improvement and exteriors. It is the trades-and-services end of the agent economy - the part that answers the phone.
