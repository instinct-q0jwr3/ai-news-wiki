The Tower Keeps Rising

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-09-28 · confidence: high

armin-ronacher agentic-systems small-specialist-models ai-coding-agents

Summary

Armin Ronacher reads the Tower of Babel as a story about technology, not hubris. The biblical text begins with a technology upgrade - burnt brick for stone, bitumen for mortar - and the people use it for a civilizational project. When God intervenes, what concerns him is not the bricks but the unity: one people with one language, from whom 'nothing will be restrained'. The confusion of tongues is, on this reading, the first coordination failure imposed to slow technological progress.

He connects it to the vibe-coding present: software that changes randomly and unexpectedly feels like working after Babel - the shared language that made the tower possible is gone. The essay's quiet point is that coordination, not raw capability, is what makes large technical projects work, and what AI-assisted development is currently dissolving.

Highlights

  • Babel as a technology story: new materials, one language, one project.
  • What God disrupts is unity, not engineering.
  • The first imposed coordination failure.
  • Vibe-coded software feels post-Babel: changes random, unexpected.
  • Coordination, not capability, makes big projects work.
