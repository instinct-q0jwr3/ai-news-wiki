Cognition's SWE-2.1

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-25 · updated: 2026-11-25 · confidence: high

Summary

Cognition releases SWE-2.1, describing pure-RL post-training on Kimi K3 and improvements in coding and long-horizon security work. It also announces a faster Cerebras-backed serving option and a Cyber variant with more permissive safeguards for defensive use, while describing training-time sandbox monitoring.

Vendor benchmark methods materially shape the figures: ExploitBench takes the union of flags over five attempts, whereas ExploitGym uses pass@1 with a six-hour timeout and mitigations disabled. Models use different preferred or internal harnesses, and timing is normalized rather than a direct wall-clock comparison. Neither an 80% ExploitBench score nor reported throughput is a universal real-world compromise or end-to-end speedup rate. No security workflow was run.

Highlights

  • Post-training is described as pure RL on Kimi K3
  • Long-horizon control is part of the training account
  • Cyber and standard safeguards differ
  • ExploitBench aggregates five attempts
  • ExploitGym reports pass@1 and separately judged success
  • Mitigations are disabled in that evaluation
  • Simulated time budgets differ from observed wall-clock delivery
  • No benchmark or vulnerability claim was independently reproduced
