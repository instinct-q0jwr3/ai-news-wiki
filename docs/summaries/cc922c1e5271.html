Analysis of 857 releases from nine Chinese AI labs from 2021 to September 2026: just 3.6% included safety results from the developer and only 1.1% did at launch

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-09 · updated: 2026-10-14 · confidence: high

Summary

SemiAnalysis reviewed 857 releases from nine Chinese AI labs between 2021 and September 2026 and found just 3.6% included safety results from the developer - and only 1.1% did so at launch. The analysis lands amid a heated September: Dario Amodei's essay urging labs to slow capability gains (backed within a day by Altman and Musk, mocked by Trump as something only China benefits from, dismissed by the Global Times as a 'Cold War playbook'), followed by the first US-China AI dialogue, Xi's state visit, the 'US-China Super Intelligence Dialogue' and the voluntary White House Accord on Super Intelligence.

Both sides of the US argument invoke China - for Trump, competition is a reason not to slow down; for Amodei, preserving the lead is what makes slowing possible - while, the authors note, nobody asked what China is actually doing. Their answer: Beijing's stated position is the most safety-forward of any major AI power outside Europe, even as its labs publish almost no safety evaluations.

Highlights

  • 857 releases from nine Chinese labs (2021-Sep 2026): 3.6% included developer safety results, 1.1% at launch.
  • Backdrop: Amodei's slow-down essay, Trump calling AI fears 'a HOAX', the Trump-Xi summit.
  • The US-China Super Intelligence Dialogue and White House Accord on Super Intelligence formalized in September.
  • Beijing's stated position is the most safety-forward outside Europe - but its labs barely publish safety evals.
  • The report aims to replace both sides' China rhetoric with what China actually does.
