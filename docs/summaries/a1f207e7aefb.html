AI in schools – The choice we keep making

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-20 · updated: 2026-09-20 · confidence: high

hacker-news

Summary

by Ivan Johnson, Head of School Every time a new technology arrives, it finds its way into education, and every time, we are handed the same choice: listen to the people trying to sell it to us, or listen to teachers. School districts wired classrooms for broadcast so a single “master teacher” could deliver a lesson to hundreds of classrooms at once.

It was pitched exactly the way every new education technology gets pitched: as a way to reach more kids, more consistently, for less money.

Highlights

  • In the 1950s and 60s, television was going to be the answer.
  • A kid watching a screen is not the same as a kid in a room with an adult who knows them.
  • In 2012, Stanford’s then president John Hennessy, gave a keynote called “The Coming Tsunami in Educational Technology.” He predicted that technology was about to hit higher education the way it had already hit newspapers and music, and…
  • He pointed to Khan Academy, and to the first wave of MOOCs (Massive Open Online Courses offered to an unlimited number of learners worldwide) like Coursera and edX as proof it was already underway.
  • He told the room to be the disruptor, not the disrupted.
  • “The tsunami is coming whether we like it or not,” Coursera founder Daphne Koller famously told The Atlantic .
  • What works in education hasn’t changed: one skilled, present teacher with a small group of kids.
  • Every teacher I knew worried about the same thing.
