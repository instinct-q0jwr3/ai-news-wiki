The Illusionist and the Conjurer

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-10-07 · confidence: high

Summary

Scott Werner divides makers into illusionists and conjurers. The illusionist is Penn & Teller: months rehearsing a single trick because the medium is live and there is exactly one shot - scarcity demands perfection. The conjurer is the sports photographer rattling off 3,000 frames in a night and keeping twelve: summon abundance, then choose. The finding of the thing is overflow; only the last mile - crop, contrast, color - is illusionist devotion.

Photography itself made this jump within a generation: when every frame cost money, everyone shot like an illusionist; when digital made photos free, mourners declared the craft dead while a teenager with an iPhone out-shot professionals by taking 400 frames on the walk to school. His real subject is what grew in the new soil: Flickr's communities, Google Photos' search, Instagram's mass curation, Lightroom's hobbyist darkrooms - entirely new ways of relating to a medium, not just the old ways done faster. When the scarcity constraint lifts, people do new things entirely - and that, he says, is the question AI now poses, teeing up his own experiments (Monkey's Paw, his prompt-driven web framework) as an early answer.

Highlights

  • Illusionist vs conjurer: Penn & Teller's years-per-trick perfection vs the sports photographer's 3,000 frames for 12 keepers
  • Scarcity media demand rehearsal; abundance media reward generating a pile and curating it
  • Photography went from scarcity (36 exposures, each an investment) to abundance within a generation
  • 'The craft is dying' missed the teenager with an iPhone accidentally out-shooting professionals
  • Abundance grew new species: Flickr communities, searchable archives, Instagram curation, consumer darkrooms
  • The pattern: when the constraint lifts, people don't do the old thing faster - they do new things entirely
  • Werner's question: what are the 'new things' for AI? His Monkey's Paw framework is his probe
