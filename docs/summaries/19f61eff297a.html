Q&A with NYC Mayor Zohran Mamdani on new municipal AI policy measures, like an AI ban for some students, support for a one-year data center pause, and more

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-04 · updated: 2026-11-11 · confidence: high

Summary

WIRED's Katie Drummond interviews New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani a year after their first conversation, ranging over his first year, his endorsement of Gov. Kathy Hochul, and AI. On AI he says the housing bubble and the state of AI in the economy share 'far too many similarities', that he worries it will pop, and that he does not trust the Sam Altmans of the world.

His municipal agenda has a few concrete parts. He wants one of the most comprehensive AI moratoriums in any American school system, banning generative AI for students from pre-K (2K) through eighth grade, on the grounds that students need room to try and fail and should not be 'guinea pigs' for tech profit motives. A city consumer-protection rule bars realtors from using AI-generated apartment images, and another limits chatbot 'doom loops'. He also frames AI's impact as emotional as well as economic, citing job meaning, and points to AI errors with serious consequences, saying he sees a federal abdication.

Much of the interview covers other politics; the AI section is the relevant core. His claims about AI incidents are his own characterization.

Highlights

  • Proposes banning generative AI for students from 2K through eighth grade
  • New city rules: no AI-generated apartment images from realtors; limits on chatbot doom loops
  • Frames AI as an emotional and job-meaning issue and criticizes federal inaction; incident claims are his own
