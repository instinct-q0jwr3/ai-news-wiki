Ahead of US midterms, ChatGPT draws images "depicting election interference or that may discourage voting" in a cartoon style, ignoring photorealism requests

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-27 · updated: 2026-10-31 · confidence: high

Summary

OpenAI is tightening ChatGPT's image rules ahead of the November 3 US midterms: ask it for an image 'depicting election interference or that may discourage voting' and it will comply - but only in an illustrated or cartoon style. Requests to make such images photorealistic are to be ignored, per OpenAI's October 20 update.

The policy targets the moment when AI deepfakes became practically indistinguishable from real photos, just as voters hunt for reliable information on candidates and polling sites. CNET's tests show the line in practice: the chatbot will produce the content, but strips the realism that would make it a weapon. Tech issues, including AI and data-center concerns, are central in many midterm races.
