Breaking news: U.S. banks are taking hits from AI agents

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-22 · updated: 2026-10-30 · confidence: high

Summary

Gary Marcus rounds up evidence that US banks and financial infrastructure are starting to take real hits from AI-enabled attacks. Generative AI, cheap and improving, is a near-perfect tool for attackers: it produces plausible phishing text and deepfaked video at virtually zero cost, and one cybercrew has already bilked a Hong Kong bank out of $25 million. LLMs also remain notoriously susceptible to jailbreaks and prompt-injection attacks, for which no known solution exists.

Marcus adds two structural worries: AI-generated code is being deployed by people who don't fully understand it, and autogenerated code has already introduced new security holes in some cases; and the current US administration is deregulating and cutting the federal workforce - including, he warns, the cybersecurity capacity meant to defend exactly this infrastructure. His question: who knows what piece of infrastructure might be attacked next.

Highlights

  • Marcus: US banks are beginning to take hits from AI-enabled attacks
  • Generative AI enables cheap, plausible phishing and deepfakes at near-zero cost; a Hong Kong bank lost $25M to one crew
  • Jailbreaks and prompt injection remain unsolved, leaving LLM-integrated systems exposed
  • AI-generated code deployed without full understanding has introduced new security holes
  • Simultaneous deregulation and federal cuts erode the cybersecurity capacity meant to defend infrastructure
