Artificial Analysis v5 and the benchmarker's dilemma

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-29 · updated: 2026-10-30 · confidence: high

Summary

Interconnects uses Artificial Analysis' Intelligence Index v5 update to examine the benchmarker's dilemma: making scores go up is expensive but increasingly a solved playbook, while knowing what is worth measuring at the frontier is genuinely hard. The v5 index spreads weight evenly across four pillars - knowledge work (AA-Briefcase, AutomationBench-AA, GDP.pdf), coding (FrontierCode 2.0, Terminal-Bench 4.0), science (Terminal-Bench-Science, CritPt v2 hard) and general capability (AA-Omniscience, HLE-Diamond) - in pursuit of a single number that matches vibe checks today without going stale in three months.

The argument cuts both ways. A single aggregate number is what makes a benchmark discussable and comparable, but every aggregation scheme embeds contestable trade-offs, and with trillions of dollars at stake the referee effectively dictates winners and losers - and steers what labs optimize for. The piece credits Artificial Analysis as the most prominent independent index, with Epoch AI's Capabilities Index a distant second, calls all of them simultaneously useful and massively flawed, and predicts the era of singular intelligence indices should - slowly - be phased out.

Highlights

  • AA v5 weights four pillars at 25% each: knowledge work, coding, science, general
  • Hill-climbing a benchmark is a known playbook; knowing what to measure is the hard part
  • Single numbers enable comparison but embed contested aggregation trade-offs
  • Independent indices now steer a trillion-dollar market and the labs' optimization targets
  • Artificial Analysis leads; Epoch AI's ECI a distant second
  • Verdict: all current indices are useful and massively flawed; singular-index era should end
