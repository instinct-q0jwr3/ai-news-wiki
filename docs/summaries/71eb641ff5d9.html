Ohio voters approved data center bans or restrictions in at least 14 municipalities across 21 ballot measures, adding to 125 local moratoriums in the state

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-04 · updated: 2026-11-14 · confidence: high

Summary

Ohio voters rejected AI infrastructure almost everywhere it was on the ballot, with at least 14 municipalities passing bans or restrictions on data centers across 21 ballot measures - more than half of the 41 data-center measures nationwide. The state already has 125 local moratoriums and more than 200 operational data centers, and companies plan up to $40 billion in AI infrastructure there by 2030.

The results ran the same way across the state. Sunbury passed a charter amendment banning data centers of 25 MW or more, aimed at a possible Amazon project. Pataskala approved a large-data-center ban. Trenton's passed 80 to 20, aimed at an 18-building AWS campus (a four-building one under construction likely isn't covered). Wilmington rejected four rezonings by about 80%. Ashville voted to block a campus already under construction. Perry approved financial-guarantee and noise requirements for tax-break seekers. Most measures came from 13 municipalities banning or heavily restricting 'high impact' sites.

Polling matches: Gallup found about 70% of Americans oppose local data centers, and an Ohio poll found 55% opposed versus 22% in support, with 65% opposed to tax breaks. A professor quoted by Fortune says Ohio is becoming the prototype for how voters respond to AI buildout.

Highlights
