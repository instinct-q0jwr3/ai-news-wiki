Distributing LLM inference in DwarfStar

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-10-08 · confidence: high

Summary

antirez surveys the economics of local LLM inference and what they mean for his DwarfStar engine. High-end NVIDIA setups are out of reach for most, and the RAM shortage makes a hypothetical M5 Ultra Mac Studio unlikely. A Mac Studio M3 Ultra 512GB runs DeepSeek v4 PRO - even at 2-bit quantization - at 150 t/s prefill and about 10-13 t/s decoding for roughly 12k total, while today's best deal is arguably a laptop: an M5 Max 128GB MacBook runs DeepSeek v4 Flash and Mimo V2.5 at about 500 t/s prefill and 35-40 t/s decoding for 6-7k.

That hardware reality makes distributed inference interesting: what can two or four Macs do together? He walks through the classic options - splitting layers across machines (simple activations-only traffic, and micro-batching can even speed up prefill), splitting MoE experts across machines over Apple RDMA (activations are tiny; suits models with large routed experts), and tensor parallelism, which he rules out without NVLink-class interconnects. His preferred third way is newer: run two different open-weight models shared-nothing on separate machines and ensemble them, picking the lower-perplexity continuation or combining logits - an implicit two-expert mixture that recent papers suggest genuinely beats either model alone.

Highlights

  • Mac Studio M3 Ultra 512GB: DeepSeek v4 PRO at ~150 t/s prefill, 10-13 t/s decode, ~12k total
  • Even 2-bit quantized, DeepSeek v4 PRO 'resists very well' - it wrote a C compiler
  • RAM shortage makes an M5 Ultra unlikely; an M5 Max 128GB laptop is the current best deal
  • M5 Max: DeepSeek v4 Flash / Mimo V2.5 at ~500 t/s prefill, 35-40 t/s decode, for 6-7k
  • Distribution option 1: split layers across machines; activations-only traffic, micro-batched prefill
  • Option 2: split MoE experts over Apple RDMA; tiny activations, suits big routed experts
  • Tensor parallelism ruled out - interconnects are far too slow
  • Option 3: two different models on two machines, ensembled by perplexity or logits
