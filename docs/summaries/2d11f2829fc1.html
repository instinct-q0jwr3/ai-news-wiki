The problem is not AI code, but not knowing about system architecture or intent

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-28 · updated: 2026-10-04 · confidence: high

Summary

Simon Späti argues the real danger of AI-generated codebases is not code quality but organizational amnesia: teams that no longer understand their own system architecture or the intent behind past decisions. He anchors the essay in a striking anonymous account from an engineer at a large company where specs, tests, PRDs, tickets, and reports are all produced by Claude Code, engineers work 12-13 hour days 'just to press enter', and management treats code output as a solved bottleneck while wondering why delivery is still slow. Späti's counterpoint is that AI can lift a below-average codebase to average, but only people who understand the system can tell whether what ships is right.

Highlights

  • The core problem is teams not knowing their system architecture or intent, not AI code quality
  • An anonymous engineer describes a big company where everything from specs to tickets is made by Claude Code
  • Engineers there work 12-13 hour days 'just to press enter' while nobody reads or reviews anything
  • Management reasons that since writing code is no longer the bottleneck, slowness must have some other cause
  • Späti's observation: AI writes average code, so it can raise a below-average codebase to average
  • Data teams adapt better because they always had to understand the product and business from day one
