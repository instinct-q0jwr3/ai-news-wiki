So Far, AI Pessimism Is Observationally Equivalent to AI Optimism

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-14 · updated: 2026-11-14 · confidence: high

Summary

Bryan Caplan argues that people predicting both beneficial and harmful AI futures anticipated capability gains, so those gains alone do not settle their disagreement. He frames the dispute as one about prior beliefs, historical analogies and the weight placed on harms relative to expected benefits.

The essay argues for tolerating substantial downsides, a normative stance rather than a measured risk-benefit result. Its declaration that AGI has arrived uses the author’s definition; neither that claim nor the proposed analogy to older technologies is established by the essay as a consensus finding.

Highlights

  • Capability forecasts differ from welfare forecasts
  • Author challenges one-sided claims of vindication
  • Historical analogies drive the argument
  • Risk tolerance is a value judgment
  • AGI terminology is the author’s own framing
