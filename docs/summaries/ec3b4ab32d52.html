On AI writing

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-20 · updated: 2026-10-21 · confidence: high

Summary

An essay on what AI writing costs the reader. The author, whose bachelor thesis showed even simple word-statistics can identify an author, argues AI averages style away, so the person behind the text disappears and with them the context that helps a reader interpret it. It also loses precision: to stitch text together, models make assumptions and choose ambiguous wording, which the writer, holding a clear idea in mind, cannot easily spot. Writing is also how we challenge our own ideas, and skipping that step removes the chance to catch inconsistencies. His measurable claim: he reads AI-generated text at least a third slower, and model verbosity multiplies total reading time, quickly evaporating whatever time the writer saved once more than a few readers are involved.

Highlights

  • AI averages away authorial style, and with it context
  • Models choose ambiguous, less precise language to avoid logical errors
  • Writing is thinking: skipping the draft skips the self-critique
  • Author reads AI text at least a third slower at equal length
  • Verbosity compounds: writer's time savings evaporate across a few readers
  • Grounded in a thesis showing simple statistics can identify authors
