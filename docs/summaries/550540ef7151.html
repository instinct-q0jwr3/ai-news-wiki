Liability, regulation, and AI’s new false dichotomy

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

marcus-on-ai ai-policy-regulation

Summary

Gary Marcus opens his post by agreeing with Senator Josh Hawley: AI companies should be liable for the damage they cause, a position he says he took in his book Taming Silicon Valley. He endorses Hawley's jab at AI CEOs who 'say the end of the world is near' yet want an antitrust exemption - 'How about instead we make them liable for whatever bad stuff their bots do.'

The post reproduces a hearing exchange where Hawley, skeptical that a new agency would avoid capture ('they usually get controlled by the people who they're supposed to be watching'), proposes instead a federal private right of action: open the courthouse doors, let anyone harmed - by medical or election misinformation, say - sue, including class actions. Altman answers that existing laws already allow suits and clearer consumer-protection laws would be good. Marcus's reply: today's laws predate AI and leave gaps - copyright, wholesale misinformation at billion-piece scale, and even whether Section 230 applies is 'not solid.' Litigation alone, he tells Hawley, can take a decade, though he does not want it off the table. Hawley's counter: the threat of litigation is itself a powerful tool - 'how would IBM like to be sued for a hundred billion dollars?'

Highlights

  • His agency critique: regulators get captured by the interests they watch
