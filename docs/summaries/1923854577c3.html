Pluralistic: Adversarial interoperability is a team sport (21 Oct 2026)

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-21 · updated: 2026-10-25 · confidence: high

Summary

Cory Doctorow's Pluralistic for 21 October argues adversarial interoperability is a team sport. Digital technology should be hard to enshittify because every computer is a Turing-complete universal machine: when a boss removes a valued feature (Sonos bricking speaker functionality) or adds a hated one (Chamberlain forcing seven ads before your garage door opens), a programmer can write an interoperable program that restores the good or blocks the bad, distribute it costlessly, and get paid for disenshittification. In a state of nature, that threat alone disciplines companies. His point in this edition: vibe-coded individual jailbreaks are fun but do not solve the structural problem; the counterpower has to be organized.

Highlights

  • Adversarial interoperability is a team sport, not a solo jailbreak
  • Universal computers make enshittification technically reversible
  • Examples: Sonos feature removal, Chamberlain's seven garage-door ads
  • Disenshittification tools can be distributed costlessly and sold
  • Vibe-coded jailbreaks: fun, but structurally insufficient
