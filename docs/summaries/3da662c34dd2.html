Don't say 'Europe Must Invest in XYZ'

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

bert-hubert

Summary

Bert Hubert opens with Christopher Hitchens ('Letters to a Young Contrarian'): be suspicious of anyone who says 'we' or 'us' without your permission - a surreptitious conscription into interests and identity that were never agreed. Always ask who this 'we' is.

His target: 'Europe must invest more in AI'. The sentence is a red flag on two levels. First, 'Europe' is a land mass, maybe a continent, possibly a state of mind - it doesn't 'do' anything; at best the EU or its member states act. Second, the sentence smuggles in the conclusion that investment is the collective's job and priority without ever arguing it. Sloppy 'we'-thinking, he argues, is how bad policy gets a mandate it never earned.

Highlights

  • Hubert's target: the phrase 'Europe must invest more in AI'
  • Hitchens framing: unauthorized 'we' is surreptitious conscription
  • First flaw: 'Europe' is a land mass/state of mind - it doesn't act
  • Second flaw: the sentence smuggles in its conclusion without argument
  • His rule: always ask who this 'we' is before accepting 'our' priorities
