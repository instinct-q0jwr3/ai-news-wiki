Parents pay $75k for Alpha's AI school. Half of its students missed math target

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-15 · updated: 2026-11-21 · confidence: high

Summary

Fortune reports that Alpha School’s internal results fall short of its advertised goal of twice-as-fast learning for many pupils. Citing a New York Times review, it says 42% met that target in both math and reading, while about half of K-8 pupils at private campuses missed the math target.

These are results against the school’s own acceleration benchmark, not a finding that half failed ordinary mathematics or that AI tutoring necessarily caused worse learning. The report also describes workload concerns and expansion into public schools, where student selection and incentives differ. The underlying records and comparison design were not independently examined here.

Highlights

  • Report distinguishes math from combined targets
  • Tuition reaches $75,000 at some campuses
  • Missing a growth goal is not failing a subject
  • Workload concerns are attributed to reports
  • Public-school rollout changes the testing context
