What can I realistically do with local AI on a Mac mini M5 Pro (48GB)?

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-08 · updated: 2026-11-10 · confidence: high

Summary

A Lobsters user asks what is realistic to run locally on a Mac mini with an M5 Pro and 48GB unified memory, asking for real tokens-per-second numbers and 'waste of time' stories. Replies say Qwen3.8-27B is the best local coding model but slow, short on long-context headroom, and unsuited to parallel sessions; it suits background or overnight chores or an advisor role. Smaller models are called too weak for serious coding, 4-bit quants are the sweet spot, and gemma4-e4b works as a lossy wikipedia or smarter regex.

One commenter says even 96GB is barely usable for real coding and that DeepSeek 4.1 Flash is smarter, four times faster and avoids out-of-memory issues. Another runs Qwen3.6-27B at 6-bit on MLX-LM and finds it worse than Claude but fine for routine tasks and bug scans if the model must verify bugs.

Highlights

  • 27B is the practical local coding ceiling on 48GB, and it is slow.
  • 4-bit quants are the best size-quality tradeoff.
  • Small models suit lossy search and summarization, not edits.
  • A hosted model like DeepSeek Flash may beat local hardware on speed and quality.
  • Low-power models can find bugs if forced to verify them.
