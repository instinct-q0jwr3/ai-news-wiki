Can AI self-improvement overcome diminishing returns?

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-28 · updated: 2026-10-03 · confidence: high

Summary

Ramez Naam asks whether recursive AI self-improvement would actually produce a runaway intelligence explosion, assembling the freshest primary evidence from Anthropic's own system cards. The key data points: Anthropic's survey of technical staff finds a wide distribution of productivity uplift from Claude Mythos Preview with a geometric mean around 4x, and reaching 2x overall R&D progress through that channel would require uplift roughly an order of magnitude larger; Anthropic says internal AI use has maintained the current rate of progress but shows no clear signs of dramatic acceleration; Opus 5.5's card admits the model is weaker on open-ended research, preferring incremental ideas and deferring to published literature; and METR expects full automation of AI R&D to require large improvements in foresight, feedback loops, and researcher 'taste'. Eli Lifland's counterpoint: even if Anthropic is right that dangerous self-improvement has not arrived, alarm bells should be ringing because our processes are not ready.

Highlights

  • Anthropic staff survey: geometric-mean productivity uplift from Mythos Preview is ~4x, with a wide distribution
  • Anthropic estimates 2x overall R&D progress via this channel needs ~10x more uplift than observed
  • Fable 5.1/Mythos 5.1 card: AI use maintains the current progress rate, with no clear signs of dramatic acceleration
  • Opus 5.5 card: the model prefers incremental ideas and struggles with novel open-ended research
  • METR: full AI-R&D automation needs big gains in foresight, prediction, and researcher 'taste'
  • Eli Lifland: alarm bells should be going off - our processes are not ready for an intelligence explosion
