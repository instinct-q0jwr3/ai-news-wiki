Pluralistic: Israeli ad-tech company was a delivery system for "mercenary spyware" (17 Oct 2026)

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-17 · updated: 2026-10-29 · confidence: high

Summary

Cory Doctorow writes up Citizen Lab's 'Exposing AdFection' report, which shows that the largest ad-tech platforms - Google and Facebook among them - were hijacked by an Israeli company, Discover-Tech, that masqueraded as an ad-tech firm while acting as a delivery system for mercenary spyware. The ad system let it locate targeted individuals and serve zero-click malware that compromised devices with no action from the victim.

The mechanism is structural, not a one-off bug: ad platforms hold detailed surveillance dossiers with persistent per-user identifiers, and they let anyone posing as an advertiser serve code - which is all an ad is - to people selected by those identifiers. Doctorow's conclusion: blocking every programmatic ad is now basic self-defense, and Doc Searls's 'largest consumer boycott in history' framing of ad-blocking looks, if anything, understated.

Highlights

  • Citizen Lab report 'Exposing AdFection' exposes spyware delivery via ad networks
  • Israeli firm Discover-Tech posed as ad-tech while selling to governments and corporations
  • Targeted individuals located through ad identifiers, then served zero-click malware
  • Google, Facebook and other major platforms were used as the delivery pipe
  • Structural flaw: ads are code, targetable by persistent surveillance identifiers
  • Doctorow: blocking all programmatic ads is now basic self-defense
