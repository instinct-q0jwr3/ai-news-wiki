Reasons to dislike AI coding

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-07 · updated: 2026-10-07 · confidence: high

Summary

This essay separates several objections to AI coding instead of treating them as one technical argument. It examines software as art, anxiety about automation, loss of human relationships at work, the idea that engineering rules uniquely justify human authorship, and objections to the companies supplying the models.

The author argues that customers usually pay for useful software rather than the act of typing, and that programmers already have a long history of adapting code empirically. But the essay gives the dehumanization objection a different explanation: drawing on Marx, it links alienation to how production is organized, not simply to a particular tool. Its target is the assumptions behind objections, not a measured claim that AI code is reliable or cheaper.

Highlights

  • Objections about art, jobs and corporate power are distinct.
  • Typing code and delivering valuable software are different activities.
  • Empirical copying and adaptation predate AI coding.
  • Alienation is framed as a production-system problem.
  • Opposing dominant providers need not mean opposing every model.
  • The essay offers argument rather than productivity measurements.
