The Most Important Charts in AI are Wrong

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-23 · updated: 2026-11-28 · confidence: high

Summary

Ramez Naam argues that benchmark task horizons and extrapolations can greatly overstate performance on messy internal research work. He compares METR/ECI-derived estimates and AI Futures scenarios with disclosed OpenAI and Google task data, distinguishing 50% from 80% success. His updated analysis accepts exponential growth as a working fit while finding weaker evidence and slower improvement at higher reliability.

The large ratios depend on cross-dataset comparisons, human-time definitions, selected model matches and adjustments to internal-task estimates. They are his analysis, not direct like-for-like benchmark measurements. He acknowledges updated forecasts and METR's efforts to improve task realism. The article challenges transfer from a curve to a workplace, not the existence of all capability progress, and does not establish that rapid R&D acceleration is impossible.

Highlights

  • The critique concerns benchmark-to-workplace transfer
  • It compares different datasets and human-time baselines
  • 50% and 80% success lead to different reliability conclusions
  • Updated analysis treats exponential growth as plausible, not disproved
  • Headline ratios rely on adjustments and matching choices
  • It does not establish that all rapid-acceleration scenarios are impossible
