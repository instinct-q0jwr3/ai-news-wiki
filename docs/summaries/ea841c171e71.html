Review of 173 studies: AI's dual effect on how students think

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-22 · updated: 2026-09-25 · confidence: high

hacker-news google

Summary

Norwegian researchers reviewed 173 international studies on how generative AI affects students' ability to think critically and solve problems, and found a clear dual effect: the same technology can strengthen or weaken learning depending on how it is used. AI tools support important skills when they are used to challenge, ask questions, compare viewpoints and build one's own reasoning; used as a shortcut to a ready-made answer, they raise the risk of overdependence, more superficial thinking and weaker independent problem solving.

The backdrop is a rapid shift in young people's habits. The Norwegian Media Authority's survey of 9 to 18-year-olds shows Google searches falling from 72% in 2024 to 47% in 2026, while 39% of Norwegian 11 to 12-year-olds already use AI. Lead author Hilde Storrøsæter, a PhD research fellow at NTNU's Department of Teacher Education, says the most important finding is that AI in itself makes students neither better nor worse at thinking; the review, conducted with colleagues from Czech universities, also found we know very little about AI's impact on younger school children.

Highlights

  • Literature review of 173 international studies on generative AI in school and education.
  • Lead author Hilde Storrøsæter, PhD research fellow at NTNU's Department of Teacher Education, with Czech university colleagues.
  • Core finding: a dual effect, the same technology can have constructive and destructive effects on learning.
  • Constructive use: challenging, asking questions, comparing viewpoints, building one's own line of reasoning.
  • Destructive use: AI as a shortcut to ready-made answers breeds overdependence and superficial thinking.
  • Google searches among Norwegian 9 to 18-year-olds fell from 72% in 2024 to 47% in 2026.
  • 39% of Norwegian 11 to 12-year-olds use AI.
  • The review found very little is known about AI's impact on younger school children.
