Pluralistic: Priceful (28 Sep 2026)

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-28 · updated: 2026-10-05 · confidence: high

Summary

Cory Doctorow defends the term 'IP' against critics who call it deceptively imprecise and rhetorically dishonest, arguing the breadth of the category is precisely the point. Copyright and trademark, his central contrast, rest on entirely different policy foundations: copyright is a constitutional incentive for investment in creative works (one of only two constitutional clauses that states its own rationale), time-limited and balanced by fair use; trademark is a consumer-protection tool that deputizes companies to sue deceivers customers would never bother suing themselves. The essay develops into a broader argument that lumping these regimes together is not a bug but a feature for analyzing how companies convert legal entitlements into pricing power.

Highlights

  • Digital-rights critics call 'IP' deceptively imprecise and dishonest; Doctorow thinks both objections miss the point
  • Copyright is designed to incentivize investment in creative works through limited, time-bounded rights
  • The copyright clause is one of only two constitutional clauses that includes its own rationale
  • Trademark is consumer protection: it deputizes companies to sue deceivers that individual customers never would
  • Fair use is a broad, highly situational set of rights to use works even over the rightsholder's objection
  • The 'big three' of IP sit beside an 'arcane bestiary' of anticircumvention, trade secrecy, and other doctrines
