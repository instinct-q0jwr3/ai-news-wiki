Meta says if its AI makes a mistake, it will legally be the customer's fault

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-24 · updated: 2026-10-27 · confidence: high

Summary

Meta's updated Commercial Terms, due to take effect on October 30, transfer legal responsibility for mistakes made by its AI features to enterprise customers, including changes made by features Meta switches on itself. The new 'AI Features' section states that customers are solely responsible for all risks from AI outputs, a shift from the current terms where liability attached only when advertisers chose to use the AI tools. Analysts quoted by CIO.com call the move an inversion of accountability, since only Meta controls the models.

The same update bundles two other contested changes. It consolidates crawling language that appears to let Meta scrape domains associated with a business account, though a Meta spokesperson told CIO.com the company is clarifying that this applies only to ads and business products and does not override robots.txt. It also broadens the content license Meta takes over material posted 'in connection with' its products, and a July change made that license perpetual, surviving termination. Critics note the license grows whenever Meta revises its product list, and that a recent tweak removing explicit training-on-enterprise-data language amounts to legal sophistry rather than a real concession.

Highlights

  • From October 30, Meta's Commercial Terms make enterprise customers solely responsible for mistakes made by Meta's AI features.
  • The new 'AI Features' section covers features Meta enables itself, not only tools advertisers deliberately switched on.
  • Analysts argue the clause inverts accountability: Meta builds and controls the models but assigns all output risk to the customer.
  • Meta said it is clarifying web-scraping language after consultants flagged it; it claims robots.txt preferences are unaffected.
  • The content license covers anything posted 'in connection with' Meta products and, since a July change, survives contract termination.
  • Enterprise CISOs warn of customer flight risk if platforms impose such terms around content usage and AI liability.
