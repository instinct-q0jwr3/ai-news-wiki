Anthropic IPO filing: $1.7B quarterly payroll, about $107K per employee monthly

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-03 · updated: 2026-11-13 · confidence: high

Summary

The amended S-1 keeps giving: Anthropic's personnel expenses averaged about $107,300 per employee per month in Q3 - $1.7 billion across July-September for 5,300 staff, or $322,000 per head per quarter. The majority, $1.1 billion for the quarter, is stock-based compensation - an accounting expense for share value employees vest into, not cash out the door. Cash salaries, bonuses, benefits and payroll taxes make up the rest.

Two framings matter more than the headline. First, the wage bill is shrinking as a share of the business: personnel was more than 2x revenue in 2023, but only 15% of the $33.2 billion nine-month revenue in 2026. Second, the IPO itself triggers the cliff: Anthropic says that had it listed on September 30, it would have booked an $8.2 billion stock-comp charge that day for RSUs whose conditions include a listing event - more than the entire nine-month personnel expense - with another $24.1 billion to recognize over a weighted-average two years.

So the $107K/month number is less 'AI salaries are insane' than 'the IPO's real cost to the P&L is already written into the cap table.' Combined with yesterday's Business Insider report that internal AI usage at retail prices exceeds the payroll, the S-1's labor story is really about equity overhang, not cash burn.

Highlights

  • Anthropic's Q3 personnel expense: $1.7B across 5,300 employees - ~$107K per employee per month, with $1.1B of it stock-based comp
  • Personnel costs were 2x revenue in 2023 but only 15% of revenue in the first nine months of 2026
  • IPO day itself would trigger an $8.2B one-time stock-comp charge, with $24.1B more to recognize over ~2 years
  • The labor story is equity overhang, not cash burn - and internal AI spend already exceeds payroll
