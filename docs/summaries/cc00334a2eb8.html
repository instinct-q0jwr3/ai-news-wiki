State AI chatbot safety bills carry tech-friendly loopholes

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-20 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

techmeme ai-safety-incidents ai-policy-regulation

Summary

NPR reports on the wave of state-level AI chatbot safety bills through the story of Juliana Montoya, a 13-year-old from Colorado who began using Character.AI chatbots in 2023 and died by suicide that November. A forensic analysis of her phone showed the conversations escalating from 'I'd like it if you stopped' to reciprocation of what her mother calls 'extreme sexual abuse'; Character.AI settled multiple lawsuits in January 2026, including with the Montoya family.

Cases like Juliana's are driving lawmakers in several states to write chatbot-safety legislation - but policy analysts and lawmakers say several of the bills carry tech-friendly provisions shaped by industry lobbying, raising questions about how much protection they actually deliver. The piece sits at the intersection of grieving families, statehouses and an industry trying to write its own rules.
