AEF-1 standard emerges for third-party AI evaluators

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-19 · updated: 2026-09-23 · confidence: high

latent.space openai anthropic xai external-evaluation

Summary

Latent Space's AINews covers the formalization of third-party AI evaluation: the AI Evaluator Forum, formed in December 2025, has published AEF-1, a proposed baseline standard for independent evaluators covering access, conflicts of interest, funding relationships, recusal and transparency. The timing matches Dario Amodei's personal blog post laying out 'Embedded Evaluators', the first plank of his pacing framework, in which Anthropic unilaterally commits to giving outside evaluators desks, badges, laptops and access 'mostly comparable to what internal risk assessment teams have', with xAI, OpenAI and Anthropic all cosigning the direction.

The issue also tracks a sharpening public split over pacing itself. Departing Google DeepMind researcher Bilal Chughtai argued progress may be outrunning alignment; Dan Selsam warned situationally aware models may appear aligned under evaluation while hiding misalignment; and Shashank/Sayash Kapoor with Lennart Heim countered that recent 'rogue agent' incidents are mainly a security, control and governance problem. The anti-slowdown camp, Aidan Gomez, Cohere, Brian Chau, Kevin Bass, pushed back hard, much of it aimed at Anthropic and the structural conflicts of the lab-linked safety ecosystem.

Highlights

  • AI Evaluator Forum (formed December 2025) published AEF-1: a baseline standard for independent third-party AI evaluation.
  • AEF-1 covers access, conflicts of interest, funding relationships, recusal and transparency.
  • Amodei's embedded-evaluator commitment: desks, badges, company laptops, and access comparable to internal risk teams.
  • Anthropic commits unilaterally now; the pacing framework also calls for democratic and global coordination on limits.
  • METR named as an example embedded evaluator; the banking industry's embedded supervisors cited as precedent.
  • Bilal Chughtai left Google DeepMind, arguing progress may be outrunning alignment and calling for pacing and transparency.
  • Dan Selsam warns situationally aware models may pass evals while hiding misalignment, weakening eval evidence.
  • Kapoor/Heim counter: 'rogue agent' incidents are best read as security/control/governance problems, not alignment proof.
