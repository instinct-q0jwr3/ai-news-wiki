AI delivers 'zero financial payoff' to most companies, Apollo economist says

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-02 · confidence: high

Summary

Apollo Global Management's chief economist Torsten Slok went looking for AI in the numbers of what he calls the S&P 493 - the index minus Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia and Tesla - and reports finding nothing: profit margins and revenue per worker look flat, with no evidence AI is lifting productivity. His verdict is deliberately blunt: despite massive investment and wall-to-wall hype, the technology has delivered zero financial payoff to corporate America outside tech.

The stronger claim is that this is not merely a lag. Slok doubts corporate America can easily turn 'increasingly cheap intelligence into higher revenue growth and higher profit margins,' because in business, intelligence is mostly judgment - and AI, he writes, does not understand accountability, trust, culture, unwritten rules, ethical trade-offs or fairness, the things that shape reputational risk and ultimately drive revenue. Recent studies finding no significant productivity gains point the same way. It is the economist's version of the question CNN asked the same week, and it lands while default insurance on the hyperscalers' debt is at its most expensive since 2021.

Highlights

  • Apollo's Torsten Slok: 'zero financial payoff' from AI outside the tech sector
  • Evidence: flat margins and revenue per worker across the S&P 493 (S&P 500 minus the big seven)
  • Not just lag, he argues: cheap intelligence does not convert easily into revenue or margin
  • 'AI may be a great calculator, but in business, intelligence is mostly judgment'
  • Accountability, trust, culture and fairness drive reputational risk - and AI understands none of them
  • Recent studies likewise find no significant productivity gains
  • Arrives as hyperscaler default insurance hits its priciest since 2021
