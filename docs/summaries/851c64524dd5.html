Nvidia-backed data centre firm scraps IPO as AI valuation concerns deepen

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-11 · updated: 2026-11-11 · confidence: high

Summary

Firmus, the Nvidia-backed Australian data-center company, has scrapped what would have been one of Australia's biggest-ever stock market listings - a debut valuing it above $30 billion - citing 'recent market volatility and prevailing market conditions.' One investment firm told the BBC it had already decided not to participate over valuation concerns. Firmus builds liquid-cooled 'AI factories' for clients including OpenAI and Meta, with backers including Nvidia, Blackstone and Jane Street; it will now raise privately instead.

The timing makes it a signal event: the IPO was pulled in the same news cycle as Anthropic's amended S-1 showing slowing growth and Oracle's punished $20 billion raise. Public-market investors are now discriminating inside the AI trade - rewarding revenue, punishing leveraged capacity - and a $30 billion listing for an infrastructure builder is exactly the kind of paper that no longer clears.

Watch the pattern rather than the company: if AI-infrastructure IPOs keep withdrawing while private rounds keep closing, the valuation reset happens in private markets first and quietly.
