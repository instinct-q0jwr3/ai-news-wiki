Open Source, SaaS, and the Silence After Unlimited Code Generation

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

works-on-my-machine

Summary

Scott Werner's parable for open source in the age of unlimited code generation is a seed library. For years the deal was: take seeds, grow tomatoes, bring some back. Then bulk seed generators got cheap, and the drop-off box filled with hundreds of perfectly labeled packets - half of which would not germinate, some mislabeled, some choking out everything around them, a few empty envelopes with convincing labels. The librarian spent a spring triaging the avalanche, then just locked the box.

The flood stopped - and so did everything else. The quiet gardeners with one envelope of weird, wonderful, sun-adapted seeds stopped coming too; the same tools that flooded the box let everyone grow whatever they wanted at home. 'The gardens have never been better,' the librarian says. 'I just can't see what's in them anymore.' Her answer, when Werner returns: a corkboard map of the neighborhood on butcher paper, asking people to pin where their garden is and what they changed - inspired by a woman in the clay-soil part of town who had spent three years crossing library seeds into something that thrived there and never thought to bring any back, while six other clay-soil houses still struggle. The gardens have never been better, but the gardeners don't know about each other. The thing she needs - a way to see all the gardens at once, what changed and where - does not exist yet. She put the map up anyway.
