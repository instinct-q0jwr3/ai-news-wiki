AI Tutoring with Khanmigo in a Two-Year School Experiment

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-06 · updated: 2026-10-09 · confidence: high

Summary

A two-year randomised trial in 18 Tennessee middle schools tested Khan Academy's Khanmigo, set to coach rather than give answers, in daily remedial maths sessions. Assignment raised achievement by about 1.3 national percentile ranks per term, 0.06 to 0.08 standard deviations over a year, similar to Khan Academy practice without AI. The authors point to low engagement: 96% tried it, but the median student messaged it on only a third of practice days and in 17% of sessions where they made a mistake, mostly with bare answers or suggested prompts. Getting students to use it is the constraint.

Highlights

  • Randomised trial, 18 Tennessee middle schools, two years.
  • Gain of 0.06-0.08 SD per year, like Khan practice without AI.
  • 96% tried Khanmigo; median use on a third of practice days.
  • Engagement, not access, is the bottleneck.
  • Basis: paper abstract.
