From Nodes to Stories, Fiction as a Tool for Thinking

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-10-06 · confidence: high

Summary

Scott Werner pairs his own AI-economics science fiction with Citrini Capital's viral '2028 Global Intelligence Crisis' - a fictional macro memo from June 2028 (S&P down 38%, unemployment 10.2%, mortgage market cracking) that models the displacement spiral when AI takes white-collar jobs. Same premise, he notes, very different place to stand: Citrini counts the jobs lost; Werner has been writing about the jobs created.

His method note is the point of the essay: fiction works as a tool for thinking because it lets you inhabit a scenario instead of just charting it. Where the macro memo extrapolates a feedback loop, stories force you to specify what people actually do all day on the other side of the explosion - which is where the blind spots in both optimism and pessimism show up.

Highlights

  • Citrini's fictional 2028 macro memo: S&P -38%, 10.2% unemployment.
  • Same starting premise, two stances: jobs lost vs jobs created.
  • Werner writes SF about what comes after the cost explosion.
  • Fiction as a thinking tool: inhabit the scenario, don't just model it.
  • Stories force specifics that spreadsheets can skip.
