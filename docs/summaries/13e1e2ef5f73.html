India plans to invest up to $25B in its nascent deep tech industry, as it looks to catch up with the US and China, after investing $11.6B over the past decade

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-29 · updated: 2026-10-07 · confidence: high

Summary

India is preparing to deploy up to $25 billion into deep tech start-ups - AI, semiconductors, advanced manufacturing, drones, and space tech - as it tries to cut reliance on foreign frontier technology amid US-China tensions. The government has committed $11 billion under the Research Development Infrastructure Fund, to be matched by VC and PE managers, with $3-4 billion more expected, per IVCA president Rajat Tandon; India invested $11.6B in deep tech over the whole last decade. Investors say US tariffs and export curbs make Washington look unreliable and actually help the local segment, a fear realized in June when Anthropic disabled access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for foreign nationals under a US export-control directive.

Highlights

  • India plans up to $25B for deep tech: AI, chips, advanced manufacturing, drones, space
  • $11B committed under the Research Development Infrastructure Fund, matched by VC/PE, plus $3-4B more
  • India invested only $11.6B in deep tech over the past decade
  • Motive: frontier tech is 'very critical' amid geopolitical tension; US export curbs make it unreliable
  • Anthropic disabled Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for foreign nationals in June under a US directive
  • Investors: US tariffs actually help the local deep-tech segment
