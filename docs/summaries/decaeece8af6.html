How AI is creating a lopsided US economy

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-03 · updated: 2026-11-04 · confidence: high

Summary

The ABC's data-visualization piece on America's lopsided economy starts at last month's White House AI Summit - the richest people on Earth clustered around President Trump, a table worth north of A$2.5 trillion - and asks what it means that one theme now dominates the entire US stock market. Tech accounts for about 44% of S&P 500 value; six of the eight biggest companies are tech even on a definition narrow enough to exclude Amazon and Tesla; Nvidia alone is worth about A$7.5 trillion, and over six months only Apple, Microsoft, Micron and AMD added more absolute value.

The concern the experts in the piece raise is not whether the AI companies are real businesses - Forager's Steve Johnson calls them some of the fastest-growing the world has seen - but what their dominance hides and concentrates. The boom's success has not been replicated across the economy; it may be masking weakness elsewhere, and a market this lopsided transmits any AI repricing into every retirement account in the country.

The piece is Australian, which gives it a useful outside framing: in the US, AI companies have become what banks and miners are to Australia's market - the index's center of gravity. The difference is that Australia's pillars pay dividends from existing cash flows, while the AI trade prices in a buildout whose returns are still projected.
