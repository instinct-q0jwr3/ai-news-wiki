How a wave of startups are betting on AI to make formal software verification practical and essential, including &Truth, which launches with a $70M seed

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-30 · updated: 2026-10-31 · confidence: high

Summary

WIRED chronicles formal verification's breakout moment. Stanford researcher Chuyue Sun's team used an AI tool to complete a verification project in under half a day for less than $500 - work that took three highly skilled experts five months manually - and demonstrated the approach on part of the code that encrypts Signal conversations. Results like that are waking up a field that for decades promised mathematically bug-free software but was too laborious for anyone but the most safety-critical shops to afford.

Now startups are racing to productize it. Sun joined &Truth, which launched publicly Thursday with $70 million in funding and chief science officer Ilya Sergey arguing that bridges do not collapse because people did the math first - code should be no different; the company aims to verify any programming language eventually. Rival Harmonic, cofounded by Robinhood CEO Vlad Tenev with $295 million raised, announced its math-focused agent Aristotle now verifies C and Verilog hardware designs, evaluating with chipmakers including Nvidia.
