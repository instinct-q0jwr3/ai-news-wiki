Pluralistic: The age of disinvention (25 Aug 2026)

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-10-01 · confidence: high

Summary

Cory Doctorow's thesis: we live in an age of disinvention, where incumbent industries don't just compete with threatening technologies - they work for decades to erase them. His canonical case is the VCR, one of the fastest-adopted technologies in history, which let people record shows, skip ads, build libraries and lend tapes. Studios and broadcasters hated it from the start and never stopped trying to kill it until they finally succeeded.

The VCR survived its first assault only because litigation reached the Supreme Court, whose 1984 Betamax decision held that a new technology does not violate copyright if it can sustain a substantial non-infringing use. Doctorow's point is that this protection is fragile and exceptional: given enough time and lobbying, incumbents can disinvent even wildly popular tools, and the pattern keeps repeating with newer technologies.

Highlights

  • Thesis: industries don't just fight disruptive tech - they disinvent it
  • The VCR was among the fastest-adopted technologies ever, and studios wanted it dead from day one
  • Recording, ad-skipping, libraries and lending threatened the broadcast model
  • The 1984 Betamax decision: substantial non-infringing use keeps a technology legal
  • The VCR's eventual disappearance was a decades-long kill, not natural obsolescence
  • Doctorow warns the disinvention pattern keeps repeating
