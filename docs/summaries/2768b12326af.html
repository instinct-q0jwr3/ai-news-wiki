I stopped drinking the AI Kool-Aid

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-20 · updated: 2026-09-23 · confidence: high

hacker-news

Summary

A developer's personal essay describes hitting his limit with AI boosterism at work: his non-engineer manager literally told him 'you need to drink the Kool-Aid' more than once, and the repetition turned a metaphor into a threat that planted seeds of fear. He is no skeptic by temperament: an early GitHub Copilot adopter who championed it for his team, he spent the year building his own agent harness and running throwaway experiments on old domains with AI as the labor source.

The turn came via a call with an exhausted human being, and the essay pivots on the difference between trusting models with a vibe-coded weekend site and trusting them with a revenue-generating production system. His objection is cultural as much as technical: 'drinking the Kool-Aid' names belief demanded under pressure, and he traces his own history with the literal product, a childhood collection of Kool-Aid points zeroed out when Kraft ended the rewards program, to explain why he is done with both.

Highlights

  • A manager (not an engineer) repeatedly told the author 'you need to drink the Kool-Aid' about AI.
  • The author was an early GitHub Copilot adopter and championed team access to it.
  • He built his own agent harness and ran AI-directed experiments on long-parked domains.
  • His trust line: throwaway weekend sites vs revenue-generating production systems.
  • The pivot: a call last week with a human being who was exhausted.
  • The essay's charge: 'Kool-Aid' talk turns a tool decision into a demanded belief, which reads as a threat.
