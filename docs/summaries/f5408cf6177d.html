No Signs of AI Payoff Outside the Tech Sector

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-24 · updated: 2026-10-25 · confidence: high

Summary

Apollo chief economist Torsten Slok argues that, despite massive investment and hype, AI has delivered zero measurable financial payoff to corporate America outside the tech sector. Revenue per worker and profit margins are rising for the Magnificent 7 but flat for the S&P 493, and productivity growth shows no AI boost. His bottom line: the AI story will be decided in the S&P 493, not the Mag 7, and so far there is no evidence of improvement outside tech.

Slok adds a behavioral wrinkle: with no bottom-line impact, mainstream companies are starting to treat token spending as a cost center that weighs on margins rather than a source of growth, and are pulling back even as VC-backed startups spend more on tokens. He closes with a structural argument for why cheap intelligence may not convert into profits: business intelligence is mostly judgment, which depends on context, accountability, trust, culture and unwritten rules - things AI does not understand but which drive reputational risk, revenue and margins.

Highlights

  • No signs of AI boosting economy-wide productivity growth (utilization-adjusted TFP).
  • Mainstream firms now view token spending as a margin-weighing cost center, not a growth driver, and are pulling back.
  • VC-backed companies keep increasing token spend, widening the gap with mainstream corporate America.

  • His conclusion: the AI story will be decided in the S&P 493, and there is no evidence of improvement there yet.
