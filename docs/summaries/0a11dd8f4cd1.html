Show HN: Carton Clock – A working pendulum clock made from empty drink cartons

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-15 · updated: 2026-11-16 · confidence: high

Summary

Niklas Roy describes building a working pendulum clock from drink cartons and simple household materials. A browser tool developed with Claude helped design and simulate the escapement, but physical prototypes exposed the limits of cardboard precision: a simpler, larger five-tooth wheel worked where the first design failed.

The project shows AI-assisted tooling inside an iterative mechanical build rather than a model independently producing a finished clock. Material friction, gearing and pendulum timing still required hands-on adjustment, and the completed clock runs a little over an hour when wound.

The five-tooth mechanism belongs to the proof of concept. The later hour-running clock uses a larger 15-tooth escape wheel, full hour and minute gearing, and another AI-assisted browser tool for gear stencils and assembly tools. It still runs fast: operating duration and timekeeping accuracy are separate results.

Highlights

  • Cartons supply much of the structure
  • Browser tool assists escapement design
  • First physical prototype failed
  • Simpler geometry improved feasibility
  • Physical testing remains central
