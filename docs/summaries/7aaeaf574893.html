AI People Are Extremely Rich

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-22 · updated: 2026-11-22 · confidence: high

Summary

Noah Smith uses Anthropic's reported stock-market debut to explore the social consequences of concentrated AI wealth. The essay cites a breakdown of founder and employee holdings at a hypothetical $2 trillion valuation, including an employee equity pool averaging $62 million across about 5,300 people. Smith argues that the speed and scale of those paper gains could change career expectations, elite competition and attitudes toward inequality.

Smith discloses that he bought Anthropic shares and remains bullish, so his account combines investment opinion with personal observations of Silicon Valley social life. The ownership figures are quoted estimates at a specified valuation, not verified employee payouts, and an average obscures differences in grants, vesting, taxes, dilution and liquidity. The essay's valuation descriptions are not fully consistent with one another; no live market or filing check was performed here. Its core contribution is an argument about incentives and status, not an audited wealth census or investment recommendation.

Highlights

  • Smith examines the social effects of rapid AI equity gains
  • The quoted employee figures assume a $2 trillion valuation
  • Estimated equity value is not realized after-tax wealth
  • Averages do not describe the distribution across employees
  • Smith discloses owning Anthropic shares and a bullish view
  • Personal anecdotes do not establish a population-wide wealth census
  • Market and ownership claims remain attributed and unverified here
