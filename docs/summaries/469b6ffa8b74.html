AI and hiring: bad for employees, bad for employers

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-22 · updated: 2026-11-24 · confidence: high

Summary

Bronte founder John Hempton describes how cheap AI-assisted applications have disrupted his firm's unusual policy of briefly interviewing almost every plausible applicant who sent a tailored CV. He reports roughly five times as many applications as before and says polished initial answers sometimes failed to carry over to longer in-person discussion. His concern is that cheap fluency destroys an inexpensive signal of curiosity and ability, forcing more aggressive screening that can exclude unconventional candidates.

The piece adds secondhand accounts from a consulting firm and Australian public-complaint handling, then asks for better ways to triage submissions. These are employer anecdotes and reported conversations, not a representative hiring study or a proven diagnosis of why particular candidates performed differently. Hempton suspects AI assistance in some interviews but does not demonstrate it. His suggested handwritten cover note is a tentative response, not a tested solution, and his broad claims about entry-level work go beyond the evidence of this one firm.

Highlights

  • Hempton reports a fivefold application increase at his own firm
  • The old process used short interviews to screen unconventional candidates
  • He suspects AI-assisted phone answers but does not prove individual use
  • The concern is loss of fluency as a cheap selection signal
  • Consulting and public-complaint examples are secondhand accounts
  • No representative labor-market estimate or causal test is supplied
  • Handwritten notes are a tentative proposal, not a validated fix
