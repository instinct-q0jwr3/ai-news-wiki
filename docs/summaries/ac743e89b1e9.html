GLM-5.3's open release has yet to produce major attacks despite Anthropic's warnings about its Mythos-level cyber risk, undercutting calls to ban open models

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-07 · updated: 2026-10-17 · confidence: high

Summary

Nathan Lambert argues that GLM-5.3's open release - which has so far produced no major attacks despite Anthropic's report warning of Mythos-level offensive cyber risk - undercuts the push to ban open-weight models. His problem with Anthropic's report is not the technical work, which he calls largely reasonable, but its failure to engage the cross-cutting questions: what actually happens if open models are banned, and why Chinese labs keep releasing strong open cyber-capable models.

Lambert maps the debate into three camps: frontier-lab leadership and the US national security community who see open weights as an untenable risk; moderates (including himself, Hugging Face after the OpenAI incident, and Joshua Saxe) who argue open models are necessary for defense; and Chinese companies simply continuing to release. He worries the loudest risk framing - including ad hominem claims that Chinese labs don't care about safety - is pushing policy toward a lose-lose: limits on American AI competitiveness that also increase long-term cyber risk.

Highlights

  • GLM-5.3's open release has produced no major attacks despite Anthropic's warnings.
  • Lambert: Anthropic's technical research is fine, but it skips what a ban would actually do.
  • Three camps: ban advocates (labs + natsec), open-for-defense moderates, and Chinese labs who keep releasing.
  • Very little substantive engagement with how Chinese companies assess risk.
  • His fear: policy that limits US competitiveness while raising long-term cyber risk.
  • Frames the moment as needing nuance, trade-offs and scary realities, not siloed thinking.
