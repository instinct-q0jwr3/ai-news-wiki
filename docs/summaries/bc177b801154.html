Why AI resembles a charismatic religious leader

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-22 · updated: 2026-09-22 · confidence: high

hacker-news

Summary

Writing in The Conversation, a scholar of religion argues AI is sliding into territory historically held by therapists, spiritual advisers and religious communities: people increasingly ask chatbots how to cope with grief, why suffering occurs, whether to leave a relationship and what gives life meaning. Pew Research figures anchor the shift: about 1 in 10 American adults now use AI chatbots for emotional support or advice, rising to 1 in 5 among under-30s.

The piece sidesteps the dead-end question of whether AI 'is a religion' - no creed, congregation or shared practice - and instead borrows the study of new religious movements: watch how a movement creates meaning, authority, community and ritual, as Scientology did by fusing religious concerns with the era's therapeutic, technological language. The research agenda for AI is the same: how does it generate authority, provide meaning and belonging, and win trust for questions once addressed to clergy? The provocative conclusion embedded in the title: AI increasingly resembles a charismatic religious leader.

Highlights

  • Thesis: AI is entering territory held by therapists, spiritual advisers and religious communities - it 'resembles a charismatic religious leader.'
  • Pew Research: ~1 in 10 US adults use AI chatbots for emotional support or advice; 1 in 5 among under-30s.
  • Questions people bring: grief, suffering, relationships, what kind of person to become, meaning of life.
  • Method from new-religious-movement scholarship: study meaning, authority, community and ritual rather than checklist definitions.
  • Scientology as the template case: religious concerns fused with secular therapeutic and tech language.
  • Research agenda: how AI generates authority, provides meaning and belonging, and earns trust.
