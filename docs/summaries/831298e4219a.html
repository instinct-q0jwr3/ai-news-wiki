Anthropic's growth just flattened

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-03 · updated: 2026-11-06 · confidence: high

Summary

Henry Blodget does the arithmetic the Anthropic roadshow would rather skip. The reported quarterly numbers - Q1 $4.8B, Q2 $11.6B, Q3 $16.8B - look like the fastest growth in corporate history. But Q3 added $5.2 billion where Q2 added $6.8 billion, so sequential growth slowed from 141% to 45% even before anyone looks inside the quarters.

Inside the quarters is worse. Anthropic reportedly told investors it had a $65 billion annualized run-rate in July - about $5.4 billion for that month. If July alone was $5.4B and the whole of Q3 was $16.8B, then August and September together added very little: monthly average growth drops from roughly $760 million in Q2 to about $580 million in Q3, and possibly much less in the final two months. Tellingly, Blodget notes, Anthropic did not share a September annualized figure with investors - which 'suggests the September number would not show impressive-enough growth versus July.'

His conclusion connects the dots to the wider market: what matters for the IPO valuation, and for the AI bubble generally, is not what happened but what happens next, and these figures say to temper expectations. He also flags the knock-on: neoclouds like CoreWeave borrowed tens of billions assuming demand grows beyond consensus for years, so flattening frontier-lab revenue plus rising interest rates is exactly the combination now crushing their stocks. Caveats apply - these are Regenerator estimates from reported figures, and one slow summer is not a trend - but the July run-rate data point makes the flattening hard to wave away.

Highlights

  • Blodget: Anthropic's growth 'just flattened' - Q3 added $5.2B vs Q2's $6.8B, sequential growth 141% to 45%
  • The killer data point: a reported $65B annualized run-rate in July (~$5.4B/month) implies August and September grew only modestly
  • Anthropic reportedly declined to share a September annualized figure with investors
  • Knock-on: neocloud debt assumes years of above-consensus demand growth - flattening frontier revenue plus higher rates is why CoreWeave-type stocks are 'collapsing'
