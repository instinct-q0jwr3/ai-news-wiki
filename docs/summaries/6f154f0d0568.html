How to talk about "AI" without adding to the anthropomorphization

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-23 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

lobsters

Summary

Emily M. Bender and Nanna Inie follow up their Tech Policy Press op-ed with a practical program for de-anthropomorphizing how we talk about 'AI': the habit is deeply ingrained, so changing it takes at least three deliberate steps - noticing anthropomorphizing language, categorizing it, and adopting substitutes.

Their research supplies the first two steps: a categorization of anthropomorphizing language with organized alternatives. De-anthropomorphized language describes systems by functionality, assigns agency to the people building and using them rather than to the systems, and avoids aggrandizing cognition metaphors. The substitutes are designed to be self-explanatory; some are clunkier and longer than the shorthand, which the authors frame as a feature - we should stop and think about what we mean.

Highlights

  • Practical program for breaking anthropomorphizing language habits around AI.
  • Three steps: notice, categorize, substitute - research covers the first two.
  • Principles: describe functionality, assign agency to people, avoid cognition metaphors.
  • Substitutes aim to be self-explanatory; clunkiness is acknowledged and partly the point.
  • From Emily M. Bender and Nanna Inie, following their Tech Policy Press op-ed.
