Show HN: ADRs for AI – Deliberation records for human–AI work, versioned in Git

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-26 · updated: 2026-10-31 · confidence: high

Summary

ADRs for AI adapts the architecture decision record - the software-world habit of writing down why a decision was made - to human-AI collaboration. The author's premise is that the most valuable output of working with an AI is not the code or the draft but the thinking, and in ordinary practice all of it evaporates at the end of the session. The method stores deliberation records in Git: questions, options, recommendations and rationale, versioned alongside the work itself.

The project does not try to make an AI persistent or give it memory; it makes the work resumable and lets decisions compound. The repository shows the method applied to itself, with live records like a bitemporal-policy question - whether corrections should overwrite records, append-only, or keep valid-time and observed-time - complete with the recommending model's rationale and confidence. It is a discipline for keeping the 'why' of AI-assisted work auditable after the chat is gone.

Highlights

  • ADRs for AI versions human-AI deliberation records in Git, modeled on architecture decision records
  • Premise: the thinking produced in AI sessions is the most valuable byproduct and evaporates by default
  • The method makes work resumable rather than making the AI persistent - decisions compound instead of disappearing
  • Records capture the question, why it is being asked, options, a recommendation with rationale, and confidence
  • The public repository demonstrates the method on itself, including a bitemporal policy for preserving historical truth
  • Recommendations are attributed to specific models, keeping provenance auditable
