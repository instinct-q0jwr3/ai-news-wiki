GPU-accelerated Weight Teleportation

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-03 · updated: 2026-11-08 · confidence: high

Summary

Large-scale RL has a plumbing problem pretraining never had: the sampler fleet must run on the same weights the trainer just produced, so every training step ends with terabytes of weights shipped trainer-to-sampler - over intra-cluster RDMA if you're lucky, with the samplers paused and burning money meanwhile. This post (from the Ampersand production RL setup) introduces GPU-accelerated weight teleportation: a Blackwell-native compression/decompression pipeline for delta weight sync that brings sampler pause time below 0.23 seconds and end-to-end weight sync under 4 seconds - faster than intra-cluster P2P RDMA for most real workloads.

The design builds on delta weight sync (XOR masks against the old weights) and pushes the compression work onto the GPUs themselves, saturating Blackwell's dedicated decompression ASIC - which becomes the bottleneck, meaning the pipeline is hardware-limited rather than algorithm-limited. The proxy workload is a 445 GB NVFP4 GLM-5.3 checkpoint with a 0.085% steady-state mutation per step, across 64 B300 training nodes and TP4 rollout receivers.

The bigger point: as RL post-training becomes the main capability lever, the trainer-sampler weight-sync loop is one of its defining systems bottlenecks. Sub-quarter-second pauses change what training configurations are practical - including samplers and trainers that aren't even co-located in the same cluster.

Highlights

  • Weight teleportation for RL: Blackwell-native delta compression cuts sampler pause below 0.23s and full weight sync under 4s
  • Faster than intra-cluster P2P RDMA for most workloads; the decompression ASIC becomes the bottleneck
  • Workload: 445GB NVFP4 GLM-5.3 checkpoint, 0.085% weight mutation per step, 64 B300 trainer nodes
  • Why it matters: trainer-sampler weight sync is a defining bottleneck of large-scale RL; near-zero pause makes non-co-located training practical
