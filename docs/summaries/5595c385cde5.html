China's AI mission collides with workers' displacement fears

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-20 · updated: 2026-09-23 · confidence: high

techmeme labor-displacement

Summary

China has made AI adoption a national mission - the plan is to have AI in every aspect of the economy and society by 2035 - but The Atlantic argues the country is racked by the same AI anxieties as the rest of the world. Chinese social media is filled with workers and professionals worried that AI will steal their jobs, and Beijing's leaders appear increasingly concerned about the political and economic risks of the disruption, even as Xi Jinping calls for regulation to 'ensure that AI is always under human control.'

The piece contrasts the American picture - where fears about AI are debated openly and will, in theory, be managed in courts and on Capitol Hill - with China, where no such public avenues exist for workers to shape the outcome. China's steady release of open-source, high-performing, cheap-to-run models has rattled Silicon Valley and Wall Street, but the domestic labor anxiety underneath the technological triumphalism mirrors the West's.

Highlights

  • China's plan: AI in every aspect of the economy and society by 2035
  • Chinese social media is full of workers worried AI will take their jobs, mirroring Western anxiety
  • Xi Jinping has called for regulation to 'ensure that AI is always under human control'
  • In surveys Chinese respondents are more enthusiastic about AI and adopt it faster than Americans
  • China's open-source, high-performing, cheap models are 'sending shivers' through Silicon Valley and Wall Street
  • Key contrast: US tensions are debated openly and managed in courts/Congress; Chinese workers lack such avenues
