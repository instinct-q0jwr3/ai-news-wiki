Why is AI generated language so hard to understand?

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-24 · updated: 2026-10-27 · confidence: high

Summary

An arXiv study investigates the widespread sense that AI-generated text has become harder to understand, testing psycholinguistic features known to correlate with human processing difficulty: object relative clauses, long syntactic dependencies, low-frequency n-grams and similar. Across a new corpus of AI text in diverse domains, all nine frontier and near-frontier models use more inanimate subjects with animate verbs and more low-frequency n-grams than human writing, and most also produce longer dependencies, more stacked nouns and more reduced object relatives in conversation, with substantial variation across model families.

The authors propose an explanation: human language is optimized for successful communication, while AI text is increasingly optimized for other goals - math, code, reasoning - where the pressure is information density rather than readability. As a proof of concept, they fine-tune a model under a length penalty and show its linguistic behavior diverges from human-like patterns. The paper frames this as a measurable axis of misalignment between human processing and LLM output, and points toward remedies.

Highlights

  • Study confirms AI text overuses psycholinguistic features linked to human processing difficulty.
  • All nine frontier/near-frontier models use more inanimate subjects with animate verbs and more low-frequency n-grams than humans.
  • Most models also show longer dependencies, more stacked nouns and more reduced object relatives in conversation.
  • Hypothesis: AI text is optimized for information density (math, code, reasoning), not for successful communication.
  • Proof of concept: fine-tuning under a length penalty makes a model's language diverge from human-like patterns.
  • AI text uses these hard features even more than round-trip-translation paraphrases.
  • The authors frame the gap as an axis of misalignment between human linguistic processing and LLM output.
