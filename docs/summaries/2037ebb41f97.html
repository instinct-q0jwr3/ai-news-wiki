Pluralistic: Google skates (05 Sep 2026)

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-10-04 · confidence: high

Summary

Cory Doctorow surveys another antitrust case in which Google escapes real consequences - 'another federal judge loses a game of peek-a-boo' with a monopolist that can afford to wait out, lawyered-up, any remedy a court is willing to impose.

He sets the failure in historical context. America got its first antimonopoly law, the Sherman Act, in 1890, yet it took 22 years before the law could be used to crush John D. Rockefeller's corrupt, sprawling empire. Senator John Sherman had warned the Senate about the 'autocrats of trade' that monopolies create. Oligarchies are stubborn: once entrenched, they command resources, power blocs and mercenaries to repel dethroners - which is why letting Google skate again is not a verdict on the case but on the era's enforcement will.

Highlights

  • Another antitrust ruling lets Google off with little consequence
  • Doctorow frames remedies as a judge losing peek-a-boo with a monopolist
  • Sherman Act passed 1890; Rockefeller's empire was only broken 22 years later
  • Sherman's warning: monopolies breed 'autocrats of trade'
  • Entrenched oligarchies deploy resources and power blocs against enforcement
