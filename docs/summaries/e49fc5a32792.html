Michigan prof creates Python-like Jac language for AI agents

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-26 · updated: 2026-10-30 · confidence: high

Summary

University of Michigan professor Jason Mars has built Jac, a Python-like programming language, and Jaseci, its runtime, that treat AI and agents as first-class entities. Demonstrated at the All Things Open conference, the project's premise is that today's languages and toolchains were written for human programmers before generative AI - and are ill-fitted to agents, which now do the consuming.

Mars tells the audience he does not want new tools; he wants tools for his agents, and cites Jarred Sumner's expensive AI rewrite of Bun as the cautionary tale: agents drowning in polyglot complexity. Jac's pitch is to give the agent stack a language designed for it, rather than burning six figures forcing agent output through human-shaped stacks.

Highlights

  • Jac is a Python-like language from Michigan professor Jason Mars, with the Jaseci runtime
  • Design premise: AI agents are first-class entities, not an afterthought
  • Current languages and toolchains were built for humans and ill-fit agentic work
  • Mars cites Bun creator Jarred Sumner's costly AI rewrite as the polyglot trap
  • Demoed at the All Things Open conference in Raleigh
