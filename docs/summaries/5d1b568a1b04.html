Postgres 19 is late, and it's AI's fault

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-07 · confidence: high

Summary

PostgreSQL's release train has been clockwork for a decade - mid-September to mid-October - and this year it will miss: version 19 most likely slips to November, possibly further, and three large features (UPDATE/DELETE FOR PORTION OF, SQL Property Graph Queries, ALTER TABLE ... MERGE/SPLIT PARTITION) were reverted unusually late in the beta. Robert Haas's short answer to what happened: LLMs happened. Postgres has always had plenty of bugs in thirty-odd years of code; what changed is that finding them became vastly easier, while fixing them got only somewhat easier.

The numbers make the case. Between feature freeze and late September, back-patched commits to the in-development stable branch jumped more than 60 percent year over year (724 to 1169) while master-branch commits slightly declined - and back-ports to the prior release jumped over 74 percent (248 to 433). Nobody on the project believes committers suddenly got sloppier; a huge number of people on the internet are now reporting LLM-found bugs, and the committers have been very, very busy fixing them. That flood explains both the late reverts and the delay: LLMs can draft fixes, but the fixes are not always correct or compliant with project conventions, so each report still costs senior reviewer time. The bug backlog of a 30-year-old codebase is being drained at machine speed, and the bottleneck is the humans who merge.
