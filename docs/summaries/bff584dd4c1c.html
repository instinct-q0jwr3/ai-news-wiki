Frontier models' ability to control robot arms is doubling every 54 days

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-27 · updated: 2026-10-30 · confidence: high

Summary

A LessWrong evaluation tested nine frontier multimodal models across fifteen configurations on controlling a real bimanual I2RT YAM robot, using proprioceptive feedback plus wrist and overhead cameras on six RoboDojo benchmark tasks, 20 trials per configuration. The headline finding: the ability of frontier models to control robot arms is doubling roughly every 54 days.

The author argues manipulation matters more than locomotion for biological and chemical research - a pair of arms and cameras can operate a wet lab - and flags the misuse implications: once vision-language models steer robots reliably, synthesizing pathogens stops requiring skilled wet-work. Models reportedly mastered sensing in 2024-2025 and remain relatively poor at locomotion.

Highlights

  • Robot-arm control capability doubling about every 54 days
  • Nine frontier models, fifteen configurations, six RoboDojo tasks, 20 trials each
  • Manipulation, not locomotion, is the key capability for wet-lab work
  • Misuse framing: VLM-controlled arms lower the bar for biological misuse
