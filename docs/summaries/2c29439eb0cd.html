Hirundo, Lazarus, and others are applying machine unlearning and weight edits to Chinese models to remove censorship and harden security for Western companies

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-13 · confidence: high

Summary

Rest of World profiles a growing niche: startups that perform surgery on Chinese open-weight models to make them palatable for Western deployment. Hirundo, co-founded by Israeli entrepreneur Ben Luria, applies machine unlearning and weight edits to models like DeepSeek's - 'like neurosurgery, an intervention that's surgical and very specific' - to realign values, censorship and state-aligned bias to what American and Western companies expect, while hardening security and reducing dangerous knowledge such as bioweapon know-how. His defense against the obvious charge: 'We're not making the model anti-Chinese. We are bringing it up to speed on safety.'

The demand side is real: Chinese open-weight models from DeepSeek, Moonshot, Z.AI, Alibaba and Tencent have won users - including American businesses - on cost, capability and customization, while CrowdStrike's analysis of R1 found prompts touching politically sensitive topics up to 50 percent more likely to yield code with severe security vulnerabilities. Skeptics like Stanford DigiChina's Graham Webster draw a risk line by use case: a Taiwanese product should worry more than a coffee-shop website builder. With China's first frontier-AI export controls landing in the same news cycle, the open-weight fork in the road is getting traffic from both directions.
