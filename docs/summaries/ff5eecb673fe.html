Big AI's content problem: Take the work, keep the money

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-28 · updated: 2026-09-30 · confidence: high

hacker-news

Summary

Steven J. Vaughan-Nichols' Register column argues that the more we learn about how AI companies do business, the more unfair it looks: they must ingest everyone else's work to train their models, and they hate paying for it, obeying licenses, or sharing revenue. The fresh evidence is 404 Media's reporting on newly unsealed documents in the NYT copyright case against OpenAI and Microsoft - plaintiffs' arguments the court has not yet ruled on - in which Microsoft's own Director of Applied Science, Dr. Brent Hecht, is quoted calling the scraping 'an astonishing theft of unprecedented proportions', possibly 'the largest theft of labor in human history.'

Hecht wasn't alone: an internal Microsoft policy document, also quoted, admits generative AI could 'significantly disrupt the employment of the very people who generated the data on which the foundation model was trained' because 'LLMs are a product that destroys its supply chain.' The column's point: the industry's own scientists described the business model as theft and supply-chain destruction - while the companies kept taking the work and keeping the money.

Highlights

  • Column: Big AI's model is 'take it now, argue about legality later' - and it's getting more blatant
  • Unsealed NYT-case docs (via 404 Media): Microsoft scientist called the scraping 'an astonishing theft of unprecedented proportions'
  • Also quoted: possibly 'the largest theft of labor in human history' - per the plaintiffs' brief
  • Internal Microsoft doc: 'LLMs are a product that destroys its supply chain'
  • Caveat: these are plaintiffs' arguments from unsealed filings; the court has not ruled
