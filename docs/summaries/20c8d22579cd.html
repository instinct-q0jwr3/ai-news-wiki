What VP ≠ VNP Means

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-25 · updated: 2026-11-26 · confidence: high

Summary

Gil Fried explains VP versus VNP through the contrast between efficiently computing determinants and the apparently harder permanent. The essay presents arithmetic circuits as a restricted computational model and uses that restriction to explain why an algebraic separation does not directly settle Boolean P versus NP.

This is an informal explainer, not an independent review of the newly claimed proof. Its broad permanent language should be read alongside the original theorem's field and characteristic conditions; it does not establish an unrestricted Boolean lower bound or a practical impossibility for every input class. No proof, prize eligibility or formal checker output was verified here.

Highlights

  • Determinant and permanent motivate the distinction
  • Arithmetic circuit size is the relevant model
  • Boolean computation is a different scope
  • New proof is assumed rather than audited in this essay
  • Permanent claims require field restrictions
  • No P versus NP conclusion or prize award is established
