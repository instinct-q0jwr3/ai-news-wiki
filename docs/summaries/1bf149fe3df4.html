First response from the Project Suncatcher prototype satellite

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-01 · updated: 2026-11-03 · confidence: high

Summary

Google reports the first AI response from a data-center-class TPU in orbit. Project Suncatcher's prototype satellite, launched October 1, finished commissioning - host and TPU trays powered, early ML tests run - and this week hit its Hello World: during a ground-station pass, the team uplinked a prompt to the onboard Gemma 4 open model asking for a haiku about being in space, and the downlinked poem came back as the satellite passed Svalbard. Follow-up tests ran space-themed limericks and telemetry prompts reporting payload status.

The milestone is small on purpose. Suncatcher is a long-term research moonshot exploring whether AI compute can be powered in space, and the meaningful next tests are environmental: how the TPUs hold up under thermal stress and radiation in orbit. If the chips prove reliable, the next milestone is a pair of interconnected satellites in 2027 - the first step toward the distributed orbital compute the project is ultimately probing.

Highlights

  • First AI response from a data-center-class TPU in orbit: a haiku downlinked over Svalbard
  • The onboard model is Gemma 4, Google's open model, on the Suncatcher prototype launched October 1
  • Commissioning done: host and TPU trays powered, early ML tests passed
  • Next: thermal-stress and radiation testing of the TPUs in orbit
  • If reliability holds: twin interconnected satellites in 2027
  • Goal of the moonshot: explore powering AI compute in space
