The AI Hater's Manifesto

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

ed-zitron

Summary

Ed Zitron's 'AI Hater's Manifesto' is a sweeping case against the current AI build-out. Against the 'humans make mistakes too' defense he argues humans learn on the job and self-correct in ways LLMs cannot, and that the most common use of a personal Jarvis seems to be tinkering with one's personal Jarvis. His team, he writes, has observed a year and a half of AI projects with zero successes - often failing not because of AI itself but because companies are terminally bad at running software projects, and AI adds its own novelty risk on top.

On infrastructure he is scathing about scale: AI data centers run 10 to 100 times larger and more power-intensive than those for social media or streaming. Meta's largest pre-AI data center in Pineville, Oregon has 30MW; the smallest AI announcement he has seen in a year was 100MW, most between 300MW and 1.2GW. By contrast UBS's Hayes facility - operationally important to one of the world's largest banks - has 5MW, and JPMorgan Chase's Orangetown data center 45.7MW. Unlike post-dot-com fiber, he argues, AI data centers are only good for AI: there is no second-act story. He closes by quoting Accel partner Miles Clements - 'Most people don't need to operate at the frontier' - and notes switching from closed frontier models to open ones has already saved companies money.
