AI fan wikis riddled with errors are muddying the waters of games culture

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-11 · confidence: high

Summary

A Eurogamer writer googles the companion characters of Nighthawks, clicks the first result - the 'Nighthawks Wiki' - and finds a site that is not a wiki at all: no editing, corrections by email only (the address bounces), and stuffed with confident falsehoods - companions and skills that do not exist - alongside irrelevant filler about the 1981 film and the Edward Hopper painting of the same name, even cited as 'sources.' Tracing the domain leads to a website builder offering an 'AI Website builder,' and a quick search turns up many more lookalike sites, typically targeting indie games whose real communities are too small to outrank them.

The publishers are feeling it directly. Hooded Horse, which unusually maintains official wikis for its own games with fan help, now plays 'whack-a-mole' with AI-generated competitors for the same search results - one, covering their Corsair Cove, belonged to a 20-plus-game collection whose creator admitted using generative AI. CEO Tim Bender names the model: commercial operations mass-producing sites to flood search results and soak up traffic. The damage is double - legitimate wikis get elbowed down the rankings, and players searching for help land on invented facts. For anyone running a wiki, human or otherwise, the piece is a reminder of what the format's credibility is up against.
