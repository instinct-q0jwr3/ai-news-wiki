Toronto-based Ada and SF-based Dialpad, both makers of AI customer service software, agree to merge, sources say in an all-share deal valuing Ada at ~$430M

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-09 · updated: 2026-11-12 · confidence: high

Summary

Toronto's Ada, which makes AI customer-service software, has agreed to combine with US firm Dialpad in an all-share deal valuing Ada at about C$600 million (US$430 million), according to two sources cited by The Globe and Mail. That is roughly a third of the US$1.2 billion valuation from its pandemic-era US$130 million round led by Spark Capital.

The paper ties the discount to the slide in tech valuations since late 2021 and to doubts over how much LLM software will disrupt legacy providers. Ada cut half its marketing team in July and laid off 25 people last week, per other sources the Globe did not name.

Highlights

  • All-share merger of Ada and Dialpad, per two unnamed sources
  • Ada valued about C$600M / US$430M
  • Down from US$1.2B at its peak round
  • Recent layoffs: 25 people last week, half of marketing in July
  • Context: pressure on AI customer-service vendors
