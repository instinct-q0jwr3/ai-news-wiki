  • One of the most useful ways to understand the present and anticipate the future is to measure the things that happened in the past, find the correlations among them, and extrapolate likely future outcomes from those correlations.
  • It is a productive and reliable way to uncover the causal relationships between natural phenomena, and to find ways to influence the world.
  • However, the fact that this method works for some things does not mean that it works for all things.
  • Naive, "theory-free" statistical extrapolation (the method that LLMs rely on) has hard limits.
  • Our intuition insists that the world is textured, but it turns out that there's far more smoothness in the distribution of natural phenomena, including the actions that we take of our own free will.
