IFR: ~7,000 autonomous humanoids were sold globally in 2025

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-21 · updated: 2026-09-22 · confidence: high

techmeme

Summary

About 7,000 humanoid robots were sold worldwide last year for industrial and professional service use, according to figures from the International Federation of Robotics reviewed by Reuters ahead of publication. The tally, one of the first industry-wide measures of the sector, is tiny next to the 542,000 regular industrial robots installed in 2024 and an estimated 199,000 service robots sold that year - a striking contrast with the billions being invested and the boom forecasts, especially in China, the world's largest market for industrial robots.

The IFR counts a robot as humanoid if it has a human-like appearance and operates autonomously in an environment designed for humans; legs are not required. IFR secretary general Susanne Bieller noted that many of the humanoids sold in 2025 were not doing productive work but were bought by research institutions or companies using them to generate training data for AI models. Carmakers, among the key early adopters, are running pilots.

  • To qualify as humanoid, a robot needs a human-like appearance and autonomous operation in human environments; legs not required
  • Many 2025 humanoid buyers were research institutions or companies generating AI training data, not doing productive work
  • China is the world's largest industrial-robot market and showcased humanoid running and boxing at its robot games last month
