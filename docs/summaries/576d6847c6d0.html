The Robot I Didn't Expect to Build

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-22 · updated: 2026-10-27 · confidence: high

Summary

Agility Robotics co-founder Jonathan Hurst reflects on the launch of Digit 5, the company's first practical robotic worker - and admits it is nothing like the robot he dreamed of building out of his Oregon State lab in 2015. Digit 5 is engineered for the warehouse: it lifts 23-kilogram bins onto high shelves, runs 90 minutes on a 9-minute charge, and is meant to work shift after shift on tasks too physically stressful or dull for people. Hurst frames it as the first humanoid that can safely and economically do useful work at scale, with tens of thousands of units as the ambition.

The essay is candid about the compromise: Digit 5 is heavy and bulky, lacks the graceful natural motion of Agility's earlier research robots, and is farther from the sci-fi humanoid than Hurst imagined a decade ago. The arc of the piece is a founder making peace with pragmatism - the elegant dynamic-balance research mattered, but the product that finally works is an appliance shaped by economics, safety cases, and the specific dull jobs customers will pay to automate.
