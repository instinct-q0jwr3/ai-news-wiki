AI wasn't overhyped. In the last 18 months, almost everyone underestimated it

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-26 · updated: 2026-10-29 · confidence: high

Summary

Benjamin Todd argues the usual complaint - AI is overhyped - got the last 18 months backwards. Almost everyone expected slower progress than arrived, and the best-calibrated forecasters were the most wrong: superforecasters underestimated AI the most, the general public was also too slow, AI experts did better but still lagged, and only AI company leaders were too optimistic - and only slightly. Taking CEO claims and shifting them six to eighteen months out would have tracked reality well.

Todd includes himself: his March 2025 piece asking whether AGI would arrive by 2030 was accused of buying CEO hype, but on rereading he concludes he underestimated the rate of progress. The post reviews what held up, what did not, and whose forecasts look best in hindsight.

Highlights

  • Todd's claim: over 18 months, almost everyone underestimated AI progress rather than overhyping it
  • Superforecasters - the best geopolitical predictors - underestimated it most
  • Only AI company leaders were too optimistic, and only slightly; their claims delayed 6-18 months tracked reality
  • The author revisits his own March 2025 AGI-by-2030 piece and concludes he was too conservative
  • A scoreboard of whose forecasts held up and whose did not
