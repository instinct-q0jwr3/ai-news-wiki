Amazon sends chills through its workforce with mixed message memo about AI use

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-31 · updated: 2026-11-05 · confidence: high

Summary

The Register's take on Amazon's big AI memo is half exegesis, half vibe check. Amazon VP and Distinguished Engineer Terk Terkowitz sent an internal treatise to software engineers in September - published externally this week - laying out the role of the developer in the age of AI, with emphasis on customer outcomes and engineering judgement as things not to delegate. The author thinks it is mostly a good document, and cannot help reading it cynically: a confident text, six weeks in the drawer, landing while Amazon reportedly cuts another 250 jobs and every engineer who reads it assumes the two are connected.

The piece's sharpest contribution is a litmus test for AI mandates: what happens when an engineer documents that AI made a task worse - are they taken seriously, or managed out? It revisits Amazon's KiroRank, the internal leaderboard ranking employees by token usage that was shut down in May after people spun up junk tasks to climb it, a behavior now called tokenmaxxing, which the memo itself calls out as unfrugal; Meta ran the same experiment as Claudenomics with 85,000 employees the same week. The memo's measurement advice - look at the 90th percentile of adoption, not the median - gets qualified praise, as does its honest list of work AI tools do not improve today. Underneath: a profession genuinely anxious, with senior people sincerely asking whether to leave tech for farming.
