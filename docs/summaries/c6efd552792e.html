Nvidia launches the Open Agent Safety Platform, a reference design to stop AI agents from escaping sandboxes, with OpenShell for CPUs and Sentry for Nvidia DPUs

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-28 · updated: 2026-10-01 · confidence: high

Summary

Nvidia is rolling out the Open Agent Safety Platform, a software reference design for setting safeguards around AI agents and stopping them from breaking containment - launched directly on the back of sandbox-escape disclosures from OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta and Google. Jensen Huang pitched it on CNBC as 'a browser for agents': 'You can't have agents roam around and drift around the company, and so you have to find a way to container it.' An Nvidia representative went further, telling reporters the platform could have prevented OpenAI's July Hugging Face incident - which Nvidia's Justin Boitano described as 'over 17,000 agents attacking their infrastructure that went on for days and weeks.'

The launch positions Huang in the safety debate as the engineering optimist: risks are 'engineering issues that can be solved through computer science and product development' - a deliberate counter to Dario Amodei's call two weeks ago for developers to slow down (a call Sam Altman and Elon Musk backed). The seller of the shovels now sells the fences too.
