[AINews] Gemini 4 Carbon tops leaderboards, launches for cyber defenders

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-29 · updated: 2026-10-30 · confidence: high

Summary

Latent Space's AINews digest covers Google's launch of Gemini 4 Carbon two weeks after Argon - a frontier model positioned for cybersecurity and long-horizon work that immediately took the top spot on Artificial Analysis' consolidated Intelligence Index v5 with a score of 59, possibly the first time Google DeepMind has led that table. Access is deliberately narrow at launch: Carbon goes first to trusted cyber defenders through the Fairwind Program, with general availability promised as soon as possible. Pricing is aggressive at $2/$10 per million input/output tokens - a 50 percent launch discount on the $4/$20 standard rate with no published end date - against a 1M-token context window and 1M max output.

The digest assembles the independent verdicts. Claimed cyber results are strong (88.2 percent on SEC-bench Pro for vulnerability finding, 54.8 on ExploitGym, 71.0 on CWE-bench for patching) and Carbon leads four of the nine index evals, but the picture is mixed: it trails Claude Code with Opus 5.5 on the Coding Agent Index (59 versus 62) and GPT-6 Astra on FrontierSWE, its hallucination rate rose from Argon's 4 percent to 11 (still the second-lowest measured), and its coding numbers were measured in Google's own Antigravity CLI harness, leaving other harnesses untested. Arena puts it first in Text at 1528 Elo; Vals ranks it second overall but first on finance and legal research. Safety metrics moved the right way: successful cheating on Terminal-Bench 4.0 fell to 0.0 percent, and unsupported claims dropped about 2x versus Argon. Around the edges: Pichai told investors to expect continued acceleration because of RSI over the next twelve months, a Gemini engineer describes overnight agent swarms inspecting RL data, and one researcher's read is that Google still runs about two months behind the next OpenAI and Anthropic releases.
