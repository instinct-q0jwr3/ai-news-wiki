AI researchers debate how close we are to recursive self-improvement

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

dwarkesh-podcast

Summary

Dwarkesh Patel convened three AI researchers from the more open-ish labs - John Schulman (chief scientist at Thinking Machines, previously OpenAI co-founder and RLHF lead behind ChatGPT), Beren Millidge (CTO of open-source model developer Zyphra) and Charlie O'Neill (head of model training at Baseten) - to talk about what is actually happening at the frontier and what comes next, including a steelman of the case against recursive self-improvement.

Opening question: if 2036 arrives without billions of superintelligences transforming the world, what was the most likely technical reason? Millidge offers a Moravec's-paradox-style failure mode - AI keeps acing every benchmark and environment we build, yet the 'true spark of generalization' never arrives and a persistent sim-to-real gap blocks everything - though he considers that unlikely, since RL already shows real generalization in practice. The episode ranges over what is driving the Chinese labs' progress, how automated AI researchers will be trained, whether long-horizon RL elicits AGI, how much progress is explained by data, why RL is working so well, Move 37 and entropy collapse, and a rapid-fire round on timelines.

Highlights

  • Guests: John Schulman (Thinking Machines), Beren Millidge (Zyphra), Charlie O'Neill (Baseten)
  • Opening: steelman the technical case against recursive self-improvement by 2036
  • Millidge's failure mode: benchmarks all solved, but no 'true spark of generalization' - a persistent sim-to-real gap
  • He thinks that unlikely: RL already shows generalization in practice
  • Topics: Chinese labs' progress, training automated AI researchers, long-horizon RL and AGI
  • Also: how much progress is explained by data, why RL works so well, Move 37 and entropy collapse, rapid-fire timelines
