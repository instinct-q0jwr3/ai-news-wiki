Why Anthropic's filing crushed tech stocks

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-31 · updated: 2026-10-31 · confidence: high

Summary

Matt Stoller argues in The BIG newsletter that the market's ugly week - the neoclouds crushed first, then Google dropping 12% on Thursday and the whole tech sector landing 10% below its high - runs straight through Anthropic's draft S-1, made public on Monday. What strikes him is how little of Anthropic stands alone. Amazon, Google, Broadcom and Microsoft appear in the filing as investors, customers, distribution partners, suppliers and competitors simultaneously, and Anthropic's own risk language concedes these relationships create potential conflicts of interest that are difficult to fully anticipate or mitigate. Stoller counts the mentions: Amazon over 150, Google over 100 - against a maximum of 16 (Silicon Graphics) in Google's 2004 S-1. His verdict: Anthropic looks like four monopolies in a trench coat, and that entanglement is why the entire tech complex now rises and falls as a single trade.

He also dwells on what the draft omits: key financial details are missing, but what is visible is stark - Anthropic lost $80 billion last year, per his reading, while Amazon ran negative $42 billion in free cash flow. The traditionally profitable giants have shifted from minting cash to burning it, and Wall Street's wobble is investors noticing. The policy implication he draws: this is entangled monopoly documented in an IPO prospectus, not a normal stand-alone business going public.

Highlights

  • Anthropic's own risk section: partners act as investors, customers, distributors and competitors at once
  • Contrast: no outside company got more than 16 mentions in Google's 2004 S-1
