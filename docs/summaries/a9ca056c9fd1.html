Five months treating bugs like patients and coding agents like a medical team

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-11 · updated: 2026-10-21 · confidence: high

Summary

Cockroach Labs describes five months of running coding agents like a medical team inside a system they call MOLT Sinai, a coding hospital where issues are patients, merging is discharge and the humans in charge are Chiefs of Medicine. The showcase result: their MOLT migration tooling gained IBM Db2 support between a Wednesday evening and a Friday afternoon, with a token bill of $4,172, versus the 9 months and roughly $160K the comparable Oracle support took in 2024; no human wrote any of the code.

The machinery mattered more than the speed: a planning agent decomposed the ask into fifteen sub-issues with an explicit dependency graph, and by the end thirty-two sub-issues had been opened, twenty-seven pull requests merged, reviewers had sent work back fifty-five times, and nine issues were escalated, two of them to a human. Test coverage matched their best-tested dialect. The point of the hospital design is trust: they would rather agents run 10x longer than land the wrong data in a customer migration, a contrast with throughput-optimized factories like Gas Town.

Highlights

  • Db2 migration in two days, no human code, $4,172 in tokens
  • Comparable Oracle work took 9 months and ~$160K in 2024
  • MOLT Sinai: issues are patients, merging is discharge
  • Planning agent split the work into 15 dependent sub-issues
  • 32 sub-issues, 27 merged PRs, 55 review send-backs
  • Quality gates over throughput, unlike parallel factories
