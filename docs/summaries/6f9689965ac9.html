Apple v. OpenAI: Apple asks the court to let its own experts review forensic images used in the case, and seeks documents tied to parts of OpenAI's hardware R&D

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-22 · updated: 2026-09-22 · confidence: high

techmeme openai apple amazon ai-policy-regulation

Summary

In the Apple v. OpenAI trade-secrets case, Apple has asked the court to let its own experts review the forensic images the defendants' experts relied on, and is seeking documents tied to parts of OpenAI's hardware R&D. Apple sued former employees, OpenAI and io Products in early July, alleging trade-secret misappropriation; it says a forensic inspection of a MacBook used by former employee Chang Liu showed he ran a simulation using a confidential Apple circuit schematic while at OpenAI, and it wants access to a Mac mini the MacBook pointed to.

OpenAI calls the situation 'a mess of Apple's own making,' arguing lax offboarding and security practices let employees unintentionally retain access to confidential material. The immediate fight is procedural: Apple wants expedited discovery and a preliminary injunction restricting OpenAI's use of the alleged secrets while hardware development continues - arguing delay lets the material become further intertwined and harder to undo. OpenAI opposes both as unwarranted and overbroad. The joint filing laying out the impasse was made public last Friday.

Highlights

  • Apple asks the court to let its own experts review forensic images used by the defense, and seeks OpenAI hardware R&D documents.
  • Filed early July against former employees, OpenAI and io Products over alleged trade-secret misappropriation.
  • Apple: a MacBook used by ex-employee Chang Liu ran a simulation with a confidential Apple circuit schematic while at OpenAI; a linked Mac mini is also sought.
  • OpenAI's rebuttal: 'a mess of Apple's own making' - lax offboarding and security practices.
  • Apple also alleges Liu coached an Apple employee on what to review before an OpenAI interview, and Tang Tan directed candidates to bring hardware components.
  • Disputed remedies: expedited discovery and a preliminary injunction that could affect OpenAI's ongoing hardware work.
  • Joint filing on the discovery impasse made public last Friday; reported by 9to5Mac's Marcus Mendes.
