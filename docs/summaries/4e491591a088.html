Cancer Capital: It sucks for founders, too!

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

anil-dash microsoft apple amazon ai-safety-incidents ai-policy-regulation

Summary

Anil Dash's 'Cancer Capital' series turns from theory to what the new venture capital does to founders - meaning people with an idea they are obsessed with, not the cohort who 'identified a pile of money they wanted to grab.' The old deal was legible: build as far as your own resources take you, treat investors as a necessary evil ('vulture capital,' said to their faces), and walk in skeptical. The best firms competed on founder-friendliness: USV in New York with straightforward communication and an understandable thesis, so founders could self-select and nobody wasted time.

The norm with real teeth was the conflict rule: a VC would generally refuse to invest in a competitor of an existing portfolio company - not as a favor, but because you cannot sit on two boards in the same market or ask a founder to open their books to their rival's backer. A legible thesis let you find those conflicts before spending three months on a pitch. By 2013, when Dash pitched Bloomberg Beta, the firm had published its operating manual on GitHub and let him publish the term sheet there too. That was a dozen years ago; many NYC firms still hold those values, he notes, though it leaves them smaller players in deals the giant firms dominate. In recent years, for the giants and their aligned smaller firms, 'the mask has fully come off' (the excerpt cuts off as the indictment begins).
