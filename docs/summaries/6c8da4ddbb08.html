The anti-marketing aspect of the Gemini network

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-12 · updated: 2026-11-14 · confidence: high

Summary

Ploum discusses the Gemini network protocol, explicitly distinct from Google's AI product. The essay argues that a small network can remain healthy without correlating growth to publicity or maximizing clicks. It uses reported capsule counts to question whether marketing metrics capture the value of a community.

This is an argument about internet culture and measurement, not a claim about Gemini model performance. A lack of correlation in one observation does not prove that publicity never matters. The author favors meaningful participation over audience-growth incentives.

Highlights

  • Gemini here is a network protocol
  • Essay questions click-based measures of value
  • Reported capsule counts motivate the argument
  • Community health differs from marketing reach
  • Limited observation does not establish universal causation
