The Great Unbundling of Intelligence

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-22 · updated: 2026-09-23 · confidence: high

tldr-ai

Summary

For the last three years, the miracle of the LLM has been bundling .

Want to search for information, rank five options, decide whether something looks suspicious, choose the next tool, browse a website, write the response, verify the work, or solve a genuinely difficult reasoning problem?

I think we are entering the Great Unbundling of Intelligence .

Highlights

  • GPT, Claude, Gemini and Kimi collapsed an extraordinary number of previously separate capabilities into one general-purpose product.
  • That was one of the great product breakthroughs of generative AI: instead of stitching together a dozen narrow systems, developers could put one sufficiently intelligent model in the middle of an application and let it do almost everything.
  • These capabilities have radically different computational requirements, and different models are already better at different parts of the bundle.
  • Smaller and open-weight models are good enough to absorb much more routine work.
  • And paradoxically, frontier models becoming much better makes unbundling safer: an application can aggressively use cheap intelligence because a frontier model is waiting at the top of the stack whenever the cheaper layer won’t work.
  • With the massive inference scale of agents,different steps inside the same objective deserve radically different amounts and kinds of intelligence.
  • Unbundling asks a much more uncomfortable question: what is the independent market price of every capability hiding inside that call?
  • The threat to frontier inference isn't just cheaper frontier inference.
