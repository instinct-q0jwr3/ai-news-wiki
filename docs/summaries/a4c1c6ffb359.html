Ship Your Interpreter

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-17 · updated: 2026-10-21 · confidence: high

Summary

Basis argues that as LLMs get alarmingly good at finding and exploiting bugs, the counterweight is their growing ability to write verified software - programs with machine-checked proofs that rule out entire bug classes. The post tracks rapid progress: in April their agents built a JavaScript-to-WebAssembly compiler but could not prove it preserved behavior; months later they verified Linux's nftables compiler; in September MIT's Kaashoek and Zeldovich used AI agents to verify the xv6 kernel down to machine code.

The thesis of the piece is that the bottleneck is shifting from theorem proving to specification: before proving anything you need a mathematical description of what the program and its language actually do, and the hard question is whether the statements proved capture the behavior we care about. Hence the title - ship your interpreter, the formal meaning of the language, with the software.

Highlights

  • Argues verified software is the answer to AI-powered exploitation
  • Timeline: JS-to-WASM compiler attempt, then verified nftables compiler, then MIT's verified xv6
  • Basis aims at verification methods that scale to all software
  • Bottleneck shifting from theorem proving to specification
  • Specification problem: proofs must capture the behavior we actually care about
