The Hitchhiker's Guide to Training on Compute Islands

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-03 · updated: 2026-11-04 · confidence: high

Summary

A training-runs manifesto for the stranded-compute era. The authors trained a sparse mixture-of-experts model across two clusters of different sizes, with different GPU types, nearly halfway around the world apart, over a 25 Gbps bidirectional link - and it worked. They also revisited 'butterfly mixing,' replacing the all-reduce with pairwise exchanges between eight learners, and matched standard training.

The motivation is the supply crunch itemized with sources: power, grid equipment and land - not chips - are the binding constraints through 2028; gas turbines are spoken for through 2028; large transformers take three-plus years; local opposition killed at least 20 projects in a quarter; Goldman expects only 50-60% of scheduled US datacenter capacity to arrive on time (historical rate: ~72%). Meanwhile the market's available compute sits scattered across neoclouds in clusters of hundreds-to-thousands of GPUs. Musk's line - 'a lot of stranded compute in partially complete data centers' - is the epigraph.

The position: distributed training across heterogeneous, geographically separated 'compute islands' is the way to aggregate that stranded capacity into frontier-scale runs, and the paper is a proof with a call to action. If the techniques generalize, the moat of the giant contiguous datacenter weakens - training scale becomes a networking and scheduling problem as much as a real-estate one.

Highlights

  • Trained a sparse MoE across two hetero clusters (different sizes, different GPUs) nearly halfway around the world over 25 Gbps - 'it was glorious'
  • Butterfly mixing (pairwise exchanges between 8 learners) matched standard all-reduce training
  • The constraint stack: turbines sold out through 2028, 3-year transformers, 50-60% on-time datacenter delivery (Goldman), 20 projects killed by local opposition in one quarter
  • The bet: networking and scheduling can aggregate stranded neocloud capacity into frontier training runs, weakening the contiguous-datacenter moat
