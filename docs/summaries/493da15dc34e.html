Kimi K3.1 Pro's debut draws scrutiny over allegations that influencers were asked to promote the model without disclosing a commercial relationship to Moonshot

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-25 · updated: 2026-10-27 · confidence: high

Summary

VentureBeat's deep dive on Kimi K3.1 Pro frames it as a likely historic open-weights release - it already outscores Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro, DeepSeek V4.1-Flash, Meta's Muse Wonder and OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index, and should take the open-weights crown when weights ship November 4. The biggest independent gains are in agentic, multi-step work: Terminal-Bench 4.0 jumps from 13% to 67% (ahead of Sonnet 5.5's 64%), CyberGym-E2E from 58% to 95%, Cyber Index from 41% to 64%, with smaller gains on pure reasoning (Humanity's Last Exam 47% to 52%). Moonshot's own comparisons tell a more mixed story - Kimi trails Opus and Astra badly on Terminal-Bench Science - so the evidence points to improved task execution rather than an across-the-board reasoning leap. API pricing is $2/$10 per million input/output tokens (a third below K3, half of Opus 5.5), with a Highspeed tier at $4/$20 claiming 2-3x speed; DeepSeek and Xiaomi remain far cheaper, and self-hosting waits on the November weights, whose license terms are still unknown. Moonshot's API runs from Singapore-based servers, a data-location consideration given the DHS order excluding DeepSeek from US federal procurement.

The launch has also drawn scrutiny over an alleged paid influencer campaign on X. User Elshayib posted screenshots of what he described as Kimi promotional instructions, which he says he declined; they required draft approval and directed creators not to use the paid-partnership label - a potential violation of FTC disclosure guidance and X policy. Pekingnology's reporting notes the document is not independently authenticated: it is unclear whether it came from Moonshot, an agency or another party, how many creators received it, or whether any undisclosed endorsements ran. VentureBeat stresses the allegations do not invalidate the independent benchmark results, but they do underline the need to separate promotional enthusiasm from reproducible evidence.
