An interview with philosopher Thomas Nagel, who won a $1M prize for contributions to consciousness and says the notion of AI consciousness is "just ridiculous"

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-18 · updated: 2026-11-27 · confidence: high

Summary

Wired interviews philosopher Thomas Nagel about consciousness and AI after his philosophy prize. He argues that lifelike language behavior is not enough to establish subjective experience and challenges the assumption that the material substrate is irrelevant.

This is a philosophical position in an edited interview, not an empirical test proving that all present or future AI systems lack consciousness. The examined passages distinguish his reaction to impressive behavior from his account of the conditions consciousness would require.

Highlights

  • Interview addresses subjective experience
  • Nagel distinguishes behavior from consciousness
  • Physical substrate is central to his objection
  • Views are philosophical rather than a diagnostic test
  • No universal empirical consciousness result is established
