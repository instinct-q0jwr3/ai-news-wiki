Thinking fast and slow in AI: The role of metacognition (2021)

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-28 · updated: 2026-10-07 · confidence: high

Summary

This 2021 paper argues that state-of-the-art AI, for all its progress, remains narrow - and that Kahneman's 'thinking fast and slow' distinction is a useful blueprint for what to build next. The authors propose a multi-agent architecture in which incoming problems route to either System 1 ('fast') agents that react from past experience, or System 2 ('slow') agents that are deliberately activated when a problem needs reasoning and search beyond the fast agent's reach.

Both agent kinds sit on two shared structures: a world model holding domain knowledge about the environment, and a model of 'self' holding the system's past actions and each solver's skills - the metacognitive layer that decides who should handle what. The framing reads prescient now: the field has since rebuilt this split in practice, with fast model calls for routine work and deliberate test-time reasoning for hard problems.

Highlights

  • 2021 paper proposing Kahneman's System 1/System 2 split as an AI architecture
  • Fast agents answer from experience; slow agents are activated for deliberate reasoning and search
  • A world model plus a 'self' model (past actions, solver skills) form the metacognitive layer
  • Motivation: AI successes are narrow - human-like competence needs metacognition about when to think
  • Anticipates today's fast-call vs. reasoning-model division of labor
