Anger, Anxiety and Agency

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-10-08 · confidence: high

Summary

Armin Ronacher responds to Sean Goedecke's case against being angry at work - a position he endorses - via the Lobsters question that stuck with him: how can you work in tech right now and not be angry? His answer is that anger is the wrong frame for the AI transition. Anxiety needs no villain and is a reasonable response to genuine uncertainty about professions, skills and the world our kids will enter; anger needs a target, and with a change this disruptive it is easy to pick the wrong one - your manager or leadership team may be just as uncertain, projecting clarity to steady themselves.

He offers two alternatives to anger. Uncertainty can become curiosity: we have access to 'magic machines' and can poke at them to see what happens. And curiosity can become genuine excitement: many AI gains are showing up not as company productivity but as side projects shipped on personal time, which suggests owners and founders don't know what's coming either - agency is not foresight. He closes by holding the contradiction honestly: tremendously excited and unsure at once, and worried about what the industry is ignoring - societal impact, climate, and Europe's lack of ambition and growing dependence on other countries.

Highlights

  • Prompted by Sean Goedecke's 'never be angry at work' and a Lobsters comment: 'How can you work in tech right now and not be angry?'
  • Anxiety needs no one to blame and fits an uncertain future; anger requires a villain and easily picks the wrong one
  • Anger feels actionable because it turns 'I don't know' into a story with someone to blame - that's its trap
  • First alternative: stay unsure and let uncertainty become curiosity - poke the magic machines and see what happens
  • Second alternative: genuine excitement - AI gains are leaking out as personal side projects, not just corporate productivity
  • Leaders projecting confidence are often placing bets while privately uncertain; ownership gives agency, not foresight
  • He holds both feelings at once: liberated some days, ground crumbling on others
  • Real worries named: societal impact, climate, and Europe's dependence and lack of ambition
