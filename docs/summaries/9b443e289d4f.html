Anger, Anxiety and Agency

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

armin-ronacher meta agentic-systems small-specialist-models

Summary

Sean Goedecke wrote a post arguing that you should never be angry at work — a post with which I strongly agree.

Anger can be a useful signal, but being angry at work rarely improves the situation.

More often, it makes life worse for the people around you, many of whom have no more power over the source of your anger than you do.

Highlights

  • I did learn that lesson, but it did not come naturally.
  • One thing in particular that I learned is that in a company there is a shared vision, and if you don’t agree with it and are not in a position to change it, you should not start a mutiny, not even a small-scale one.
  • In the discussion around that topic, one of the most upvoted comments on the Lobsters thread asked a question I had to think about quite a bit: How can you work in tech right now and not be angry?
  • For me, the emotions I would expect in tech vis-a-vis these new developments are disorientation and anxiety, but not anger.
  • Anxiety as an emotion does not require someone to blame.
  • Right now, I find it reasonable to feel anxious about an uncertain future.
  • But anger is different from anxiety because anger needs to be directed somewhere.
  • The feeling of anger suggests that somebody or something is doing something to you .
