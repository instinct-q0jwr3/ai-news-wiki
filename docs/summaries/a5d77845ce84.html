Summary

Cory Doctorow argues that privacy campaigners lose by fighting on the wrong battlefield: property. After fifty years of neoliberalism, even capitalism's critics frame everything in 'property talk' - but privacy is a human right, not a property right, and human rights aren't for sale (you can't sell yourself into slavery or sell a kidney for rent).

If privacy were property, Facebook's industrial-scale invasions would be legitimate trades - you 'sold' your privacy for the privilege of talking to friends. Doctorow targets the proposal to make Facebook pay cash for privacy as the same trap in progressive clothing, and makes the case for unpermissioned research and action that asserts rights directly instead of negotiating their price.

Highlights

  • Doctorow: privacy is a human right, not a property right, and human rights aren't tradable
  • Framing privacy as property legitimizes Facebook's surveillance as a 'trade'
  • He rejects making platforms pay cash for privacy as the same trap in progressive form
  • Critics who fight in 'property talk' choose a battlefield where oligarchs have the advantage
  • The essay defends 'unpermissioned research' - asserting rights rather than pricing them
