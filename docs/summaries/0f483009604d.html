Fujifilm to boost tape production 5x as AI fuels archiving demand

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-03 · updated: 2026-11-08 · confidence: high

Summary

The AI buildout's most anachronistic beneficiary yet: magnetic tape. Fujifilm announced it will invest roughly 10 billion yen (~$63 million) to increase LTO tape production capacity fivefold against fiscal 2025 levels - its largest-ever investment in dedicated tape facilities - explicitly attributing the demand surge to generative AI's 'exponential growth' in data archiving needs. The expansion at the Kanagawa Odawara site runs fiscal 2026-2028 and focuses on the latest LTO Ultrium10 generation.

The logic is cold-storage economics plus security. Cloud giants never abandoned tape: it stores vast volumes cheaply, and cartridges can be air-gapped - physically disconnected and shelved - which Fujifilm pitches as a last line of defense against ransomware. As AI training and inference multiply the data worth keeping, the archive layer grows with it, and the archive layer runs on a medium consumers left for dead decades ago. Fujifilm has made magnetic tape since the 1950s and shipped the first barium-ferrite LTO tape in 2011; Ultrium10 uses a hybrid of barium and strontium ferrite particles.

File it next to the natural-gas turbines and the grid-interconnection queues: the AI boom's supply chain keeps reaching further back into industrial history.
