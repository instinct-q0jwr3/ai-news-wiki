Utah to let AI examine patients and prescribe medication without human oversight

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-06 · updated: 2026-10-17 · confidence: high

Summary

Utah is the first US state to let an AI examine patients and prescribe medication without direct human oversight, via a one-year pilot limited to mild-to-moderate acne. Patients use Nolla Health's $4.99/month app to scan their face and complete a medical history; the AI rates oiliness, generates an acne severity score, and recommends from a list of eight approved treatments.

The rollout is staged: doctors review the first 100 prescriptions before they reach pharmacies; the next 400 are submitted by the AI and reviewed retrospectively each week; the final phase has a physician reviewing at least 10% of prescriptions monthly. The report recalls the risks of AI medical advice - including last year's case of a man who developed bromism after ChatGPT advised replacing salt with sodium bromide.

Highlights

  • Utah pilots AI examinations and prescriptions without direct human oversight - a US first.
  • Scope: one year, mild-to-moderate acne only, via Nolla Health's $4.99/month app.
  • AI scores acne severity and picks from eight approved treatments.
  • Staged rollout: 100 doctor-reviewed prescriptions, then retrospective weekly review, then 10% monthly sampling.
  • Cited cautionary case: bromism after following ChatGPT dietary advice.
