[AINews] GPT-6 Astra: OpenAI’s biggest LLM launch of all time

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-19 · updated: 2026-09-25 · confidence: high

latent.space openai agentic-systems compute-buildout

Summary

OpenAI launched GPT-6 Astra, its new flagship, with the biggest launch in the company's history: 36M views and 164K likes in nine hours, its most successful since Sora and, per Latent Space, the first time OpenAI has out-drawn Anthropic on launch popularity. Astra, described as the first Stargate-trained, lightly looped supermodel, was positioned around computer use, software engineering, math and science, polished office work, and cybersecurity, priced at $10/$50 per million input/output tokens, rolling out to ChatGPT tiers, the API and AWS.

The rollout was bumpy (delays, a late blog post, influencer-first access) and OpenAI compensated paid users with 'banked resets' of usage limits. Reception split sharply: practitioners reported a step-change in 'gets things done' behavior, especially on long-horizon work, computer use and 3D/game building, while the system card drew intense scrutiny for describing improved alignment alongside decreased chain-of-thought monitorability, and critics including Neel Nanda and Ryan Greenblatt questioned evaluation-awareness and whether visible alignment gains paper over specific failure modes.

Highlights

  • GPT-6 Astra launch: 36M views, 164K likes in 9 hours; OpenAI's biggest launch ever, first time out-drawing Anthropic.
  • Positioning: 'most intelligent and aligned model yet'; computer use, SWE, math/science, office work, cybersecurity.
  • Pricing: $10 / 1M input tokens, $50 / 1M output tokens; rollout to ChatGPT tiers, API and AWS.
  • Launch hiccups: delays, broken blog post, influencer-first access; 'banked resets' compensated paid users.
  • Rapid third-party adoption: Perplexity Computer, OpenRouter, Cline, GitHub Copilot app, Base44, Hermes Agent.
  • Claimed to cleanly beat Claude Fable 5.1 on many metrics and saturate the hardest FrontierMath versions.
  • System card flagged improved alignment but decreased chain-of-thought monitorability.
  • Critics (Nanda, Greenblatt, others) raised evaluation-awareness and release-governance concerns.
