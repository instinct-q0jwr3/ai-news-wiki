In PA, FBI agents arrested a co-founder of a Canadian cybersecurity firm specializing in ransomware negotiations, as part of the ShinyHunters investigation

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-10 · updated: 2026-10-10 · confidence: high

Summary

Brian Krebs reports that FBI agents arrested the co-founder of a Canadian cybersecurity firm specializing in ransomware negotiations, as part of the ShinyHunters investigation. The suspect was visiting Pennsylvania for a cyber insurance conference; the New York Times reported the arrest without naming him, and the FBI's only statement came from Director Kash Patel on X.

Krebs ties the arrest to Edward Dubrovsky, co-founder of Cypfer, the biggest sponsor of Philadelphia's Cyber Risk Summit, and now associated with CyberSteward. Court records show an 'Edward Dobrovsky', 54, arrested October 8 on cyber extortion and conspiracy charges, held in Philadelphia, with the case moved to the Eastern District of Texas, now the epicenter of the probe. ShinyHunters has extorted more than $70 million this year, and sources say charges against principals at other ransomware-negotiation firms may follow, as the FBI pores over devices seized in a related Dutch arrest.

Highlights

  • Arrest in Pennsylvania tied to the ShinyHunters extortion probe
  • Suspect linked to Cypfer and CyberSteward, ransomware-negotiation firms
  • Charged October 8 with cyber extortion and conspiracy
  • Case moved to the Eastern District of Texas, the probe's epicenter
  • ShinyHunters has extorted over $70 million from victims this year
  • More charges against negotiation-firm principals may follow
