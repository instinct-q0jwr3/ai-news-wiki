Jev: System One models for Prod, not God — with Diogo Almeida, CEO, TypeSafe AI

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-22 · updated: 2026-09-25 · confidence: high

latent.space typesafe small-specialist-models

Summary

Latent Space interviews Diogo Almeida - coauthor of the InstructGPT paper and now CEO of TypeSafe AI - about Jev, a model family built for software rather than chat, whose launch video was viewed roughly 40 million times (for comparison, the episode cites GPT-4o at 22M and Fable 5 at 15M). Almeida argues the field over-committed to autoregressive chat-tuned LLMs, and that innovations from function calling to reasoning were 'hacks' on the sequence-to-sequence paradigm; his years at OpenAI left him convinced the core unsolved problem for putting LLMs at the heart of software is reliability.

Jev's core innovation is 'Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions' (RLCD), an unpublished technique that optimizes for 'answers with epistemically honest probabilities on System One tasks,' positioned against RLHF (which he blames for hallucination, sycophancy and dependence on human raters) and RLVR (verifiable rewards that 'exacerbate jagged intelligence'). TypeSafe rejects public benchmarks, optimizes for intelligence per dollar, calls itself a data lab rather than a model lab, and treats refusals as a defect when AI sits inside software dependencies. Almeida also teases a contrarian take on scaling laws, saying he 'wouldn't pre-train with $1 billion,' and discusses his 'Bitterest Lesson' essay: the most important part of ML isn't ML - picking the right north star makes everything else fall in line.

Highlights

  • Guest: Diogo Almeida, InstructGPT coauthor, CEO of TypeSafe AI; topic: Jev, 'System One' models for production software.
  • Jev launch video viewed ~40M times; episode compares GPT-4o at 22M and Fable 5 at 15M.
  • Core technique: RLCD - Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions - targeting 'epistemically honest probabilities' on System One tasks.
  • Almeida blames RLHF for hallucination, sycophancy and reliance on human raters; RLVR 'exacerbates jagged intelligence.'
  • TypeSafe rejects public benchmarks and a generic 'JevBench'; optimizes intelligence per dollar; self-describes as a data lab, not a model lab.
  • Programming model: small measurable decisions, structured state instead of giant prompts; Jev should 'disappear into the background' like regex.
  • Use cases highlighted: computer use, dark-data entity resolution, coding agents beyond the KV cache, multi-agent shared state.
  • Almeida's 'Bitterest Lesson': 'the most important part of ML isn't ML at all' - the north star metric decides everything.
  • Contrarian scaling tease: he says he wouldn't pre-train even with $1 billion.
  • Episode runs ~1h41m with a full chapter list, including 'Why powerful AI still hasn't automated the economy.'
