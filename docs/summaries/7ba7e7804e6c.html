DetectorScope: AI text detectors that show their work

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-24 · updated: 2026-10-30 · confidence: high

Summary

DetectorScope, a Google DeepMind project, turns a black-box AI text detector into an auditable one whose every prediction decomposes into named, validated features with locations in the text. Given a sentence, it reports not just a label and probability but the evidence: for example, that 'the word about used as a preposition' pushed the logit up by 0.23 while a 'conversational filler phrase' pushed it down by 0.05, with each contribution traceable to token positions.

The pipeline is a concept bottleneck over sparse autoencoder features: SAEs encode the detector model's residual stream, each feature gets a natural-language description validated against its own activations, and a sparse linear head over the validated features makes the final call, so the logit literally equals bias plus the sum of feature importances. A tau parameter (default 0.85) sets the interpretability threshold: higher tau admits fewer, better-described features and more legible evidence at some accuracy cost. Across COLING and RAID benchmarks the auditable detectors stay close to the fine-tuned black-box classifier they are built on, matching it even at thresholds of 0.7 and above. Released on PyPI and Hugging Face with a demo.

Highlights

  • DetectorScope makes AI text detectors auditable: predictions decompose into named, located, validated features.
  • Example output: 'the word about used as a preposition' (+0.23), 'repeated phrases' (+0.14), 'conversational filler like i said' (-0.05).
  • Architecture: SAEs on the detector's residual stream, validated natural-language descriptions per feature, sparse linear head over them.
  • The logit equals bias plus the sum of per-feature importances, so the evidence is the computation, not a post-hoc explanation.
  • tau parameter trades interpretability for accuracy; default 0.85, range to 0.95.
  • On COLING and RAID benchmarks the auditable pipeline nearly matches the underlying black-box detector's performance.
  • A Google DeepMind project, released via PyPI and Hugging Face with a live demo.
