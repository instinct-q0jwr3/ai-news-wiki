[AINews] Behind Latent Space: Kill My SaaS, Jev & AI in 2026 | AIEi Shanghai

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-04 · updated: 2026-11-13 · confidence: high

Summary

This Latent Space AINews issue admits the Weight-Space Programming claims were presented in an overly favorable light but says the finding is still a big wake-up moment with several independent replications, so it runs no retraction. It then summarizes the day's replications and caveats: Alex Krentsel's cross-generation replication found a sharp capability jump, with the latest models producing Breakout networks scoring up to 22x an RL baseline; Hugging Face reproduced the paper with its ML Intern harness; and a user had a model hand-write a 100K-parameter classifier for fake legal citations that reportedly beat a trained one.

A Zhihu reconstruction of the Qwen edit says the gain is narrower than the headline: 576 of 579 repaired answers had hit the 32K-token limit because of looping, Astra located a 'repetition direction' mainly in layers 15 and 19 MLPs and subtracted it from top neurons, and held-out accuracy rose from 10.6% to 60.4%, mostly existing reasoning that could now finish. Simon Willison reports a laptop reproduction with Qwen 3.8 27B. The issue also features AI Engineer Shanghai and a history-of-Latent-Space interview, and is long; only the opening section was read.

Highlights

  • Latent Space: Weight-Space Programming overstated in places but independently replicated
  • Replications: Krentsel (Breakout nets up to 22x an RL baseline), Hugging Face, a hand-written citation classifier
  • Zhihu reconstruction: Qwen gain mostly fixes looping; held-out accuracy 10.6% to 60.4%
  • Opening section only; remainder of the issue not read
