Qwen 3.8 27B on my laptop can interpret model weights, too

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-03 · updated: 2026-11-14 · confidence: high

Summary

Simon Willison shows a research result that runs on a laptop: Qwen 3.8 27B can interpret model weights, echoing a larger finding in the work he is covering. In that research, GPT-6 Astra reads raw weights and works out what a network was trained to do, and without environment feedback writes small game-playing networks that beat PPO-trained ones from 100 million steps. Asked to improve Qwen3.5-0.8B at math without gradients, it spots a reasoning-loop issue named in Qwen's own model card and edits 0.016% of the weights, lifting MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1% and tripling IFEval. The authors call this Weight-Space Programming.

The post's demo gives a model one policy network's weights and asks which game it was trained on, with no tools; the model answers with a description and a confidence level (the first guess, a Pac-Man-style maze at 72%, was not necessarily right). Willison's contribution is that a smaller open model does a version of this locally, which makes the technique reproducible by anyone.

Highlights

  • Weight-Space Programming: a model reads weights, infers training purpose, and edits them directly
  • Reported edit of 0.016% of Qwen3.5-0.8B's weights lifted MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1%
  • Qwen 3.8 27B can do a version of the interpretation task on a laptop
  • Reproducibility on open models is the post's contribution
