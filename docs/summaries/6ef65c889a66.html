Hello Deep Learning: Reading handwritten digits

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

bert-hubert

Summary

A chapter of Bert Hubert's 'Hello Deep Learning' series that starts reading handwritten digits, building on the FastAI MNIST notebook. Its centerpiece is the universal approximation theorem, quoted from FastAI: it can be mathematically proven that a small function of two weight matrices can solve any computable problem to arbitrary accuracy - approximate any wiggly function with joined line segments, using shorter lines for a closer fit - if you can find the right parameters and make the matrices big enough.

Hubert recommends reading the FastAI notebook after finishing his from-scratch series: it will then make sense and convert from-scratch understanding into the frameworks people actually use.

Highlights

  • The chapter grounds MNIST digit-reading in the universal approximation theorem
  • Quoted via FastAI: any computable problem, approximated by joined line segments
  • The catch: 'if you can find the right parameters' and make matrices big enough
  • Hubert's advice: finish his from-scratch series, THEN read FastAI to bridge to real frameworks
