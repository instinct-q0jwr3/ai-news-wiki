Analysis: AI political groups have spent $78.5M on US federal candidates during the 2026 cycle; AI accelerationists spent $34M and safeguard-focused groups $39M

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-27 · updated: 2026-10-27 · confidence: high

Summary

A CNBC analysis of FEC filings finds AI political groups have spent $78.5 million on US federal candidates in the 2026 cycle, split between accelerationist groups ($34 million) favoring faster development and lighter, uniform rules, and safeguard-focused groups ($39 million). In general-election congressional races with a week to go, accelerationist groups have spent about $8 million against $6.9 million from the safety camp.

Florida's 25th district is a direct face-off: pro-acceleration Think Big spent $310,000 backing Democrat Jared Moskowitz, while safeguard-aligned Defending Our Values spent nearly $857,000 boosting Republican Scott Singer plus $740,000 against a primary rival. The piece contrasts the split with crypto's unified 2024 playbook, where Fairshake's $130 million-plus won 53 of 58 targeted races, and notes the feud risks weakening Silicon Valley's overall clout.

Highlights

  • $78.5M in AI-linked federal political spending this cycle: $34M accelerationist, $39M safeguard
  • FL-25 is a direct proxy fight between the two camps
  • Both sides favor innovation and some guardrails; they differ on speed and state power
  • Contrast: crypto's unified Fairshake strategy went 53-for-58 in 2024
