Study: non-English languages can consume far more AI tokens, with the most expensive using 1.5x-5x more than the cheapest, resulting in a "tokenizer tax"

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-27 · updated: 2026-10-27 · confidence: high

Summary

Rest of World covers a Coalition for Language Equity in AI Research study finding that AI models consume far more tokens in some languages than others: among the 20 most popular internet languages, the most expensive use 1.5 to 5 times more tokens than the cheapest, and across 200 languages the gap grew to 17x in one model. The researchers call it a hidden 'tokenizer tax'.

Beyond price, higher token counts mean higher latency and a smaller effective context window, which degrades service quality - a tutor that forgets earlier mistakes sooner. The study notes rarity is not the only factor; script plays a big role. Interviewees point to open-data efforts like RESPIN, which collected text from native speakers of 38 Indian dialects, as part of the fix.

Highlights

  • 'Tokenizer tax': costliest popular languages use 1.5-5x more tokens than the cheapest
  • Gap reaches 17x across a 200-language set in one model
  • Higher token counts also cut effective context and raise latency
  • Script, not just language rarity, drives token inflation
