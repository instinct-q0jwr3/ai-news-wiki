Growth of AI Through a Cloud Lens

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

mitchell-hashimoto

Summary

Mitchell Hashimoto reads the rise of AI through the historical lens of the rise of cloud. AWS's 2006 launch of S3 and EC2 kicked off the cloud-native platform shift; today's LLM advances feel like the start of another one - not a shift from cloud, but a shift in a different category with the same potential to change how software is built and delivered. His method: compare a few categories of thought and derive predictions.

Category one: immediate value. Early EC2/S3 gave small projects the fastest server and easiest storage, with HTTP APIs that invited automation; AI has the same feel - sentiment analysis is trivial, Copilot code generation is 'kind of decent,' and everything is getting an HTTP API. But immediate value is often mistaken for durable value; crypto failed that test for him ('someone sent me a bitcoin in 2010 and I thought: okay, now what?'). Category two: impracticality is contextual. Early cloud lacked long-lived IPs until 2008, persistent storage until 2008, private networks until VPC in 2009 - impractical for business software, irrelevant for small projects, so it grew where it was practical while the impractical contexts kept shrinking. And the dismissals moved with them: real businesses, then Fortune 500s, then regulated companies, then governments - until US national defense ran on cloud. AI, he argues, sits in the same position today.

Highlights

  • Hashimoto: AI is a platform shift like cloud - compare the categories, derive predictions
  • Immediate value marked early cloud (EC2/S3) and marks AI now (trivial sentiment analysis, decent codegen)
  • Warning: immediate value is often mistaken for durable value - crypto failed his test
  • Early cloud was raw: no long-lived IPs or persistent storage until 2008, no VPC until 2009
  • Impracticality is contextual - cloud grew where it was practical and shrank the rest
  • The dismissal goalposts moved: businesses, Fortune 500, regulated, government - all fell
  • Today US national defense relies on cloud; AI is in the same early position
