Perplexity and Intelligence

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-22 · updated: 2026-11-27 · confidence: high

Summary

OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki proposes using perplexity, alongside other measurements, to support shared development limits and safety standards. His argument is that improvements in pretraining prediction can expose capabilities that task-specific evaluations did not anticipate. The published figures are log-perplexities on a fixed snapshot of OpenAI's internal codebase: he places GPT-3.5 near 0.99, GPT-4 near 0.91 and a recent model associated with strong mathematics results near 0.76. He warns that further capability changes could appear around 0.68-0.70 and that AI-assisted pretraining research might accelerate as soon as 2027.

Those thresholds are OpenAI's own dataset-specific observations and forecasts, not transferable safety boundaries already agreed across labs. Pachocki explicitly notes differences in training mixtures and test sets, the metric's distance from practical tasks, an unknown entropy floor, and gains from reasoning that do not reduce pretraining perplexity. He proposes that labs agree what monitoring or understanding milestone would be required before crossing successive limits; the post does not announce an adopted cross-industry agreement. Its mathematical and acceleration claims were not independently reproduced in this pass.

Highlights

  • Pachocki proposes shared pacing limits informed by perplexity and other measurements
  • Published losses use a fixed snapshot of OpenAI's internal codebase
  • Lower log-perplexity means better average next-token prediction on that dataset
  • The 0.68-0.70 window is a company forecast, not an established universal danger threshold
  • Different datasets and training mixtures impede cross-lab comparison
  • Reasoning can improve capabilities without lowering pretraining loss
  • No adopted industry agreement or independent reproduction is established
