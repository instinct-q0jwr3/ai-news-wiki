Show HN: A USB-C LTE modem that gives my computer a phone line

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-31 · updated: 2026-11-13 · confidence: high

Summary

Koppar's USB-C LTE modem gives a computer its own phone line. The 4-layer board takes a nano-SIM and a single USB-C cable and presents three ordinary USB devices through an on-board hub: LTE data, SMS, and voice calls with call audio over USB, so the computer can answer a call through a headset, hand it to a script, or let an AI agent pick up. There is no battery, screen or speaker - three LEDs report network, ring and call state.

The repo is a model of show-your-work hardware. The board was designed from a one-page spec, then reviewed part by part against the datasheets; REVIEW.md documents every finding with numbers, including the rev-A mistakes now fixed - an antenna feed at the wrong impedance hopping layers, a buck converter 60 mm from its inductor, and a SIM socket facing the wrong way. An RP2040 drives the modem's power and reset lines, fabrication files are ready for JLCPCB's 4-layer stackup with specific via and plugging options, and the KiCad project includes the footprints and 3D models. VoLTE is optional on the EC25-EUX module, with notes on the regions where 2G fallback still matters.
