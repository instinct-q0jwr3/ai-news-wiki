Show HN: Subvocal – Talk to your computer without making noise

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-08 · confidence: high

Summary

Show HN from two Georgia Tech students (YC F26): Subvocal, smart glasses that decode silent mouthing into text at the speed of speech - no audible voice required. The sensing approach is radio-frequency rather than camera-based: because RF 'sees' inside the mouth, the system works with lips closed and no tongue movement, tuned to what the founders call internal speech. The origin story is a TreeHacks hack: two AI-native builders annoyed that they couldn't use Wispr Flow in libraries, offices or public.

The pitch lands on a genuine interface gap. Voice is the natural input for AI assistants but socially unusable in exactly the shared spaces where people spend their days; silent input that keeps pace with speech would unblock voice agents in public. The thread's 223 comments suggest the want is real even if the hardware path - custom glasses, trained per-user - is long. They cite AlterEgo, MIT Media Lab's earlier silent-speech wearable, as prior art.

Worth tracking less as a product than as a signal: the application layer is now mature enough that YC companies are forming around the input bottleneck, not the models.
