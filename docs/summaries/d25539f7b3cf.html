How I use AI

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-04 · updated: 2026-11-07 · confidence: high

Summary

An engineer's annual 'How I use AI' check-in, now from the author's new role as a research engineer at Salesforce AI Research after a year on Microsoft's Windows AI team. He says he spends about $3,000 a month on tokens, and has drafted a personal AI policy - a document about how he wants AI to fit into his life - which he treats as a first draft that may be wrong.

The headline change is coding. He says he no longer writes much code: his workday is managing five or six Claude Code, OpenCode or Codex agents, each in its own Git worktree, writing code, running tests and creating and reviewing PRs. He lists what that buys: prototypes in about 20 minutes, willingness to try crazy ideas because agents fail ultra-fast, and relief from drudgery like diagnosing urgent bugs from a one-line description. He adds that it lets him be kinder in code review because agents do the writing and sweeping change proposals are cheap. The post is long; only the first part was read, so his views on writing and other uses are not covered here.

Highlights

  • Author moved from Microsoft's Windows AI team to Salesforce AI Research; spends about $3,000/month on tokens
  • Drafting a personal AI policy to separate where AI helps from where it hurts
  • Daily work is orchestrating 5-6 coding agents in separate worktrees; he rarely hand-writes code
  • Benefits cited: 20-minute prototypes, ultra-fast failure, no drudgery, kinder code review
