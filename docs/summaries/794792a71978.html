Nvidia's $20 billion Groq deal faces lawsuit alleging startup's stockholders were shortchanged

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-06 · updated: 2026-10-31 · confidence: high

Summary

Two former Groq engineers, Joshua Rubin and Benjamin Serebrin, sued over Nvidia's $20 billion deal to acquire Groq's assets, alleging in Delaware's Court of Chancery that the structure 'squeezed out' stockholders at a lowball price. The December deal was framed as a licensing agreement for Groq's inference technology, with founder Jonathan Ross, president Sunny Madra and other senior leaders joining Nvidia, while Groq continued as an 'independent company' that has raised around $1 billion since June.

The suit alleges Nvidia allocated $17 billion to a license it labeled 'non-exclusive', set aside $3 billion in RSUs for the Groq employees who moved over, and structured the deal so investment funds with board designees enjoyed windfall returns from the later squeeze-out - leaving a conflicted board majority that 'sold the company to Nvidia without the stockholder vote Delaware law requires'. Groq calls the suit meritless and says it will vigorously defend itself.

Highlights

  • Lawsuit: Nvidia's $20B Groq deal shortchanged stockholders, filed in Delaware Chancery.
  • Alleged structure: $17B 'non-exclusive' license + $3B in RSUs for departing Groq employees.
  • Claims Groq's board sold the company without the required stockholder vote.
  • Groq leadership including CEO Jonathan Ross joined Nvidia; Groq continues independently.
  • Groq calls the lawsuit meritless; Nvidia declined comment.
