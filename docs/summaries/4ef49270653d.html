AI-Assisted Paper on Z-Library Cites a TorrentFreak Article That Doesn't Exist

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-24 · updated: 2026-10-28 · confidence: high

Summary

TorrentFreak reviewed a peer-reviewed paper about Z-Library in the journal New Review of Academic Librarianship and found that at least ten of its 44 references could not be located, including a TorrentFreak article that was never published. The invented citations are plausible-looking: they credit real publications and real experts in fitting contexts, such as a Harvard Journal of Law & Technology article attributed to professor Daniel Gervais and a College & Research Libraries article attributed to Carol Tenopir, neither of which exists in the cited issues. The paper also misdescribes the Z-Library criminal case, claiming the DOJ arrested individuals in 2022 when the two alleged operators were arrested in Argentina at the request of the United States.

The paper's acknowledgements hint at the cause: the authors confirm Consensus AI was used to sift the literature and extract information, and ChatGPT to improve the language. TorrentFreak also ran the abstract and conclusion through the AI detector Pangram, which rated both as likely AI-generated. The piece notes the irony that a Taylor & Francis journal paper about a shadow library may have been corrupted by AI, while rightsholders are simultaneously suing AI companies for training on shadow libraries.

Highlights

  • A peer-reviewed paper on Z-Library cites at least ten references that cannot be found, out of 44 total.
  • One fabricated citation points to a TorrentFreak article, 'Publishers continue crackdown on shadow libraries,' that was never published.
  • Invented citations look plausible: real journals, real scholars (Daniel Gervais, Carol Tenopir), fitting contexts, wrong issues and pages.
  • The paper misstates the Z-Library case: the alleged operators were arrested in Argentina at the US request, not 'arrested' by the DOJ.
  • The authors acknowledge using Consensus AI for literature sifting and ChatGPT for language polishing.
  • AI detector Pangram rated the abstract and conclusion as likely AI-generated; such tools can produce false positives.
  • AI chatbots are known to invent references with plausible titles credited to real experts - a documented failure mode now surfacing in published literature.
