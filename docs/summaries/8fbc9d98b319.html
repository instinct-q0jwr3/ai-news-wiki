Involution Without Export Is Wasted Effort

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-23 · updated: 2026-09-25 · confidence: high

tldr-ai

Summary

A DCM investor's essay from Beijing argues the American picture of Chinese AI misses its economic backdrop: 17.9% youth unemployment, retail sales up just 0.6% year over year against 1.5% expected, graduates of top universities driving Didi, and a real estate crash that still weighs on everything. Chinese AI, in this telling, is less a sector emerging from uniform strength than the one engine of the economy the state has decided must work - propped up with subsidized electricity, fast-tracked IPOs and encouraged price wars.

The piece collects quotes from Chinese AI circles that cut against US assumptions: 'When you trail the frontier, openness maximizes reputation per unit of capability. The moment you lead, you close'; 'It's just talking. Everyone is waiting for the American labs to release something'; and bafflement at the phrase 'permanent underclass' and at how many Americans seem to hate AI rather than treating it as inevitable.

Highlights

  • Frames Chinese AI as the one sector of a struggling economy the state has decided must work.
  • Cites 17.9% youth unemployment and retail sales up 0.6% vs 1.5% expected.
  • Argues openness is a strategy of frontier-trailing labs: leaders close up.
  • Chinese practitioners expect the next move to come from American labs.
  • Notes state support via subsidized electricity, fast-tracked IPOs and price wars.
