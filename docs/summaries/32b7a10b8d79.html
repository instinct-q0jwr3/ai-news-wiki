Filing: Vancouver, WA-based Powerlattice, which makes a power delivery chiplet to reduce AI compute power needs by 50%+, raised $86M, after raising $31M in 2025

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-31 · updated: 2026-10-31 · confidence: high

Summary

Powerlattice, a Vancouver, Washington startup building chips that cut the power demands of AI processors, has raised $86 million according to a Friday SEC filing - nearly triple the $31 million it had raised when it emerged from stealth last November with a $25 million Series A led by Playground Global and Celesta Capital, GeekWire reports. The filing shows the company seeking $111 million in total with 18 investors so far; it names neither the investors nor the round.

The product is a power delivery chiplet that sits inside the processor package: conventional designs route current over long paths and waste energy, and Powerlattice says shortening the path can cut energy use by more than half while boosting performance. Board members include former Intel CEO Pat Gelsinger - 'AI is not constrained by capital, it's constrained by power' - and the company was already sampling for GPUs, CPUs and custom accelerators last year. CEO Peng Zou, Gang Ren and Sujith Dermal founded it in 2023.
