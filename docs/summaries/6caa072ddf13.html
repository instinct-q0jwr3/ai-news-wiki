AI: Considerations for people who make decisions

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

bert-hubert ai-policy-regulation

Summary

Bert Hubert turned two recent presentations - to the Dutch Network of Government Service Providers and the Dutch Advisory Council for Science, Technology and Innovation - into an essay for AI decision-makers, and he warns up front it will make everyone angry about some part. His one candidate for consensus: what exactly is the rush? Does every municipality really need to deploy Copilot this year? If AI proves to be a good idea, it will still be there next year.

The piece is deliberately not a both-sides argument. For AI sceptics he concedes the technology can do truly remarkable things - scientists won a Nobel Prize for one useful instance. For enthusiasts he lists reasons for care: producing larger documents faster only for other departments to summarize them again with AI is not progress; AI disrupts organizational career structures, since juniors who no longer do junior work never become seniors; and senior staff may not enjoy careers spent checking AI output. Organizations are acting from FOMO, he argues, yet for most companies and institutions there is no real urgency. (He also discloses that the English version was mostly translated by ChatGPT from his Dutch original, then thoroughly edited by him - which he rationalizes as cheaper than everyone translating it individually.)
