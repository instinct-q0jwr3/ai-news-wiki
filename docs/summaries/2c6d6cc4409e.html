OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Anthropic say they will adopt a new standard that publishers can use to opt out of being scraped by AI across multiple crawlers

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-31 · updated: 2026-10-31 · confidence: high

Summary

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic say they will adopt a shared standard letting publishers opt out of AI scraping across multiple crawlers at once, The Verge reports. The mechanism is a 'policy token' proposed by Kevin Kelley, a technical lead for standards at Anthropic, through the IETF: it lives in a site's robots.txt and applies the publisher's AI training preferences to every participating crawler, instead of sites signaling each AI company separately. Meta's communications head Andy Stone signaled support too.

The context is an escalating scraping conflict: publishers face a continuing influx of AI crawlers, Really Simple Licensing (RSL) offers a way to charge crawlers, and Cloudflare now blocks AI crawlers by default. The four companies say they will restrict their crawlers from any site carrying the shared token.

Highlights

  • One signal applies AI training preferences across all participating crawlers
  • Meta's Andy Stone also signals support
