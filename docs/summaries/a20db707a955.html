Why I'm Building Muse

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-28 · updated: 2026-10-01 · confidence: high

Summary

Alexandr Wang's essay announcing Muse is a manifesto about unexpressed wanting. His premise: people want fiercely but rarely say what they want, and unnamed dreams die 'by a thousand paper cuts' in forms, gatekeepers, and phone calls never made - every unrealized want a quiet tragedy of human potential. Muse is pitched as a second mind, a general manager whose whole job is to find out what you want and make it happen: it hears the half-sentence, builds the plan, sends the email, makes the call, finds the funding, and keeps you on schedule until only the human part - the wanting and dreaming - is left. Wang closes on the uncertainty as the exciting part: the world has been shaped by the few fanatics who made their wants real, and nobody knows what eight billion people with fully switched-on agency will ask for.
