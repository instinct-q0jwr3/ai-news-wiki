Levent Alpöge has solved the Riemann Hypothesis with help of Claude! What does it mean?

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-22 · updated: 2026-11-26 · confidence: high

Summary

Gary Marcus comments on Levent Alpöge's reported claim of a Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, emphasizing prolonged human guidance and the role of Lean and mathlib rather than a single autonomous answer. He argues that success in a formally checkable mathematical domain would not, by itself, establish general intelligence, dependable business automation or a change in commercial margins.

At the 22 November reading, this source supplied commentary rather than an independent verification; the later certification is recorded below. Marcus's descriptions of a 250,000-page artifact, its formal status, the extent of human work and its market reception remained attributed assertions at that reading; no proof artifact or market data was inspected for the commentary summary. The claim is distinct from the earlier hypothetical interview video and from other claimed-proof PDFs already indexed. Neither the title nor repetition by commentators settles the mathematical result or establishes how much of the work was produced by AI.

Verification chronology: the joint Lean FRO/Mathlib certification and MIT register strengthened the evidence on 23 November, when two checks were still pending. At 24 November 05:25 UTC, one end-to-end run was still active. Current status, 25 November at 22:05 UTC: Andrew Sutherland's MIT register says all listed checks have finished. The final lake comparator run on the canonical challenge accepted at 21:49 UTC, after 122 hours 45 minutes. Earlier one- and two-pending counts are historical, not the current state. This source-reported verification is not a build performed for this wiki. It does not validate Wang's all-fields extrapolation or resolve the distinct Masullo manuscript. The register's GRH scope is Dirichlet L-functions. See the verification report.
