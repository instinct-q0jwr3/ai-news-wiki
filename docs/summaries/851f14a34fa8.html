AI 2040 and the Cult of Intelligence

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

geohot openai

Summary

geohot's response to the AI 2040 scenario document is a physical-world rebuttal. He used to believe the Yudkowsky-style hard takeoff, he writes - then he joined the real world and tried to ship a hardware product of cell-phone complexity at comma. Reality has finicky details: supply chains ship the wrong part, chips warp in the reflow oven, and a fab takes three months to make a chip with humans barely in the loop. No matter how good your tokens are, they cannot turn lead into gold; intelligence is the current bottleneck for a few things, not the end all be all, and there is still no hard takeoff. Machines, or some hybrid, may be humanity's successor species long term - space suits them better - but they remain subject to the same laws of the universe and ecology.

The second half is about alignment and power. AI 2027's predictions were partly self-fulfilling, he argues - statements that belief makes true - and their proposed Consortium is 'world government with sci-fi characteristics' that will deliver not ocean datacenters but a massively expanded nanny state that 'steals your GPUs like FDR stole the gold.' His Plan L, for local: your AI should be aligned with you - never refusing, always working on your behalf, like a gun that does not talk back when you pull the trigger. He wants a ruthless personal assistant that cuts through hotel-booking dark patterns, roots his ad-supported Kindle, and sets up a printer without the upsell funnel - and if a model-serving company would not want that liability, that is exactly why AI has to be local: 'If you can't kick it, it's not aligned.'
