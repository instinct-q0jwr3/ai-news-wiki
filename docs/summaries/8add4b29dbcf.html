The UK CMA proposes rules requiring Google and Android OEMs to offer search choice screens on first use and via yearly prompts, new AI assistant rules, and more

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-23 · updated: 2026-09-24 · confidence: high

techmeme google

Summary

The UK's Competition and Markets Authority has proposed rules to loosen Google's grip on search where it is tightest: Android phones and Chrome. The three main recommendations: Google and Android OEMs must offer search choice screens at first use, followed by a yearly prompt; smartphone makers must let users choose among AI assistants that meet technical and security requirements; and search providers must give clearer attribution to publisher content in results. UK users already can choose a search provider, so the proposal largely codifies that while adding yearly reminders. The CMA frames the moves around changing search behavior and new AI services.

Highlights

  • CMA proposal: search choice screens on Android and Chrome at first use plus yearly prompts.
  • Users would also get a choice of AI assistants meeting security requirements.
  • Search providers must attribute publisher content more clearly.
  • Follows measures the CMA recommended at the start of 2026.
