(One) Good AI Is Here

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

anil-dash openai amazon external-evaluation small-specialist-models compute-buildout

Summary

Anil Dash asks a question he says a small cohort keeps circling: what would 'good' AI look like? His framing: the fight over AI has hardened into two camps that both make things worse - critics with legitimate grievances (training without consent, environmental cost, closed and unauditable systems) and zealots who dismiss those grievances while making outlandish capability claims and scaremongering. No wonder public sentiment has turned negative.

Against that backdrop he argues LLMs as a technology might still be separable from Big AI as an industry: earlier eras of machine learning produced useful tools without massive destruction, so the harms may not be intrinsic. The piece builds toward a concrete example he stumbled on in an unexpected place - the YouTube filmmakers at Corridor Digital, where cofounder Niko Pueringer, not a software developer, built something Dash sees as a first glimpse of AI that is actually good: personal, non-surveilled, and useful.

Highlights

  • Dash's core question: can LLM technology provide real benefits without the attendant harms of Big AI?
  • He credits the critics' grievances (consent, environment, closed weights) and blames zealots for dismissing them
  • Argues prior ML eras were useful without being destructive, so harm may not be intrinsic to LLMs
  • The example that convinced him: Corridor Digital's Niko Pueringer, a non-developer, building with AI
  • The contrast he draws is with commercial software and the surveillance model of Big AI companies
  • He calls this case a possible first glimpse of an AI that is 'actually good'
