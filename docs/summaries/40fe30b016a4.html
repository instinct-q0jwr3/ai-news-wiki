LLMs can now identify public figures in images

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-10-02 · confidence: high

Summary

Max Woolf, working on a pipeline to turn images into structured semantic data, tested whether today's multimodal LLMs can name public figures in photos. Through OpenRouter he sent the same images to six models - GPT-4.1, Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Llama 4 Scout, Mistral Small 3.2 and Qwen 2.5-VL - with a system prompt demanding only names, left to right.

The answer: the capability exists, but willingness varies by vendor. Given a famous 2008 Obama campaign photo, GPT-4.1 and Claude refused outright while the other four all answered correctly. Given Woolf's own profile photo - a deliberately non-notable person - every model correctly said there was nobody notable, though Mistral hit a guardrail and falsely claimed it cannot identify people at all. A two-person shot of Mark Zuckerberg and Priscilla Chan also tested ordering: Gemini returned Chan, then Zuckerberg, as instructed. The experiment underlines both how useful identification is for tagging and search pipelines and how inconsistent refusals make it unreliable.
