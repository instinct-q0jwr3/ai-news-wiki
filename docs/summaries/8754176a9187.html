S&P downgraded Meta's outlook from stable to negative, citing high capex and negative free cash flow; META fell 15% in October, its worst month in four years

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-31 · updated: 2026-10-31 · confidence: high

Summary

S&P Global Ratings cut Meta's outlook from stable to negative on Friday, citing 'uncertainty' over elevated capex and negative free cash flow, CNBC reports. The rating itself stays AA-, but the agency questioned 'Meta's ability to monetize its AI investments' relative to peers going cash-flow-negative on data center buildouts, and said of the Muse agent app and foundation models that 'it is unclear when these revenue streams will generate material contributions that exceed their rising costs over the next few years.'

Meta pushed back - 'We respectfully disagree with S&P's assertion about Meta's AI monetization capabilities' - but the numbers behind the worry are public: full-year capex guidance of $135-145 billion, a first-ever negative free cash flow quarter (-$2.12 billion), a stock that fell 7% on earnings despite a revenue beat and finished October down 15% (its worst month in four years), and a plan to raise $95 billion for AI, starting with $35 billion in underwritten public equity.

Highlights

  • S&P cuts Meta's outlook to negative (rating stays AA-), citing capex and negative free cash flow
  • Questions Meta's AI monetization versus peers; Muse revenue timing unclear
  • Meta: 'We respectfully disagree'; sees a very strong credit profile
  • Capex guidance $135-145B; first negative free cash flow quarter at -$2.12B
  • META fell 15% in October, worst month in four years
  • Meta plans to raise $95B for AI, starting with $35B in public equity
