There is no single AI adoption rate

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-05 · confidence: high

Summary

State of AI's methodological note starts with a blunt point: there is no single AI adoption rate, and the numbers people quote at each other are measuring different things. Start with the denominator - a global manager survey, a US spending-platform customer sample, and a representative US business survey describe different populations; their percentages cannot be averaged, and a gap between them is not evidence that one source is wrong. Then check what the measure can see: free tools leave no payment trace, paid seats can go unused, and one team's production deployment is not company-wide change.

Finally, align period and question: a monthly payment measure and a two-week use measure are not interchangeable, and changes in wording, supplier coverage or sample can move a series without any behavior changing - exactly the break Simon Willison documented when the Census Bureau's AI question became a 'Super Intelligence' question. The note is the reference companion to the 2026 report's business analysis, and the right lens for this week's Slok-vs-adoption-chart argument: most disputes about the AI adoption rate are disputes about measurement wearing a policy costume.

Highlights

  • State of AI: there is no single AI adoption rate - sources measure different populations
  • Denominators differ: manager surveys, spending-platform samples, business surveys cannot be averaged
  • Measurement blind spots: free tools, unused seats, team-level vs company-wide use
  • Period and wording changes move series without behavior changing (see: the Census 'Super Intelligence' rewording)
  • Most adoption-rate arguments are measurement arguments in disguise
