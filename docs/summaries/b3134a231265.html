Pluralistic: On the sincerity of AI bosses (17 Sep 2026)

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-10-04 · confidence: high

Summary

Cory Doctorow asks whether AI bosses are sincere in the apocalyptic and utopian claims they make, and answers through an anatomy of fascism. The word comes from the fasces, a bundle of sticks: one twig snaps, a bundle holds. Bundling itself is sound politics - coalitions are everything - but the fascist bundle is incoherent: a coalition of groups who want mutually incompatible things and dream of exterminating one another, kept in line by a strongman's flattery, favors and threats.

The AI movement, he argues, has the same structure: an incoherent bundle of doomers and accelerationists, safetyists and libertarians, all repeating contradictory prophecies about the same technology. Asking whether any individual AI boss 'really believes' the pitch is the wrong question - in a coalition built on contradiction, sincerity is not the load-bearing element; power and the strongman's management of the bundle are.

Highlights

  • Question: are AI bosses sincere in their claims?
  • Fascism's fasces: strength through bundling, but the bundle is incoherent
  • A coalition of enemies wanting incompatible things, held by a strongman
  • The AI camp bundles contradictory prophecies the same way
  • Sincerity is the wrong test - power and coalition management are what matter
