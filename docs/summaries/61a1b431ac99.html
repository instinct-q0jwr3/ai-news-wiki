Here Comes (Forward Deployed) Everybody

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-10-01 · confidence: high

works-on-my-machine

Summary

Scott Werner argues that enterprise software is heading the way of the self-checkout: vendors will no longer ship finished software but 'the raw materials of software', leaving the customer to do the labor. The grocery self-checkout did not eliminate the clerk's work - it transferred it to the shopper. The enterprise version is the forward-deployed-engineer model and AI-customizable platforms: the work of making the software fit moves from the vendor to the customer's own people.

For enterprises this is not a choice made by the user - nobody chooses to enter the Salesforce store. His warning is to notice who ends up holding the bag: when vendors ship raw materials plus AI tooling, 'customization' becomes unpaid implementation labor done by the buyer's staff, and the vendor books it as product.

Highlights

  • Self-checkout economics are coming to enterprise software.
  • Vendors will ship raw materials, not finished software.
  • The clerk's labor did not vanish - the consumer now does it.
  • Forward-deployed/AI-custom models transfer work to customers.
  • Enterprise buyers cannot opt out the way shoppers can.
  • 'Customization' risks becoming unpaid customer labor.
