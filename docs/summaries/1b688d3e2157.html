Police Are Pulling People over Because AI Deems Their Driving "Suspicious"

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-27 · updated: 2026-10-28 · confidence: high

Summary

The ACLU warns that US police are pulling people over because AI deems their driving 'suspicious', opening with Grace Knowles' first-person account: on a September 2025 road trip she was stopped by an officer who cited left-lane driving, then identified himself as an FBI special agent working trafficking and said her plate scan showed suspicious travel patterns.

The officer showed her the software in his car - a heat map of the US with circles marking where she had been, each clickable to a photo of her license plate, collected by cameras 'everywhere', including on the backs of highway signs. The ACLU frames this as a growing pattern of AI-driven suspicion without a specific crime, with more fraught encounters ahead for many drivers.

Highlights

  • First-person ACLU account of an AI-flagged 'suspicious travel' stop
  • Officer showed a heat map of the driver's movements from plate scans
  • Cameras sit on highway sign backs, out of sight
  • Framed as suspicionless surveillance turning into traffic stops
