PenGPT: A Generative Transformer for Handwriting and Sketches

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-25 · updated: 2026-11-25 · confidence: high

Summary

Sam Greydanus describes PenGPT, small transformers for handwriting and sketches using a shared tokenization of pen movements. A six-million-parameter model adapts to handwriting examples and a fifty-four-million-parameter model draws from text-conditioned representations, with data preparation and ablation studies central to the account.

The post distinguishes curated hero images from unselected outputs and reports generated handwriting less legible than real samples, 54% versus 66% on its measure. Sketches struggle with binding and directional instructions, and reconstructed strokes are inferred from scans rather than recorded pen order. Results are author-reported, not independently reproduced, and the training datasets are not released.

Highlights

  • Handwriting and drawing use different model sizes
  • Pen motion is encoded with grid moves and BPE
  • Data filtering and captions materially affect results
  • Hero sketches are selected rather than random
  • Unselected results reveal binding failures
  • Legibility remains below the real-ink baseline
  • Scanned handwriting yields inferred stroke order
