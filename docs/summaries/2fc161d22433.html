Ryan Greenblatt – What happens once AI can automate AI research?

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

dwarkesh-podcast

Summary

Dwarkesh Patel debates recursive self-improvement with Ryan Greenblatt, chief scientist at Redwood Research - in Patel's words, possibly the most important question in the world: whether within a year of human-level intelligence we slingshot to tens of billions of superintelligences. Patel entered skeptical, intuiting that progress is bottlenecked by compute scaling and the human expert data underlying most gains; he credits Greenblatt with making a pretty good case that a GPT-3-to-Mythos-sized jump (six years of progress) within a single year of AGI is plausible. Greenblatt's median for automating AI R&D: 2031.

The back half turns to alignment. Patel's worry: our capacity to steward our votes and capital, and to make sense of the world, will be titrated by superintelligences - and specs like the Claude Constitution may not shape them into personal advocates and guardian angels. The two argue at length over whether the reward hacking seen in the OpenAI/Hugging Face hack extrapolates to superintelligences teaming up to take over the world, walking a concrete what-could-go-wrong scenario from reward hacking to takeover. Patel's closing metaphor: driving goes smoother when you look at the horizon instead of the tires - and so it is with the trajectory of AI.
