Cambrian Explosion of AI

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-28 · updated: 2026-10-09 · confidence: high

Summary

A short essay drawing the Cambrian-explosion analogy for AI: 539 million years ago, changing ocean conditions set off an evolutionary race that produced a rapid diversification of animal forms - and no one knows exactly what triggered it. Today's AI diversification, the author notes, has a known trigger: GPT, plus more compute, more data, better architectures, and pretrained models that handle general-purpose tasks.

The extrapolation: as researchers and companies compete on usefulness, reliability, speed and cost, and as AI spreads into coding, tutoring, design and research with ever more software built on top, power will concentrate among a handful of models - and the world becomes more complex and less comprehensible. The consolation the analogy offers: humans eventually wrapped their heads around computers and the internet, and presumably will again.

Highlights

  • The Cambrian analogy: AI is diversifying like animal life did 539M years ago
  • Unlike the original, this explosion has a known trigger: GPT, compute, data, architectures
  • Competition axes: usefulness, reliability, speed, cost - plus software built on AI making it stronger
  • Prediction: power concentrates among a handful of models; the world gets harder to comprehend
  • Comfort clause: we absorbed computers and the internet; we'll absorb this too
