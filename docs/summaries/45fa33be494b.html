0rwx – A simple model of AI usage, inspired by Unix permissions

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-23 · updated: 2026-11-25 · confidence: high

Summary

0rwx proposes a small vocabulary for discussing AI involvement in work, borrowing the idea of read and write permissions from Unix. Its stated aim is to distinguish cases in which AI cannot access the work, can read it, can change it, or produces work that the person can no longer meaningfully scrutinize. The key axis is the human's ability to inspect the result, not simply the tool's brand or whether a model was used at all.

This is the site's author's proposed communication framework, not an operating-system permission mechanism, a certification or a measured scale of risk. The retrieved page's display styling obscures portions of the level names, so the summary describes the distinctions rather than assigning uncertain exact labels. Nothing here establishes that disclosing a level makes a workflow safe or that two people can apply the labels consistently.

Highlights

  • A proposed vocabulary inspired by Unix read/write permissions
  • The framework concerns the ability to scrutinize generated work
  • It distinguishes no access, read access and permission to change work
  • Its strongest boundary concerns work the human can no longer inspect
  • A conceptual label is not an enforced security permission
  • Styled level names were only partly exposed by text extraction
  • No validation of consistency or safety outcomes is supplied
