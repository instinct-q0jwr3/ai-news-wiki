Researchers face record-low chances of getting a grant partly due to rise of AI

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-05 · confidence: high

Summary

Grant agencies are drowning in polished applications, and experts tell Trouw that AI-generated proposals are a big reason. For the European Research Council's 2027 Starting Grant, 7,710 early-career researchers applied and 373 will be funded - a success rate below five percent. The EU's Marie Curie program drew 21,627 submissions, up nearly 27% in a year. Dutch funder NWO says it aims for 20-25% award rates 'because otherwise too many scientists are busy in vain,' but expects to announce a sub-10% success rate on at least one program soon.

The mechanism is familiar from journals and conferences: AI makes it cheap to produce applications that are uniformly tight and polished, so the old filters stop working. Radboud University's Serge Horbach, who reviews for the Flemish funder FWO, says weak applications used to be easy to spot by poor content or language; 'now almost everything is tight and polished.' The polish survives until the interview: invited candidates sometimes 'fall rather short,' unable to explain how they'd actually carry out the research - having already consumed an interview slot a stronger candidate needed.

The result is a system experts call unsustainable: fixed grant counts, surging submissions, success rates at record lows, and reviewers burning time on applications whose authors may not be able to execute them. It's the academic-funding edition of the same volume-over-signal dynamic AI has brought to hiring pipelines, conference reviewing, and search results.
