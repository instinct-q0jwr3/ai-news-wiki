The Surprising Effectiveness of Deleting Weights in LLM Post-Training

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-11 · updated: 2026-11-13 · confidence: high

Summary

A paper shows that zeroing fewer than 0.05% of a pretrained LLM's weights, with nothing else changed, matches full fine-tuning across three post-training regimes: RL with verifiable rewards, supervised fine-tuning and on-policy distillation. The method, Deletion Tuning, learns a binary mask over the gate projection of transformer blocks. The mask is hundreds to thousands of times smaller than a full checkpoint, and the model forgets far less: under RL, prior-task accuracy stays near base level while full fine-tuning, LoRA and random sparse tuning lose up to 5.4 points.

Highlights

  • Deleting under 0.05% of weights matches full fine-tuning
  • Works across RLVR, SFT and distillation
  • Mask is hundreds to thousands of times smaller
  • Much less forgetting than FFT or LoRA
  • Analysis of why masking suffices via stable rank
