Award-Winning AI Literature

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-22 · updated: 2026-10-24 · confidence: high

Summary

A running list documents literary awards granted to works where AI was used - or suspected - in the writing process, and the cases show award bodies struggling to define the category. Kamome Ashizawa's 'Are You There?' won a 2022 Nikkei Hoshi Shinichi Excellence Award after he generated 101 stories in three weeks with AI; the winner was mostly his, the other hundred mostly machine. Toh Katagiri's 'The Last Painter' (2025 Excellence Award) was classified AI-assisted without the organizers saying how, though a fellow finalist says Katagiri claimed every sentence was written personally.

The list gets stranger: Shamizui's 'Genome Tower' took the 13th Hoshi Shinichi Grand Prix in 2026 with disclosed AI use apparently limited to inventing a fictional disease name - and organizers still counted it among the year's AI-assisted winners. The catalog is becoming the reference record for a debate literary institutions are having in real time: how much machine involvement makes a work 'AI literature,' and who gets to decide.

Highlights

  • 2022: 'Are You There?' - 101 AI-generated stories in three weeks; the winner was mostly human-written
  • 2025: 'The Last Painter' classified AI-assisted, though the author reportedly claimed every sentence was his
  • 2026 Grand Prix: 'Genome Tower' counted as AI-assisted for an AI-invented disease name
  • Shows award bodies improvising definitions of AI literature case by case
