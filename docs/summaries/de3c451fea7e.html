AI unlocks Gödel's notebooks, said to contain hundreds of proved theorems

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-13 · updated: 2026-11-14 · confidence: high

Summary

The Guardian reports Jan von Plato’s use of Claude to interpret transcriptions of Kurt Gödel’s 1,009-page workbooks alongside scholarly material. Von Plato says the model connected ideas across notebooks and produced candidate theorem reconstructions that his team checked with the Coq proof assistant.

The reported results include statements verified as written, others repaired with clearer assumptions, and some rejected as false. This is not evidence that every AI reconstruction was correct or that Gödel had completed all the later results: an outside logician stresses the historical difference between promising notes and a finished published proof.

Highlights

  • Transcriptions and scholarship were supplied to Claude
  • Human page-by-page review remained necessary
  • Coq was used to check reconstructed results
  • Some statements required repairs or were false
  • Historical credit remains a separate question
