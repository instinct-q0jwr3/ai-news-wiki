Everything is an AI scam now

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-05 · updated: 2026-11-06 · confidence: high

Summary

David Ralph Lewis argues that everything online is becoming an AI scam. Videos on YouTube now carry ads for scammy products, and the junk is leaking elsewhere. His real complaint is how little verification there is: platforms take anything an advertiser pays for, and scammers only need a tiny share of people to get through to profit.

He gives a personal example from the spam in his inbox. A pitch for a blackout-poetry book sounded engaged and complimentary, but it clearly came from someone who had not read his own description of the book. Spam has gotten more sophisticated at imitating care. His conclusion is that society has decided AI is inevitable and accepted the breakdown of truth as the cost.

Highlights

  • YouTube and other platforms increasingly carry scammy AI-made ads, with little verification
  • Scammers need only a tiny response rate for the economics to work
  • A spam email imitated close reading of his book while clearly not having read it
  • Opinion post: the cost of 'AI is inevitable' is the breakdown of trust
