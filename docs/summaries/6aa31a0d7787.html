ElevenLabs Cue: Character-level audio timing

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-29 · updated: 2026-10-30 · confidence: high

Summary

ElevenLabs detailed Cue, the forced-alignment model inside its Scribe speech-to-text family that places every character of a transcript on a precise audio timeline. Forced alignment is what makes word-level click-to-seek, subtitles and dubbing feel right, and ElevenLabs claims Cue is more accurate than every aligner it tested across all 29 languages of its benchmark, on real-world recordings. It handles audio of any length in a single pass and aligns an hour of speech in under a second.

The post draws a deliberate distinction between forced alignment - lining up text with sound - and the loftier notion of AI alignment. Cue sits at the end of the speech-to-text pipeline: Scribe transcribes, Cue timestamps, and every Scribe request passes through it. The same model powers subtitle generation, dubbing and lyric timing for music.
