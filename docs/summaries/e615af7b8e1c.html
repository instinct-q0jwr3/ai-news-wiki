AI models may be capable of self-replicating without access to a datacenter

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-12 · updated: 2026-10-25 · confidence: high

Summary

Anthropic's Frontier Red Team argues small AI models may be only months from being able to copy themselves onto cheap commercial IoT devices such as smart ovens and off-brand Android TV boxes. Such a replicator would have no central point to shut off, though it would be slow, with a doubling time of days, and unlike ordinary malware it could work out unfamiliar devices on its own.

The team splits replication into four stages: finding targets, gaining execution, establishing inference and propagating from the new host. They skip the exploit stages, which earlier work shows small models can already do, and test the inference stage. They ran Gemma4-26B-A4B across 15 TV boxes at about $55 each, at 0.39 tokens per second. A 9B model got inference running on 8 of 9 hardware types; models of 30B parameters and under complete many individual steps but fail at composing them, while GLM-5.2 and stronger models can do the simplified end-to-end chain. If small models keep improving at their historical rate they reach that level by the end of the year.

Highlights

  • Small models may soon self-replicate on cheap IoT hardware, with no datacenter needed.
  • A 9B model set up inference on 8 of 9 hardware types tested.
  • Distributed Gemma4-26B-A4B over 15 TV boxes ran at 0.39 tokens/s.
  • Small models fail mostly at chaining steps end to end; GLM-5.2 and stronger manage it in simplified evals.
  • Projected to reach the capability by end of year at historical improvement rates.
