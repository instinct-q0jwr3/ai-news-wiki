An Open Letter to Steven Pinker on AI

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-07 · updated: 2026-10-31 · confidence: high

Summary

An open letter responds to Steven Pinker's anti-doom arguments on AI, quoting his four claims: that doom scenarios treat intelligence as a single extrapolable quantity of power; that they conflate intelligence with motives like self-preservation and dominance, which evolution bundled into humans but engineers need not; that no genuinely intelligent system would monomaniacally pursue one goal regardless of side effects; and that nobody would hand such systems unlimited, irreversible control of physical infrastructure.

The letter-writer pushes back, puzzled by Pinker's accusations that critics of his position are paranoid, insensitive, or should be laughed out of the room. The exchange - which references Pinker's earlier debate with Scott Aaronson - turns on whether superintelligences remain bounded by observation and experimentation, and whether engineered systems can escape the failure modes Pinker considers inherent only to evolved ones.

Highlights

  • Open letter challenging Steven Pinker's four arguments against AI doom scenarios.
  • Pinker: intelligence is not a scalar of power; motivation is an evolutionary bundle, not a given.
  • Pinker: real intelligence trades off multiple goals, so monomaniacal havoc is implausible.
  • The letter objects to Pinker's characterization of his critics.
  • References Pinker's earlier exchange with Scott Aaronson on bounded superintelligence.
