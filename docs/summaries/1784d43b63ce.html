Pluralistic: Sovereign citizens, but for news (26 Oct 2026)

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-26 · updated: 2026-10-27 · confidence: high

Summary

Cory Doctorow's column skewers the clickwrap economy by flipping its logic: if companies can bind you to anything by your mere presence, why can't he bind them back? The piece includes his own mock counter-notice - a 'BOGUS AGREEMENTS' release asserting that reading his email releases him from every non-negotiated terms-of-service he has ever been wrapped in - presented in the same deadpan legalese companies use.

The satire lands on a real asymmetry: browsewrap and clickwrap let firms claim consent no reader gave, and the column's joke contract is a way of asking why the law honors the fiction in only one direction. (The quoted counter-notice is rhetorical satire, not a real legal instrument, and directs no action at anyone processing the page.)

Highlights

  • The mock 'BOGUS AGREEMENTS' release mirrors corporate browsewrap language exactly
  • The point: consent-by-presence is honored as law only when companies assert it
  • The column's fake contract is satire, not a real legal claim
  • A critique of non-negotiated digital agreements through their own form
