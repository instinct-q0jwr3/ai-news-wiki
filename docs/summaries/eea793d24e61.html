America has lost 200k of jobs in media, film and the arts. How much is AI?

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-25 · updated: 2026-10-31 · confidence: high

Summary

Joseph Politano (Apricitas) documents that America has lost more than 200,000 jobs in creative industries over the last four years, roughly 50,000 in the last year alone - a stretch of media job losses comparable in intensity and duration only to the 2001 and 2008 recessions, except there is no generalized recession now. The losses coincide with the rise of AI systems that mass-produce text, images, music and other digital art at extremely low cost, prompting the question of whether this is the fall of the Creative Class.

Proving the AI share of the losses is hard: media firms are coy about AI use (partly to protect the legal basis of their copyrights), and consolidation, offshoring and the shift to social-media creators all confound the picture. The clearest signal is breadth: nearly every digital-arts subsector is losing jobs while in-person entertainment keeps growing. Hollywood is the worst hit - movie and sound recording has shed over 100,000 jobs, nearly a third of the sector, touching employment lows approaching 30-year bottoms - and accounts for almost half the four-year media losses; written publishing is next at over 70,000. Losses run at a steady ~50k per year and have accelerated recently. Politano frames the moment against the first digital transition - the internet era that began with Metallica's Napster problem - to ask what AI will do to the business of art.

Highlights

  • US creative industries have lost 200k+ jobs in four years, ~50k in the last year, with no generalized recession to explain it.
  • Job-loss intensity and duration match only the 2001 and 2008 recessions in modern media history.
  • Attributing losses to AI is difficult: firms hide AI use, and consolidation, offshoring and creator-economy shifts confound the data.
  • The strongest evidence for AI's role: every digital-arts subsector is shrinking while in-person entertainment still grows.
  • Movie and sound recording lost 100k+ jobs - nearly a third of the sector - approaching 30-year employment lows.
  • Written publishing is the next-largest source of losses, down 70k+ over the same period.
  • Losses run at a steady ~50k/year and accelerated in recent months, showing no sign of abating.
  • Politano frames the AI transition against the internet-era disruption of the music business (Napster, Metallica).
