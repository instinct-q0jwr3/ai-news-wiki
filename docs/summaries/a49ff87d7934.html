Is Compute the New Oil? (And Are We All F**ked?)

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-17 · updated: 2026-11-18 · confidence: high

Summary

Joan Westenberg explores the analogy between compute and oil, arguing that inference and agent workloads make compute a recurring economic input rather than a one-time software cost. The examined passages connect chip scarcity, infrastructure spending and rising consumption per task.

The analogy is an analytical framing, not a physical equivalence or a guaranteed forecast of prices. The long essay was read in part, and quoted market figures were not independently audited. Hardware efficiency, utilization, competition and energy all complicate the simple scarcity story.

Highlights

  • Essay treats compute as a recurring input
  • Agent workflows can multiply inference demand
  • Oil analogy has explicit limits
  • Selected market figures are not independently audited
  • Future prices remain uncertain
