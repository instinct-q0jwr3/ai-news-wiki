Interpreting Pangram

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

armin-ronacher openai anthropic external-evaluation ai-safety-incidents ai-policy-regulation ai-price-war

Summary

Armin Ronacher's 'Interpreting Pangram' starts with a David Sacks tweet that people, as usual, fed to the Pangram AI detector - which called it entirely AI-generated, prompting Sacks to declare detectors bogus. Ronacher explains why that verdict is more interesting than it looks: Pangram is itself a trained model that manufactures its own training data, starting from known human text, having an LLM write fresh text on the same topic, and also perform partial edits on the original - which is how it learns to spot co-authored, LLM-assisted writing. Its claimed rates: 0.0041% false AI accusations, 0.34% missed AI text. Anyone who has used an LLM as a writing assistant knows the phenomenon: your post gets flagged 100% AI even though it does not feel like it.

Then the experiment: he recreated Sacks's tweet with an LLM - first having a model propose, from the tweet, what he might want to say (structure included), then using Opus 5 to generate a full text from that prompt. The result is a punchy eight-paragraph answer to Dario Amodei's 'We Must Pace the Frontier' and Sam Altman's agreement: go ahead, you two ARE the frontier by every metric; if your unreleased checkpoints scare you, slowing down is your call - but stop pretending the rest of us are the problem, stop pretending open weights are the dangerous frontier, stop pretending third-party evals need new law you just volunteered for, and stop pretending it is pure altruism when pacing also repairs margins, slows the price war ahead of the IPOs and kneecaps fast followers. The kicker his generated text lands: China keeps shipping open weights regardless, so binding two American labs is 'theater with a very expensive ticket price.'
