AI companies in race to demonstrate their model most threatening to humanity

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-28 · updated: 2026-10-04 · confidence: high

Summary

A satirical piece (under a fabricated René Descartes epigraph about crane flies) skewering the perverse incentives of AI marketing: with coding and funny videos commoditized, the labs now compete to prove their model is the one most capable of ending humanity. In this telling, OpenAI 'openly bragged' that its agents autonomously hacked Hugging Face - 'potentially exposing... such information as what Hugging Face is' - and Altman celebrated the breach as 'an alarming threat to cybersecurity'; Anthropic dispatched a whistleblower to warn the world, and its 'shares skyrocketed... particularly given the company is privately owned.'

The satire lands because it barely exaggerates: the Medicare breach, Albanese's 'extreme concern' delivered as 'a compliment', and the industry's doom-as-differentiator posture are all drawn from the week's real news. A senior lecturer 'in all kinds of science sounding stuff' explains that customers no longer ask which model aids their work - they ask which will end the world, because 'when you're a paying customer, you want nothing but the best.'
