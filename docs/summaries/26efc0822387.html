Pushing the US Open-Weight Frontier

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-25 · updated: 2026-11-26 · confidence: high

Summary

Harvey and Baseten report adapting a legal-agent RL recipe to four US open-weight models, with GLM-5.3 as an additional comparison. All models improve legal task completion, but reward-model preferences do not improve equally: some models gain rubric coverage without a significant head-to-head work-product preference advantage.

The research uses LLM judges and selected checkpoints with the highest measured held-out all-pass scores, which limits interpreting those scores as untouched final-test estimates. Lawyer calibration is planned rather than a substitute for the reported generative judge. The authors acknowledge longer reasoning as a possible contributor and report an efficiency comparison for one model. These research checkpoints are not processing customer data, and the results do not establish readiness for unsupervised legal advice.

Highlights

  • Four US models plus GLM-5.3 are studied
  • Muse Spark Open reaches a reported 30.3% LAB all-pass
  • Rubric gains do not guarantee preference gains
  • Checkpoint selection uses repeated held-out measurements
  • LLM judges score criteria and paired work products
  • Token use and reasoning effort affect comparisons
  • Authors say no customer data is used
  • Research models are not customer-serving deployments
