The Facebook Fake-out

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-29 · updated: 2026-09-30 · confidence: high

anil-dash openai meta ai-policy-regulation

Summary

Anil Dash (or a similar longtime Meta-watcher) lays out 'the Facebook fake-out': the cycle by which Meta ships something transgressive, weathers the outrage until attention moves on, then brings the thing back and points to the vanished backlash as proof it was fine all along. The essay frames this as part of Silicon Valley's broader 'inevitability' strategy - treating new technologies as forces of nature so society goes along with extreme plans - enabled by short attention spans, weak institutional accountability, and the coercive lock-in that makes opting out of Meta's platforms costly. Written as a reference to send to legislators and journalists 'the next time they're getting fooled by Mark Zuckerberg', and acutely relevant in the Muse era.

Highlights

  • Names the cycle: ship the transgressive thing, wait out the outrage, reintroduce it later
  • Inside Meta, the moved-on public is read as proof the idea was never bad
  • Part of the 'inevitability' strategy: present new tech as a force of nature
  • Relies on short attention spans, unaccountable institutions, and lock-in coercion
  • Written as a reference for legislators and journalists covering Meta
  • Timely again as Muse's permission scandals repeat the pattern
