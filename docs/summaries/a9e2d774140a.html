College Is Coming Apart After Successive, Overlapping Crises

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-21 · updated: 2026-09-21 · confidence: high

hacker-news

Summary

Ian Bogost argues in The Atlantic that AI did not break American college so much as expose that it was already breaking. Students can now do essentially all schoolwork automatically, problem sets, code, essays, leaving professors unsure whether they are teaching or LARPing for students pretending to learn, while some faculty offload recommendation letters, grant proposals and even papers to AI. Meanwhile universities invest in AI initiatives, infrastructure and degree programs, racing toward and away from the technology at the same time.

His deeper claim: AI combined with two other recent crises, the pandemic and the second Trump administration's research-funding cuts, to amplify a structural truth, that American college is incompatible with itself. 'College' is not one thing but a bundle of unrelated functions, teaching, research, credentialing, socialization, held together by custom, history and accident. The bundle was always tenuous, and the institutions inside it are too entrenched to separate even as the bundle loses its reason to hold together.

Highlights

  • Thesis: AI, the pandemic, and Trump-era research-funding cuts amplify that American college is 'incompatible with itself'.
  • Students can automate nearly all schoolwork: problem sets, code, essays.
  • Professors describe not knowing whether they teach or 'LARP' for students pretending to learn.
  • Faculty themselves offload recommendation letters, grant proposals and papers to AI.
  • AI genuinely benefits research: protein-structure prediction, materials design, drug discovery.
  • Administrators simultaneously police classroom AI use and launch AI initiatives, infrastructure and degree programs.
  • 'College' is a bundle of unrelated things held together by custom, history and accident, with little reason to remain together.
  • Author: Ian Bogost, writing in The Atlantic.
