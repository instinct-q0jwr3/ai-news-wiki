AI Workers' Inquiry 2026

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-24 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

lobsters labor-displacement

Summary

AI Workers' Inquiry 2026 collects first-hand testimonies from workers about how AI is landing in their jobs, and much of it is ambivalent or worse. A software engineer says they 'started enjoying my job again as soon as I stopped using AI to code'; another warns that the more you use a coding assistant, the worse you get at doing it yourself; a data analyst notes there is no learning through exposure and mistakes when AI aggregates and distills away the details.

Others describe coercion and misaligned incentives: AI baked into KPIs with remuneration contingent on use; staff told to burn as many tokens as possible, then punished for using too many. A research scientist writes, 'I want the AI I build to benefit humanity, not to facilitate a genocide,' and one worker reports, 'Nobody was consulted.'

Highlights

  • A 2026 inquiry gathers worker testimonies on AI at work.
  • 'I started enjoying my job again as soon as I stopped using AI to code.'
  • Skill atrophy: heavy assistant use erodes the ability to do it yourself.
  • AI use is being baked into KPIs, pay and bonuses.
  • Mixed messages: pushed to use tokens, then disciplined for using too many.
  • 'Nobody was consulted.'
