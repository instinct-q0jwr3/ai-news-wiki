Woman evicted after landlord's AI assistant told her she could delay rent

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-28 · updated: 2026-11-28 · confidence: high

Summary

A Substack report describes a North Carolina tenant, Laura Mack, who was evicted on 19 November after emails signed by her apartment complex appeared to accept a 30 November payment date for nearly $4,000 in overdue rent. She says a leasing employee later attributed the exchange to an AI assistant named Olivia, without disclosure in the emails themselves.

The account relies on emails reviewed by local broadcaster WRAL and the tenant's statements; the property has not publicly confirmed that an AI assistant sent the messages or whether it was authorized to approve extensions. Whether the emails formed an enforceable agreement is unresolved, and the cited Air Canada chatbot ruling is a different jurisdiction, not legal precedent here. A state senator's disclosure bill has not advanced. This is a reported dispute, not an adjudicated finding about AI liability.

Highlights

  • Emails reportedly approved a later rent date before eviction
  • Tenant attributes the messages to an undisclosed AI assistant
  • Property owner has not confirmed that explanation
  • Enforceability of the exchange remains legally unresolved
  • Air Canada ruling is illustrative, not controlling
  • Proposed disclosure bill has not passed
  • No legal or housing action was taken
