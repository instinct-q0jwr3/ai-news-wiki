We're going to need default hard budget caps on pretty much everything

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-04 · updated: 2026-10-08 · confidence: high

Summary

Simon Willison argues that pay-by-usage services and APIs should ship hard budget caps by default: a monthly limit after which the service is cut off and returns errors, not a warning email. Coding agents and personal agents make it easy to spin up code that spends money, and nobody wants to find at midnight that a rogue service ran up thousands of dollars.

He admits some businesses would not want their application to start failing when a cap is hit, but expects most people to prefer errors to a surprise bill of $10,000 or more. Anyone who wants to live dangerously should be able to opt out with a clear checkbox, such as the 'Remove the budget cap' wording he quotes from a hosting platform.

Highlights

  • Hard caps cut a service off; soft caps only warn.
  • Agents make accidental spending much more likely.
  • Caps should be on by default, with an explicit opt-out.
  • Counterargument: business apps erroring out when the cap hits.
