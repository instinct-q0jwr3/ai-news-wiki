Summary

Cory Doctorow's 'Eternal Sloptember' opens with a theory of Zuckerberg: working in social media - treating people as mass statistical abstractions - is a cognitohazard that could make anyone solipsistic. But for Zuck, Doctorow argues, the causal arrow points the other way: he founded Facebook because he doesn't really believe other people are fully real, at least not as real as he is.

He traces this from Facebook's dorm-room origins to its present, arguing the same disregard surfaces at every critical juncture of the company's history - the through-line behind the platform's slop era. (Summary based on the article's opening sections.)
