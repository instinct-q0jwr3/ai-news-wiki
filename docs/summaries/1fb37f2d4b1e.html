AI: Overwegingen voor wie erover gaat

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

bert-hubert

Summary

Bert Hubert wrote this for people who must actually decide on AI policy, after talks at the Dutch public-service network NPD and the science advisory council AWTI. His honest position: AI the phenomenon is genuinely Nobel-worthy and can do amazing things - and organizations still have no business rushing. 'Is there really such a hurry? Does every municipality need Copilot this year?'

His checklist for decision-makers: don't buy FOMO - if AI is a good idea it will still be one next year; the IP, climate, energy and digital-autonomy problems are real and vendors will make them yours (ask Microsoft to indemnify you for Copilot's output and enjoy the silence); juniors replaced by AI means no future seniors; almost nobody measures whether AI rollouts actually made people more productive or happier; and a burst bubble won't kill AI but will make it suddenly expensive - a problem if your organization has grown dependent. (The page is in Dutch, with an English version linked; summary from the Dutch original.)

Highlights

  • Hubert's advice to AI decision-makers: impressive tech, but no hurry
  • FOMO is the main driver - 'there is no boat to miss' for most organizations
  • Ask vendors to indemnify AI output IP claims; watch them dodge
  • Replacing juniors with AI leaves you without future seniors
  • Rollouts happen without success criteria or measurement
  • A bubble burst makes AI expensive, not gone - dependency is the risk
