Agents Are Cheap. Agency Is Everything.

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-23 · updated: 2026-10-28 · confidence: high

Summary

JA Westenberg's essay 'Agents Are Cheap. Agency Is Everything.' starts from the Latin agere - to do, to act - and notes that software agents are collapsing the cost of doing things toward zero: booking flights, writing code, drafting pitches. The bottleneck moves to the principal. Being a principal means actually wanting something: choosing what needs doing, judging the finished product without self-deception, and eating the blame when it blows up. None of that gets easier because the robots got smarter - it gets harder.

The essay's provocation is that agency is the scarce skill of the agent era: when anything can be done at the push of a button, the question becomes what is worth doing and who answers for it. Delegation without judgment produces slop at scale, so the people who thrive will be those with taste, conviction, and tolerance for responsibility - the un-automatable parts of being in charge.

Highlights

  • Thesis: agents make doing cheap; agency - wanting, choosing, owning outcomes - becomes the scarce skill
  • Principal-agent framing: software acts, but you decide what needs doing and judge the result
  • Smarter agents make the principal's job harder, not easier
  • Delegation without judgment produces slop at scale
  • The valuable traits: taste, conviction, tolerance for responsibility
