Or: The Beaver Who Learned To Measure Twice The beaver once asked the Oracle if this was technically a contrapositive.

“To the thing logicians complain about,” said the Oracle.

“Don’t tell them.” Scott Werner Jan 28, 2026 103 12 20 Share There’s a beaver who lives upstream from here.

Highlights

  • But this beaver kept building dams that... well… One collapsed in spring.
  • It speaks in a voice like wind through telephone wires, if telephone wires grew in forests, which they don’t, but… whatever… just roll with it… The beaver asked the Oracle: “Build me a dam.” The Oracle hummed.
  • “You didn’t say no fish,” said the Oracle.
  • But this time, before asking for a dam, he asked: “What makes a dam good?” The Oracle thought about this for a long time.
  • This is Deductive Prompting , which is a fancy way of saying: make the Oracle say what good is, out loud, before you make it do good.
  • This is classic few-shot prompting, but viewed through a “think before you build” lens.
  • Manuel said: “Before you build a dam, build a language for describing dams.” The beaver didn’t understand.
  • So the beaver asked the Oracle to design a language.
