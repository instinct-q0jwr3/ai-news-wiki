Oracle files to sell $20B in new stock, bonds to finance AI buildout

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-04 · confidence: high

Summary

Oracle filed Monday to sell $10 billion in common stock and $10 billion in senior notes to finance its AI buildout - exactly the $20 billion in additional capital-market funding it previewed at its investor day last week for the fiscal 2027-2030 cycle. Shares still fell more than 6%. 'This seems to be exactly what they previewed,' Baird's Rob Oliver told CNBC. 'So I'm not sure why the market is freaking about it.'

The freak-out is about the accumulated balance sheet, not the announcement. Oracle shares are down 33% this year and 50% over twelve months. Its investment-grade 4.95% bond due 2031 now yields 7.267%; S&P cut the rating to BBB- in July, one notch above junk. Five-year credit default swaps trade at a spread of 296, up 270% in a year - 'more in line with a high-end junk bond rating,' per S&P Global Market Intelligence. Debt stood at $125.34 billion as of August 31.

The company says proceeds go to general corporate purposes - capex, debt repayment, investments - and that vendor financing, customer-provided hardware and customer prepayments will carry it to positive free cash flow in the second half of 2028. It also forecast at least $225 billion in fiscal 2030 revenue. Dynasty Financial's Pat Nerney states the execution question plainly: if Oracle delivers on the plan it will be rewarded, but 'if they have to come back to market in the back half of '27 after telling everyone this was the last of the capital needed... look out.'

Highlights

  • Oracle filed to sell $10B in stock and $10B in senior notes - the $20B it had already capped for the FY2027-2030 buildout; shares fell 6%+ anyway
  • The credit market is pricing real stress: 2031 bonds yield 7.27%, CDS spreads up 270% in a year, S&P rating cut to BBB- in July
  • Debt totals $125.34B; Oracle projects positive free cash flow only in H2 2028, leaning on vendor financing and customer prepayments
  • Analysts frame it as pure execution risk: deliver the $225B FY2030 revenue forecast or face a punitive return to capital markets
