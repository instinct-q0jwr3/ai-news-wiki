In LLM era, can software startup survive?

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-09 · confidence: high

Summary

An Ask HN thread poses the question every small software founder is circling: in the LLM era, can a software startup survive? The poster's version of the dilemma: vibe coding makes turning an idea into software 'just a matter of tokens,' users increasingly expect open source or free tools, and posts about paid software draw instant downvotes - while large companies keep the compute and the customer base.

The thread's answers converge on the usual hard truth, updated for 2026: the code was never the moat. Distribution, promotion, and channels are the hard part ('getting it promoted like nord vpn is the hard part'), and a service model or productized niche beats selling licenses. Development being cheap cuts both ways - it lowers the cost of competition for you and for everyone copying you.

Highlights

  • Ask HN: if building software is nearly free, what's left for a software startup?
  • Consensus answer: distribution and channels are the moat now, not the code; service models beat licenses
  • The anxiety is symmetric: cheap development lowers your costs and your competitors' equally
