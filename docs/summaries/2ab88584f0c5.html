AI models need moral support to make discoveries

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-26 · updated: 2026-10-07 · confidence: high

Summary

Sean Goedecke argues that giving LLMs a personality is simply good engineering, against skeptics - most recently Nathan Beacom - who say models should be explicit tools like calculators, since human-like presentation invites overestimation and AI psychosis.

His evidence is the 2026 flood of LLM-produced mathematical results: what was a once-a-year trickle of disputed proofs is now near-daily. The curious part is the prompting: getting Claude Mythos to produce a cryptographic breakthrough looks like asking 'please come up with a breakthrough' and then checking in every few hours to say 'keep looking'. If encouragement measurably helps, treating the model as something with morale isn't sentimentality - it's what works.

Highlights

  • Thesis: giving LLMs a personality is good engineering, not a hazard
  • Contrast: skeptics want calculator-style tools to avoid overestimation and psychosis
  • 2026: LLM mathematical results went from yearly trickle to near-daily flood
  • The prompting style: ask for a breakthrough, then periodically say 'keep looking'
  • If moral support improves output, personality is an engineering feature
