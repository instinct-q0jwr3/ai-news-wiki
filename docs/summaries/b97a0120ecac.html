The Wizard With the Very Defensible Pond

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-10-01 · confidence: high

Summary

Scott Werner offers a fable about data, analytics and defensibility. A wizard lives beside a small pond - the kind a real-estate listing would call a water feature - and proudly declares it his moat. Across the story, goblins are hired, an apprentice takes notes, and a sorcerer looks over her shoulder, while the wizard argues about whether a bucket is a feature or a bug.

The moral, sharpened in a commenter exchange the author highlights: the wizard had ten years of data on how many frogs there were, but never decided what a good number of frogs was. Dashboards and meticulous counts are not strategy. What belongs in the notebook is what the pond is for, which side to defend, and what 'optimize' means before you say it aloud to a goblin who will wait - patiently, expensively - for an answer you never wrote down.

Highlights

  • A fable: a wizard calls his small pond his moat
  • Satire of analytics culture and AI-era defensibility talk
  • Ten years of frog counts, no decision about what a good number is
  • Data is not strategy: define what the pond is for before optimizing
  • The goblin waits, at considerable expense, for the answer you haven't written down
