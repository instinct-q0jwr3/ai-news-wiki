Amazon retires the Fire brand for tablets after 15 years, replacing it with Alexa Tablets that run Android and ship on October 14, including the $499 12 Pro

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-08 · updated: 2026-10-18 · confidence: high

Summary

Amazon is retiring the Fire tablet brand after 15 years and replacing it with Alexa Tablets: three Android devices built to put Alexa Plus at the center of the computing experience. All ship October 14: the $229.99 Tablet 8 for reading, the $329.99 Tablet 11 for watching, and the flagship $499.99 Tablet 12 Pro, which hardware chief Panos Panay presents as the real statement of intent.

The big shift is software: these run full Android with Google Play - 'a necessity, end of story', says Panay - instead of Fire OS. The 12 Pro is 6.5mm thick and 550 grams with a MediaTek Dimensity 8400, specs Panay calls secondary to the design reset: he told the team to start from zero with no reused components. Amazon's apps and Prime hooks remain, but the pitch is a proper, AI-forward Android tablet aimed at the iPad.

Highlights

  • Amazon kills the Fire brand after 15 years; new line is called Alexa Tablets.
  • Three models ship October 14: Tablet 8 ($229.99), Tablet 11 ($329.99), Tablet 12 Pro ($499).
  • First Amazon tablets running full Android with Google Play support.
  • Alexa Plus is positioned as the center of the experience.
  • 12 Pro: 6.5mm thick, 550g, Dimensity 8400, 8-16GB RAM, 128-256GB storage.
  • Panos Panay ordered a from-zero redesign with no reused components.
  • Aimed at the iPad Mini, base iPad and 13-inch iPad Pro.
