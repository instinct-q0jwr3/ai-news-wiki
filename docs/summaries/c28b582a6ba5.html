Pluralistic: Autodoxing (02 Nov 2026)

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-02 · updated: 2026-11-05 · confidence: high

Summary

Cory Doctorow's 'Autodoxing' is a history lesson with a bill attached. For twenty-five years, he writes, the wreckage of bad tech policy was both foreseeable and foreseen: when Scott McNealy declared privacy dead, digital rights advocates answered that a society-wide abolition of privacy sets up infinite harms. Clinton tried to backdoor every microchip; the NSA banned civilian access to working encryption; when that fell, governments encouraged the private sector to spy without limit, and anticircumvention law made it illegal to reverse-engineer our own devices to bolt privacy back on. The resulting private troves of intimate data became a warrantless, off-the-books surveillance system that cops and spies tap at will - and the firms holding them cannot keep them safe. Exhibit A is Equifax, which doxed 148 million Americans nearly a decade ago and has since been allowed to absorb more commercial surveillance firms on three continents.

The column's turn is what happens when that machinery meets AI-era identification: the same harvested data that powers ad-tech and government lookups now lets anyone be unmasked at scale - autodoxing - and the policy response arriving first (age verification, ID mandates) builds yet more identity databases on top of the ones that keep leaking. Doctorow's argument runs the other way: the road out is not more collection but less - the data that is never gathered cannot be breached, subpoenaed or repurposed. After a quarter century of being right too early, the essay reads as a demand that the next pile of identity data not be built at all.

Highlights

  • Doctorow: the harms of abolishing privacy were foreseen - and the bill is arriving as 'autodoxing'
  • History: Clipper chip, the encryption ban, then state-encouraged private surveillance troves
  • Anticircumvention law blocked reverse-engineering our own devices to add privacy back
  • Equifax doxed 148M people and was still allowed to buy surveillance firms in the US, Canada and Brazil
  • AI-era identification turns those troves into mass unmasking of ordinary people
  • Age-verification and ID mandates answer by building more identity databases on the leaky pile
  • The counter-rule: data never gathered cannot be breached, subpoenaed or repurposed
