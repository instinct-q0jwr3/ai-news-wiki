Buckle Up the AI Bubble Is Popping

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-23 · updated: 2026-11-25 · confidence: high

Summary

Cathy O'Neil argues that opposition to data centers and constraints on power, water and permits undermine compute-growth assumptions embedded in AI valuations and financing. Her essay predicts that a mismatch between expected construction and achievable growth could spread through interconnected debt arrangements. She treats Huang's return as insufficient to change that structural problem.

This is an opinion and recession forecast, not an audited model of loan exposures or proof that defaults have begun. The claimed size of the compute-growth shortfall is not derived in a reproducible calculation in the post. Its speculation about an internet failure is separate from evidence of an ongoing outage. Readers should distinguish a proposed risk mechanism from a measured timing or probability of collapse.

Highlights

  • O'Neil links permitting resistance to compute-growth expectations
  • The proposed transmission channel is interconnected financing
  • Recession and debt unwinding are forecasts
  • The asserted shortfall is not backed by a reproduced model
  • No current default wave or internet failure was established
