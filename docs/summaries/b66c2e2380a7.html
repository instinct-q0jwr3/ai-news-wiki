Just a little AI resistance, as a treat

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-03 · updated: 2026-11-08 · confidence: high

Summary

Terence Eden files a small act of resistance: after reporting malformed calendar invites to Ocado through the only channel available - the general customer-service form - he received exactly the response his complaint predicted, a robotic AI-flavored non-answer, followed by a second message insisting 'I promise you there is a real person typing this out!' The post is half comedy, half field report: the supermarket's feedback loop couldn't route a precise bug report to anyone technical, and the human apologist's message is itself indistinguishable from the AI tone it apologizes for.

The frame comes from EMFCamp and Professor Kate Devlin's talk about the 'AI Resist List': loudly complaining about bad uses of AI is itself a useful civic act. Eden's version is deliberately small-bore - calendar files, not existential risk - which is the point. The mundane failures are where the automation of customer contact actually bites, and where documented, specific complaints still occasionally get through.

A tonic of a post, and a quiet empirical note for the 'AI will handle support' ledger: the failure mode isn't the model answering badly, it's the organization removing every path that could route a real bug to a real engineer.
