Developers are downgrading their AI on purpose – and it's working

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-03 · updated: 2026-11-06 · confidence: high

Summary

The New Stack talks to the developers on the receiving end of Anthropic's new Claude Code restrictions, and the story is the collateral damage. After Anthropic confirmed adding two system-prompt sentences to Opus 5.5 to stop recreations of specific existing games, developers report the model now refuses far more: reverse-engineering and software-analysis tasks it previously handled, and even work on their own original games. One developer says paid client projects have stalled; another got back to work only by switching to an older Claude model - the 'downgrading on purpose' of the headline.

Anthropic's statement, posted by Addy Osmani, says original games, genre games and work from a user's own designs are meant to keep working, and invites false-positive reports. But the company has not confirmed whether the reverse-engineering refusals come from the new instructions or from separate cybersecurity restrictions - leaving developers unable to tell which policy they're hitting or whether it's intentional.

The piece captures a structural complaint about agent-era development tools: capabilities can change under you overnight, via a prompt edit you can't see, announced after the fact on social media. 'Work that was possible one day may suddenly be blocked the next.' For teams building paid work on a specific model version, the downgrade workaround is a rational response - and an awkward look for the lab asking enterprises to standardize on its toolchain.

Highlights

  • Developers report Opus 5.5 refusing reverse-engineering and software-analysis tasks beyond the confirmed game-clone limits; some client projects stalled
  • The workaround of record: deliberately downgrading to an older Claude model to restore previous behavior
  • Anthropic has not said whether the analysis refusals are the game-IP sentences or separate cybersecurity restrictions
  • The structural issue: prompt-edit policy changes ship silently and land mid-project, with clarification coming ad hoc on X
