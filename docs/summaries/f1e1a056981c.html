Jony Ive, ex-OpenAI product designer Joey Flynn, and others on Silicon Valley's obsession with "taste" as a moat against AI slop and whether AI can learn it

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-22 · updated: 2026-11-23 · confidence: high

Summary

Jonah Weiner examines Silicon Valley's use of taste as a proposed defense against generic AI output. The accessible opening defines taste as judgment about aesthetic rules and changing cultural conventions, then sets up a disagreement: some see that judgment as a human advantage, while others expect models to learn the patterns behind it. The article also treats the debate as a question of status and self-image among technology elites, not only one of model capability.

The feed describes interviews with Jony Ive, former OpenAI product designer Joey Flynn and others, and the primary page confirms months of reporting, but only the opening of the article was accessible here. No detailed position is assigned to those interviewees without their passages. The opening does not demonstrate either that AI has taste or that taste is a durable commercial moat. This is partial-access cultural reporting and argument, not an aesthetic benchmark or proof of a uniquely human capacity.

Highlights

  • The article asks whether aesthetic judgment can remain a human advantage
  • The opening contrasts human-moat claims with pattern-learning arguments
  • Taste is discussed as cultural judgment and a status concern
  • The feed names Ive and Flynn, but their detailed arguments were not accessible
  • No experimental test of machine taste is established
  • Primary opening plus feed excerpt only; full article not reviewed
