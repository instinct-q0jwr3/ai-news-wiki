Pope: AI must serve humans not become tool of domination

type: news-summary · created: 2026-09-25 · updated: 2026-09-26 · confidence: high

hacker-news

Summary

Pope Leo XIV used a speech at UNESCO headquarters in Paris on Friday to warn that artificial intelligence must 'remain at the service of the human person' rather than become 'yet another instrument of domination and injustice.' AI, he said, risks impoverishing human 'ideas and relationships' through a 'process of dehumanisation', and judging people by the 'criterion of efficiency' undermines human dignity and leads to 'those who are no longer productive' being discarded.

AI has been a prominent theme of his pontificate: in May he published his first encyclical, the 42,300-word 'Magnifica humanitas', on the risks of unchecked AI and the case for regulatory oversight. In Paris he demanded adequate safeguards for children, whose vulnerability must 'never become an opportunity for exploitation' - a live topic in Europe, where school phone bans exist and the Commission's proposed Kids Act could restrict social media for under-13s. He also ranged over war, peace and institutions, warning that scientific progress 'severed from wisdom' can become a weapon of destruction, calling for greater trust in dialogue, restating opposition to nuclear weapons, and describing the right to life as the foundation of every other human right. The visit, part of a four-day France trip, included a meeting with President Macron and ended with a parade, Vespers at Notre-Dame and a youth vigil at the Stade de France.
