The AI boom is making the cheapest smartphones disappear

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-06 · updated: 2026-10-09 · confidence: high

Summary

Rest of World reports that AI data centres are absorbing memory chips and pushing up smartphone costs, so the cheapest phones are disappearing. Existing models cost about 15% more this year and new launches about 25% more. Rises are steepest in India (21%), Asia-Pacific (19%) and the Middle East and Africa (18%), against 5% in the US. Chinese makers, about 60% of global shipments, have cut entry-level projects to chase margins. Counterpoint says phones that cost under $150 may now cost $250 to $300, and GSMA warns this could widen the digital divide for first-time users.

Highlights

  • Memory shortage from AI data centres lifts phone costs.
  • Existing models up about 15%, new ones about 25%.
  • India up 21%, US only 5%.
  • Chinese makers cut sub-$150 products for premium ones.
  • GSMA fears a wider digital divide.
