The personal data of 41,000+ DHS employees and contractors, including CBP and ICE agents, leaks online after hackers claim they breached DHS' Okta login system

type: news-summary · created: 2026-10-29 · updated: 2026-10-31 · confidence: high

Summary

Wired reports an unprecedented leak of DHS records exposing personal data of more than 41,000 employees and contractors across the department and seven component agencies including ICE and CBP: email addresses, phone numbers, office addresses, plus references to internal systems and sensitive details on undercover and surveillance programs - a map of how DHS operates.

Transparency nonprofit DDoSecrets published the archive, called 'Icepick', after previewing it with researcher maia arson crimew; the group behind it calls itself the Department of Peace and claims the data comes from DHS's Okta login system. Okta says it has no evidence of a breach. The leak lands as DHS aggressively pursues its own critics - unmasking anonymous accounts and seeking protesters' records.

Highlights

  • 41,000+ DHS employee and contractor records leaked
  • Includes ICE/CBP facility locations and undercover program references
  • Hackers ('Department of Peace') claim DHS Okta breach; Okta denies
  • Published by DDoSecrets as the 'Icepick' archive
