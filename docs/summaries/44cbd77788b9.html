I'm Not Convinced the AI Bubble Exists

type: news-summary · created: 2026-11-09 · updated: 2026-11-10 · confidence: high

Summary

A blogger argues the AI bubble may not exist. He grants that AI companies have committed to at least $3 trillion and spent an estimated $1.9 trillion, around $5 trillion in total, and that opaque financing vehicles make true costs hard to see, which he calls the strongest point for the bubble view. But he says spending need not continue: most of it is one-off data center build-out, not regular operating cost, so it should fall once the buildings are up, and AI is otherwise cheap to run.

His claim is that the 'any day now' pop is taken for granted online, while there is a realistic chance that growth is justified with more room left. Only the first part of the post was readable, so the rest of his case is not covered.

Highlights

  • Commitments of $3T+ and about $1.9T already spent, roughly $5T in all.
  • Opaque financing makes real costs hard to know.
  • Build-out spending is one-off, so it can drop once data centers exist.
  • He sees a realistic chance the growth is justified.
  • Only the opening was readable.
