Simon Willison notes that Python 3.15.0 has been added to actions/python-versions, which means you can now add '3.15' to a GitHub Actions testing matrix and run tests against the new release. (read more).

Agent Standup turns a Claude Code session into a live team meeting: the lead agent gives orders, sub-agents join, share their screen and report back, so you can see who is waiting on whom and who is blocked, at zero token cost. Its makers call it a bit silly and weirdly useful. (read more).

ManimGX reimplements Manim CE's API on a Rust and wgpu engine for making 3D videos in Python, aimed at the video-as-code workflow that took off with the Opus 5.5 release. On their benchmark, 36.2 seconds of video across five scenes rendered in 3.08 seconds at 1080p60 on an Apple M4 Pro: 90.6x faster than ManimCE, 11.5x faster than ManimGL and 18.7x faster than Blender Workbench. (read more).

Research, models and money

Models fall short of a human algorithmic innovation, Microsoft ships a decision model, and two raises land.

Microsoft unveils Microsoft-Decision-1, a fast decision-scoring model trained on Qwen3.5-9B, available in Microsoft Foundry and through OpenRouter, and says it will soon rebase it on MAI, OpenAI and other models. (read more).

General Medicine, an online storefront for medical care founded by PillPack founders TJ Parker and Elliot Cohen, has raised a $120 million Series B led by a16z. The thesis: AI makes healthcare advice easy to access, but turning advice into actual care remains the hard part, and few companies have built the marketplace and fulfillment layer required. (read more).

Rein Security, an Israeli startup founded in 2024 by Matan Bar-Efrat and Netanel Rubin, raised a $25 million Series A co-led by Glilot Capital and Sienna Venture Capital, bringing its total funding to $35 million. The company builds runtime tools to secure enterprise AI agents and to defend against adversarial AI agents, and says revenue has grown eightfold since January, with its platform now securing thousands of agents executing millions of actions. (read more).

Humans, tools and the AI backlash

Today's reading is about what AI does to people and creative work: a premium on human-made art, a study on persistence, an actor refusing an AI waiver, and an AI-built Adobe clone.

A UC Berkeley news piece describes a peer-reviewed study, presented at the Conference on Language Modeling, which found that about ten minutes of relying on an AI assistant makes people less able to persist at hard tasks. Co-author Brian Christian, of the Center for Human-Compatible AI, says the result matches a feeling many people already have. (read more).

The LA Times reports that Nicolas Cage said at New York Comic Con that he refused to sign an AI waiver for the Amazon-backed series Spider-Noir and does not expect to work with the studio again. He linked it to Amazon's reported 50 billion dollar investment in OpenAI and called himself 'not an AI-friendly actor'. Amazon had not responded to a request for comment. (read more).

A BBC technology column argues that as generative AI floods creative fields, people are starting to pay extra attention to work that is visibly made by humans. The author opens with a night he DJs in Falmouth, where the live, one-off, human nature of the event is the point, and uses it to ask whether audiences care who or what made the art they enjoy. (read more).

PetaPixel reports that software engineer Brandon Thomas released ArtCraft, a free open-source set of apps modelled on Adobe's Creative Cloud: Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere, Lightroom, Acrobat, After Effects and InDesign equivalents for macOS, Windows and Linux. He says he built them in Rust with heavy help from an Anthropic model, while stressing he is an experienced engineer and not a casual 'vibe coder'. (read more).

Robots, data and road tolls

Physical AI funding built on filmed human work, plus a data page on European driving costs.

Basis: headline and feed excerpt only; the Financial Times article could not be fetched. The report, by Rafe Rosner-Uddin, cites PitchBook data that robotics and physical AI companies have raised about 48 billion dollars so far this year. It says these developers are putting cameras in homes, offices and factories and recording people doing tasks, to fill a gap in training data for robots. (read more 🔒).

A Copilot Travel reference page compares what a car driver pays for motorways, vignettes, tolled bridges and fuel across Europe in 2026. France is the most expensive per kilometre, at about 11 euros per 100 km. Eleven countries have free motorways for cars, five of them with tolled bridges, tunnels or city charges, and eight sell a time-limited sticker instead. (read more).
