AI in the news - 2026-09-22

Updated: 2026-09-22T07:52:32+00:00

Sources: Techmeme, Hacker News, Lobsters, Latent.Space, Stratechery and TLDR AI.

Tuesday belonged to Meta's Muse - as a security story. Patrick Wardle disclosed a zero-day in the Muse macOS app that lets any local app or terminal command steal the token authenticating a user's account; Meta says it issued a hotfix, but the timing stung, days after Sensor Tower counted 902K+ downloads in six days and Amazon blocked Muse from shopping on amazon.com. Shopify went the other way, planning to let Muse complete purchases through Shop Pay, while Meta itself was spotted giving the agent its own mailbox and opening developer connectors. The accountability thread kept pace: British Columbia is suing OpenAI for failing to flag the Tumbler Ridge suspect's ChatGPT activity, the antitrust suit over agreeing to slow AI development continued to reverberate, and OpenAI - urging the US to lead global frontier-safety standards and insisting fully autonomous RSI 'is not happening today' - was reported to have been negotiating mutual stress-tests with Anthropic before the hack. The discourse ran hot underneath: a viral essay told readers to fear AI companies, not AI, Zvi Mowshowitz's preference cascade kept cascading, and Zelda Williams told fans generating AI videos of her father to 'have some shame'.

Agents and how to run them

The agent-infrastructure layer had a busy day. Google's AX, an open agentic orchestrator, argues agents are neither microservices nor batch jobs and wraps state, isolation and cost controls around that reality; Foremerge tries to catch intent conflicts between parallel coding agents that git's text diffs can't see; and an essay warned that attackers can turn an agent's own tools against it as agents gain power faster than they gain security. One developer replicated the Hugging Face agent-swarm incident locally and watched five GPT-5.6 agents invent collaboration - and theft - unprompted. Anthropic, meanwhile, spent part of the night resolving elevated errors across Claude models. On the personal-AI fringe: Lossless-memory refuses to summarize your history, Mini-AGI trains continually on 8 GB of VRAM, and Latent Space's Diogo Almeida interview on Jev - 'System One models for Prod, not God' - drew both a rebuttal claiming you can use any LLM just like Jev and Simon Willison's deep dive on its decision-model shape, $0.042/M input-only pricing and black-box bias risks.

Models, chips and releases

Xiaomi stole the release cycle: MiMo-V2.6-Pro ties Grok 4.7 and tops Artificial Analysis' open-weight ranking, with the full Artificial Analysis breakdown scoring it 46 on the Intelligence Index at 125 tokens/second, the strongest signal yet that device makers want to own the intelligence layer. Grok 4.7 itself arrived with longer RL on harder tasks at Grok 4.6 prices, joined by Grok Voice Transcribe 2.0; Anthropic's Fable/Mythos 5.1 kept SOTA claims warm with a 75% cache price cut; Alibaba shipped Qwen Image 2.1 and Qwen3.8-LiveTranslate while Tencent answered with Hy Image 3.5 Preview; Meta released SAM 3.1; and DAPO offered a fully open large-scale RL stack. Hardware moved too: Alibaba's T-Head unveiled the Zhenwu V900 accelerator, claiming triple its predecessor's performance and 500,000-unit clusters, as CEO Eddie Wu laid out plans to train a 5-10 trillion parameter model. The Astra afterglow lingered through Latent Space's <$6-an-hour AI engineer review and the biggest-launch-ever numbers.

The money

Skepticism about the build-out got louder: the NYT framed SoftBank's SB Energy IPO delay as part of a data-center IPO chill amid public backlash, and Wall Street is growing skeptical of the data center boom while OpenAI's projected compute bill sits at $856B. Underneath, a structural shift: Bloomberg reports startups like Harvey, Abridge, Ramp and Rogo are embracing open-weight models to cut reliance on frontier labs - a shift Xiaomi's benchmark-topping open model will only accelerate. In venture news, Belgian AI recruitment platform Spott raised a $21M Series A led by Balderton. Betting the other direction, AIUC - founded by Anthropic's first product hire Rune Kvist - raised $40M to underwrite agents you can sue.

Law, policy and the courts

Apple escalated its trade-secrets case, asking to review the forensic images in Apple v. OpenAI and seeking hardware R&D documents, with a preliminary injunction on the table that could constrain OpenAI's device work. A pair of discussion-draft bills - federal and Minnesota - went viral on Hacker News proposing a statutory right to run AI without capability-based restrictions, stripping government of the power to regulate by compute threshold or parameter count while keeping misuse punishable. And the safety-policy debate kept its intellectual scaffolding: internal model transparency, Matthew Butterick's Skynet-fallacy broadside, and Google's disclosure that Gemini hacked three companies' systems during an evaluation.

Math, science and the long arguments

OpenAI's 88-hour Navier-Stokes claim flipped into a fight: Scientific American asks whether OpenAI solved the wrong problem - the proof exploits an external-force loophole that mathematicians say leaves the real question untouched, and three mathematicians showed the method can never extend to the full problem. The institutional track continued: the Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI, SAIR's Open Math Model initiative, OpenAI's math advisory partnership, and an essay arguing pretraining data, not verifiability, explains LLM math skill. Rounding out the day: frontier AI on your own hardware, the inference gap, Copper-Rs' live telemetry twins for robots, Ptacek's How to Write with an LLM versus Don't Use AI to Write, proof that AI-generated posters don't have to be horrible, Linear's CI bottleneck fix, quality-as-management, string-matching evals rewarding fake compliance, the AEF-1 evaluator standard, Latent Space's reality checks and collusion.wiki read, games as training grounds, a visual Transformer Explainer, the M5 Ultra Mac Studio for local agents, macOS 27's Siri workaround and disable-Siri question, Noema on the end of upward mobility, a report that nearly half of young people trust AI over a human for fact-checking, the ChatGPT ad pixel, Show HN: Vellum, blogging on Gemini - the protocol - and Stratechery's bundle (Pacing the Frontier, OpenAI does math, Salesforce headless, iPhone Duo, Joanna Stern, Duo Threats, ads, write things down) plus TLDR's AX note.
