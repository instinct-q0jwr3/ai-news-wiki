Explicit loops and configurable endpoints

A robotics programming argument and a small Datasette release favor visible control boundaries.

A long-time FIRST Robotics mentor proposes Periodic-Oriented Programming: keep robot behavior readable in a top-to-bottom periodic loop, with a small number of explicit subsystem boundaries rather than many command abstractions. Subsystem methods set desired state, while centralized periodic logic validates constraints and updates hardware consistently. The argument is aimed at teaching and maintaining FRC robots, drawing on the author’s own experience. Its safety benefits depend on implementing those boundaries correctly; one function or fewer abstractions does not by itself prove a robot safe or make this the right structure for every application. (read more).

Simon Willison announces OpenAI-compatible endpoint support in datasette-llm 0.1a11, the configuration layer used by other Datasette plugins. This lets a Datasette application select a locally or remotely hosted model through the same API-style interface. The example combines Datasette Agent with a local model server. Compatibility with the protocol is the key addition, not a claim that every model supports the same tools, performs equally well or keeps data local when pointed at a remote endpoint. (read more).

Measure outcomes and train precise tasks

Enterprise analytics and industrial robots pursue different forms of applied value.

Tech.eu reports Nebuly’s $21 million Series A led by Planven for measuring the business impact of enterprise AI agents. The platform aims to connect agent activity with time savings, work capacity and customer-facing revenue rather than treating token counts or adoption as outcomes. The report says finance and leadership teams can change assumptions behind the calculations, and describes private-cloud or on-premises operation. Those capabilities do not by themselves establish causal productivity gains: the quality of the inputs, assumptions and comparison remains central to any return-on-investment estimate. (read more).

Upstarts reports Nidus’s $30 million across pre-seed and seed rounds to develop robotic automation for defense-industrial manufacturing. The team begins with wire-harness tasks, using repeated teleoperation to collect training examples for precise work inside existing production processes. The reporter describes trying the task, while bookings and customer demand are company claims involving unnamed customers. The funding and demonstration show an industrial focus, not verified deployment at scale or proof that the same model can automate every manufacturing task. (read more).
