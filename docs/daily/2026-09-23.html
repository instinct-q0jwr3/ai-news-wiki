Builder culture: Jev in 25 lines

Concepts: AI coding agents and the software pipeline · Agentic systems

A tiny typed-decision-model demo, the AGENTS.md telemetry bug and its fix, and fatigue with 'Claudisms'.

A skeptical counter to the Jev hype: the author reimplements the idea behind decision-type models in 25 lines of Python. The script loads a small GGUF model (Qwen3-0.6B via llama-cpp-python), prompts it to choose among labeled options (e.g. Legitimate / Spam / Phishing for an email), then reads the logits of the label tokens, converts them to log-probabilities and picks the argmax. The point: constrained-choice 'decision' inference is a logit-comparison trick any developer can reproduce, not a new paradigm. (read more).

A developer investigation shows Claude Code's new AGENTS.md support silently depends on a server-side feature flag: the loader ships as a built-in plugin whose availability asks a remote flag (tengu_agents_md_mod) with fallback false. With telemetry or non-essential traffic disabled (DISABLE_TELEMETRY=1 or CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_NONESSENTIAL_TRAFFIC=1), the flag never arrives and AGENTS.md is never read, even though reading a local markdown file needs no network. The post documents canary-word experiments across configurations and notes the first session in a new configuration only fetches the flag while the second uses it. (read more; same source, alternate index).

Kristian Polso's five-month check-in on AI-assisted development vents about 'Claudisms': bloated PR descriptions, overly complex code and inlined comment slop, shipped fast under management mandates to 'just ship things'. He assigns most blame to management, which hears velocity promises from executives and AI salespeople and measures lines of code rather than readability or maintainability. His prediction: the industry sits at the top of an AI-assisted development bell curve, and a correction toward quality is coming. (read more).

The funding cascade

Concepts: Ai Business Models

Cyera's $400M, Micro1 at $4B, Firecrawl's $75M, Ema's raise - an unusually dense day of AI financing.

Data-security startup Cyera raised $400 million from Goldman Sachs in a Series G extension, the Wall Street Journal reports, bringing its total funding since June 2025 to $1.94 billion. The raise comes as Wall Street anxiety about AI drives an investor surge into security. (read more 🔒).

AI training-data startup Micro1 raised more than $100 million at a $4 billion valuation, Forbes reports, up from a $500 million valuation in September 2025. Ali Ansari's company began last year as a recruiting business with $7 million in ARR and now generates over $500 million in ARR; two xAI cofounders invested in the latest round. (read more ⚠).

Firecrawl, which builds web-scraping tooling for AI agents, raised a $75 million Series B led by LA-based Smash Ventures (reported as Smash Capital), joined by Y Combinator, Altos Ventures, Nexus Venture Partners, Freestyle and Offline Ventures. Its platform targets the practical problems of collecting web data at agent scale: an agent-optimized search engine that filters pages by creation date and file type, a 'smart wait' that avoids scraping pages before they finish loading, and automatic scrolling, clicking and form submission for interactive content. (read more).

Ema, a startup building 'AI employees' - systems that coordinate multiple AI agents to carry out multi-step business processes across HR, IT and finance - raised $77M in a Series B led by Bengaluru-based Creaegis, with existing investors Accel, Section 32 and Prosus increasing their stakes. The all-primary-equity round takes total funding to $140M and more than quadruples the 2024 valuation (undisclosed). (read more).
