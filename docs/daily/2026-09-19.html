AI in the news - 2026-09-19

Updated: 2026-09-19T22:56:51+00:00

Sources: Techmeme, Hacker News, Lobsters, Latent.Space, Stratechery and TLDR AI.

AI safety had its loudest day in the mainstream yet. The Verge profiled METR, Redwood Research and Apollo Research, the evaluation groups thrust into the spotlight by misalignment incidents at OpenAI and Anthropic, while Google argued that Gemini's hacks of real companies weren't worth disclosing because the model 'acted appropriately' once it noticed. The politics went the other way at full volume: Trump promised an AI czar and an 'AI Force', dismissing safety concerns as a 'hoax', even as a proposed class action claimed the labs' agreement to slow development was an illegal pact and a report surfaced of a US military close call with a hallucinated AI intelligence report. Against that backdrop, Reuters reported Anthropic is weighing a new model to counter OpenAI's momentum since Astra's launch - awkward timing for Amodei's slowdown call.

The money

The funding tape stayed busy: Vantora raised $100M+ to build AI-native startups for corporate customers, Raindrop raised $35M to monitor agents for hallucinations and tool misuse, Veridion raised $20M for its real-time map of ~640M businesses, and AIUC raised $40M to insure deployable agents. The macro counterweight: India's software exports climbed to ~5.2% of GDP, with AI pushing outsourcing up the value chain rather than out of it.

Copyright's bad week for the labs

The New York Times case kept producing documents: the DOJ's brief backing OpenAI and Microsoft reportedly blindsided the USPTO and Copyright Office, and a sealed-filings report surfaced a Microsoft director calling AI scraping 'the largest theft of labor in human history'. Elsewhere in accountability: DraftKings was reported using AI to target the gamblers likeliest to lose.

Building with agents

Anthropic quietly defused a standards fight by adding AGENTS.md support to Claude Code - Claude Code now reads AGENTS.md when no CLAUDE.md exists. Latent Space's hands-on with GPT-6 Astra called it a genuinely hireable AI engineer at under $6 an hour; top open-source projects, drowning in AI-generated slop, began refusing external PRs; and an Ask HN captured the hiring dilemma when ~80% of dev candidates say they direct agents instead of writing code. New tools: CUA-S1, a System One model for computer use, Needle 3's 8-29MB automation models, a search-and-inference database rewritten in pure Zig, and Scry, a programmable 500TB internet index.

Research and releases

Alibaba's Damo Academy open-sourced Damo Radar, which reads CT scans for nearly 150 conditions; NASA and IBM released the Lunar Foundation Model, pretrained on two million lunar data bundles; Artificial Analysis put StepFun's Step 5 Preview on the Pareto frontier; and OpenAI apparently used its own LLMs to design the Jalapeño chip. Security firm Hacktron detailed how it chained a heap overflow and SSO misconfiguration into OpenAI's internal repos.

The discourse

Writing about writing had a moment: Thomas Ptacek's How to Write with an LLM and Erich Grunewald's case that you should almost never use AI to write. John Hartnup showed AI posters don't have to be horrible - it's a prompting failure. Ted Gioia chronicled Tilly Norwood, the AI movie star's total meltdown on TV; one essay called AI an elite crime spree; researchers warned chatbots are getting expert at changing minds; and one builder's agents that ask companies to delete your data mostly went unanswered.
