Editing the thinking loop

A weight-editing experiment separates benchmark gains from changes to reasoning habits.

Beaker reports experiments in which an agent edits a small language model's weights directly, without gradient-based training. On Qwen3.5-0.8B, a one-neuron change reduced a repetitive thinking habit and raised MATH-500 accuracy from 7.4% to 37.4% in one run. A simple decoding ban on the recurring token achieved a similar result, suggesting much of the gain came from avoiding wasted reasoning rather than adding mathematical ability. The team also combined edits, reproduced a stronger MIT result and tested brevity and abstention goals. The tradeoffs matter: shorter answers often lost accuracy, and a score could miss unwanted side effects. These are experiments on a small model and selected tasks, not evidence that direct weight editing generally improves every capability. (read more).

Choices become interface building blocks

Decision models and native interface protocols divide work between models and application code.

AINews frames November 11 as a convergence around decision models: Google launches Gemini Decision 1, while conference demonstrations show classifiers taking over narrow routing and extraction tasks from larger reasoning models. The newsletter contrasts Google's favorable launch benchmarks with a more mixed independent evaluation, where task cost, latency and document-level accuracy differ across competitors. The architectural lesson is to assign fast bounded choices to a classifier and reserve a generative model for work that needs it. Google releases A2UI 1.0 as the first stable version of an open protocol for agent-generated interfaces. An agent describes a view, while the host application renders it with its own components. Testing a biological warning system

An offline model proposes threat scores, with validation still in progress.

Wired reports that it combines predictions about pathogenicity, possible engineering and taxonomy into a threat score. The proposed advantage is recognizing functional patterns beyond exact matches to known organisms. RamaSite packages a small content-management system around Laravel, Filament and SQLite. Research beyond visible words

Cell-specific genetics, filler-token computation and derived kernels.

DeepMind introduces AlphaGenome 2 as a conditional DNA-sequence model that predicts effects for a specific cell type or state rather than broad tissue averages. The announcement says it was trained on more than 100 million cell profiles across 68 tissues and can condition predictions on a researcher's own single-cell data without a new training run. The intended use is to prioritize and interpret variants in disease-relevant cells. DeepMind reports improved detection of variants affecting gene expression, but model predictions are not clinical confirmation of causality. Access is described as available for non-commercial research, with commercial use in private preview. (read more).

A research update investigates whether language models can perform multistep reasoning while intermediate positions contain repeated filler tokens rather than visible explanations. The authors adapt existing models with LoRA and train internal positions against reasoning targets, using a setup that differs from ordinary next-token generation. The work matters because unrelated-looking output need not imply that no computation is occurring between positions. It also separates filler-token reasoning from steganography, where information is encoded in the emitted words themselves. The experiments do not establish arbitrary serial computation without limits, nor that ordinary deployed models routinely reason this way. (read more).

The tang project argues that GPU kernels can be derived from a compact mathematical representation rather than hand-written separately for every operator and device. Its representation uses affine views and reductions, with fusion and differentiation expressed as transformations and hardware choices handled by schedules. The article reports results across several backends and is explicit about weaknesses: derived kernels sometimes match or beat its baselines, but attention can be much slower than specialist implementations, and one integration misses an agreement threshold. Formal results cover particular rewrite and memory-bound properties, not universal optimality or correctness of every generated program. (read more).
